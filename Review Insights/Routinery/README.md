# Routinery — Review Insights

Written by Claude (Claude Code), 26 November 2026, at the user's request.

What Routinery's store reviews tell us about **who uses a routine app, why, and what they pay for**, as research for a
new product. Routinery Corp.'s routine timer app ("Routine Planner, Habit Tracker"; it began in Korea in 2019 as
굿모닝, "Good Morning"). App Store `1450486923`, Google Play `com.alt.goodmorning`, 9,644 reviews collected 8 September
2026. The data is the checked labels in [`Review Labels/Routinery/`](<../../Review Labels/Routinery/App.md>).

**What holds every year:** people use it because it **walks them through a routine one step at a time with a timer**,
so they don't have to decide, remember or find willpower. **Payers mostly pay for the whole app**, not a feature;
when they name one, it's **more routines** (the free plan allows two). Objections are **subscription instead of a
one-time price**, and price. **Korean reviewers pay at 3–4× the English rate.**

**2023 in one line:** the same pains, a younger crowd (school mornings, a July wave of short "glow up" reviews), fewer
people with ADHD (30%, from 45%), and **payers who mostly write because the paid app broke**: their average rating fell
to 2.97★ (3.91★ in 2022), 54 of 71 report a bug, and unexpected charges rose to 20 (6 in 2022). Ads and, in a few
reviews, a one-routine free plan appear for the first time.

**2024 in one line:** people with ADHD (42%) and people who lose time to their phone (33%, the most ever) are most of
the users; students fell away (10, from 31). **Payers rate it 2.72★**, only 10 of 46 give a reason to pay and none
pays for more routines; 9 of 21 Korean payers want a refund and 22 people were charged unexpectedly. **Bugs are now the
top reason to refuse** (30% of reviews report one), and an AI chatbot and forced logins are new dislikes.

**2025 in one line:** the most people naming a pain point of any year (237), **81 with ADHD**, and the timer on every
step liked by 103 (21 of them with ADHD). The rating recovered (4.06★) and bugs fell (23%), and 29 payers give a reason
to pay, but the paywall moved **to the trial and ads**: 15 of 22 unexpected charges came from a free trial, the trial
is the top reason to refuse, and free users now watch ads to archive or swap routines. Social features are a new
dislike.
