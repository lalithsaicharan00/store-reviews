# Today Screen, Question 10: Switching Day, Week, Month, Year — How Often, and Where

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 24 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion.

**Screen.** The round-1 design puts a full-width **Day · Week · Month · Year** segmented control on the top row (Question 4).

**The question.** Do people switch views often, or pick one and stay there? If switching is rare, a whole row is being spent on it.

**Basis.**
- **Screened:** all 1,487,223 reviews for default or start-up views, "remember my view", and switching between views.
- **Read in full:** all 122 habit-app matches.
- **Re-read:** the round-1 view codes (688 reviews).
- **External:**
  - Apple's pop-up button and segmented control guidance;
  - Google Calendar's help pages;
  - the 49-app census.

---

## What to do

1. **Take the view control off the top row.** The title itself becomes the control: **"Today ⌄"**. It names what you're looking at, and the chevron says it can be changed.
2. **Tapping the title opens one "go to" sheet** holding the two rare jobs together:
   - **Day · Week · Month · Year** at the top;
   - a **month calendar** with a ring on each day, to jump to any date;
   - a **Today** button.
3. **Remember the last view,** and let people choose the one the app opens on in Settings. Most people pick one and keep it.
4. **Keep the everyday jobs out of the sheet.** Moving between recent days stays on the week strip (Question 12). Coming back to today stays one tap (Question 14).
5. **This revises round 1.** Question 4 put the switcher on the top row and advised against a title menu. That advice read 27 "love switching views" reviews as praise for one-tap switching. On a second read, 23 of them praise *having* several views, and only 4 mention switching itself. A title with a visible chevron is not the hidden menu those reviews complained about.

---

## Key findings

**1. People set a view and stay on it.**

| What reviewers say | Reviews | Apps |
|---|---|---|
| Want to choose the view or tab the app opens on | 20 | 13 |
| Want the app to remember the last view or filter | 7 | 3 |
| Switch between views often, or want it faster | 2 | 2 |

The first row combines round 2 (16) and round 1 (10), with duplicates removed.

- "It is bad experience to switch tab every time I start the app" `A85#1698`
- "can i set the default view to be monthly view rather than weekly one?" `P130#175`
- "I wish there was a way to either select a default view like always show my monthly calendar" `A23#4099`
- **Preferences are split:** month is the most-named favourite (28), then week (17), list (5) and year (3), from round 1. So the app needs all four views, but each person mostly uses one.

**2. Having several views is praised, and that doesn't need a row of buttons.**
- 23 of the 27 round-1 "views" reviews praise that the views exist. For example, "I like the different views-daily, weekly, monthly." `A55#1875`
- Only a few mention switching itself: "I can easily switch views" `P9#319`.

**3. What people punish is hiding the everyday controls, not the views.** When Habitify tucked its **day** bar away, reviews turned sharp:
- "Now I have to tap a disclosure triangle to show the top bar first." `A33#3440`
- "Difficult to sort through dates (need to use drop down now)" `A33#1725`

So day-to-day movement stays visible (the week strip), and only the rare scale change moves into the title.

**4. Familiar apps do exactly this.**
- **Google Calendar:** the views live in a menu. "At the top left, tap the month name" opens a calendar to jump to a date, and "Jump to today" sits at the top right.
- **Apple:** a pop-up button presents "a flat list of mutually exclusive options". It "can update its content to indicate the current selection". Apple also says: "Consider using a pop-up button when space is limited and you don't need to display all options all the time."
- **Segmented controls:** Apple's example is the Health app's time ranges. There, people switch constantly while comparing charts, which is not our case.

---

**Limits.**
- **Reviews don't measure how often people tap.** "Pick one and stay" comes from how people describe their use. Confirm it with analytics after launch: switches per active day.
- **Small counts:** the counts are small (20 against 2). The direction is clear, but it is not a precise ratio.
