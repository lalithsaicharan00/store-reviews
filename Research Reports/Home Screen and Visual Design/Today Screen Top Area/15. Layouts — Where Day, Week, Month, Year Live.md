# Today Screen, Question 15: Layouts — Where Day, Week, Month, Year Live

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 24 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion. Round 3.

**Screen (round 2).**
- Tapping the title "Today ⌄" opens a "Go to" sheet.
- The sheet has **Day · Week · Month · Year** above a month calendar.

**The feedback.**
- The calendar is right, and it should stay.
- Day, Week, Month and Year are not dates. They are **layouts of the habit cards** (a list, a week grid, month calendars, year heatmaps).
- Next to a calendar, they read as "pick a week or a month", which is wrong.

**The question.** Where should the layout choice live so it reads as a layout, costs no permanent space (it's rarely changed), and is easy to find when someone looks for it?

**Basis.**
- **Reused:**
  - round 2's view-frequency finding: set once, 20 reviews against 2 (Question 10);
  - round 1's view preferences.
- **Read in full:** 43 new matches on switching layouts.
- **External:** how Apple Reminders and Files and Todoist place layout choices.

---

## What to do

1. **Take the layouts out of the calendar sheet.** Tapping the title opens only the calendar and **Today**: a "go to a date" job.
2. **Put layouts at the top of the options sheet** behind the button next to +, and name that sheet **View**. It then holds two clearly separate parts:
   - **Layout**, how cards look: four tiles, each with a tiny picture and a plain name:
     - **Day list**;
     - **Week grid**;
     - **Month calendars**;
     - **Year heatmap**.
   - **Show**, which habits appear: day sections, groups and Hide completed (Questions 17–18).
3. **Name layouts by what you'll see, not by a time word alone.**
   - "Week grid" says it's a shape of the screen. "Week" alone reads like a date range.
   - The picture on each tile removes any doubt.
4. **The choice sticks.** The app reopens on the last layout, and the default can be set in Me → Settings.
5. **The button is an icon of sliders**, the common "view options" symbol. It shows a small count only when a filter is on, so the layout choice alone never looks like a filter.

---

## Key findings

**1. Layouts are chosen once and kept, but people do want them.**
- Round 2 found 20 reviews asking to set the view the app opens on, against 2 that switch often.
- Settings-only placement has its own problem: people set it and then can't tell why it doesn't apply. "always opening on monthly view even though I have weekly view selected in settings" `P24#3789`
- Some are praised just for having the options: "I love the simplicity of it and the daily/weekly/monthly view options." `A55#1754`
- People look for them when a list grows: "It would be amazing if there was an option to change the layout so you can have a better overview." `P8#6616`

**2. The time words alone confuse layout with date.**
- One reviewer wanted a swipe to change "from weekly to to monthly goals", and complained that "Right now the swipe changes the date" `A31#2245`
- Layout and date are two different axes. They need two different controls, and the words must keep them apart.

**3. Familiar apps put layout next to "what to show", in one options menu.**
- **Apple Reminders:** the list's "•••" menu holds "View as Columns" alongside "Show Completed".
- **Todoist:** the Display menu groups layout (list, board, calendar) with sorting, grouping and filters.
- These are the patterns people already know for "change how this list looks".

**4. Why not the alternatives.**
- **A third header button:** round 1 removed an unlabeled layout icon (▦) because it was ambiguous and added clutter (Question 7).
- **Settings only:** too far for an occasional change, and the complaint above shows it confuses people.
- **The title menu:** that is where the date lives. Mixing the two caused this round's feedback.

---

**Limits.**
- **Small counts:** direct reviews about where the layout control sits are few (13 in this round). The placement leans on Apple's and Todoist's conventions.
- **Test the names:** check that the four tile names read as layouts with first-time users.
