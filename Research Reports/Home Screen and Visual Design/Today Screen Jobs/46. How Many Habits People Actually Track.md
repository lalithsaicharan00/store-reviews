# Today Screen, Question 46: How Many Habits People Actually Track

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 25 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision. This is data only; nothing here is designed.** Decisions live in Notion.

**The question.**
- We design for everyone, not for the extremes. So we need real numbers, not guesses:
  - the fewest habits people track;
  - the most, in practice rather than in theory;
  - the average and the most common number;
  - what percentage of people track how many.
- Then we can see who most people are, and which cases are rare.

**Basis.**
- **Screened:** every habit-app review in our App Store and Play Store corpora (part of the 1,487,223 reviews). A review matched if it had a number within two words of "habit(s)". That gave 6,084 matches.
- **Read by hand:** 5,559 of them. Left out: 525 whose only number was "I tried 10 habit apps".
- **Coded:** 1,231 statements about counts. Every quote was checked against its review by script.
- **Main measure:** **446 people who say how many habits they track**, leaving out anyone whose count was set by a free-plan limit (104 more).
- **External:** searched for published usage data (app makers, research papers). Almost none exists; what was found is in section 6.

Citations such as `A1#994` point to line 994 (from 0) of `App Store Reviews/1. …/reviews.jsonl`. `P` means Play Store.

## The short answer

| Measure | Habits |
|---|---|
| **Fewest** | **1** (7% of people) |
| **Most common (mode)** | **3** (20%) |
| **Median (the middle person)** | **5** |
| **Average (mean)** | **9**, pulled up by a few very large lists |
| **Middle half of people** | **3 to 10** |
| **Middle 80%** | **2 to about 16** |
| **95% of people have at most** | **about 24** |
| **Largest real lists** | 50, 60, 100, 120, 300 (1 in 50 people) |

- **Half of people track 5 or fewer.**
- **Four in five track 10 or fewer.**
- **Nine in ten track 15 or fewer.**
- **Past 20 habits is about 1 person in 20.**

## 2. What pushes the numbers down: free-plan limits

- **The spike at 3 is partly price.**
  - Many apps stop the free plan at 3 habits: Productive, Done, Habitify, Way of Life, Everyday, Atoms Pro.
  - **42 of the 81 people who say exactly "3" use one of those apps.**
  - Leave those apps out and the **median is still 5** (286 people).
- **104 more people** give a count set by a free-plan limit, like "I added 3 habits, and then it told me I had to subscribe" `A20#282`. They are left out of the table above.
  - Their counts are cut short: median 4, mostly 3–5. By definition they wanted more.
- **Where limits don't apply, the middle is higher:**
  - Loop, which is free with no limit: median **6** (24 people);
  - Streaks, paid with a cap: median **6**;
  - Habit Tracker, whose free plan allows 5–6: median **8** among people not at the limit.
- **So:** without a free-plan limit, the typical engaged user has **about 5 or 6 habits**, not 3.

## 3. How many is "enough"? What people say about limits

When an app caps the number of habits, reviewers say whether that cap is enough.

| Cap | "That's enough" | "Not enough" | Share saying not enough |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 46 | 63 | 58% |
| 4 | 10 | 19 | 66% |
| **5** | **65** | **69** | **51%** |
| 6 | 30 | 24 | 44% |
| 12 | 12 | 24 | 67% (heavy Streaks users) |

**What this shows:**
- **At 5, people split almost exactly in half.**
- **At 6**, a small majority is satisfied.
- **At 3**, most say it is too few.

The two sides in their own words:
- **Enough:**
  - "5 habits at a time is more than enough for me" `P24#9144`;
  - "Limitation of 6 habits is good. Forces you to choose those habits that have priority" `A23#5843`.
- **Not enough:**
  - "5 IS NOT ENOUGH" `A1#1889`;
  - "I have more than 3 habits to track before I have breakfast" `A16#515`.

**What free users say a free plan should allow (193 people):**
- median **6**;
- the two most common answers are **5** (49 people) and **10** (40 people).

## 4. People start small and grow; some cut back

**Starting.** 29 people say how many they started with:
- median **2.5**;
- the most common answer is **1**.

**Advice to others.** 59 people advise others how many to start with:
- median **1.5**;
- "Starting with 12 habits is much harder to compete everyday than 2" `A26#3604`.

**Growth, from people who give both numbers:**

| Started with | Now |
|---|---|
| 1 | 4, 5, 7, 8 |
| 2–3 | 20 |
| 3 | 25 |
| 5 | 20 |
| 6 | 15 |

- "I started with like 5 habits and now have almost 20" `A1#51828`.

**Cutting back:** 16 → 5, 10 → 3, 12 → 8 and 5 → 3.
- "I made the mistake of having 7 habits and it grew to 10. I'm down to 3 now" `A76#5340`.

**So the count changes over a person's life in the app:**
- it starts at **1–3**;
- it settles around **5**;
- for a minority it grows past **15**.

## 6. Outside data

- **Loggd, August 2026:** 7,800+ habits across 4,200+ users. That works out to **about 1.9 habits per user**, and it counts every habit ever created, including abandoned ones.
  - 45% of those habits were never checked off once.
  - Loggd's own note: the sample "over-represents the motivated".
  - This is the only published per-user figure found. It describes everyone who installs, not only people who stick with the app.
- **"Most people plateau around 5–7 tracked habits"** is claimed by a habit-app blog, with **no source or data**. It matches our median of 5–6, but it is not evidence.
- **Streaks' cap history:** 6, then 12, then 24 habits. The makers set it on the view that fewer habits stick better. It is a design decision, not usage data.
- **No research paper** found reports habits per user.

---

## 7. How far to trust these numbers

1. **Only reviewers who wrote a number.**
   - These 446 people are engaged users who chose to write a review; they are not all users.
   - Everyone who installs, including people who give up, tracks fewer (Loggd: about 1.9 created).
   - Our median of 5 describes **people who keep using the app**.
2. **Numbers get mentioned for two reasons.**
   - Heavy users mention them when they complain about limits or lag.
   - Light users mention them when they praise the free plan.
   - Both ends may be slightly over-represented, the middle under-represented.
3. **Free-plan limits cut counts short without anyone saying so.**
   - People who hit a limit are left out, but others may simply stay under it without saying so.
   - So the true typical count is probably **a little higher than 5, not lower**.
4. **Counts are self-reported** and some include tasks and chores. A few people describe routines rather than separate habits.
5. **The screen needed a number next to "habit".** "I track seven things" was missed. 525 reviews about "N habit apps" were not read.
6. **Both stores tell the same story:** App Store median 5 (306 people), Play Store median 5 (140 people).
