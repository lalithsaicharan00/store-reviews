# Today Screen, Question 28: The Day Card — What It Shows, and in What Order

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 24 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

> **Revised by Questions 31–33:** the detail line became fixed slots (streak and frequency on line 2, time top right), the streak rule is in Question 31, and the goal moved into the button (Question 32).

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion. Day cards round.

**Screen.** Each card in the Day list carries:
- an icon and a title;
- a sub line ("5 of 8 glasses") plus a group dot;
- a 7-day dot strip, and "23d · 4/7" beside a flame;
- on the right, a ring, sometimes with a separate ▶ button beside it.

**The feedback.**
- The card is cluttered.
- The 7-day dots repeat what the Week view is for. A Day card should show **today**.
- Is the habit's time important? A Notion card seemed to say so.
- Put fixed-format items (time, streak) first and variable-length text last, so nothing dangles.
- Every card should work the same way.

**Basis.**
- **Read in full:**
  - 72 matches on seeing a habit's time on the list;
  - 29 on history and stats on the main screen;
  - 166 on busy or cluttered cards and lists.
- **Reused:**
  - the Home Screen Cards report (what people want on a card);
  - the Day Structure reports (137 want an exact clock time);
  - the Notion explainer cards C183, C038, C095, C006 and C172.
- **External:** NN/g and list-design guidance on scanning.

---

## What to do

**One anatomy for every habit.**

```
[icon]  Title                                   [button]
        14:00 · 🔥 58 days · 3 times a day
```

1. **Icon** on the left, in the habit's colour.
2. **Title** on line 1.
3. **Detail line** on line 2, always in this order. Each part appears only when it applies:
   1. **Time**, only if the habit has a set time: `14:00`.
   2. **Streak**, only from 2 upwards: `🔥 23 days`, or `🔥 4 weeks` for weekly habits.
   3. **Goal**, in words, which is the only part that varies in length: `8 glasses`, `20 min`, `3 steps`, `2 of 3 this week`. If it's too long, it is cut off with "…".
4. **One button** on the right: today's progress, and the way to log. See Question 29.

**Take off the Day card:**

| Item | Why |
|---|---|
| 7-day dot strip | The **Week** view is that history, and the app remembers the chosen view. |
| "4/7" | Unexplained, and a second history number beside the streak. |
| "5 of 8 glasses" as text | The button shows 5/8, and the goal says "8 glasses". |
| Group dot | Groups are shown by the filter. A second colour code needs a legend nobody sees. |
| Note icon | Notes belong on the record: the week, month and year grids and the habit screen (C172). |
| Separate ▶ and +1 buttons | They are folded into the one button (Question 29). |

## Key findings

**1. Show the time when a habit has one. Reviews ask for it, and none object.**
- **10 of the 72 matches** ask to see a habit's set time on the list. None ask not to. Most of the other 62 are about something else, like "one at a time instead of…".
  - "see the time you set the reminder for to appear on each task down the list next to the title" `A20#1298`
  - "in the Today tab, seeing the reminder time next to the habits" `P19#372`
  - "I have to click on the habit to see the reminder time" `P3#6444`
  - "main screen only has the tasks and no times" `A59#142`
- **The Day Structure verification** found 137 reviews wanting an exact clock time.
- **Notion "Tell me what the day looks like" (C183)** asks whether time is visible: what is now, what is next. The time is optional per habit.
- **So your memory is right in spirit.** The card doesn't literally say "on the card", but it asks for the day's timing to be visible. A habit's set time is the finest-grained part of that.
- **Only when set:** most habits have no clock time, and the section already gives the rough time of day. A blank or "—" would be noise.

**2. History on the Day card is split. The Week view already answers it.**
- **9 want recent days on the main screen:** "see last 7 days insted of just last 5 days on app main screen" `P3#9466`
- **3 find it clutter, or discouraging:**
  - "an option to hide past days on the home screen. Often I get discouraged from seeing my past days failure" `A1#55870`
  - "I would prefer if the analytics is put on the analytics screen and the habits screen is a bit less cluttered" `P14#242`
- The Home Screen Cards report found history on home is valued (397 praise). **Our Week view is on the home screen.** It is one choice in View, and the app reopens on it (Question 15).
- So the people who want days of history get a whole screen of it. The Day card stays about today.
- **The streak stays:** it is the one-number summary of the past, and 192 reviews want it on the card.

**3. The streak must say what it counts.**
- Notion "The number has to be right" (C038) asks: "Is it clear what a streak counts for weekly or monthly habits?"
- "23d" and "4 wk" are shorthand. **"23 days"** and **"4 weeks"** name the unit.
- Notion "Never shame a missed day" (C095): no "0-day streak". The streak simply isn't shown until it reaches 2.

**4. Busy cards are a real complaint.**
- "The Home Screen cards appear busy and the amount of text content combined with colors is not easy to digest" `A24#29531`
- "habit info should be collapsable in the "habits" screen (it's cluttered with information)" `P2#10403`
- "I just wanted to be able to check my habit off and move on." `A24#41676`
- Most of the 166 clutter matches are general praise ("not overwhelming"). They say calm matters, but they don't specify a card.

**5. Why fixed items first, and the variable one last.**
- **Consistent position helps scanning.** Scannability guidance says to keep each item in the same position and layout in every row. NN/g's eye-tracking found that people scan in whatever way suits the task, so a predictable layout speeds them up.
- **Reasoned from first principles:**
  - A time is always 5 characters, and a streak is almost always "N days". Put first, they sit at nearly the same spot on every card, so the eye finds them in a column.
  - The goal text ranges from "20 min" to "2 of 3 this week". Put last, its ragged end falls at the end of the line, where nothing follows it.
  - Only the last item ever needs cutting off, so the important numbers are never the ones truncated.
  - This is also how tables are designed: fixed columns first, the one free-text column last and allowed to wrap or truncate.

**Limits.**
- **Small counts:** 10 time requests, and 9 against 3 on history. The direction is clear, but these are small numbers.
- **Checked only by reasoning:** the exact order and the "only from 2" streak rule. Test them.
