# Often Enough — How the Logo Took Shape

Written by Codex, 11 October 2026, from the user's feedback, the preserved exploration reports and the actual Figma artwork. User selections are distinguished from agent suggestions and static visual checks (Rulebook W2/U29).

The selected direction developed from **building blocks → full stacked squares → colored stack → one color family → tonal stack → neutral light/dark identity**. Earlier rings, letters and construction experiments explain why we chose this route. The final logo was selected through visual review; memorability and worldwide uniqueness were not measured.

## Meaning before form

The app is named **Often Enough**. Its welcome screen says: “Habits grow through repetition. You choose how often is enough.” The user approved the design brief **“Small actions, repeated often enough, build something lasting.”** This describes starting small, repeating at a sustainable frequency and gradually building something durable. It became the starting point for the shape, rather than a story added afterward.

The requested character was simple, clean, professional, memorable and readable at app-icon sizes. Early studies used image generation as requested. Later, the user explicitly authorized direct editable Figma work on colors, corners and spacing; this method change enabled exact repeated geometry without discarding the generated exploration history.

## Why these final choices work together

**Three pieces and upward layering.** The repeated units carry the small-action idea. The base is behind the middle, and the middle behind the newest top-right piece. This corrects the earlier impression that progress was being cut away. The logo expresses accumulation without becoming a literal building or brick wall.

**Rounded squares.** The user liked the square family. Consistent rounded corners preserve that reference while fitting the softer silhouettes of app-icon masks. The final tile corner radius is 28% of the tile side, with 0.6 smoothing. This is our artwork choice, not a platform-mandated radius.

**12° clockwise tilt.** The user selected the more expressive pose after reviewing circular compositions. A rigid centered rotation preserves radial distance from the center while changing the axis-aligned outline. It gives the circle more character without a separate circle-only logo. Figma reports the selected rotation as −12°; the exported SVG uses +12° in screen coordinates.

**52 inside 60.** The user made this reduction and liked the result in both masks. The ratio is 52:60, or 86.6667%, applied to the whole nested artwork frame, including its transparent padding. It is not a 52-pixel tight visible glyph. On a 1024 canvas, that frame is 887.4667 pixels; the unrotated visible construction spans 624 pixels. The breathing room was a visual choice, not a universal safe-area formula.

**Tonal identity.** Colored studies gave the tiles separate begin/build/last roles, but at small sizes their contrast and shadows did not feel right. A blue family demonstrated coherent tonal progression. Neutral tones retained that separation and shallow sense of layering while fitting an app whose interface has no brand accent colors. The final mark uses solid flat fills and genuine transparent seams; heavy shadows, texture and thick 3D edges were left behind.

**Background-specific direction.** On white, the pieces progress from 75% gray to 40% to 20%; on 5% gray, they progress from 25% to 65% to white. The top has the strongest background contrast in each appearance. The user explicitly confirmed the finalized order: top darkest on light, brightest on dark. Three equal single-ink pieces lose the selected tonal character, so they are excluded from current assets.

## Research that influenced the process

The tutorial review used accessible descriptions, chapter lists and transcript excerpts from Satori Graphics and The Futur. Recorded views were a relevance signal, not proof of quality; no full-video viewing is claimed. The practical lessons were to start from a brief, simplify, maintain consistent geometry and inspect the mark in context at small sizes. Source links and access limitations remain in the exploration archive.

A focused official-brand screen found a credible visual association with Jira's three rising folded forms, while noting different rounded-square geometry. It also examined Atlassian's other products, Linear, Dropbox, Asana and Buffer. This supported examining silhouette and layering; it did not establish an exact copy or exhaustive uniqueness clearance. No other company's artwork was copied into the assets (D11).

Color research distinguished context-dependent associations from proposed brand meanings. The amber/blue/green proposal was approximately complementary amber/blue on a digital RGB hue wheel with green as a cool accent, rather than an exact triadic scheme. It informed exploration but does not define the finalized neutral identity.

## Guide, exports and remaining implementation

The final board preserves 72 actual-size previews across two appearances, four masks and nine sizes, plus four enlarged previews. The visual guide adds 16 previews at 60/48/32/24 pixels. The existing official Apple **OS27** construction grid/outline was used; the user's reference to an iOS guide was not treated as a claim that an iOS 11 template had been obtained. Android's 108/72/66-dp guide was reconstructed from published dimensions and labeled accordingly. Guide circles are construction references, not a universal mandatory containment boundary.

When preparing this repository package, the former export board `1160:5` had been removed. Its sources were reconstructed by cloning surviving unmasked editable artwork inside the approved final review, preserving paints, proportions, seams and tilt. The new clean board is [1166:2](https://www.figma.com/design/Ncccsm1l2O62GJ5xLSInqk/Design?node-id=1166-2); the Figma guide's source references were updated. This is source recovery, not a new design round. Earlier report IDs remain historical references.

The [asset manifest](<Assets/manifest.json>) records actual downloaded files and proportionally derived sizes, replacing the earlier export-settings-only handoff. File checks and static Figma inspection establish artifact delivery. Native asset catalogs, Icon Composer/tinted/clear appearances, adaptive launcher motion and real-device rendering remain separate under U9. They do not reopen the user's visual approval.
