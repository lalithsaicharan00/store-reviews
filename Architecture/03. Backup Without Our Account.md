# 03. Backup Without Our Account

*Written by Claude (Claude Code), 26 Sep 2026. Topic 3 of the [architecture list](README.md). A proposal, not yet built.*

**Scope:** people who never sign in. How their data is backed up to *their own* cloud (iCloud, Google Drive, a folder they choose) and to the phone's own backups. How the app **knows** a backup exists and how old it is. How restoring works. All at no cost to us.

**How each rule is backed:**
- **Users show**: backed by review evidence.
- **Platform rule**: an Apple or Google requirement, with a link.
- **First principles**: reasoned from how the system works.

**Evidence:**
- **A fresh screen:** 1,487,223 reviews, 14 backup patterns in about 12 languages, 3,901 matches.
- **262 read in full and coded,** with quotes verified.
- **Reused:** the Data Safety report (section B) and ledger C020.
- **Files:** [`Backup Evidence/`](<../Research/Research Reports/Data, Sync and Accounts/Backup Evidence/>).

## 1. The design on one page

1. **Automatic by default, no setup:** on iPhone, backups go to the user's **iCloud** with no prompt, whenever iCloud is signed in. On Android, **one tap connects Google Drive.** Until then, Android's own Auto Backup covers them.
2. **Backups are dated snapshots, not a second sync system.** A compressed copy of all data, kept as 7 daily, 4 weekly and 6 monthly copies. It's small: well under 1 MB for most people.
3. **The app always knows the backup state:** where the backup is, when it last succeeded, what it holds, and any error, in plain words. A backup only counts once it has been **read back and checked.**
4. **Alerts only when something is wrong:** "No backup for 3 days: iCloud is full". No nagging once backups are working.
5. **"We found your backup" before onboarding:** a small marker that survives reinstalls tells a fresh install that a backup exists. It lives in iCloud's key-value store on iPhone and in Google's **Block Store** on Android.
6. **Restore previews first and never destroys:**
   - it shows the date and what's inside;
   - it snapshots the current data first, so the restore can be undone;
   - the user chooses Replace or Merge;
   - it works on any device, any platform, from any app version.
7. **An open file format:** a zip with JSON + CSV and a documented manifest. It can be imported on iPhone and Android, and read in Excel.
8. **Free, unlimited, no cooldowns:** backup, restore, export and import are never paid, never rate-limited, and never tied to a device.
9. **The phone's own backup and transfer must include us:** iCloud device backup, Quick Start, Android Auto Backup, device-to-device transfer and Samsung Smart Switch, with the device's identity excluded.
10. **Multi-device sync needs an account** (one-tap Apple or Google, topic 1). Backup alone is not sync. The folder export is the "sync-ish" option for people who refuse accounts.

## 3. The design in detail

### 3.1 Where backups go (people without an account)

| # | Destination | iPhone | Android | Setup | Can we see its status? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Local snapshots** (on the phone) | ✅ | ✅ | none | ✅ |
| 2 | **Phone's own backup / transfer** | iCloud device backup, Quick Start | Auto Backup (25 MB), device-to-device transfer, Smart Switch | none (the user's phone settings) | ❌ no public API. We can only detect *after* a restore (§3.5) |
| 3 | **Our backup in their cloud** | **iCloud (CloudKit private database)**, on automatically if iCloud is signed in | **Google Drive app folder** (`drive.appdata`), one-tap consent | iOS: none. Android: one tap | ✅ |
| 4 | **Folder auto-export** (optional) | Any Files location: iCloud Drive, Google Drive, On My iPhone | Any folder via the system picker, including Drive | Pick a folder once | ✅ |
| 5 | **Manual export / import** | ✅ | ✅ | none | – |

**Why CloudKit on iPhone (platform rule + first principles):**
- It is stored in the user's private iCloud, counts against **their** quota, and **costs us nothing.**
- It needs no permission prompt beyond being signed into iCloud.
- It can be queried (list, check and download snapshots), unlike iCloud *device* backups.

**Why Google Drive's app folder on Android (platform rule):**
- `drive.appdata` is a *non-sensitive* scope, so no security review is needed.
- It needs only a Google authorization, not our account.

**Two caveats:**
- The Drive app folder is **hidden** from the user, and is **deleted if they disconnect the app in Drive.** The folder export (#4) is the visible copy.
- **Android is not automatic** until that one tap. Until then it relies on #1 and #2, and the app says so: "Backed up only on this phone. Connect Google Drive or sign in."

### 3.2 What a backup is
- **A file named `YYYY-MM-DD-HHmm.habits.zip`** containing:
  - `manifest.json`: format version, app version, platform, created at, record counts per table, and a SHA-256 of the data;
  - `data.json`: every habit, schedule version, check-in, note, part of day, setting, **archived and recently-deleted items**, and attachments;
  - `csv/`: one readable CSV per table, using **local calendar dates**.
- **The format is documented** in the repo and is **backward-readable forever:** every app version can read every older format. Tests keep a sample of each format.
- **Health-sourced entries are left out of iCloud copies** (CloudKit snapshots and automatic iCloud Drive exports). Apple's guideline 5.1.3(ii) says apps may not store personal health information in iCloud. After a restore they are re-read from Apple Health. *Added by [09 Privacy §10](<09. Privacy and Account Deletion.md>); confirm with App Review.* Google Drive and manual exports keep them.
- **No encryption beyond the cloud provider's own.** A lost key would mean lost data (topic 1 evidence). Users who want more can turn on iCloud Advanced Data Protection themselves.

### 3.3 When backups run
- **After meaningful changes:**
  - check-ins trigger a backup at most every 6 hours;
  - habit edits and deletes trigger one within 5 minutes, because they are riskier.
- **Nightly:** a scheduled task (iOS `BGProcessingTask`, Android `WorkManager`) runs if nothing ran that day.
- **Before risky moments:** before every app update's database migration, before a restore, before signing in or out, and before "delete all data".
- **Retention:** 7 daily, 4 weekly and 6 monthly copies, per destination. Pruning never deletes the newest successful backup.
- **No cooldowns or quotas** for the user (users show: `A52#3395`, `P2#2415`).

### 3.4 Knowing a backup exists and how old it is
- **A backup ledger in the local database:** one row per attempt, with destination, time, size, counts, checksum, result, and error text.
- **"Succeeded" only after verification** (users show: false "success", `P65#7648`):
  - iCloud: the saved record is fetched back and its checksum compared;
  - Drive: the file's returned `md5Checksum` or size is compared;
  - folder: the file is re-read.
- **Settings → Backup** shows each destination:
  - "iCloud: last backup today 09:14 · 86 habits · 1,240 check-ins · ✅";
  - "Google Drive: not connected · [Connect]";
  - "Folder: iCloud Drive/Habits · last Sunday · ✅";
  - "Phone backup: depends on your iPhone settings" (with a link that explains how to check).
- **Health rule:** if **no destination beyond the phone** has succeeded for 3 days, show a dot on the avatar and one notification naming the reason: "iCloud storage is full", "You're signed out of iCloud", "Google Drive access was removed", or "The backup folder was moved". **Nothing is shown when things work** (users show: nag complaints, `A31#4681`).

**The marker that survives a reinstall:**

| | iPhone | Android |
|---|---|---|
| Store | iCloud key-value store (`NSUbiquitousKeyValueStore`: 1 MB, 1,024 keys; survives delete and reinstall; follows the Apple ID) | Google **Block Store** (up to 16 entries × 4 KB; survives reinstall when Google Backup is on; carried by device-to-device transfer; `setShouldBackupToCloud(true)`) |
| Contents | `{lastBackupAt, destination, snapshotId, counts, appVersion}` | same |
| Used for | the fresh-install screen: "We found your iCloud backup from today 09:14 (86 habits). Restore?" | "We found a Google Drive backup…", then one tap to re-authorise Drive |

### 3.5 Detecting that the phone's own backup restored us
- **The device ID lives outside backups:** Keychain "this device only" on iOS, the `no_backup` directory on Android. The database keeps its own copy.
- **On launch:** if the database exists but its device ID doesn't match this device's, the data came from a phone backup or transfer.
- **Then we show:** "Welcome back: your habits came across from your old phone (last change 3 Oct 21:40)." We register as a new device, keep all the data, and run a fresh backup.
- **If the database is empty but the marker exists,** the phone backup missed us, so we offer the cloud backup.

### 3.6 Restore
1. **Pick a source:** iCloud or Drive snapshots listed by date, or a file.
2. **Preview:** the date, the device it came from, and counts, compared with what's on this phone now.
3. **A snapshot of current data is taken automatically:** "Undo restore" stays available for 30 days.
4. **Choose:**
   - **Replace:** the phone becomes exactly the backup;
   - **Merge:** combine by IDs; the newer edit wins; nothing is deleted.
5. **Validate before committing:** parse the file, check the checksum and counts against the manifest, and migrate old formats forward. **If anything doesn't match, stop. Nothing is changed** (users show: a partial restore then synced, `P84#10009`).
6. **Signed-in users:** the restored data syncs as ordinary edits only after the user confirms. A restore can't silently overwrite the server copy.
- **It works across devices, platforms and app versions.** Never paywalled.

### 3.7 The phone's own backups must include us (platform rule)
- **iOS:**
  - the database and snapshots live in `Application Support` (backed up, and kept on offload), never in `Caches`;
  - the device ID and tokens are excluded;
  - test Quick Start and an iCloud restore on every major iOS release.
- **Android:**
  - `allowBackup="true"`, plus `dataExtractionRules` with **both** `cloud-backup` and `device-transfer` sections including the database and snapshots, and excluding the device ID and tokens (`no_backup`);
  - keep the total under 25 MB;
  - test on Pixel device-to-device transfer **and Samsung Smart Switch** (users show Smart Switch misses: `P84#8853`, `P126#11004`).
- **Low storage:** a write that fails for lack of space is reported ("Your phone is full. Changes can't be saved.") and **never** truncates or resets the database (users show: `N2#14395`).

### 3.8 Signed-in users
- **Our server is primary** (topic 5 and 6).
- **The iCloud or Drive snapshot stays on by default** as a separate, independent net. It is cheap, private, and survives even if our service disappears (main doc §7). It can be switched off in Settings.

### 3.9 People who refuse every cloud
- **Local snapshots, the phone's backup and manual export still work.**
- **The status says so honestly:** "Backed up only on this phone."
- **One gentle suggestion to export once a month,** switched off with one tap.

## 4. Platform facts used

| Fact | Source |
|---|---|
| CloudKit private database storage **counts against the user's iCloud quota**, not the developer's. A full iCloud returns an error | [Rambo Codes: CloudKit 101](https://www.rambo.codes/posts/2020-02-25-cloudkit-101), [Fatbobman](https://fatbobman.com/en/posts/my-eight-years-with-cloudkit/) |
| `NSUbiquitousKeyValueStore`: 1 MB, 1,024 keys; survives delete and reinstall with the same Apple ID | [Apple docs](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/nsubiquitouskeyvaluestore), [Fatbobman](https://fatbobman.com/en/posts/nsubiquitouskeyvaluestore/) |
| iOS backs up `Documents/` and `Library/Application Support/`, not `Caches/` or `tmp/`; `isExcludedFromBackup` excludes a file | [Apple: iCloud backup](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundation/optimizing-your-app-s-data-for-icloud-backup) |
| Android Auto Backup: 25 MB, about daily, only the latest copy kept, databases included by default; `dataExtractionRules` separates cloud and device-transfer | [Android Auto Backup](https://developer.android.com/identity/data/autobackup) |
| **Block Store:** 16 entries × 4 KB; survives reinstall (with Google Backup on) and device-to-device transfer; optional cloud backup; end-to-end encrypted with a screen lock | [Android Block Store](https://developer.android.com/identity/block-store) |
| Drive `appDataFolder`: hidden, app-only, deleted if the user removes the app from Drive; the `drive.appdata` scope is non-sensitive | [Drive app data](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/appdata), [Drive scopes](https://developers.google.com/workspace/drive/api/guides/api-specific-auth) |
| Android folder access persists with `takePersistableUriPermission`, but is lost if the folder is moved or deleted | [Android SAF](https://developer.android.com/training/data-storage/shared/documents-files) |
| There is **no public API** to read whether iCloud device backup or Android Auto Backup is on, or when it last ran | First principles (none exists in either SDK). This is why §3.5 detects restores instead |

## 5. Test checklist
1. **Fresh iPhone, iCloud on:** a backup appears within 5 minutes of the first habit and is verified, and Settings shows it.
2. **iCloud full** (simulate the quota error): after 3 days, one alert naming "iCloud storage is full"; the folder export is suggested.
3. **Delete and reinstall on iPhone:** the first screen says "We found your iCloud backup from …", and restoring brings back everything, including archived habits and notes.
4. **Android, Drive connected, uninstall and reinstall:** the Block Store marker leads to the Drive restore.
5. **Android device-to-device transfer and Samsung Smart Switch:** the data arrives, the "came across from your old phone" message shows, and it registers as a new device.
6. **Restore a backup from 3 app versions ago,** and restore an iPhone backup file on Android.
7. **A corrupted backup file:** a clear error and no change to current data. Replace, then Undo restore, returns exactly the prior state.
8. **Merge restore:** no duplicates; the newer edits win; nothing is deleted.
9. **Low storage during a write:** a clear message, and the database is intact after freeing space.
10. **Airplane mode for a week:** backups queue; the alert fires on day 3; everything catches up after reconnecting.

## 6. Parked (added to [Backlog.md](Backlog.md))
- **CloudKit *sync* (not just backup) for people without our account** (iPhone ⇄ iPad without signing in). The lean is **no**: two sync systems double the failure surface. Signing in with Apple is one tap and private. Revisit if people ask.
