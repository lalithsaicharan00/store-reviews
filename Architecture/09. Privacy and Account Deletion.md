# 09. Privacy and Account Deletion

*Written by Claude (Claude Code), 26 Sep 2026. Updated 27 Sep 2026: free users are local-only with no account and no email; accounts are Plus-only (Apple or Google); one email ever (the purchase confirmation); no iCloud or Drive copies of ours. Topic 9 of the [architecture list](README.md). A proposal, not yet built, and not legal advice. Updated 28 Sep 2026: no lawyer review; the privacy policy is generated and checked against the data map (§11).*

**Scope:**
- what we collect, and what we refuse to collect;
- permissions;
- app lock and privacy on the lock screen and widgets;
- analytics without tracking;
- deletion of an account, of data, or both, across the phone, their cloud, our server, backups and logs;
- export;
- the store privacy forms;
- the law.

**Builds on:** [01 Accounts §3.7](<01. Accounts and Identity.md>) (the deletion flow and Apple token revocation), [03 Backup](<03. Backup and Restore.md>), [06 Server §11](<06. Server on Cloudflare.md>) (server security) and [08 Release §9](<08. Release Safety and Operations.md>) (support).

**How each rule is backed:**
- **Users show**: backed by review evidence.
- **Platform rule**: an Apple or Google requirement, with a link.
- **First principles**: reasoned from how the system works.

**Evidence:**
- **A fresh screen:** 1,487,223 reviews, 9 privacy patterns, 6,227 matches. "Spam emails" and "can't delete my account" are among the angriest patterns in the corpus: 85% and 81% one-star.
- **190 read in full and coded,** with quotes verified. 95 are about this topic; most "unsubscribe" reviews are about billing (topic 2).
- **Files:** [`Privacy Evidence/`](<../Research/Research Reports/Data, Sync and Accounts/Privacy Evidence/>).

## 1. The design on one page

1. **Collect only what the app needs to work,** and say so in one screen. No onboarding questionnaire about someone's life, no phone number, no location, no contacts, no camera, no IP logging beyond abuse protection (users show: 11 "collects far more than needed" reviews, mean 1.5; 9 thanking apps for "no unnecessary permissions", mean 5.0).
2. **No tracking, no ads, no selling, no data brokers.** Analytics are anonymous product counts, never habit content, with an off switch. So no App Tracking Transparency prompt is needed (users show: 9 selling or ads-consent reviews, mean 1.1).
3. **The free app has no account and no email, forever** (topic 1). Accounts exist only for Plus, and even then the account step can be skipped (users show: forced sign-up, 6 reviews, mean 1.2).
4. **Permissions only at the moment of use, with a one-line reason first.** Notifications when the first reminder is set; Health when the first Health habit is made; nothing at launch (users show: “I wished that the app somehow tells in advance why it asks for a permission.” (`P84#44723`)).
5. **Optional app lock with Face ID / Touch ID / fingerprint, falling back to the device passcode.** Never an app-only password that can be forgotten and lock the data away (users show: 11 lock requests; 2 users locked out of their own notes).
6. **Private habits stay private on screen:** hide names on the lock screen, in notifications and in widgets while the phone is locked, as an option.
7. **Deleting is easy, complete and honest.** Delete the account, or erase data without an account, from Settings in two taps and on the web. It covers every copy we control, it's confirmed with a date, and it warns clearly that a store subscription must be cancelled in the store (users show: 11 "can't delete / ignored" reviews, mean 1.4; 5 "still charged after deleting", mean 1.6).
8. **Export is always free** and complete, in the open format (users show: export behind a paywall called out as a GDPR breach).
9. **One email, ever:** the purchase confirmation, to a Plus buyer with an account. No sign-in codes, no marketing, no reminders, nothing after deletion (users show: “I then chose to delete my account but I’m still getting daily spam messages that contain all my data.” (`A33#1119`)).
10. **Apple's health rule:** data read from Apple Health is never used for anything but showing it to the user. We keep no copies in iCloud (topic 3), so Apple 5.1.3(ii) is met by design.

## 3. The data map

What we hold, where, and for how long. This table is the source for the privacy policy and both store forms.

| Data | Where | Why | Kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| Habits, schedules, entries, notes, settings | **the phone** and its on-device snapshots (topic 3); **our server only for Plus with an account** | the app itself; sync | until the user deletes them |
| Health-sourced values (steps, sleep, …) | the phone; our server for Plus with an account (as habit entries) | show progress | as above |
| Sign-in keys (Apple `sub`, Google `sub`) | server D1 directory | sign-in | until account deletion |
| Email (from Apple or Google, possibly a private relay address) | server | the one purchase confirmation; support lookup | until account deletion |
| Email delivery record | backend and selected sender | the purchase confirmation | minimal; removed on deletion |
| Session tokens | server, hashed | stay signed in | until sign-out, rotation or deletion |
| Device list (name, platform, app version, push token) | server | sync, push, "your devices" | until removed or account deletion |
| Purchase records (transaction IDs, product, dates) | server | entitlements (topic 2) | account life; then **de-linked** and kept only as long as tax law needs |
| Request logs (route, status, time, hashed account ID) | Cloudflare Workers Logs | security, debugging | 3–7 days |
| Product analytics (events like "habit created", app version; no content) | analytics provider, EU region | improve the app | 12 months; off switch in Settings |
| Crash reports (stack traces, device model; no content) | crash tool | fix crashes | 90 days |
| Support messages and optional diagnostics | support inbox | help the user | 12 months after the conversation ends, or on request |

**Never collected:**
- location;
- contacts;
- photos or camera;
- microphone;
- phone number;
- advertising ID;
- IP beyond the abuse limits (topic 6 §6);
- age, gender or life questionnaires;
- anything from other apps.

## 4. Permissions

| Permission | Asked when | The one line shown first |
|---|---|---|
| Notifications | the user sets their first reminder | "So we can remind you at 8:00." |
| Apple Health / Health Connect | the user makes a habit linked to Health | "To count your steps automatically. We only read the types you pick." Read-only, per type |
| Files | the user exports or imports a file | none needed: the system share sheet and picker grant access to that file only |
| Face ID | the user turns on the app lock | the system text |

- **Nothing is asked at first launch** (users show: permission walls and a "commitment" fingerprint at onboarding).
- **Declining never blocks the app** (Apple 5.1.2 forbids requiring system features to use the app). *Platform rule.*
- **Android:** only `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`, `SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM` / `USE_EXACT_ALARM` if reminders need it, `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED`, `INTERNET` for sync and backup, and the Health Connect read types. The app works fully without the network.

## 5. App lock and on-screen privacy

- **Settings → Privacy → Lock with Face ID** (or Touch ID / fingerprint / the device passcode).
- **It uses the device's own authentication** (iOS `LocalAuthentication` with passcode fallback; Android `BiometricPrompt` with device credential). So forgetting is impossible in the way an app password can be forgotten, and the data is never locked away (users show: `N3#27521`, `N3#24095`).
- **It locks when the app is backgrounded,** after a delay the user picks (immediately, 1 minute, 5 minutes). The app switcher shows a blurred screen.
- **Hide on lock screen**, per app or per habit:
  - notifications say "Habit reminder" instead of the name;
  - widgets redact names while the phone is locked (iOS `privacySensitive`; Android lock-screen visibility);
  - Live Activities show only a timer.
- **Private habits** can be marked so they never appear in widgets, Watch complications or Siri suggestions (users show: `P125#18959`).
- **The lock is a screen, not encryption.** The data is already encrypted at rest by the OS (iOS Data Protection; Android file-based encryption). The settings page says this plainly.

## 6. Analytics without tracking

- **What:** counts of product events (app opened, habit created, reminder set, sync failed, purchase started), app version, OS, country from the store locale. **Never** habit names, notes, entry values or Health data.
- **Identity:** a random install ID, reset when the user erases data. It is not linked to the account, has no advertising ID and no fingerprinting.
- **So:** no App Tracking Transparency prompt, because we don't track across other companies' apps (Apple 5.1.2). *Platform rule.*
- **Off switch:** Settings → Privacy → "Share anonymous usage". Crash reports have their own switch.
- **Provider: PostHog, EU cloud** (decided 28 Sep 2026). Its privacy manifest must be bundled. **Crash reports: Sentry** (topic 8), with no habit content.

## 7. Deletion

Four different things, all in **Settings → Privacy & data**, and all also available on the web page Google requires ([Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111)):

| Action | For | What it removes |
|---|---|---|
| **Delete my account** | signed-in users | everything on our server (below), then signs out every device. Asks: "Also erase this phone's data?" (default: keep it, as a local-only app) |
| **Erase all my data** | anyone | the phone's data and local snapshots; and, if signed in, the server data too (the account itself stays, empty) |
| **Delete one habit and its history** | anyone | that habit everywhere, with 30-day undo (topic 5 §7), then permanent |
| **Turn off sync** | signed-in users | server copy deleted; the app keeps working locally |

**Before deleting an account** (from topic 1, kept here as a checklist):
- offer a free export;
- show an active store subscription with **Manage subscription** and the sentence "Deleting your account does not cancel your subscription. Cancel it in the App Store / Google Play first." (users show: 5 "still charged" reviews). Apple says the same;
- confirm with the account's sign-in (Face ID / a fresh Apple or Google sign-in), never an email round-trip or a form (users show: `P12#53654`).

**What "everything on our server" means:**
1. The account's Durable Object storage is wiped (topic 6).
2. The D1 directory rows (sign-in keys to account) are removed.
3. Its R2 snapshots are deleted at once. They are stored per account, so this is one prefix delete.
4. Sessions, devices and push tokens are removed.
5. Email is removed from the database and the mail provider's lists.
6. Apple: Sign in with Apple tokens are revoked through Apple's REST API (topic 1).
7. Purchase records are de-linked: the account ID and email are removed, and the transaction IDs are kept only as long as tax law requires.
8. The account ID goes on a **deleted-accounts list** (IDs only). Any restore from an older backup (point-in-time recovery, R2 snapshots) checks this list and re-deletes. So no backup can bring a deleted account back.
9. Logs age out within 7 days; they only hold a hashed ID.
10. Support messages are deleted on request.

**Timing:** live data is gone immediately. Every copy, including point-in-time recovery, is gone within **30 days**, well inside GDPR's one-month limit for answering a request. The confirmation screen (and the web page) give the date; no email is sent.

**Apple's server notifications** (topic 1): `account-deleted` from Apple starts this same deletion; `consent-revoked` signs out that key's sessions.

**Local-only users:** "Erase all my data" erases the phone's data and snapshots. Files the user exported are theirs.

## 8. Export and the user's rights

- **Export is free, complete and one tap:** Settings → Privacy & data → "Download my data", producing the `.habits.zip` (topic 3), with JSON and CSV. Signed-in users can also download the server's copy on the web (topic 6 `/v1/account/export`), which covers the right of access and portability.
- **Correcting data** is just editing it in the app.
- **Requests by email** (for people who can't use the app) are answered within 1 working day and done within 30 days. The support console (topic 8) can run export and deletion for an account after verifying the requester through their sign-in provider.

## 9. Store forms and required files

- **Apple privacy label** (App Store Connect):
  - collected and linked to the user: contact info (email, if signed in), identifiers (user ID), user content (habits, if signed in), purchases;
  - not linked: usage data (anonymous analytics), diagnostics;
  - tracking: **none**.
- **Apple privacy manifest** (`PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`): declare the "required reason" APIs we use (UserDefaults, file timestamps, disk space). Bundle the third-party SDKs' own manifests.
- **Google Play Data safety form:**
  - data collected, data shared (none), encrypted in transit (yes);
  - "users can request deletion" (yes), with the web deletion link.
- **Privacy policy:** linked in both stores and in Settings, in plain English. It must cover:
  - what is collected (§3), why, and each processor (Cloudflare, analytics, crash tool, email sender, Apple, Google);
  - retention;
  - how to delete and export;
  - how to contact us.
  
  Apple 5.1.1(i) requires those points ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)) (users show: policies that "don't load", `P4#23446`).
- **Account deletion in the app** is an Apple requirement for any app that allows account creation (5.1.1(v)), and a Google one plus the web link. *Platform rule.*

## 10. Health data (Apple 5.1.3 and Health Connect)

- **Apple 5.1.3(i):** Health and fitness data may not be used or disclosed for advertising, marketing or data mining. We use it only to show the user their own progress. *Platform rule.*
- **Apple 5.1.3(ii):** apps "may not store personal health information in iCloud" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). *Platform rule.* We keep no copies in iCloud (topic 3). The phone's own iCloud device backup is Apple's system backup, which we don't control.
- **Health Connect** requires a privacy policy that covers health data, read-only access to the types used, and no use beyond the feature. The Play declaration lists each type.
- **On our server** (signed-in users), Health-sourced entries are stored like other entries, never shared, and never used for analytics.

## 11. The law, briefly

**No lawyer review** (decided 28 Sep 2026). The privacy policy is made with a reputable generator and checked line by line against the data map (§3) and the store forms. That is what small apps normally do. Revisit only if the company grows or a complaint arrives.


- **GDPR / UK GDPR** (anyone in the EU or UK):
  - rights of access, correction, erasure and portability (§7–8), answered within one month;
  - a lawful basis: contract for sync, consent for analytics;
  - a processor agreement with Cloudflare and each tool.
  - Habit names can reveal health, religion or sexuality, which are special categories. The safest reading is to treat all habit content as sensitive, keep it out of analytics, and ask for **explicit consent** when a user turns on sync ("Your habits will be stored on our server to sync your devices").
  - A non-EU company may need an **EU representative** (Article 27); low-cost representative services exist if it becomes relevant.
- **EU data residency: yes** (decided 28 Sep 2026). EU accounts' Durable Objects use `jurisdiction: "eu"`, and their snapshots go to an EU-jurisdiction R2 bucket, at no extra cost (topic 6 §15).
- **US state laws (CCPA and others):** no selling or sharing for ads makes most duties simple; the policy must still say so.
- **India's DPDP Act** and other local laws: cover them in the generated policy for where the company is registered.
- **Children:** the app isn't aimed at children and collects no age. With no ads and no tracking, COPPA exposure is low; confirm the age rating.

## 12. Test checklist

- A fresh install → no permission prompts until a feature needs one; the app is fully usable with every permission denied.
- Delete an account with an active subscription → the warning and "Manage subscription" are shown; after deletion, the server has no rows for the account, R2 has no objects under its prefix, and D1 has no keys.
- Restore the server from a point-in-time bookmark older than a deletion → the deleted account is re-deleted.
- "Erase all my data" without an account → the phone's data and snapshots are empty.
- Export → the file opens on another device and in a spreadsheet.
- App lock with Face ID disabled at the OS level → falls back to the device passcode; never locked out.
- Lock-screen privacy on → notification and widget show no habit names while locked.
- Analytics off → no analytics network calls (checked with a proxy).
- The Apple privacy label and Play Data safety form match the data map (§3) line by line.
