*Updated 29 Sep 2026: **interactive home-screen and lock-screen widgets are free** and show every free habit; Plus adds more widget designs and sizes, and more than 5 habits on a widget ([02 §3.1](<02. ## 1. The design on one page

1. **The phone app's database is the hub.** Widgets, notification actions, Siri and Live Activities never open that database from another process. They read a small **snapshot file** and hand writes to the app. *Platform rule:* SQLite in a shared App Group container gets iOS apps killed (0xDEAD10CC). This **changes [05 §10](<05. Sync Engine.md>)**, which put the database in the App Group.
2. **Every tap outside the app creates its entry ID at the moment of the tap.** Whichever path delivers it, and however often, it counts once (users show: a Watch tap that “will double down” when retried (`A23#5950`)).
3. **Every surface shows the same numbers,** because they are all computed by the one core from the same data. The snapshot is rewritten after every change, from any source (users show: a complication stuck on “1/1 habit complete” while the app says 0/3 (`A33#89`)).
4. **Done means gone everywhere.** Completing a habit anywhere clears its notification, its Live Activity and its reminder on every device (users show: reminders that keep coming “even after I complete it” (`P12#61458`)).
5. **Check off from anywhere:** notification, widget, lock screen, Watch, Siri. None of them opens the app (users show: 11 notification-action reviews, 14 complication reviews).
6. **The Watch is a full device.** It has its own small database, works on its own, and syncs with our server when signed in. Otherwise it goes through the phone.
7. **iPad and tablets are the same app,** with a proper large-screen layout. They sync through the account (users show: 10 reviews of phone and iPad that "dont link").
8. **Web and desktop come later,** on the same protocol. They are never a way around store billing (users show: web-only subscriptions are the angriest group here, 8 reviews at a mean of 1.0).
9. **One KMP core, tested across platforms:** day, streak, schedule and merge rules have one implementation, a written contract, and shared fixtures exercised through each supported platform caller.

## 3. Phone-side surfaces

### 3.1 The snapshot and the hand-off (iOS)

**The problem:** widgets, Siri and notification extensions run in *other processes*. The obvious design is to put the SQLite database in the shared App Group container so every process can open it. But iOS kills a suspended app that holds a lock on a file in a shared container (the 0xDEAD10CC crash). The workarounds are fragile, and one production app cut its crashes by 55% by moving the database out ([write-up](https://ryanwesley.com/sqlite-databases-in-app-group-containers/); [GRDB guide](https://swiftinit.org/docs/grdb/grdb/databasesharing)). *Platform rule.*

**The design:**
- **The database stays in the app's own container.** Only the app process opens it.
- **The snapshot:** after every change (a tap, a sync, a day rollover), the app writes a small JSON file to the App Group. It holds what every surface needs:
  - today's habits in order, with due, done, count and streak;
  - the last 7 days' grid;
  - the day start and week start;
  - the snapshot's own time.
  
  It is written atomically (write to a temporary file, then rename). Then the app asks WidgetKit and the Watch to reload.
- **Taps from outside the app** are App Intents that run in the **app's** process (the app is launched in the background to perform them). The intent writes the entry through the normal path, with its outbox row (topic 5). Confirm the exact intent setup on the target iOS version at build time.
- **Safety net: the tap journal.** If an intent must run in the extension, it appends one line (`entry ID, habit, day, value, time`) to a small journal file in the App Group, using file coordination. The app reads the journal at every launch and background refresh, imports the lines (the IDs make this idempotent), and clears it. The widget shows the tap at once by overlaying the journal on the snapshot.
- **Day rollover without opening the app:** the snapshot includes tomorrow's view and the rollover time. The widget timeline switches at the user's day start without waiting for the app (widget study: widgets that don't roll over at midnight).

**Refresh budget:** WidgetKit gives a frequently viewed widget roughly 40–70 background reloads a day. Reloads requested while the app is in use don't count against that budget ([WWDC21](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10048/)). So the app reloads widgets after every change it makes. Changes from sync arriving in the background use the budget, which is plenty for a habit app.

**Android is simpler:** widgets (Jetpack Glance), notification actions and tiles run **inside the app's own process**, so they use the app's Room database directly. They still go through the same repository and outbox, and still refresh every widget after any change. `updatePeriodMillis` (30 minutes at minimum) is only a backstop; real updates are pushed on change.

### 3.2 Notification actions

- Every habit reminder has **Done** and, for count habits, **+1**. There is also **Snooze 1 h**. On iOS the actions run without opening the app; on Android through a broadcast receiver.
- A tap from a notification creates its entry ID immediately (rule 2), writes it, then **removes every other pending and delivered notification for that habit and day**, on this device and, through sync, on others.
- If the write fails (very rare), the notification is re-posted with "Couldn't save, tap to open". A tap is never silently lost (users show: `P3#16074`, `N1#2791`).
- Once added, the action is never removed in a redesign (users show: `P8#754`).

### 3.3 Siri, Shortcuts and assistants

- **iOS:** App Intents for "Log [habit]", "Add 1 to [habit]", "What's left today", "My streak for [habit]" and "Open [habit]". These also appear in Spotlight and the Action button. Reads come from the snapshot; writes go through the app (§3.1).
- **Android:** app shortcuts, plus Assistant App Actions if still supported when we build. *Confirm at build time;* Google has changed this area often.
- Intents never need an account or the network.

### 3.4 Live Activities (iOS)

- Only for **timed habits** in progress (a 20-minute meditation, a focus session), and a "morning routine" run. Not for every habit.
- **Started and updated locally,** with no push server needed at launch. The system caps how long an activity can stay live (8 hours in the versions we checked) ([guide](https://newly.app/guides/ios-live-activities)).
- **Ends the moment the habit is done,** on any device, and when the timer runs out. Any stale activity is cleared at app launch (users show: `A33#3176`, `A5#596`).

## 4. The Watch (Apple)

*Replaced 10 Oct 2026: with no server (Rulebook D16), the Watch design is now [Apple Watch App — What People Want, What Breaks, and How Ours Works](<../iOS/Docs/Apple Watch/Research/Apple Watch App — What People Want, What Breaks, and How Ours Works.md>): its own database from the shared core, syncing by WatchConnectivity and its own `CKSyncEngine`. The text below is the old server design, kept for history.*

**Plus only** (decided 27 Sep 2026). Without Plus, the Watch app shows "Apple Watch is part of Plus" with [Get Plus on your iPhone]. Payers ask for it and some buy for it; broken Watch apps are the risk (2.22★), so it ships only when check-in works both ways ([evidence §5](<../Research/Research Reports/Business Model and Monetization/Plus Scope and Account at Purchase.md>)).

**An independent watchOS app** with its own small SQLite database and the same core.
- **Signed in:** the Watch syncs **directly with our server** over its own network (Wi-Fi, LTE or through the phone's connection), using the same `/v1/sync`. So it works when the phone is off or far away (users show: praise for LTE use in `A23#5370`).
- **Plus without an account:** the Watch and phone exchange ops through Watch Connectivity's queued user-info transfer, which delivers in the background and survives app restarts. The phone is then the Watch's server.
- **Either way, ops carry the entry ID made on the Watch,** so a tap relayed twice counts once (users show: `A23#5950`).
- **Reminders:** a notification scheduled on the phone mirrors to the Watch automatically when the phone is locked. The Watch app also schedules its own for habits the user marks "remind on Watch" (users show: `A31#4551`). The two are de-duplicated by the same notification ID.

**Complications** (WidgetKit accessory widgets): circular progress ("3 of 6"), a single-habit ring, and a rectangular "next up". They read the Watch's own snapshot. **Tapping one opens straight to check-off** for that habit. They are offered in every accessory family, so they work on most faces (users show: “there is only one watch face that’ll work with this app” (`A86#1215`)).

**Health on the Watch:** the Watch never imports Health data itself. Only the one importing device does (topic 5 §9).

## 5. Wear OS

- **Plus only**, like the Apple Watch.
- Same model: a **standalone Wear OS app** with its own Room database, syncing directly with our server when signed in. Otherwise it goes through the phone over the Wearable Data Layer.
- **Tiles** (today's list, one habit) and **complications** (progress, one habit), read from its own database.
- Galaxy Watch and Pixel Watch are the targets. Fitbit and Garmin have no general third-party app model that fits; parked (users ask: `A24#43186`).

## 6. What "stale" can never mean

For every surface, one test: **after any change anywhere, every surface on every device shows the new state within its limit.**

| Surface | Updated by | Worst-case delay |
|---|---|---|
| App on the same device | the write itself | instant |
| Widgets and lock screen, same device | snapshot rewrite + reload | about a second |
| Notifications, same device | cleared on write | instant |
| Live Activity, same device | updated on write | instant |
| Watch (signed in) | direct sync + silent push | seconds when open; next refresh otherwise |
| Watch (not signed in) | queued transfer from the phone | usually seconds; minutes if the Watch is asleep |
| Other devices | sync (topic 5 §11) | about 10 s when open |
| Widgets on other devices | sync in background, then reload | minutes (background budget) |

**Day rollover** happens on every surface from its own timeline, not from a sync (widget study: widgets stuck on yesterday).

## 7. iPad and Android tablets

- **A tablet on its own is free; a tablet together with a phone is Plus** (decided 1 Oct 2026; replaces "tablets are Plus only", 27 Sep).
  - Free works on **any one device**: 5 habits, no account, no sync, the same as a free phone. Tablet-only people get something to try instead of a paywall on first launch (users show: 29 "iPad is my main device" reviews; "I thought it was free" reviews average 1.20★; an iPad user's 1★ said the app should say up front that you have to pay, `A4#8418`).
  - **Plus** is what payers ask for: the same habits on phone and tablet, kept in sync (58 "I paid but it's not on my iPad" reviews, none objecting that a tablet is paid; [evidence §4](<../Research/Research Reports/Business Model and Monetization/Plus Scope and Account at Purchase.md>)).
  - The listings say: "Free: 5 habits on one device. Plus: the same habits on all your devices, kept in sync." 
- **The same app,** universal, with a real large-screen layout: a sidebar and a detail pane, landscape, Split View and Slide Over on iPad (users show: “Please add slide-over and split-window support for the iPad.” (`A53#1007`)).
- **First screen on a tablet:**
  - **No Plus:** the normal free onboarding, plus one line that it stays on this device (§7.1). [I already have Plus] is in the corner (store restore, or sign in if Plus was bought on Android).
  - **Plus found on the same Apple ID or Google account:** unlocks automatically, then "Sign in to bring your habits from your phone" with Continue with Apple / Google. A small "Use on this iPad only" link stays, because Apple requires it (topic 1 §4).
  - Nothing is silently empty (users show: “it won’t have the data from the Fabulous app on the phone” (`A24#25500`)).
- **Sync is one system, through the account.** No iCloud sync, no CloudKit. A tablet used "on this iPad only" and later signed in merges by ID with a preview (topic 1 §3.4); it never overwrites.
- **Everything the phone shows, the tablet shows,** including history, not just the habit list (users show: habits appeared “pero no los días que hice” (`P10#12843`)).

### 7.1 Free on more than one device: what people see

**Free has no account, so there is nothing to sign in to or out of.** Each device is its own app with its own habits. *First principles: say it once, early, and never show anything that looks like sync.*

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| Free on phone and tablet, tick a habit on the tablet | Only the tablet changes. The phone stays as it was. Each device has its own 5 habits |
| **First launch on a second device, iPhone ⇄ iPad on the same Apple Account** (the app sees the other device's safety copy key in iCloud Keychain) | One card in onboarding: **"Your iPhone already has habits. On the free plan each device keeps its own habits; they won't stay in sync. [Copy my iPhone's habits here once] [Start fresh] · Want the same habits on both, kept in sync? That's Plus."** "Copy once" brings the iPhone's latest safety copy over. After that the two devices go separate ways, and each keeps its own safety copy |
| **First launch on any tablet** (Android tablets can't see the phone) | One line in onboarding: **"Free keeps your habits on this device. To use the same habits on your phone and tablet, kept in sync, get Plus."** Shown once |
| Every day after that | Nothing on Today. Settings → Backup & Devices shows "Habits on this device only · Free" with [About Plus]. No badges, pop-ups or reminders (users show: repeated prompts average 1.75★) |
| Reinstall or a new device replacing an old one | The safety copy restore ("Welcome back") picks up where that device left off. The restore list names each copy by device ("iPhone 16 · last night 21:40") |
| **They buy Plus later and sign in on both devices** | Both devices' habits merge into one list by ID, with a preview first (topic 1 §3.4). Nothing is overwritten or lost. Two habits made separately with the same name show side by side, and the preview offers to combine them (detail to design) |

**Plus, for comparison:**
- **Sign in once per device; sessions never expire** (topic 1 §3.5). Nobody signs in again every time.
- A tick on the tablet shows on the phone within seconds while the app is open, otherwise at the next open.
- **Sign-out** asks "Keep a copy of your habits on this device?" (default yes) and never wipes data. Signing back in merges by ID, so changes made while signed out are kept and nothing duplicates.

## 8. Web and desktop (later)

### 8.1 Web

- **The same protocol and core.** The web app is a client like any other: a local SQLite database in the browser (SQLite WebAssembly on the Origin Private File System), an outbox and `/v1/sync`. It works offline once loaded.
- **Plus and an account required,** because a browser tab is not somewhere data should live alone. Sign-in is Apple (Sign in with Apple JS) or Google (Google Identity Services).
- **Parity by construction.** The web uses the same core (§9), so its numbers match the app (users show: web and app out of step, `P8#7013`, `A85#3125`).
- **Installable** as an app on Windows, ChromeOS and Mac. This is how Windows is covered first.

### 8.2 Mac and Windows

- **Mac:** a native SwiftUI Mac app built from the same code as iOS, with keyboard shortcuts and a menu-bar item for quick check-off (users ask: `A46#1253`, `A25#832`). It uses the same local database, sync and snapshot design as iOS.
- **Windows:** the installable web app first. A native Windows app only if usage shows demand.
- **Old desktop versions can't damage data:** the protocol keeps fields a client doesn't know (topic 5 §12), and the server accepts old protocol versions (topic 6 §8). A lagging Mac build can never wipe what a newer phone wrote (users show: `A23#4793`).

### 8.3 Billing on the web

- **No web sales** (decided 28 Sep 2026). The web and Windows apps unlock Plus from the account, bought in the App Store or Google Play (topic 2).
- **If web sales are ever added** (parked): they must be cancellable in the app with one tap, shown in "Your purchases", and never create a second subscription alongside a store one (users show: 8 web-billing reviews, mean 1.0 — “you have to go to the website to delete your account” (`A19#95`)). Store rules on linking out to web purchases differ by country and change often; check them at the time.

## 9. One core, every platform

**Accepted, 26 Sep 2026:** use **Kotlin Multiplatform (KMP)** for shared domain logic, keep platform UIs native, and maintain a written behavior contract plus shared fixtures. This resolves Backlog #15. The authoritative scope, platform mapping and implementation requirements are in [Shared Core Decision](<Shared Core Decision.md>); production examples and support routes are in [Shared Core Research](<Shared Core Research.md>).

The rules implemented once in the KMP core include:

- which day a tap belongs to (explicit time-zone context and day start);
- which week a date is in (week start);
- due or not due (schedule rules and their effective dates);
- streaks, progress and totals;
- field-level merge, deduplication, tombstones and explicit restore semantics;
- semantic snapshot contents.

**Platform delivery:** SwiftUI apps on iPhone/iPad and Mac call Kotlin/Native frameworks. Android and Wear OS use the Kotlin library through the standard Android runtime. Web and the initial Windows PWA use Kotlin/JS. Apple Watch uses a watchOS build; the Cloudflare adapter consumes the pure merge/validation subset through Kotlin/JS. Target builds and integration checks are implementation work, not a pending language decision.

**Native boundaries:** UI, database drivers, network transport, notifications, Health APIs, billing and OS scheduling remain platform adapters. They call the core and execute its results; writes and outbox changes remain atomic. No separate Swift, Kotlin and TypeScript copies of domain rules are planned.

**Documentation and tests:** keep authoritative rules in the planned `Core/spec/` and language-neutral inputs/expected outputs in `Core/fixtures/`. Update the contract, Kotlin implementation and fixtures together. Run the same cases through each supported platform caller in CI, including date boundaries, backfills, merge convergence and mixed app versions. Shared source does not remove the need for platform integration tests.

## 10. Test checklist

- Tick from a widget with the app force-quit → the entry is saved once, the widget updates at once, and it syncs on the next app launch or background refresh.
- Tick the same habit from the notification and the widget within a second → one entry per tap ID; the habit shows done.
- Suspend the app mid-write for 10 minutes → no 0xDEAD10CC crash (the database isn't in a shared container).
- Complete a habit on the Watch while the phone is off → it syncs directly (signed in) or queues (Plus without an account), and appears once on the phone.
- Relay the same Watch op twice → counted once.
- Complete a habit on the iPad → the phone's notification for it disappears.
- Leave a Live Activity running and complete the habit on the Watch → the activity ends.
- Day start 03:00, phone locked overnight → at 03:00 the widget shows the new day without the app being opened.
- The shared fixture suite passes through the KMP core and its Swift, Android, web and server callers in CI; shipped Mac and Watch targets are covered too.
- An old Mac build syncs after a newer phone added a field → the field survives.
- An iPad on first open → sign in brings the full history.
- A tablet, Watch or Wear OS device without Plus → shows the Plus screen; with Plus on the same store account it unlocks automatically.
