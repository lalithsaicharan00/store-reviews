# Habit Check Calendar (App Store ID 1629764198) — Full Review Analysis

**App:** Habit Check Calendar / 習慣チェックカレンダー — a weekly-grid habit tracker
**Developer:** Naoki Otsu (individual developer) · bundle `com.weeklychecklist`
**Corpus:** all 151 App Store text reviews, 23 storefronts, 2022-06-25 → 2026-09-02
**Extracted:** 2026-09-08 · **External data accessed:** 13 September 2026
**Decision this informs:** whether, and how, to change the trial-and-paywall presentation of a
one-time-purchase habit tracker whose product quality is not in dispute.

---

## How to read this

Every number here is a count over a fully hand-classified corpus. All 151 reviews were read
individually in their original language; none was sampled, keyword-filtered or skipped. Each review
carries one or more theme codes from a 106-code taxonomy, and the map from review ID to codes is
reproducible from `temp/74-cls/`. Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — a review that praises simplicity
and asks for a widget is counted in both. Composite unions (`U:…`) count each review once.

Percentages are shares of 151 unless a different denominator is named. Signal labels follow the
project's threshold table. Because 151 is small, every headline figure also carries a **Wilson 95%
confidence interval**, and no finding rests on a single review unless the report says so explicitly.

### ⚠️ Nine warnings before any number here is used

1. **This is a text-review corpus, not a rating corpus.** Japan's 103 text reviews sit on a public
   base of **3,151 JP ratings** — this corpus is **3.27% of it**. The corpus 1★ share in Japan is
   24.27% against a public JP 1★ share of **3.11%**. **People who are angry write; people
   who are content tap a star.** Do not read this corpus as the user population.
2. **The paywall event is not externally dated.** Version history was not retrievable (see Part 9 §D).
   The date used here, 2025-01-04, is the first review reporting it — a lower bound on when it shipped,
   not the ship date.
3. Country claims are made for **Japan only**. It is the one storefront at or above 50 reviews.
   Every other storefront is 12 reviews or fewer and is treated as a lead, never a conclusion.
4. **No refund, cancellation or chargeback exists in this corpus.** A one-time purchase produces no
   renewal and no cancellation event, so no churn-after-purchase rate is derivable.
5. **21 reviews (13.91%) carry sentiment but no product detail** ("Goood",
   "Bellissima"). They count in denominators and in ratings, and contribute nothing to theme evidence.
6. Reported prices in the corpus span ¥600 → ¥800 → ¥1,000 and $2.99 → $3.99, while the listings today
   show ¥780 and $4.99. These are **reviewer reports at the time of writing**, not a price history.
7. The free-window length is reported as ~1 week by 21 reviewers and as 3 months by one. The corpus
   cannot say whether the rule changed, differed by cohort, or was misremembered.
8. Theme assignment is a human judgement. Borderline calls are listed in Part 9 §C.
9. Reviews are a self-selected sample with no verified-purchase flag. "Paying reviewer" means the
   reviewer **said** they paid.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

## 0.1 The product is not the problem. The disclosure is.

This corpus is unusually clean about cause and effect, because the same people who attack the app
praise it in the same sentence.

- **Trial grievance:** 36 reviews, 23.84% of the corpus, **High-priority**, mean
  **1.50★**. First 2025-01-04, last 2026-06-25.
- Those 36 reviews are **27 of the 30 one-star reviews**
  (90.00% of the 1★ population).
- Meanwhile **65 reviews (43.05%)** praise its simplicity and ease, and
  **22 (14.57%)** prefer it to competitors they have tried.

The grievance is not "this app is bad" and it is not "this app is too expensive". Read directly, it is
**"nobody told me"**. 23 reviews (15.23%) make the non-disclosure
complaint specifically, and 14 of them go further and volunteer the fix — say it
upfront, or sell it as a paid app outright.

Three reviewers state the distinction explicitly:

> 「有料化は仕方無いですが、突然過去の履歴等も見れなくなってしまうのは不親切ではないでしょうか。」
> *"Going paid is unavoidable, but suddenly not being able to see my past history either — isn't that
> unkind?"* — `12183022252` (1★, JP)

> 「良いアプリを継続していくには、利益を出さないといけないので有料版OKです。事前に告知してくれればOKです。けどなぜなんの告知もなく勝手に？」
> *"To keep a good app going you have to make a profit, so a paid version is fine. It's fine if you tell
> me beforehand. But why, with no notice at all?"* — `12182389221` (1★, JP)

> "It would be much fairer and more transparent if this limitation were clearly communicated upfront.
> Overall, the concept and execution are good, but the restrictions are disappointing."
> — `13883589639` (1★, UA)

**The external listing corroborates them.** Both the US and Japanese store descriptions
(accessed 13 September 2026) enumerate every feature — notifications, backup, CSV export, unlimited tabs —
and **contain no statement of a trial, a time limit, or any feature being paid-only**. The only
monetization signal on the page is the "In-App Purchases" ribbon. One reviewer dissects exactly this:

> 「アプリの紹介のところには有料アプリではなく｢アプリ内課金｣と書かれています。嘘では無いですが、ユーザーを増やすための意図的なミスリードにしか思えません。」
> *"The listing says 'in-app purchase', not 'paid app'. It isn't a lie, but I can't see it as anything
> other than deliberate misleading to grow the user base."* — `12177849705` (2★, JP)

## 0.2 The event, dated

Nothing resembling this complaint exists before 2025-01-04. Then it arrives all at once.

| Era | Window | n | Share | Mean ★ | 5★ | 1★ | Trial grievance |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| E1 | pre-paywall 2022-06-25..2025-01-03 | 51 | 33.77% | 4.43 | 33 | 1 | 0 (0.00%) |
| E2 | paywall shock 2025-01-04..2025-06-30 | 37 | 24.50% | 2.78 | 13 | 17 | 22 (59.46%) |
| E3 | settled 2025-07-01..2026-09-02 | 63 | 41.72% | 3.76 | 33 | 12 | 14 (22.22%) |

Era E1 contains **0** trial grievances in 51 reviews. Era E2 contains
**22 in 37 — 59.46%**. Mean rating falls from **4.43★ to
2.78★**, and 1★ reviews go from 1 in two and a half years to 17 in six months.

January 2025 alone carries 17 reviews — the busiest month in the corpus by a factor of two — of which
12 are trial grievances and 10 are 1★.

**But it did not end.** Era E3 still runs at 22.22% grievance
(14 of 63), and the most recent grievance is dated 2026-06-25. This is the single most
consequential fact in the report: **the January 2025 spike was an event, but the underlying complaint
is a steady state that is still generating 1★ reviews twenty months later.**

## 0.3 The corpus contains a directly measured lost sale

Refusals to pay are 7 reviews (4.64%, mean 1.29★). What matters is not
the count but their content — several state they were willing to pay **until the method changed their
mind**:

> 「全ての項目を作って日記がわりにチェックを入れて数ヶ月使用していました。**お金払っても良いかなって思っていました。** 急に「お金払わないと使わせない」と言ってきました。**お金払う気がなくなりました。** 作成側の態度一つで気持ちってこんなにも変わるんですね。」
> *"I'd used it for months… **I had been thinking I might as well pay.** Then suddenly it told me 'pay
> or you can't use it'. **I lost any desire to pay.** Amazing how much one attitude from the developer
> changes how you feel."* — `12670123787` (1★, JP, 4/6 helpful votes)

> 「課金も考えたが、このやり方だと先行き不安なので他のアプリに乗り換えました。良いアプリだっただけに残念です。」
> *"I considered paying, but this method makes me uneasy about the future, so I switched to another app.
> A shame, because it was a good app."* — `12187124676` (1★, JP)

This is a conversion problem caused by presentation, not by price or by product. 14 reviews
(9.27%) state they uninstalled, switched away, refused to pay, or lost trust.

## 0.4 The people who did pay are happy, and they say why

20 reviewers (13.25%) state they bought premium. Their mean rating is **4.15★**
against a corpus mean of 3.748★. Not one asks for a refund. Not one regrets the amount.

What they name, in their own words, is the **one-time** model — 14 reviews
(9.27%, mean 4.71★) praise buy-once *as such*, and
5 more prefer it specifically because it is not a subscription.

> "I hate apps that require a subscription. I love apps that are simple, 1 time purchase, easy to use…
> **Best 3.99 I've ever spent.**" — `13683417986` (5★, US)

> 「プレミアムプランをかれこれ2、3年利用しています。…プレミアムプランの方は、個人的には買い切りの部類では安い方だと感じています。むしろ値段の割にクオリティがとても高くて」
> *"I've been on the premium plan for two or three years now… for a buy-once I personally think it's on
> the cheap side. If anything the quality is very high for the price."* — `13862388358` (5★, JP)

**The pricing is not the problem. Only the moment of disclosure is.** That is an unusually cheap thing
to fix.

## 0.6 Outcomes: this app is used for medication adherence

26 reviews (17.22%, mean 4.77★) describe a concrete outcome.
Within them, **9 describe a health use** — and 5 specifically describe
**medication adherence**.

> 「毎日服用する薬を2週間毎ぐらい掛けて増量していく治療方針だったのですが、飲み忘れる事が多くて初期の投薬量にいつも戻っていました。…薬の項目１つ作って、そこに20mg.40mg.100mgと別々の欄を作りました。…無事に最終の投与量を維持できています。」
> *"My treatment plan raised the dose every two weeks, but I kept forgetting doses and always got reset
> to the starting dose… I made one medication item with separate rows for 20mg, 40mg, 100mg… I've
> safely reached and held the final dose."* — `12050608960` (5★, JP)

This raises the stakes on the lockout. One of the 1★ reviewers was tracking exactly this when access
was cut:

> 「有料になるなんて思わなくて何飲んでたかメモしておけば良かった。」
> *"I never thought it would go paid — I wish I'd written down what I'd been taking."* — `12171233300`

**A habit tracker used for medication adherence is a clinical-adjacent record.** Locking read access to
it behind a paywall is a materially different act from locking a feature.

## 0.8 The ten decisions this corpus supports

1. **Disclose the trial before install.** 23 reviews demand it; 14 write the copy for you. (§0.1, §3.3 N1)
2. **Never lock read access to existing records.** 6 reviews, mean 1.17★ — the angriest sub-theme in the corpus. (§3.3 N2)
3. **Keep the one-time price; stop hiding it.** 26 reviews are positive about the model; only 1 objects to the amount. (§5.3)
4. **Ship sync.** 7 requests plus 2 broken-restore reports = 8 reviews. (§3.6)
5. **Ship colour control.** 10 requests — the largest single request in the corpus. (§3.6)
6. **Ship a monthly view.** 6 requests, one of which names it as a purchase condition. (§3.6)
7. **Fix the onboarding first-run.** 3 reviews could not find the "+". (§3.5)
8. **Treat Japan as the product's home market and its risk concentration.** 68.21% of reviews, 31.07% of them grievances. (Part 6)
9. **Reduce the review prompt frequency.** One review, but it is a self-inflicted rating risk. (§3.5)
10. **Do not add streaks, goals or nagging.** 3 reviews praise their absence as the reason they chose it. (§3.4 P5)

## 1.4 Deduplication rule

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Dump** (`74-dump.py`) — parse, HTML-unescape titles and bodies, sort by `(date, review_id)`, assign
   a stable working index 1…151, emit `74-all-reviews.txt` and `74-index.tsv`.
2. **Read** — all 151 reviews read in full, in date order, in their original language
   (102 Japanese, 38 Latin-script, 5 Chinese, 3 Cyrillic, 2 Korean, 1 Thai).
3. **Classify by hand** — one line per review in `74-cls/`, recording the codes *and a note quoting what
   drove the judgement*. This is the expensive artefact; everything else regenerates from it.
4. **Resolve** (`74-build-classification.py`) — map working index → `review_id`, asserting
   `74-index.tsv` field-by-field against `reviews.jsonl` first; emit `74-review-classification.py`.
5. **Validate** (`74-validate.py`) — 21 integrity checks, all passing (§1.8).
6. **Aggregate** (`74-common.py`, `74-aggregate.py`, `74-extra.py`) — counts, unions, Wilson intervals,
   ratings, storefronts, eras, months, the paid cohort, bursts, contradictions, price timeline.
7. **Generate** (`74-tables.py`) — every table and 471 numeric facts to `74-gen/`.
8. **Build and verify** (`74-build-report.py`, `74-verify-report.py`) — placeholder substitution that
   refuses on any unresolved token, then post-write verification against the corpus.

**Classification is fully manual.** No clustering, embedding, or keyword rule assigned any code. The
error profile is therefore human judgement, not model error; §9C lists the borderline calls.

## 1.6 Limitations — read these before citing any figure

1. **Selection bias, quantified.** 103 JP text reviews sit on a public base of 3,151 JP ratings.
   The corpus is 3.27% of that base and its 1★ share (24.27%) is **7.8× the public JP 1★
   share (3.11%)**. This corpus over-represents grievance by roughly an order of magnitude.
2. **No version field and no retrievable version history.** No finding can be tied to a release.
3. **The paywall ship date is inferred**, not observed. 2025-01-04 is a lower bound.
4. **Small n.** 151 reviews means one review is 0.66% — already an "Emerging" signal.
   Wilson intervals are given throughout; several are very wide.
5. **Only Japan clears the 50-review bar.** No other storefront supports a standalone claim.
6. **No spend data.** Market groups are defined from public rating bases and corpus volume, both
   disclosed proxies. Neither is a download or revenue figure.
7. **No verified-purchase flag.** The paid cohort is self-reported.
8. **Translation.** Japanese, Chinese, Korean, Russian, Thai, Portuguese, Spanish, French, Italian,
   Czech, Swedish and Turkish reviews were read in the original; English glosses are the analyst's and
   are marked with *italics*.
9. **A 6-day tail.** The last review is 2026-09-02 and extraction was 2026-09-08; the final week
   is effectively unobserved.

## 1.7 Review integrity — bursts, solicitation, contradictions

- **No solicited-review pattern.** No same-day cluster exceeds 3 reviews. The 15 same-day clusters are
  mostly mixed-rating (e.g. 2025-01-13: 2★/5★/1★), which is the opposite of a seeded burst.
- **One genuine burst, and it is organic.** 2025-01 carries 17 reviews against a trailing median of 2–3.
  It is 10× 1★ and 12× trial grievance across two storefronts (JP and ES) — an event response, not
  a campaign.
- **No incentivised-review language** anywhere: no reviewer mentions being given premium, a code, a
  giveaway, or a reward for reviewing.
- **`is_edited` is true on 2 reviews** (`12642784508`, `12884192689`); 2 more carry an in-body 追記
  addendum (`9180394164`, `12642784508`). One of those addenda — `9180394164` — is a 5★ review whose
  addendum reports total data loss, which is why it counts in both the praise and the defect column.
- **Helpful votes concentrate on the grievance.** Of the top 8 reviews by vote count, **7 are trial
  grievances** and 6 are 1★. The single most-voted review in the corpus (`12177849705`, 5 of 7 helpful)
  is the 2★ analytical critique of the disclosure. Other users are validating this complaint.
- **Rating-vs-text contradictions, disclosed rather than dropped:**
  - 4 reviews rated **5★ whose entire text is a complaint or a question** — `11783851398` (tab won't
    delete), `11795393986` (memo won't fully delete), `13201852224` (wrong Russian weekday
    abbreviations), `13741075347` (*"How do I pay???????"*). These inflate the 5★ band with negative
    content and are counted under their negative codes.
  - 2 reviews rated **3★ whose text is unmixed praise** — `9530559288`, `14091004094`.
  - Net effect: the rating distribution **understates** dissatisfaction at 5★ and **overstates** it at
    3★. Both are small and both are reported rather than corrected.

## 1.8 Post-write verification

`74-validate.py` runs 21 checks on the classification (all pass): every source ID mapped and every
mapped ID real; map size equals corpus size; zero unassigned records; zero intra-review duplicate codes;
zero unknown codes; **zero unused taxonomy codes**; every union member a real code and non-empty on the
corpus; index contiguous; dates sorted; era partition exact; ratings in range; every 1★ review carries a
negative code; the four praise-rated complaints pinned by ID; no reviewer both paid and refused; no
grievance code co-occurring with `TRL_ACCEPTS`; every `TRL_*` review dated on or after 2025-01-04.

`74-verify-report.py` then re-derives the report's claims from the corpus after the file is written
(§9F).

## 4.5 Two stars — n = 6 (3.97%)

The smallest band, and almost entirely the trial: **5 of 6 carry a trial-grievance code**. These are

## 4.6 One star — n = 30 (19.87%)

**27 of 30 one-star reviews (90.00%) are trial grievances.**
The remaining 3 are: the 2022 data-loss report (`9180240356`), the failed purchase transfer from a
paying customer (`13308747446`), and the add-button failure (`13339968184`) — which may itself be the
paywall lock described as a defect.

This is the cleanest finding in the report. **Fix the disclosure and the data lock, and you address the
stated cause of nearly nine in ten of your worst reviews.** No other product change available to this
developer has comparable leverage.

## 4.7 Rating does not fix theme direction

`PAY_PURCHASED` spans 1★ to 5★. `UX_EASY` appears at 1★ (in a review that praises the app while
attacking the paywall) and at 5★. `TRL_NO_DISCLOSURE` appears at 4★ and 5★. Star rating is a summary
judgement about the whole relationship, not a verdict on any one feature, and this corpus demonstrates
it in both directions.

## 5.3 Price perception

- **10 reviews call the price good value** (mean 4.70★).
- **1 review objects to the amount** — `12450403767`, 「600円なら他にもいいアプリが
  ありそう」.
- 4 argue the feature set does not justify *any* price — a different objection,
  and notably three of the four are trial-grievance reviews, so the judgement is made while angry.
- 1 predicts escalation (`12670123787`, 「1200円、2400円…と値上げしていきます」).

**The corpus contains essentially no price resistance.** One review out of 151 objects to the amount.
Compare with 23 objecting to how the amount was revealed. If the developer is weighing a
price change against a disclosure change, the corpus is unambiguous about which matters.

## 5.4 Upgrade barriers

1. **The disclosure itself destroys willingness to pay.** Documented directly in `12670123787` and
   `12187124676` (§0.3) — both state they were willing, then were not.
2. **Distrust of continuity.** 5 say they cannot trust the developer going forward;
   1 (a *buyer*) asks the app not be shut down.
3. **Missing features named as conditions.** 5 reviews (§5.2).
4. **Cannot complete the purchase.** `13741075347` (5★, RU) — 「Кааак оплатить???????」 *"How do I
   pay???????"*. Full intent, zero friction tolerance, no sale.
5. **Purchase does not transfer.** 2 (§3.3 N6).

## 5.5 Refunds, cancellation, renewal — none exist

A corpus-wide search for refund, cancellation, chargeback and unsubscribe vocabulary across Japanese,
English, Portuguese, Spanish, Russian, Korean and Chinese returned **zero genuine matches** (one hit was
a substring false positive inside the Korean word 가능해지거나).

This is structural, not accidental: **a one-time purchase has no renewal to cancel.** Consequences:

- No churn-after-purchase rate is derivable, and none should be inferred.
- No reviewer regrets the amount after paying. The only paid-user 1★ (`13308747446`) is about the
  purchase not transferring, not about value.
- The recurring-revenue risk is zero and the recurring-revenue opportunity is zero. The commercial
  lever available here is **conversion rate**, and conversion is what the disclosure problem is damaging.

## 5.6 What the paid cohort asks for

Inside the paid cohort, the requests are: colour control (3), sync (3), mark types (2), and single
instances of numeric entry, notifications, widget, monthly view and row reordering. **Paying customers
and the general corpus want the same things**, which simplifies prioritisation — there is no separate
premium roadmap to run.

### 6.3.2 Reading the difference honestly

The gap is large — JP mean 3.49★ versus 4.31★ — but **most of it is engagement depth,
not national sentiment.** The evidence:

- **27.08% of non-JP reviews are sentiment-only** against 7.77% of JP reviews. The rest-of-world corpus

  accounts. Short reviews skew positive in every corpus.
- **Every churn signal in the entire corpus is Japanese** (14 of 14). So is every
  defect report and every usability-friction report. Japanese reviewers are doing the diagnostic work.
- The JP public rating average is **4.609** — barely below the US 4.814 and above CA 4.571. **The
  Japanese user population is not unhappy.** The Japanese *review-writing* population is.

**Conclusion.** Japan is where the product is used most seriously, where the paywall event landed
hardest, and where the recovery must be measured. It is not evidence of a market-specific product
problem. It is evidence that the market with the most invested users produced the most complaints when
their investment was locked.

Comparing the two groupings gives one genuinely useful observation: **Brazil has a public rating base of
100 — comparable to the US at 102 — but only 4 text reviews here, all short praise.** Either Brazilian
users rate without writing, or the corpus under-samples Brazil. Both are worth knowing before treating
non-JP text volume as a proxy for market size.

**No high-spend claim is made anywhere in this report.** There is no data to support one.

## 6.6 What country data does *not* support

- Not that Japanese users dislike the app — the JP public average is 4.609 over 3,151 ratings.
- Not that Western users accept the paywall — four non-JP reviewers attack it in three languages.
- Not any per-country price sensitivity — 1 price objection exists in the entire corpus.
- Not any cultural generalisation. The JP/ROW difference is explained by review depth and by the fact
  that the app's most invested users are in its home market.

## 7.3 The five trends this corpus supports

### T1 — The January 2025 spike was an event; the grievance is now a **steady state** — *persistent*

E2 at 59.46% was a spike. But E3 runs at **22.22% across 63 reviews spanning
14 months**, and the most recent grievance is 2026-06-25 — nine weeks before extraction. The rate fell;
it did not stop. **Every new install still meets the same undisclosed wall.** This is the difference
between a migration cost already paid and an ongoing tax, and the corpus says it is the latter.

### T2 — Ratings partially recovered, and did not return to baseline — *improving, incomplete*

4.43 → 2.78 → 3.76. E3 has recovered most of the fall but sits **0.67
stars below** the pre-paywall era, with 12 one-star reviews against 1 in a period twice
as long.

### T3 — The 2022 defect cluster closed and never reopened — *resolved*

All three `BUG_DATA_LOSS` reports fall inside 48 hours in October 2022. In the following four years and
146 reviews, not one further data-loss report appears. The developer appears to have fixed it
and the corpus confirms it — though the reputational tail persisted into 2024 (§3.7).

### T4 — The request backlog shifted from *capability* to *polish* — *shifting*

Early requests are for things that did not exist: notifications (2023-09), monthly view (2023-06),
widget (2023-04), sync (2023-03). Later requests are about refinement: colour variations
(`13744982720`, `13862388358`), row height (`13767028110`), per-item default icons (`13427992409`),
merged weekly display (`14500401014`). Two early requests appear to have shipped — notifications are
praised as present by `13713824996` (2026-02), and the listing now claims widgets.

### T5 — Reviews became shorter and less informative outside Japan — *rising*

`META_LOWINFO` is 27.08% of non-JP reviews against 7.77% of JP reviews, and the non-JP share of the
corpus rises across eras. As international distribution grows, the *diagnostic* value of the corpus
falls even as its volume rises.

## 7.4 What persisted, unchanged, across all three eras

Simplicity, glanceability, ease of use, competitive preference and outcome reporting appear at
comparable rates in E1, E2 and E3. **The paywall event changed what people complained about. It did not
change what people liked.** That is the central asymmetry of this corpus and the reason the
recommendations in Part 8 are all about presentation rather than product.

## 7.5 What this corpus cannot say about time

- Whether the paywall increased revenue. No conversion or revenue data exists.
- Whether total installs fell. Review volume actually rose after the change, which measures attention,
  not installs.
- Whether the E3 recovery reflects a fix or simply the exhaustion of the pre-existing free user base.
  **This distinction matters enormously and the corpus cannot settle it.** The steady 22.22%
  grievance rate is weak evidence for the second reading.
- Anything about the final 6 days before extraction, or about the 9 releases-worth of time since the
  last review — version history was unavailable.

## 8.4 Positioning and monetization strategy

- **Keep the one-time price.** 26 reviews are positive about the model against
  1 objection to the amount. Buy-once is a stated differentiator against the
  subscription category (5 reviews).
- **Consider selling it as a paid app outright.** 14 reviewers proposed exactly
  this, unprompted. It is the cleanest resolution of the entire grievance and costs nothing the corpus
  can identify — the buyers were fast deciders (§5.2), not users who needed a week of persuasion.
- **Do not add streaks, goals or gamification.** 3 reviewers named their absence as
  the reason they chose this app over the category (§3.4 P5).
- **Do not add complexity.** 65 reviews (43.05%) praise restraint; the
  competitive wins (§3.4 P4) are all against apps described as "overly complicated".
- **Protect the first-run impression.** `14351890595` praises the absence of an upfront subscription
  pitch. The task is to disclose the model *without* becoming the thing that reviewer was relieved to
  avoid — a one-line statement, not a paywall screen.

1. **Did the paywall increase revenue?** The only honest answer is unknown. This report documents a
   reputational cost, not a net commercial outcome.
2. **Was the free window ever 3 months?** `12273751279` says so; 21 others say a week.
3. **Does sync work?** The corpus contradicts itself and the listing contradicts the corpus.
4. **Is the E3 recovery a fix, or the exhaustion of the legacy free user base?** (§7.5)
5. **What happened in the 9 releases since the last review?** Version history was not retrievable.
6. **Why does Brazil rate (100 ratings) but not write (4 reviews)?** (§6.5)

## 8.7 What to do first

If only one thing is done: **recommendation 3 — never lock read access to a user's own records.** It
addresses the lowest-rated theme in the corpus (mean 1.17★), it removes the
vocabulary that appears in review titles prospective buyers read first, and it is a permissions change
rather than a feature.

If three things are done, add **1 and 2** — state the model in the listing, and show the trial clock
in-app. Together with 3, these address the stated cause of 27 of 30 one-star
reviews, and they require no new capability at all.

# PART 9 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## A. Counting rules

1. **Denominator.** Every percentage is over 151 unless a different denominator is named in the same
   sentence. Country percentages use that country's own review count. Segment rates (paid cohort, 1★
   population) always name the segment denominator and are labelled as segment rates.
2. **Non-exclusive themes.** A review carries every code that applies. Theme counts therefore sum to more
   than 151. Composite unions (`U:…`) count each review **once** regardless of how many member codes
   it carries, and are the correct figure for "how many reviews are about X".
3. **Signal labels** follow the project threshold table: <0.1% ignore · 0.1–0.5% Weak · 0.5–1% Emerging ·
   1–3% Meaningful · 3–5% Very strong · >5% High-priority. At 151 reviews **one review is
   0.66%**, which already lands in "Emerging" — so raw counts and Wilson 95% intervals are
   printed beside every share and should be read in preference to the label at low n.
4. **Mean ★** is the unweighted mean rating of the reviews carrying that code.
5. **Direction** (+/−/~) is a property of the code, not of the rating. A negative code in a 5★ review is
   still negative (§1.7).
6. **No deduplication** was applied; none was needed (§1.4).
7. **Review IDs** are the source `review_id` values, verbatim. Every ID printed in this report was
   verified to exist in `reviews.jsonl` (§F).

## C. Judgement calls and known error risk

Classification is manual, so the error mode is judgement. The calls that could reasonably have gone the
other way:

1. **`13339968184`** ("+ does nothing", 1★, 2025-11) is coded as a **defect**, not a trial grievance.
   The text describes a broken button, not a paywall. If it is in fact the paywall lock, `BUG_ADD_FAIL`
   drops to 0 and `U:trial_grievance` rises to 37. Flagged in §3.7 and §8.2.
2. **`TRL_ACCEPTS` vs `TRL_NO_DISCLOSURE`.** `14002101328` states the non-disclosure as fact and then
   pays anyway; it carries both codes. This is deliberate — the disclosure gap is real even when the
   outcome is a sale.
3. **`12273751279`** reports 3 months rather than 1 week. Coded as a trial grievance on content; the
   duration discrepancy is reported rather than resolved (§3.3 N1).
4. **`META_LOWINFO` boundary.** Reviews with one concrete noun ("easy to use") are coded `UX_EASY` *and*
   `META_LOWINFO` only where nothing else is said. Moving this boundary would change `META_LOWINFO` by a
   few reviews and would not change any finding.
5. **`PAY_FREE_SUFFICIENT` in the post-paywall era.** `13075237071` (2025-08) and `14009846033`
   (2026-04) call the app free. They are almost certainly *inside* the undisclosed window rather than
   describing a free tier. Coded at face value and noted here, because this is itself evidence of how
   the trial is perceived.
6. **`OUT_HABIT_FORMED` requires a claim of sustained use**, not merely positive sentiment. "I use it
   every day" qualifies; "great app" does not.
7. **The `U:churn` union counts intent and action together** (uninstalled, switched, refused, lost
   trust). It is a measure of expressed churn, not of observed churn.

**Estimated error risk:** low for the trial-grievance family, whose members are explicit and repetitive;
moderate for the `META_LOWINFO` boundary and for `UX_SIMPLE` vs `UX_EASY`, which overlap in meaning and
are reported both separately and as the `U:simplicity` union for exactly that reason.
