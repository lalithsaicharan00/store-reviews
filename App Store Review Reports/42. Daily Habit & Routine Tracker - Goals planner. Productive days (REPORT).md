## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Every claim traces to an exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`. **Part 10.5 is a complete 206-row index** — every single review in the corpus, with its ID, country, star rating, date, verbatim title and assigned themes. Nothing in this report is unreachable from that table.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several themes. All 206 records carry at least one theme (39 of them carry only `low_info`).
- Signal thresholds: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority. Denominator for every global percentage is **206**.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.

### ⚠️ Nine warnings before any number here is used

1. **This corpus is small (206) and bimodal.** 83 reviews are 5★ (40.3%) and 67 are 1★ (32.5%) — 150 of 206 records (72.8%) sit at one extreme or the other. Only 12 reviews (5.8%) are 4★. A corpus shaped like this has almost no middle ground: the signal is *which* population each pole represents, not the average.
2. **The corpus mean (3.136) is far below the store's displayed aggregate (4.6 from 4.7K ratings, accessed 11 September 2026).** Written reviews here are a ~4% sliver of all ratings and are heavily skewed toward people motivated enough to type. **Do not read 3.136 as this app's rating.** Read it as: *among people who bothered to write, the split is this bad.*
3. **Two review bursts inflate the positive side and must be excluded from market conclusions.** (a) Six US reviews posted 11–13 August 2020 (`6306860426`, `6307010280`, `6308178252`, `6308196493`, `6312219230`, `6316224959`) are all 5★, carry **13–30 "helpful" votes each when the entire rest of the corpus maxes out at 10 and has a non-zero median of 1**, and have nonsense titles ("Hgfgg", "H", "Bdbdbd", "The"). (b) **Eleven of Canada's twelve reviews are 5★ generic English praise posted between 9 and 21 May 2021**, nine of them inside eight days. **Canada's perfect 5.00 mean is an artifact of that burst, not a market signal.** Removing both bursts moves the global mean from 3.136 to **2.968** (n=189). Both bursts are retained in every denominator and flagged as themes `us_2020_burst` and `ca_2021_burst`.
4. **Only one storefront clears the 50-review bar: Brazil (n=55).** The United States (46) and Russia (36) are below it and are presented in Part 7 as **limited evidence**, with warnings, because they carry materially different and internally consistent findings. Every other storefront is ≤14 reviews and gets no standalone conclusion.
5. **Germany, Australia and China have zero reviews in this corpus** (confirmed `complete: true, collected: 0` in `_state.json`). No statement about those markets is made or supportable.
6. **Classification was hand-curated, not blind-automated.** All 206 records were read individually, in full, in their original languages (Portuguese, Russian, Vietnamese, Turkish, Ukrainian, Spanish, Japanese, Hebrew, Korean, Thai, Czech, Polish, Norwegian, French, English). Every theme in this report is a hand-assigned ID list, not regex output. No sampling was used. Boundary rules are in Part 10.1.
7. **At least five records have a star rating that contradicts their own text** (2.43%): `6422725217` (GB, **5★**, *"I wasted 9.99 on this. I regret subscribing… I would avoid it"*), `7023381511` (KR, **1★**, *"별이 다섯 :: 쏘굿"* / "five stars :: so good"), `12144367844` (RU, **5★**, *"Приложение не открывается"* / "The app won't open"), `11764301384` (BR, **5★**, *"comprei e só trava"* / "I bought it and it just freezes"), `12017526630` (BR, **5★**, bug report). They are retained in every denominator, but rating-only analysis of this corpus carries measurable noise at roughly 2–3%.
8. **Prices quoted by reviewers vary enormously across time and storefront** — $39.99/yr (US, 2021), $20 (US, 2023), R$79.90/12 weeks (BR, 2023), ¥6,900/yr (JP, 2024), 500₽/yr (RU, 2024), R$99.90/yr (BR, 2025), 499k₫ (VN, 2025), £9.99 (GB, 2020). The seven concurrent IAP SKUs on the store listing ($4.99 → $59.99) are consistent with heavy price testing. **Price-objection rates across eras are not comparing a constant price.** Disclosed everywhere it matters.
9. **No conversion rate can be inferred from review text and none is claimed.** 29 reviewers (14.08%) give first-person evidence of having paid. That is a self-selected sample of writers, not of buyers.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. This product's rating is set entirely at the paywall. Monetization friction, not defects, is what produces its bad reviews — and the margin is not close.**
The union of every monetization-friction theme (free-tier cap, price, no-trial, deceptive-free, immediate paywall, upsell nagging, paywalled reminders, retroactive paywalling, paywalled history, subscription-model objection, trial traps, unexpected charges, refund requests, cancellations, insufficient-value-for-money) is **87 reviews (42.23% of 206, high-priority, mean rating 1.85)**. Those 87 reviews account for **61 of the 83 one- and two-star reviews (73.5%)** — 49 of 67 one-stars and 12 of 16 two-stars. By contrast, the union of *every* reliability defect (lag, crashes, data loss, check-off failures, broken statistics, reordering, badges, post-update regressions) is **31 reviews (15.05%, high-priority, mean 2.45)** and produces only **17 of 83 one- and two-stars (20.5%)**. **Interpretation: fixing every bug in this app would recover roughly a fifth of its bad reviews. Fixing the paywall would address three-quarters of them.**

**2. The 3-habit free cap is the single largest theme in the entire corpus and it is read as the product being broken, not as a price.**
**55 reviews (26.70%, high-priority, mean 1.96)** complain that the free tier is unusable — most commonly the 3-habit cap, but also paywalled reminders, paywalled scheduling, paywalled times and dates. That is **more than one review in four**, and it is the top theme in every rating band below 4★: 28 of 67 one-stars (41.8%), 9 of 16 two-stars (56.3%), 13 of 28 three-stars (46.4%). Representative: *"A habit app that doesn't let you have more than 3 habits"* — `10918059435` (US, 1★, *"when you join, you can only make 3 habits"*); *"Tudo é pago! Anotar uma rotina só o título é grátis, pq horário é pago, data, etc."* / "Everything is paid! Writing a routine, only the title is free, because the time is paid, the date, etc." (`12798927312`, BR, 2★); *"Bir alışkanlık oluşturduktan sonra daha fazla oluşturamadım artıya bastığımda üyelik istiyor"* / "after creating 3 habits I couldn't create more; when I press plus it asks for membership" (`11316242500`, TR, 1★); *"There is no reason to subscribe to the app to only have 3 habits"* (`11319751646`, NZ, 1★). Full ID list in Part 10.3. **Interpretation: at three habits a habit tracker cannot demonstrate the thing it is for. Reviewers are not refusing the price — most never reach a price judgement. They conclude the app does not work.**

**3. There is no trial, and the absence of one is named explicitly as the reason for refusing to pay.**
**14 reviews (6.80%, high-priority, mean 1.29 — the lowest mean of any theme in this report)** state that they could not evaluate the product before being asked to pay, and several name the trial length they wanted. *"debería dar un mes de prueba para testear antes de querer cobrar 20 dolares por algo que no se sabe como funciona"* / "it should give a month's trial to test before charging $20 for something you don't know how it works" (`12666033997`, AR, 1★); *"you should at least put 3 days trial"* (`11051247830`, DO, 1★); *"deveriam dar um teste grátis de 1 dia"* / "they should give a 1-day free trial" (`9003408900`, BR, 1★); *"not even a 7 day trial or letting me preview the app before spending $40"* (`8757372166`, US, 1★); *"Я не готова платить не посмотрев что там есть"* / "I'm not ready to pay without seeing what's in there" (`9925506606`, RU, 1★). Four of the 14 report the paywall appearing **before any use at all** (`8757372166`, `8868385470`, `10824739497`, `9925506606`, all 1★) — *"Literally two seconds after downloading the app it asks me to upgrade"* (`8868385470`, US). **Interpretation: this is the most directly actionable finding in the corpus. Fourteen people wrote down, unprompted, the specific mechanism that would have converted them.**

**4. Paying customers rate this app *worse* than non-paying ones, and more than a third of them are asking for their money back.**
**29 reviewers (14.08%, high-priority) give direct first-person evidence of having paid.** Their mean rating is **2.72**, against **3.20** for the 177 non-payers. **11 of those 29 payers (37.9% segment rate; 5.34% of the global corpus, high-priority) explicitly request or reference a refund** — `10053567444`, `7113224515`, `7295269143`, `7348111127`, `7669054186`, `11164675946`, `11826835199`, `12160321648`, `12317660239`, `12413759685`, `13555244418`. **9 of the 29 payers (31.0% segment rate) were hit by a reliability defect after paying.** **5 of 29 (17.2%) say the paid product was not worth the money** — *"I paid for premium and it's worthless"* (`6276118032`, US, 1★), *"Your alarm or reminder on your phone can do everything this app does. Wasted $20!"* (`10305077033`, US, 1★), *"Paguei mais caro que o que está oferecendo como premium. Sem funcionalidades excepcionais. Basicão."* / "I paid more than what it offers as premium. No exceptional features. Very basic." (`12317660239`, BR, 1★). **Interpretation: the paywall is not just blocking conversion — it is converting people into dissatisfied buyers. That is the more expensive failure, because it generates refunds, chargebacks and 1★ reviews from people who already paid.**

**5. A hard reliability event in December 2024 – January 2025 put the app in a non-opening state, and it hit Russia hardest.**
**7 reviews (3.40%, very strong, mean 1.71) report the app crashing on launch or not opening at all**; **5 of those 7 are Russian, all inside 40 days**: `12023625574` (4 Dec 2024, *"Второй день приложение не работает!"* / "Second day the app doesn't work!"), `12135350485` (2 Jan 2025), `12144367844` (4 Jan 2025), `12160321648` (8 Jan 2025), `12172502326` (11 Jan 2025, *"iPhone 13 pro max, ios 17.6.1 app crashes on start"*). One of them is a payer three months into an annual subscription: *"Заплатил за годовую подписку. Прошло 3 месяца и приложение не открывается вообще. Верните деньги, жулики."* / "I paid for a yearly subscription. Three months passed and the app doesn't open at all. Give my money back, crooks." (`12160321648`, 1★, edited). **Interpretation: a launch-blocking regression shipped and was visible in reviews for at least five weeks. Combined with warning 5 in Part 7, Russia is also the market where users report being unable to pay at all (`14165208412`) — a market that is both the most defect-exposed and the least monetizable.**

**6. Performance degradation is a Brazil-concentrated, paid-user-concentrated problem, and the "congratulations" animation is implicated in it.**
**12 reviews (5.83%, high-priority, mean 2.58) report the app lagging, freezing or being slow; 9 of the 12 are Brazilian (16.4% of BR's 55 reviews) and 4 of the 12 are payers.** Two of them name the specific trigger: *"A cada coisa que você marca ele sobe um troféu de conquista que trava tudo mais ainda!"* / "Every time you mark something, a trophy pops up which freezes everything even more!" (`11164675946`, BR, 2★, payer, *"Demoro pelo menos uns 5 minutos para conseguir colocar duas tarefas como feitas"* / "It takes me at least 5 minutes to mark two tasks as done"); *"каждый раз, когда выскакивает поздравление… тоже не сразу дает нажать кнопку продолжить… вообще это поздравление слишком часто выскакивает"* / "every time the congratulation pops up it won't let you press continue right away… and this congratulation pops up far too often" (`11476120833`, RU, 3★). One reviewer reports the slowdown scaling with habit count: *"quanto mais metas coloca mais lerdo ele fico"* / "the more goals you add the slower it gets" (`10777125663`, BR, 2★). **Interpretation: performance is not uniformly bad — it degrades with data volume and is aggravated by a blocking celebration modal. That makes it a problem that strikes engaged, long-tenured, paying users specifically.**

**7. The congratulations modal is a standalone UX defect with four independent complaints and a clear, cheap fix.**
**4 reviews (1.94%, meaningful, mean 3.00)** attack it directly: `9211593813` (UA, 4★, *"Please let the user manage that Congrats popup in settings… there is some annoying popup where I have to press Continue just to close that stupid popup"*), `11476120833` (RU, 3★, frequency + blocking), `11164675946` (BR, 2★, performance), `8890054521` (BR, 3★, *"The congratulations messages are not coherent!!!"*). **Interpretation: a settings toggle would neutralise all four. This is the lowest-cost improvement in the report.**

**8. When the product is used, what it is loved for is simplicity — and that praise is stable across seven years and every language in the corpus.**
**22 reviews (10.68%, high-priority, mean 4.68)** praise minimalism, "nothing extra", clean interface. It appears in Russian (6), US English (5), Brazilian Portuguese (3), Canadian English (3), Ukrainian (2), Belarusian, Latvian and Vietnamese. *"Очень простой и понятный интерфейс в этом трекере привычек — ничего лишнего"* / "Very simple and clear interface in this habit tracker — nothing extra" (`5995999324`, RU, 5★); *"I am fully satisfied… because there is no any unnecessary tools for me"* (`10387592776`, LV, 5★); *"This is the most comprehensive application without fluff that I have found"* (`7318341303`, CA, 5★); *"Love the simplicity of this app! It's not over-engineered"* (`7700270494`, US, 5★). Alongside it, **14 reviews (6.80%, high-priority, mean 4.93)** describe an actual behaviour change — losing weight, quitting alcohol, daily meditation, tracking expenses, brushing teeth. **Interpretation: the core loop works. This is not a product with a weak value proposition; it is a product whose value proposition is locked behind a wall that prevents most people from ever seeing it.**

**9. Six US reviewers are asking for a capability the product does not have at all: counting multiple completions per day.**
**6 reviews (2.91%, meaningful, mean 1.50) — all US, 13.0% of the US subset** — describe trying to log water, meals or repetitions more than once a day and finding it impossible. *"Track your food? Great - you can track it ONCE/day. **10. The store listing claims Hebrew and Ukrainian support that the app does not deliver.**
**5 reviews (2.43%, meaningful, mean 2.60)** report a missing language; **the store listing (accessed 11 September 2026) explicitly lists all 36 languages including Hebrew and Ukrainian.** `7882790956` (IL, 1★): *"מציגים כאילו זה בעברית. בתכל׳ס אין אפשרות לממשק בעברית"* / "They present it as if it's in Hebrew. Three of Ukraine's eight reviews raise it, and a Ukrainian user defaulting to a Russian-language interface in 2024 is a market-specific risk beyond ordinary localization debt.**

**11. Everything got worse after 2022, and the deterioration is monotonic.**
Mean rating by year: 2020 **3.96** → 2021 **3.68** → 2022 **3.32** → 2023 **3.00** → 2024 **2.73** → 2025 **2.69** → 2026 **1.57** (n=7, treat as directional only). Across the same split, paywall complaints rise from **15.7% to 35.0%**, reliability complaints from **2.2% to 24.8%**, and praise of any kind falls from **38.2% to 13.7%**. **Interpretation: two independent forces are compounding. The paywall tightened (three reviewers explicitly describe features that used to be free becoming paid — `12021785730`, `13595332155`, `12472128598`) while engineering quality fell. Neither trend has reversed.**

**13. The cheapest high-value moves, in evidence order:** ship a real time-boxed trial with full features (14 reviews name its absence; mean 1.29) → raise the free cap from 3 to a number where a routine is demonstrable (55 reviews) → add multi-count-per-day for water/meals/reps (6 reviews, 2 of them refund-seeking payers) → make the congratulations modal dismissible/optional in settings (4 reviews, cheapest fix in the report) → fix the launch-crash and data-loss path, with visible backup/restore (7 crash + 6 data-loss reviews) → fix or remove the Hebrew and Ukrainian claims on the store listing (5 reviews; currently a factual misstatement) → stop paywalling reminders specifically (5 reviews; it is the single feature most often named as the thing behind the wall) → give payers an in-app support path that does not require the Mail app (`11826835199`).

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Parse.** All 206 JSON records loaded programmatically; schema audited field-by-field; HTML entities unescaped.
2. **Reconcile.** Main file cross-checked against all 26 country files and against `manifest.json` on count, uniqueness, country assignment and rating distribution (Part 1.3). All checks passed exactly.
3. **Read.** All 206 records were read individually, in full, sorted by country then date. No sampling, no keyword pre-filter, no truncation. Non-English records (Portuguese 55, Russian ~36, Vietnamese 14, Turkish 11, Ukrainian 8, plus Spanish, Japanese, Hebrew, Korean, Thai, Czech, Polish, Norwegian, French) were read in the original language; translations in this report are the analyst's and are marked with `/`.
4. **Classify.** 78 themes were assigned by hand, review by review, to explicit ID lists. Theme membership is non-exclusive. Every ID list was validated programmatically against the corpus — **zero unknown IDs, zero intra-theme duplicates, zero unassigned records**.
5. **Aggregate.** Counts, percentages, rating distributions, means, country splits and year splits computed from the ID lists against a fixed denominator of 206.
6. **Audit.** Every theme with n ≥ 4 was re-read in full after aggregation to check for misassignment. Five rating/text contradictions and two review bursts were found during this pass and are disclosed rather than corrected.

**Known error risk.** With hand classification on 206 records the dominant risk is boundary judgement, not miscounting — specifically the line between "the free tier is too limited" (`paywall_cap`) and "the price is too high" (`price_objection`). The rule used is in Part 10.1. A reviewer who complains about both is counted in both; 13 reviews carry both. If the boundary were drawn differently the two themes would shift relative to each other, but their **union** (66 reviews, 32.04%) is stable, and every conclusion in this report that depends on the boundary is stated against the union as well as the parts.

## 1.6 Limitations and known biases

1. **Small corpus.** At n=206, one review is 0.49%. A theme with 2 records already clears "emerging". Every theme below n=5 is reported with that caveat and none carries a recommendation on its own.
2. **Written reviews ≠ ratings.** The store shows 4.6★ from 4,700 ratings; this corpus is 206 written reviews averaging 3.136. Written reviews over-represent the motivated and the aggrieved. **Every percentage here is a share of writers, not of users.**
3. **Two solicited-looking bursts (17 reviews, 8.25% of corpus)** inflate the positive side, one of them accounting for 11 of Canada's 12 reviews. Disclosed in Warning 3 and excluded from all market conclusions in Part 7.
4. **Only one country clears n=50.** Brazil. US and Russia are presented as limited evidence; everything else is excluded from standalone conclusions.
5. **Zero reviews from Germany, Australia, China** — three large markets are simply invisible here.
6. **Version data is absent from the schema.** There is no `version` field, so "after the update" complaints cannot be tied to a specific build. The store listing's latest version (4.7.1, 22 August 2025) predates the last seven reviews in the corpus. All regression claims in Part 8 rest on reviewer wording and date clustering only.
7. **Price is not constant.** See Warning 8. Seven concurrent IAP SKUs from $4.99 to $59.99 make cross-era and cross-market price comparison unreliable.
8. **Refund and billing reports are one-sided.** The corpus contains the complaint, never the resolution. Two reviewers say a refund was requested; one says it was refused (`13555244418`). No claim about actual refund rates is made.
9. **Translation risk.** Short reviews in Vietnamese, Thai and Turkish carry the most translation ambiguity. Where a short review was genuinely unreadable as signal it was classified `low_info` rather than forced into a theme.

## 2.2 Free / paid classification

# PART 7 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 7.1 Method

Two views are used. **(a) Annual series** (Part 1.4) — n per year ranges from 1 to 48, so 2019 and 2026 are directional only. **(b) Two-era split at 1 January 2023**, chosen because it divides the corpus nearly in half by record count (89 vs 117) and falls between the two visibly different behaviour regimes. Both views are reported; where they disagree, it is said so. No trend is claimed where n < 10 in either period.

| Metric | 2019–2022 (n=89) | 2023–2026 (n=117) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean rating | **3.67** | **2.73** | **−0.94** |
| `paywall_cap` | 14 (15.7%) | 41 (35.0%) | **+19.3pp** |
| `price_objection` | 6 (6.7%) | 12 (10.3%) | +3.6pp |
| Monetization friction (union) | 29 (32.6%) | 58 (49.6%) | **+17.0pp** |
| Reliability (union) | 2 (2.2%) | 29 (24.8%) | **+22.6pp** |
| Praise (union) | 34 (38.2%) | 16 (13.7%) | **−24.5pp** |
| `paid_direct` | 8 (9.0%) | 21 (17.9%) | +8.9pp |

## 7.2 Trend 1 — The rating decline is monotonic across six years · **worsening, high confidence**

2020 **3.96** → 2021 **3.68** → 2022 **3.32** → 2023 **3.00** → 2024 **2.73** → 2025 **2.69** → 2026 **1.57**. Every year is lower than the one before, with one flat year (2024→2025). Adjusting 2020 and 2021 for the suspected bursts (3.65 and 3.12) removes the early steepness but not the direction. n ≥ 25 for every year from 2020 to 2025.

**This is the most robust trend in the report.** It is not driven by a single theme — it is driven by two independent ones moving together (Trends 2 and 3).

## 7.3 Trend 2 — The paywall complaint more than doubled · **worsening, high confidence**

By year, `paywall_cap` as a share of that year's reviews: 2020 **7.7%** (2/26) → 2021 **13.5%** (5/37) → 2022 **28.0%** (7/25) → 2023 **33.3%** (11/33) → 2024 **35.4%** (17/48) → 2025 **31.0%** (9/29) → 2026 **57.1%** (4/7, directional).

It has been above 28% for five consecutive years and shows no sign of reverting. **Three reviewers name the mechanism directly** — features moving from free to paid (`12021785730` TR Dec 2024, `12472128598` PL Mar 2025, `13595332155` TR Jan 2026, the last of whom is a returning lapsed user). **Interpretation: this is consistent with a progressive tightening of the free tier over time rather than with a constant free tier that users grew less tolerant of — but the corpus contains no version history, so the alternative reading cannot be excluded. It is a hypothesis with three supporting eyewitnesses.**

## 7.4 Trend 3 — Reliability collapsed after 2023 · **worsening then partially recovering, high confidence**

Reliability-theme share by year: 2020 **3.8%** (1/26) → 2021 **0.0%** (0/37) → 2022 **4.0%** (1/25) → 2023 **9.1%** (3/33) → **2024 39.6% (19/48)** → 2025 **20.7%** (6/29) → 2026 **14.3%** (1/7).

**2024 is the break.** Nineteen of that year's forty-eight reviews report a defect. The year contains both major clusters: the Brazilian performance cluster (Jan–Sep 2024) and the start of the Russian launch-crash cluster (Oct 2024 – Jan 2025). 2025's 20.7% is lower but still an order of magnitude above 2021.

Against this, **two payers document fixes landing** — `10474969732` (Oct 2023, lag removed, subscription restored) and `12890217155` (Jul 2025, *"как же улучшилось приложение за это время"* / "how much the app has improved in that time"). **Interpretation: the engineering team demonstrably ships fixes and reviewers demonstrably notice. The 2024 event looks like a specific bad period rather than terminal decay — but 2026's newest review is still a data-loss report (`14165208412`, Jun 2026), so it is not closed.**

## 7.5 Trend 4 — Praise collapsed · **worsening, high confidence**

Praise-of-any-kind share: 2020 **50.0%** → 2021 **40.5%** → 2022 **20.0%** → 2023 **12.1%** → 2024 **14.6%** → 2025 **13.8%** → 2026 **14.3%**. Removing the two bursts, 2020 falls to 35.0% and 2021 to 34.6% — the collapse is real but the cliff is between 2021 and 2022, not inside 2020.

Praise has been flat at 12–15% for four straight years. **Interpretation: the positive population did not disappear — it stabilised at about one review in seven. What changed is that the negative population tripled around it.**

## 7.6 Trend 5 — Direct payers are a growing share of reviewers · **emerging, medium confidence**

`paid_direct` share: 2020 11.5% → 2021 10.8% → 2022 4.0% → 2023 15.2% → 2024 16.7% → **2025 24.1%** → 2026 14.3%. Era split: 9.0% → 17.9%.

**Interpretation (labelled, and the caveat is load-bearing): this does not mean more people are buying. It means a larger share of the people who write reviews are buyers — which is equally consistent with more aggressive monetization pulling more people through the purchase, and with the free tier becoming so thin that only buyers stay long enough to have an opinion. The corpus cannot distinguish these. What it does establish is that payer complaints are current and rising, not a legacy problem: 16 of the 29 payer reviews (55%) are from 2024 or later.**

## 7.7 Trend 6 — Refund-seeking is concentrated in the recent period · **worsening, medium confidence**

Of the 11 refund requests: 4 in 2021 (`7113224515`, `7295269143`, `7348111127`, `7669054186`), 1 in 2023, 3 in 2024–25 (`11164675946`, `11826835199`, `12160321648`), 3 in 2025 (`12317660239`, `12413759685`, `13555244418`). But the *causes* changed: the 2021 cluster is entirely **"the product doesn't do what I bought it for"** (multi-count, recurring reminders, unexpected charge), while the 2024–25 cluster is entirely **"the product broke after I paid"** or **"the trial trapped me"**. **Interpretation: the same outcome, arrived at by two different routes five years apart. The first route was a capability gap; the second is a reliability and billing-mechanics gap.**

## 7.8 Trend 7 — Geographic centre of gravity moved to Brazil and Russia · **persistent, high confidence**

US reviews by year: 12, 14, 8, 6, 4, 2, 0. Brazilian: 1, 3, 10, 9, 18, 12, 2. Russian: 8 (2020), 1, 1, 5, 13, 6, 1.

**Through 2021 the US was the largest market in this corpus. From 2022 onward it is Brazil, with Russia second in 2024.** By 2025 the US contributed 2 of 29 reviews (6.9%).

**Interpretation (labelled): review volume is not install volume and the shift could be an artefact of where review prompts fire. But it aligns with the finding in Part 6.6 that this product is nearly absent from high-spend markets, and it means the recent corpus — which is where all the reliability and paywall evidence lives — describes a predominantly Brazilian and Russian experience.**

## 7.9 What did not change

- **Simplicity praise is stable across the whole span** — 2020 (`5995999324`, `6026946756`), 2021 (`7318341303`, `7192572245`), 2022 (`8315962338`), 2024 (`10832301711`), 2025 (`13138307431`). Seven years, six languages, same words: "nothing extra", "no fluff", "not over-engineered". **This is the product's durable asset.**
- **The 3-habit number itself never changes.** Reviewers cite "3" in 2022 (`8757084304`), 2023 (`10194166127`), 2024 (`10918059435`, `11120549627`), 2025 (`12133174175`, `12388272937`), 2026 (`13852099012`). Four years of the same complaint about the same number.
- **Behaviour-change stories continue to appear** in every era, including 2025 (`12611253383`, BR: *"A gente vive num mundo de scrolls, vídeos de 15s e conteúdos sem fim. Baixar esse app foi um achado na minha rotina"* / "We live in a world of scrolls, 15-second videos and endless content. Downloading this app was a find for my routine"). **The core product still works for the people who get past the wall.**

| **F7** | **Give paying users an in-app support path that does not depend on the Mail app.** | `support_channel_broken` n=1 but severe: `11826835199` (RU, 1★, payer) lost data, *"пишет не подключена почта, но в приложении нет раздела, где ее можно подключить"* / "it says mail isn't connected, but there's no section in the app to connect it", then *"Заплатил и не знаю как вернуть деньги"* / "I paid and don't know how to get my money back". | One review, but it is a paying customer with data loss and no route to a human. ## 8.2 Structural changes — high confidence in direction, uncertain in magnitude

**S1 — Raise the free-habit cap, and move the wall off the thing the product is named after.**
*Evidence:* `paywall_cap` n=55 (26.70%, high-priority, mean 1.96) — the largest theme in the corpus (§3.3.1); 37 of the 83 one-/two-star reviews touch it (44.6%); four reviewers independently argue the free tier proves there is no reason to subscribe (`10991854727`, `11319751646`, `8568438754`, `10804399038`).
*The design question is not "3 or unlimited."* Three habits cannot represent a routine, which is what this app sells. Candidate re-shapings the evidence supports: cap on **history depth** rather than habit count (`11186327600`, `11447879577`, `6695360237` show history is already a de facto paid axis); cap on **statistics depth** (already the top named purchase trigger, §5.2); cap on **reminder count** rather than reminder existence.
*Uncertain:* magnitude. The corpus has exactly one clean example of the cap converting someone (`10832301711`, who paid in 15 minutes — and note she paid on *design*, not on hitting a wall).

**S2 — Stop paywalling reminders. This is the most defensible single paywall change in the report.**
*Evidence:* `reminders_paywalled` n=5. Set against it: `praise_reminders` n=6, **mean 5.00** — reminders are among the most-loved free-tier-adjacent features when people can use them (`5990760352`, `7588653687`, `7762886727`, `6026946756`, `9671666294`, `7968248707`). And critically, `competitor_native` n=3: when reminders are the paid feature, **three reviewers independently concluded the iPhone's own clock/calendar/reminders app is the substitute** (`10305077033`, `6313215651`, `11540202999`).
*Interpretation:* paywalling reminders reframes the purchase decision from "which habit tracker" to "why pay for an alarm." That is the one comparison this product cannot win.

**S3 — Build multi-completion-per-day counting (water, meals, reps).**
*Evidence:* `multi_count_missing` n=6 (2.91%, meaningful, mean 1.50) — **all six are US, 13.0% of the US subset, and two are payers who requested refunds** (`7113224515`, `7669054186`). §3.4, §6.3.
*Why it is structural, not a feature request:* the app's positioning (health habits, water, meals) promises a counter and delivers a daily checkbox. `9838731003`: *"Track your food? Great - you can track it ONCE/day."* This is the clearest promise/product mismatch in the corpus, and it sits in the highest-spend market.

**S4 — Ship home-screen and lock-screen widgets.**
*Evidence:* `widget_missing` n=3 (1.46%, meaningful), but **all three frame it as a competitive deficit, not a wish** (§3.4): `10155196420` (ES) — *"pierde frente al resto de apps similares"* / "it loses against the rest of similar apps"; `9615589336` (VN, a payer); `10774694629` (UA). Dated 2023–2024, so the gap is current.

**S5 — Fix the performance degradation that scales with habit count, starting with Brazil.**
*Evidence:* `perf_lag` n=12 (5.83%, mean 2.58); **9 of 12 Brazilian (16.4% of BR), 4 of 12 payers** (§6.2b). `10777125663`: *"quanto mais metas coloca mais lerdo ele fico"* / "the more goals you add the slower it gets"; `10474969732` hit it at 15 habits. `11164675946`: five minutes to mark two tasks.
*Note the cruel irony:* the users most affected are the ones who bought the app to have more than three habits.

## 8.3 Experiments worth running

**E1 — Free-cap ladder.** Test 3 (control) vs 6 vs 10 free habits against trial-start rate, D7 retention, paid conversion **and written-review rating**. The corpus cannot predict the outcome; it establishes that the current setting produces 55 complaints and that two reviewers argue explicitly that a lower barrier would raise revenue rather than lower it (`10991854727`, `5629089340`).

**E2 — Trial length.** Reviewers asked for 1 day, 3 days, 7 days and 30 days. Test 7-day full-feature against paywall-first. Primary metric: paid conversion. **Secondary metric that this corpus says matters more: 1★ rate.**

**E3 — Price restructuring in high-spend markets.** §6.6 finds the high-spend group is the worst-rated segment once bursts are removed (2.587) while `paywall_cap` there is only 11.1% — meaning the objection is capability, not price. Test whether a **lower-priced tier that unlocks only habits + reminders** performs better in US/JP/GB than the current $39.99–$59.99 all-or-nothing SKUs.

**E4 — Statistics as the premium anchor.** Three of six named purchase triggers are statistics-related (§5.2). Test a paywall positioned on analytics depth rather than habit count, and fix `9673984947`'s non-daily-frequency percentage bug before doing so.

## 8.4 Research questions this corpus raises but cannot answer

1. **Why is performance so much worse in Brazil?** No device, OS or network field exists in this schema. Needs telemetry, not reviews.
2. **Did the free tier actually tighten over time, or did tolerance fall?** Three eyewitnesses say it tightened (`12021785730`, `12472128598`, `13595332155`). No version field exists to confirm.
3. **What is the real refund rate?** Eleven of twenty-nine writing payers asked for one. That number cannot be extrapolated; App Store Connect can answer it directly and should be checked.
4. **Why has US written-review volume gone to near zero?** Twelve in 2020, zero in 2026. Acquisition decline, prompt-configuration change, or both.
5. **What are the seven concurrent IAP SKUs actually doing?** Five near-identical names at $39.99/$59.99 suggests live price testing; the corpus shows the *symptoms* (wildly inconsistent quoted prices) but not the design.
6. **Is the store aggregate (4.6★, 4.7K ratings) diverging from written reviews over time?** If the written corpus fell from 3.96 to 2.69 while the aggregate held at 4.6, the prompted-rating flow and the written-review flow are sampling very different populations, and only one of them is visible here.

## 8.5 What not to change

- **Don't add features to the core loop.** `praise_simplicity` n=22 (mean 4.68) is stable across seven years and six languages, and *"nothing extra"* / *"no fluff"* / *"not over-engineered"* is the single most repeated phrase in the positive corpus (`5995999324`, `10387592776`, `7318341303`, `7700270494`, `13138307431`, `6026946756`). The complaint list in this report is about access, reliability and two missing primitives — **not** about the app being too simple.
- **Don't treat the 4.6★ store aggregate as evidence that this is fine.** See Warning 2.
- **Don't read Canada's 5.00 as a market win.** See §6.5.
- **Don't fix bugs first and call it a turnaround.** Reliability is 20.5% of bad ratings; monetization friction is 73.5% (§3.2).

**Key boundary rules, stated so the classification is reproducible:**

| Boundary | Rule applied |
|---|---|
| `paywall_cap` vs `price_objection` | `paywall_cap` = the free tier is too limited to use, or a specific capability is behind the wall. `price_objection` = the amount charged is too high for the value. A reviewer who says both is counted in both (**9 reviews carry both**). Their union is 64 (31.07%), of which 43 are one- or two-star. |
| `paywall_cap` vs `no_trial` | `no_trial` requires the reviewer to state they could not *evaluate* before paying, or to ask for a trial by name/duration. Complaining only that habits 4+ are paid is `paywall_cap` alone. |
| `paid_direct` | Requires first-person evidence: "I paid", "comprei", "купил подписку", "I have the premium version", or a refund request on a purchase they made. **Inferred interest, intent to buy, or approval of the paid tier without a purchase claim is excluded.** |
| `value_insufficient` vs `price_objection` | `value_insufficient` requires the reviewer to have paid and judged it not worth it. `price_objection` can come from a non-buyer. |
| `data_loss` vs `history_paywalled` | True loss of previously-recorded data is `data_loss`. Inability to *view* past data due to the paywall is `history_paywalled`. `11447879577` (UA) is genuinely ambiguous and is counted in **both**, which is disclosed at §3.3.4. |
| `outage_crash` vs `perf_lag` | `outage_crash` = app will not open, or exits. `perf_lag` = app works but is slow/freezes. `11719440762` (BR) describes both and is counted in both. |

| `low_info` | Assigned when no product signal is extractable — typically ≤5 words with no specific referent. `low_info` records are **not** excluded from any denominator. A review can carry `low_info` alongside a weak theme where a rating/text contradiction is the only signal (e.g. `7023381511`). |
| `us_2020_burst` / `ca_2021_burst` | Meta-labels, not product themes. Assigned on the combination of date clustering, uniform 5★, generic content, and (for US) anomalous helpful-vote counts. **Not** treated as proof of fraud — treated as grounds to exclude from market conclusions. |
| Translation | All non-English classification was done from the original text. Translations shown with `/` are the analyst's. Where a short review was genuinely ambiguous it was classified `low_info` rather than forced into a theme. |
