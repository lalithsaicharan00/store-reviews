**Corpus:** all **86** text reviews in `app store review/84. Habit Tracker - HabitGrid - Daily Habits & Goal Tracker/reviews.jsonl`
**Window:** 2025-06-08 → 2026-09-02 (451 days) · **Storefronts:** 28 · **Corpus mean rating: 4.930★**
**Developer:** Edvard Adam Eros (listing copyright "THE GREAT DIGITAL ADVENTURE SRL") · **Released:** 2025-06-04 · **Current version:** 1.0.19 (2026-09-02)
**Method:** every review read individually in its original language (9 languages); 55 theme codes across 9 families (52 hand-assigned, 3 mechanical); 405 code assignments; 35 integrity checks; 18 absence tests; all external evidence captured 13 September 2026.

## How to read this

Every quantified claim carries a **count, a percentage, the denominator, the market scope, the time window, a signal label and exact review IDs**. Review IDs are `review_id` values from `reviews.jsonl`; the bracketed `#n` is the working index (date order) used by Part 10, so any number here resolves to specific reviews and any review resolves to the findings it supports. §9.J lists every review ID behind every code.

Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — a review that praises the widgets and also reports a widget bug is counted in both. Composite unions (`U:` prefix) count each review **once**. Unless a sentence names a narrower scope, the denominator is **86 reviews, all storefronts, 2025-06-08 → 2026-09-02**.

### ⚠️ Nine warnings before any number here is used

1. **This corpus is small and almost uniformly positive.** 81 of 86 reviews are five-star (94.19%); there are **no one- or two-star reviews at all**. At n = 86, **one review is 1.16%, which the required scale already labels a "Meaningful signal"**. Every share therefore also carries a Wilson 95% interval, and any finding resting on fewer than five reviews says so in the sentence.
2. **No storefront reaches the 50-review threshold.** Russia is the largest at 37. **No standalone country conclusion is made anywhere in this report**; Russia is described as limited evidence in §6.3.
3. **The app sells nothing.** The store listing's offer metadata is `hasInAppPurchases: false`, the price is Free, and the developer's terms call it "a Free service". **Zero reviews carry purchase evidence.** Part 5 therefore analyses the free model as the adoption driver and the risk of changing it — there is no buyer segment to analyse.
4. **Reviews were solicited in-app.** Release 1.0.1 (2025-07-03) added "in-app review" and 1.0.6 (2025-08-21) says "Love the app? Now it's easier to leave a review and let us know!". **83 of 86 reviews postdate the first prompt.** Prompted and unprompted reviews cannot be separated. §7.3 shows the review rate did not rise after either prompt release; it rose after localisation.
5. **Early reviews cluster on the developer's home region.** Six of the 13 reviews in the first five months come from the Romanian and Hungarian storefronts; the listed copyright holder is a Romanian SRL; and two Romanian-storefront authors carry the developer's surname (`12886063451` "Öcsi Erős", `13282170850` "Mihaela Eros"). This is disclosed, not adjudicated. All six are retained; removing them moves the mean from 4.930★ to 4.925★ (§9.D).
6. **Most reviews are not in English.** 54 of 86 (62.79%) are in eight other languages, 39 of them Russian. All were read in the original; translations here are labelled.
7. **Many reviews are very short.** 28 (32.56%) have under 60 characters of body text, and all 28 are five-star. §9.D re-runs the headline shares without them: every specific theme share rises and only generic praise falls.
8. **Reviews carry no version field.** The version history *is* available (18 releases), so Part 7 uses release dates as era boundaries. Any link between a release and a change in what reviewers say is **timing, not proven cause**.
9. **The text corpus matches the public rating.** The ratings-weighted public mean across the same 28 storefronts is **4.939★ over 311 ratings**, against a corpus mean of 4.930★ — a gap of **+0.009★**. Unlike a corpus with a large gap, there is no sign here that text reviewers are a negatively selected minority.

## 0.3 The main risk in this corpus is a change to the free model

Nobody complains about price, because there is none to complain about (§9.F). But the corpus shows what would happen if that changed:

- **The developer's own terms reserve the right to charge.** habitgrid.net/terms: the provider may "modify the Application or **charge for their services at any time** and for any reason".
- **Two reviewers attach their loyalty to the price.** `U:future_pricing` = 2 (2.33%, Meaningful signal — **n=2, stated as such**):
  - `13682234945` (#29, us) — "**I really hope it stays free, otherwise I will have to find a different habit tracker.**"

Two reviews are a small base. They are reported here because they are the only statements in the corpus that make a rating or continued use **conditional** on anything, and the condition is price. §5.4 sets out what the corpus implies for any monetisation decision.

## 1.4 Processing method

1. `84-dump.py` — parse all 86 records, sort by `(date, review_id)`, emit `84-all-reviews.txt` (every review in full) and `84-index.tsv`.
2. **Every review read individually, in full, in its original language, in date order.** No sampling, no keyword filtering, no clustering. 54 non-English reviews (Russian 39, Spanish 6, French 4, Portuguese, German, Chinese, Arabic, Vietnamese 1 each) were read in the original; translations here are labelled.
3. `84-themes.py` — a 55-code, 9-family taxonomy built **from** the corpus after reading it, with definitions and directions, plus 17 composite unions. **Every code is used.**
4. `84-cls/001-086.txt` — the hand classification: one line per review with the working index, its codes, the language, and a note quoting the exact words that drove each code (with translations). This is the expensive artefact.
5. `84-build-classification.py` — rebuilds the index → `review_id` map from `reviews.jsonl`, **asserts it against `84-index.tsv`** (86 rows × 5 fields), refuses unknown codes, duplicate lines or unclassified reviews, then emits `84-review-classification.py`. Three codes (`META_LOW_INFO`, `META_EDITED`, `META_SURNAME_MATCHES_DEV`) are assigned **mechanically** from the source record; the build refuses to run if any appears in the hand file.
6. `84-validate.py` — **35 integrity checks, all passing**: 10 structural, 3 mechanical, 2 language, 13 that re-derive a code from literal review text, 7 consistency rules. **Three real classification errors were caught this way and fixed** (§9.C).
7. `84-aggregate.py`, `84-extra.py` — counts, unions, star bands, storefronts, languages, release eras, monthly volume, review-prompt windows, market groups, public ratings, burst checks, sensitivity and absence tests.
8. `84-tables.py` → `84-gen/` — every generated table; `84-build-report.py` assembles this report; `84-verify-report.py` re-derives its claims afterwards (§9.M).

## 1.5 Signal thresholds

Applied to the global corpus (denominator 86). **No storefront is eligible for its own thresholds** (none has 50 reviews); the Russia rates in §6.3 are shown with the band for reference only and are labelled limited evidence.

| Share | Label |
|---|---|
| <0.1% | Ignore by default |
| 0.1–<0.5% | Weak signal |
| 0.5–<1% | Emerging signal |
| 1–<3% | Meaningful signal |
| 3–5% | Very strong signal |
| >5% | High-priority signal |

**A caution on this scale at n = 86.** The smallest possible count, one review, is 1.16% — already a "Meaningful signal". Five reviews (5.81%) reach "High-priority". The labels are applied as the spec requires, but they overstate what one to five reviews can show. Read the count and the confidence interval, not the label, for anything under five.

## 1.6 Limitations, stated plainly

1. **Small corpus; no eligible country.** Nothing below the global level is more than illustrative.
2. **Almost no negative evidence.** Seven reviews report a problem. Dissatisfied users who left without writing are invisible; the one public 1★ rating with no text is the only trace of them.
3. **Solicited reviews.** An in-app review prompt has existed since 2025-07-03 (§7.3). Prompted positivity cannot be separated from organic positivity.
4. **No paid segment.** Monetisation findings are about the *free* model and stated intentions, not observed purchases.
5. **No version field.** Release links are timing only.
6. **Machine-free translation.** Translations are the analyst's own reading; short idioms ("молодцы", "10 баллов из 5") are rendered by meaning.
7. **Non-exclusive counts.** Codes overlap by design; unions give once-per-review counts.

## 2.5 Data and privacy statements

§0.4 sets the four sources side by side. The practical reading:

- The **privacy label** (analytics identifiers, usage data and diagnostics, not linked to identity) is consistent with the terms naming **Amplitude** as a third-party service.

## 5.5 What cannot be claimed here

- **No conversion, retention or churn rate** — no purchases exist, and reviews cannot measure use.
- **No price point** — no reviewer names an amount.
- **No estimate of how many users would leave if charged** — two statements are not a rate, and reviewers who care about price are overrepresented among those who mention it.

## 8.4 Experiment — if monetisation is introduced, charge for new capability, never for the free core (§0.1, §0.3, §5.3, §5.4, §7.4)

**Evidence:** `U:free_model_any` = 39 (45.35%), **stable at 44–47% in every era**; `U:paywall_contrast` = 16 (18.60%); a habit cap and paid subscriptions are what reviewers name as avoiding in competitors (`13903380759`, `13546859530`, `14227392787`); one reviewer will leave if charged (`13682234945`); the only proposed model is a one-time purchase (`13936987753`); the terms already reserve the right to charge.

**Recommendation, framed as an experiment because no purchase data exists:**

- If revenue is needed, test an **optional one-time purchase or tip** for **capabilities not yet shipped** that reviewers have asked for (watch app, cloud backup, advanced frequency rules), and announce it in advance.

**Research question:** check each of the five variants against the shipped goal settings. Where one is missing, it is the best-evidenced feature gap in the corpus. Where it exists, the fix is discoverability.

## 8.6 Research question — Apple Watch (§0.6, §3.10)

**Evidence:** `REQ_WATCH` = 2 (2.33%, n=2), both in E3 (`14005146777`, `14196935443`); the developer told one reviewer he would try. The listing shows no watch app. Two requests do not justify building one. **Research question:** is watch demand visible in the developer's own channels (Discord, Reddit), where requests are cheaper to make than reviews?

## 8.7 Research question — how independent are the ratings? (§0.7, §7.3, §7.6)

**Evidence:** an in-app review prompt in every version since 2025-07-03 (83 of 86 reviews postdate it); a launch-period cluster of 6 Romanian/Hungarian-storefront reviews including two carrying the developer's surname; 28 five-star reviews under 60 characters; website testimonials with generic names ("John Harris", "Maria Smith") that match no App Store reviewer.

**Research question:** none of this indicates a problem with the product. But the rating is the app's main acquisition asset, and the corpus cannot show how much of it is organic. Where the prompt fires, and whether early reviews came from the developer's network, is known to the developer and not to this analysis. Store rules on reviews from the developer's own circle should be checked, and the website testimonials should be real or removed.

## 9.A Counting rules

1. **Denominator** is 86 (every review in `reviews.jsonl`) unless the sentence names a narrower one: a star band, an era, Russia (37), the rest (49), or a named market group. Segment rates are always shown with their denominator.
2. **Codes are non-exclusive.** One review may carry several codes (mean 4.71). **Unions count each review once.**
3. **Mean ★** for a code is the mean rating of the reviews carrying it.
4. **Signal band** is applied to the share of the stated denominator (§1.5); Wilson 95% intervals are given throughout. Russia's band is shown for reference only.
5. **Mechanical codes** — `META_LOW_INFO` (HTML-unescaped, stripped body under 60 characters), `META_EDITED` (`is_edited` true), `META_SURNAME_MATCHES_DEV` (author contains "Eros"/"Erős" as a word) — are assigned by the build from the source record, never by hand.
6. **`PRAISE_GENERIC`** is assigned when a review approves but names no product quality; mentions of price, ads or the developer do not count as product qualities. Check C1/C2 enforce this mechanically against the hand codes.
7. **`PRAISE_BEST`** is a superlative about the app ("the best", "one of the best"). "The most convenient" is `PRAISE_EASE`, not `PRAISE_BEST` (§9.C).
8. **Eras** are release-based: E1 2025-06-04 → 2025-10-28, E2 2025-10-29 → 2026-04-18, E3 2026-04-19 → 2026-09-02.
9. **Language** is hand-assigned and checked against script detection for Cyrillic, Arabic, Chinese and Vietnamese (check L1).
10. **No deduplication** was performed; none was needed (§1.3).
