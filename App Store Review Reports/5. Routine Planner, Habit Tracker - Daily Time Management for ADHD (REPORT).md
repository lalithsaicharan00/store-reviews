# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST: THE PRODUCT IS LOVED; THE BUILD IS WHAT COSTS IT STARS

**Executive summary in one line:** Routinery has the strongest, most defensible product thesis in this category — **a sequential timer that speaks the next task aloud and continuously recalculates your finish time** — and 12.90% of all reviewers explicitly identify as ADHD/neurodivergent, rating it **4.47**. The thing that drags the rating is not price and not the paywall: it is **engineering reliability**, which appears in **50.3% of every 2★ review** and dominates the 3–4★ "almost" band.

### 1. Unlike most apps in this set, the written corpus is *not* much angrier than the tap-only rating

| | Value |
|---|---|
| App Store rating (US, all ratings incl. tap-only) | **4.72** (17,825 ratings) |
| Mean of all 3,342 **written** reviews | **4.16** |
| 5★ share of written reviews | 62.06% (2,074) |
| 4★ share | 15.29% (511) |
| 3★ share | 8.29% (277) |
| 2★ share | 4.88% (163) |
| 1★ share | **9.49% (317)** |

A 0.56-point gap between taps and prose is small for this category. It means the negative reviews here are **specific defect reports from people who still want the app to work**, not refugees from a predatory funnel. That is a materially better starting position than a scam-shaped app — and it means fixing the defects converts directly into rating.

### 2. Reliability, not money, is what produces 2★ and 3★

Globally, **609 reviews (18.22%) report a reliability defect, at a mean rating of 3.36 and a 30.9% one-and-two-star rate**. Note the shape: reliability peaks at **2★, not 1★**. That is the signature of *users who like the product and are frustrated*, which is the most recoverable kind of unhappiness in the corpus.

Compare the monetization umbrella (paywall, subscription, price, trial/billing, refunds, ads, upsell): **585 reviews (17.50%), mean 3.50**, peaking at 1★ (37.9%). Money produces the loudest 1★s; **engineering produces the volume of 2–3★s.**

**277 reviews (8.29%, high-priority band, mean 4.16)** praise the timer, countdown, or the running "all ends by" estimate. It is described in the same way over and over, across seven years and every language in the corpus: the app removes the need to hold a schedule in working memory *and* tells you, continuously, whether you are still going to be on time.

- `8204342733` (US, 5★): *"Excellent for ADHD, time blindness and procrastination… gives you a full customized visual schedule, audio reminders and a visual timer so that you are 100% on top of your day. The timer is really the life changing aspect."*

- `14115048883` (US, 5★, "Cure for Time Blindness"), `11130087506` (US, 5★), `8382971100` (US, 3★), `6883027790` (KR, 5★), `7510768204` (AU, 5★), `8016986126` (GB, 5★), `9228861293` (KR, 5★), `9499393403` (JP, 4★), `10746011966` (US, 3★).

### 4. ADHD is not a marketing angle here — it is the actual user base, and it rates a full third of a star higher

**431 reviews (12.90%, high-priority) explicitly mention ADHD, ADD, AuDHD, autism-plus-ADHD, executive dysfunction, or time blindness.** That cohort's mean is **4.47** vs **4.11** for everyone else, with only **6.0% one-and-two-star** and **70.5% five-star**. A further **14 reviews (0.42%)** name autism specifically — mean **4.64, zero 1–2★ reviews** — and **95 (2.84%)** describe depression, anxiety, bipolar disorder, OCD, PTSD or burnout, at mean **4.52**.

The ADHD share has grown: 6.8% of 2020 reviews → 18.0% of 2022 → **15.4% of 2026**. The 17+ age rating on a product whose reviews are full of 10–15-year-olds and parents is worth a separate look (see §9).

## 1.2 What actually triggers a purchase

**120 reviews (3.59%) contain direct, self-reported purchase evidence** (paid / subscribed / renewed / bought lifetime). Named triggers, in order of frequency within that segment:

1. **The free tier worked first, and they upgraded to scale it.** This is by far the dominant path. `13093885049` (US, 5★): *"You can get two routines for free which I think is enough to be worthwhile but I paid for the subscription right away."* Also `8448464887` (IN, 5★, *"Bought it finally after a year of use"*), `12444613825` (KR, 5★, *"몇 년째 너무 잘 써서, 이번에 1년권 결제"* — *[used it well for years, so I bought the annual]*), `13891754512` (GB, 5★, three years free then premium), `13595999311` (KR, 5★, four years then annual), `12900411902` (JP, 5★, subscribed explicitly "to support").
2. **Needing more than two routines** — the cap converting when the user has genuinely outgrown morning+night: `11776189779` (KR, 5★), `12899184203` (MX, 5★), `14497138417` (IT, 5★): *"Suggerisco l'abbonamento perché… l'abbonamento non ha restrizioni sulla quantità di routine (ne ho tantissime adesso)"*.
3. **Apple Watch capability** — several Korean reviewers state they bought a *Watch* because of this app, then subscribed: `6633721289` (KR, 5★, *"애플워치 나온다길래 애플워치를 샀습니다"*), `7046987901`, `9607295246` (KR, 5★, *"루티너리 쓰려고 워치 샀어요"*), `7621728149`.
4. **Price framed as trivially small.** `6669295549` (KR, 5★): *"하루 백원으로 습관을 만들수 있다면"* — *[if I can build a habit for 100 won a day, I'll pay it]*. `11392402930` (US, 5★): *"it's only $2/month to feel like a normal human"*.
5. **Demonstrated outcome after a streak.** `12625991801` (US, 5★, ~300-day streak): *"I'm paying real life human dollars for this app and it is worth every penny."*

**Do not read a conversion rate from any of this.** 3.59% is the share of *reviewers who mention paying*, not the share of users who pay.

## 4.2 Notifications: two opposite failures, both fatal for this product
- **Not firing at all — 99 reviews (2.96%), mean 3.53.** For an app whose core loop is "the app tells you to start", this is a total-loss failure. `13318004023` (CA, 1★): *"notifications no longer work and as someone with adhd the notification are the main purpose of it."* `10337926884` (GB, 2★, long, considered): *"for 3 glorious days I actually felt a little like I had my life together, but this app won't work for me if the onus is on me to remember to launch it."* Also `12327812271`, `13446802332` (CA, 5★, extremely detailed), `13830916795` (IN, 1★), `13982523131` (NZ, 4★), `9219301950` (SE, 4★), `11903518291` (DK, 1★).
- **Firing endlessly — 33 reviews (0.99%), mean 3.00**, concentrated in May 2026 (§0.6).

**A design note the corpus makes unambiguous:** the *persistent* notification is a deliberate feature that several users love (`13667837214` US 5★: *"it doesn't let me ignore it! …Hella annoying - but effective"*; `13166666978` AU 5★). Two 2026 reviews complain the app **removed** the un-dismissable buzzing alarm and thereby broke their wake-up (`14371016110` US 1★: *"They ruined it… I'm canceling my subscription after being a loyal customer for years"*; `14447685280` JP 1★; `14499455292` JP 2★). **The escalation ladder is the product. It needs to be user-configurable, not silently retuned.**

## 7.2 Korea is not a review market — it is an unstaffed support desk

**This is the most actionable geographic finding in the report.** Korea is 19.06% of the corpus but **62.6% of all billing-integrity complaints (57 of 91)**. US, at 40.5% of the corpus, contributes 22.

The reason is visible in the text: **Korean users post refund and cancellation requests directly into the App Store review as a support ticket**, often *while rating the app 5★*. Examples where the entire review body is a refund request at 5 stars: `11462945038`, `11729876017`, `11850519257`, `11940731530`, `13449732100`, `13548970666`, `11360391292`, `10174413491`, `8102214925`. Others: `10940540257` (KR, 1★): *"실수로 결제가 되어서 취소 하려고 여기저기 봤는데 어디에 문의하는건지 알수가없네요"* — *[I was charged by mistake and looked everywhere for where to ask, but there's no way to find it]*.

**Two consequences.** First, KR's billing numbers materially overstate KR-specific billing *defects* relative to other markets and understate them everywhere else — this is a channel artifact, disclosed. Second, and more importantly: **an in-app, Korean-language, self-service cancel/refund path would remove the majority of Korea's negative reviews at a stroke**, because a large share of them are not product complaints at all.

Korea also carries the app's deepest engineering feedback — Apple Watch (7.4%, the highest of any market), sync (6.1%), crashes (6.9%) — and its most detailed feature specifications (`13449391599`, a 4-point subscriber essay on widgets, fonts, repeat settings and tag filters; `9712944707`, a multi-year subscriber asking for icon search, social search and Shortcuts). **Korea is the lead-user market and should be the beta cohort.**

## 7.3 Japan and Germany are the two problem markets

**JP: mean 3.77, 21.3% one-and-two-star — the worst of any eligible storefront**, despite the highest ADHD-identification rate outside North America (19.0%) and strong timer praise (12.7%). The gap is entirely engineering and language: **data loss 9.0%** (3.3× global), battery 4.1% (2.2× global), translation complaints 3.6% (4.6× global), Watch 5.0%. Japanese reviewers write the longest, most technically precise defect reports in the corpus (`13323018578`, a five-revision running log of regressions; `12925478996`; `13203062194`-equivalent detail). **Japan is telling you exactly what is broken and rating you accordingly.**

**DE: mean 3.84, 23.5% one-and-two-star — the highest 1–2★ rate of any eligible market.** Drivers: crashes 7.4% (highest of any market), translation 7.4% (9.5× global), subscription objection 8.8%, cap 8.8%. Germany also raises a specific, cheap-to-fix localisation gripe that no other market makes as sharply: **no 24-hour time format, week starting Sunday, unchangeable date format** (`6799961112`, DE, 4★, 2020: *"Nur die Internationalisierung fehlt… es kein 24h-Zeitformat gibt, dass eine Woche immer Sonntag beginnt"*; still unfixed per `13799067782`, DE/CH, 1★, Feb 2026: *"weshalb erkennt die App keine 24-Stunden Uhrzeit"*). **Six years.**

## 7.4 Australia and Mexico are the healthy markets — and they show what "good" looks like

**AU: mean 4.49, only 7.1% 1–2★, 72.4% 5★, life-change mentions 18.4% (2× global).** Notably AU *also* has the highest cap-mention rate (9.2%) — further evidence that naming the cap is not the same as churning over it. **MX: mean 4.39, 71.4% 5★, and essentially zero reliability complaints.** Spanish-language reviews are consistently warm and specific (`8810965416`, `12459764181`, `13763004001`).

## 7.5 China: the app does not work at the storefront level
**23 reviews (0.69%) from the CN storefront report the app will not open, will not register, or shows a network error. CN mean is 2.77 with 46.7% 1–2★ across 30 total reviews.** The reports run continuously from Aug 2021 to Jan 2026: `7716753605`, `9542524738`, `9642437584`, `9708023091`, `10359901346`, `11157656351`, `11607673178`, `11810774152`, `12541720254`, `12661622269`, `12748667037`, `12966673910`, `13128697638`, `13216388903`. `9567925230` (CN, 4★) diagnoses it: *"总提示有网络问题… 是不是中国大陆登不上？"* — *[always says network problem… is it that mainland China can't connect?]* — consistent with a backend or auth endpoint unreachable from mainland China. `10359901346` (CN, 1★): *"扣了费以后根本用不了 一直显示没有网络"* — *[paid and it's completely unusable, always shows no network]* — **the app is taking money in a market where it cannot function.** Alongside 3+ explicit Chinese-language requests, this is a market that is either worth entering properly or worth withdrawing from.

## 7.6 Small-storefront caveat
77 storefronts hold fewer than 50 reviews each (502 reviews, 15.0%, mean 4.06, 17.3% 1–2★). They are fully counted in every global number above. No standalone claims are drawn from them, with these disclosed exceptions where the evidence is qualitatively material: the CN cluster (§7.5, 30 reviews — flagged as limited evidence but a coherent, seven-year, single-cause pattern); the NL `8120137325` sync-deletes-data report; the SE/TH/PL Apple Watch reports that corroborate the KR/JP pattern; and single-review Arabic/Turkish/Italian/Russian language requests, each weak but mutually consistent.

# PART 9 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

## Immediate fixes — these determine whether the rating moves off 4.2

1. **Kill the notification loop (2026 regression).** 33 lifetime reports, 12 in May 2026 alone, mean 3.00. It punishes the exact trait the product sells to and generates the app's angriest recent 1★s. → §0.6, §4.2. *Ships the biggest rating delta for the least work.*
2. **Make the notification escalation ladder user-configurable, and stop silently retuning it.** Some users depend on the un-dismissable buzz to wake up; others are driven out by it. Both are currently being served badly by the same global default. Add: `once` / `escalating` / `until-started`, per routine. → `13667837214`, `14371016110`, `14447685280`, `12780143141`.
3. **Fix background battery and thermals.** Mean 2.94, 43.5% 1–2★ — the worst non-billing theme. At least one reviewer states outright they will not renew because of it. Audit the background timer; consider `ActivityKit`/Live Activity in place of a wake-locked foreground timer. → §4.1.
4. **Add a first-class undo/back on the running routine, and separate the pause and done buttons.** Reported continuously since January 2020, trivially fixable, 42 reviews. → §4.7.
5. **Guarantee data durability.** Prompt for account creation *before* first routine is saved; make sync non-destructive (`8120137325` reports sync deleting all routines); add a visible manual backup/restore. 91 reviews. → §4.3.
6. **Fix the day-boundary handling for night routines** — log to the day the routine *started*, and honour the user's "day ends at" setting. 14 reviews, several from long-streak users whose streaks were destroyed. → §4.8.
7. **Put an in-app, localized support and cancellation path one tap from the home screen — and make it reachable when the app fails to launch** (a web fallback). This alone would materially clean up Korea's review page. → §4.4, §7.2.

## Monetization — repackage, don't reprice

8. **Stop gating on routine count. Gate on capability.** The cap is named by 168 reviewers of whom **116 rate 4–5★**: it is visible, accepted, and *not converting*, because morning+night genuinely satisfies the core job. Move the wall to where the demand actually is: analytics/history, cross-device sync, Apple Watch, widgets, icon library, family sharing. Raise the free cap to 3–4 (42 reviews ask for exactly this) and take the goodwill.
9. **Ship — and prominently surface — the lifetime purchase.** Asked for continuously 2020–2026 (46 reviews, mean 3.76, i.e. from fans). 2026 reviews suggest it now exists; if so it is under-marketed. `13987336109` bought within five minutes of finding it.
10. **Ship a family plan.** Five reviews, every one an explicitly *blocked* purchase, plus a parent segment rating 4.40. Lowest-volume/highest-intent request in the corpus.
11. **Remove ads for paying subscribers, unconditionally, including reward-gated features like streak savers.** Showing a subscriber a 30-second unskippable ad produced the single most damaging recent review from a payer (`14021959851`). This is not a revenue trade-off; it is a trust liability.
12. **Fix the trial→charge flow.** Whether it is a defect or an ambiguous plan-selection screen, the outcome is a 1.55-star average across 31 reviews spanning 2021–2026. Make the trial state explicit, send a pre-charge reminder email (`14438727762` notes none is sent), and never bill on login to a pre-existing account (`13666735555`).
13. **Retire or fully honour the 1+1 gift promo.** Twelve reviews, six years, still generating disputes in 2026. The fine print (light grey on white, per `9512372663`) is the problem.
14. **Move the review prompt off routine-completion.** It is firing at the highest-frequency event and generating 1★s from people who already reviewed. → §1.7.

## Product — converts 3–4★ into 5★

15. **Ship an untimed/checklist mode as a per-routine toggle** (not a separate object). 69 reviews, mean 4.26 — fans asking for an option, plus 15 who say the timer causes anxiety. Directly addresses the "almost" band.
16. **Ship per-task day/interval scheduling and sub-routines together.** 48 combined reviews at means 3.72/4.42. These solve the *same* root problem — forced whole-routine duplication to vary one step — which is also what pushes people into the routine cap. Highest-leverage feature work in the report.
17. **Deliver the widget/Lock Screen/Live Activity story properly.** 92 reviews, and the largest over-index in the 3–4★ band (5.5% vs 2.75% global). Users want to run the routine *without opening the app*; today the widget frequently renders blank (`12811011494`, `14018416858`, `13253617539`, `11264671674`) or fails to advance (`10696429419`, `14191150102`).
18. **Finish Shortcuts/auto-start.** 37 reviews. Several users built alarm→routine automations and then lost them to an update (`12956851447`, `13186621588`). This is the last manual step in an app whose whole thesis is removing manual steps.
19. **Human-translate the UI, or ship English-only honestly.** Translation complaints average **2.73 stars**. Priority order by evidence: DE, JA, PT-BR, FR. Simultaneously fix the DE/CH locale basics: 24-hour clock, Monday week start, system date format. Six-year-old, cheap, and disproportionately punished.
20. **Add icon skin-tone variants.** Four reviews over six years, one of them a standing 1★. Cheap; there is no defensible reason it is still open.

## Trust, safety and positioning

21. **Make the social feed opt-in, hideable, and adult-only — or remove it.** Two credible reports of minors surfaced to adult accounts on a 17+ app whose reviews are full of self-identified 9–13-year-olds. This is the one finding in the report that carries regulatory as well as product risk. → §4.9.
22. **Decide about China.** Seven years of "the app will not open", plus at least one user charged in a market where the product does not function. Either fix the endpoint reachability and localise, or geo-restrict the storefront.
23. **Treat review replies as the retention channel they demonstrably are.** Nine visible star-upgrades after developer contact, and 37 reviews praising responsiveness. But `13769267096` (GB) correctly notes that public replies are fast while email goes unanswered for months — a channel imbalance worth correcting rather than a virtue to lean on.

## Research questions the corpus cannot answer

- Is the trial→immediate-charge complaint a billing defect or a plan-selection UX failure? The reviews support both readings; instrumentation and a session replay of the paywall would settle it.
- Does raising the free cap to 3–4 routines increase or decrease paid conversion? The corpus establishes that the cap is *noticed* and *not converting*, but cannot establish counterfactual revenue.
- What share of the 5★ population would pay for family sharing? Five stated blocked purchases is a signal, not a market size.
- Did the 2026 lifetime plan cannibalise subscriptions or expand the payer base? Only three reviews mention it; too early.

## 10.3 Method and its known error rate

1. **Full manual read** of all 3,342 reviews to derive the taxonomy inductively (the themes below were written *after* reading, not before).
2. **Multilingual regex classification** — 70+ themes, each with English plus, where the theme occurs in them, Korean, Japanese, German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Chinese, Dutch, Polish, Swedish, Russian and Italian patterns.
3. **Precision validation by random sampling.** Every headline theme was sampled (8–12 reviews, seeded RNG) and each hit manually judged. Two patterns failed validation and were rebuilt:
   - `freeze_lag` (original n=499) scored **~50% precision** — it was matching the bare token "bug" including in *praise* ("bug fixes are good"). **Discarded** and replaced with two tighter themes: `perf_lag` (n=69, ~95% precision after a second fix for the Spanish/French substring collision where `lent` matched *"exceLENTE"*) and `bug_generic` (n=124, ~90%).
   - `praise_free_generous` (original n=122) scored **~70%**, catching complaints about paying. Rebuilt with a negative-context exclusion → n=68 at ~90% precision. **This is now a conservative floor, not an estimate**; the true count of reviews expressing "the free tier is generous" is higher, because any review pairing that sentiment with a pricing gripe was excluded.
4. **Estimated residual error: ~10% false-positive rate on the validated themes.** Recall is not measured and is certainly below 100% — regex under-counts paraphrase, so **every count in this report should be read as a floor.**
5. Umbrella themes (`RELIABILITY`, `MONETIZATION`, `BILLING`) are set unions of their components; a review counts once regardless of how many components it matches.

## 10.4 Known limitations and biases

- **Selection bias, amplified.** This app aggressively prompts for reviews at routine completion (§1.7). That likely over-samples *active daily users* relative to a typical store corpus — which makes the corpus more representative of engaged users but less representative of people who churned silently in week one.
- **Korea's channel artifact.** KR contributes 62.6% of billing complaints on 19.1% of reviews because Korean users file support tickets as reviews (§7.2). Global billing figures are therefore inflated by KR and deflated elsewhere. Stated explicitly rather than corrected for.
- **No purchase data.** "Confirmed payer" (n=120) means the reviewer *said* they paid. No conversion, churn, refund or ARPU rate can be or is claimed.
- **Version field is absent** from the review schema. Version-linked claims (e.g. "the 3.22.0 update broke reordering", `11099850216`) come from reviewer text, not metadata, and are attributed as such.
- **Ratings are not feature preferences.** A 5★ review reporting a total outage (seven of them in the July 2025 cluster) is counted in both the praise and the defect themes; theme membership is non-exclusive throughout.
- **Correlation, not causation.** The ADHD cohort rates 0.36 higher than everyone else. That is an association. It does not establish that the app works better for ADHD users than for others, only that self-identifying ADHD reviewers rate it higher.
- **Sub-50 storefronts** are counted globally but carry no standalone claims except the four disclosed exceptions in §7.6.
- **Small denominators.** Themes below ~30 reviews (roughly 0.9%) have wide confidence intervals. Where a sub-1% theme is promoted in this report it is on explicit safety, legal, or data-loss grounds (§4.9, §7.5) and is labelled.

## 10.5 Counting rules

- Denominator for every global percentage: **3,342**.
- Denominator for a country percentage: that country's own review count.
- Denominator for a star-band percentage: that band's review count (1★ 317 · 2★ 163 · 3★ 277 · 4★ 511 · 5★ 2,074).
- Denominator for a paid-segment rate: **120**. Always labelled as a segment rate and shown beside the global rate.
- Quarter/year buckets use the review's `date` field in UTC.
- "Mean" is the arithmetic mean of the 1–5 star ratings of the reviews in the theme.
- "1–2★" is the share of that theme's reviews rated 1 or 2.
