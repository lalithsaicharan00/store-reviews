## How to read this

Every quantified claim below carries a **count, a percentage, the denominator, the market scope, the time window, a signal label and the exact review IDs** that support it. Review IDs are the `review_id` values from `reviews.jsonl`; the bracketed `#n` is the working index (date order) used by Part 10, so any number in this report resolves to specific reviews and any review resolves to the findings it supports.

Theme counts are **non-exclusive**: a review that praises the design and objects to the price is counted in both. Composite unions (`U:` prefix) count each review **once**.

### ⚠️ Nine warnings before any number here is used

1. **No storefront reaches the 50-review threshold.** The largest is the United States at 38. **Part 7 therefore makes no standalone country claim.** Every per-storefront figure is presented as limited evidence.
2. **n = 172 is small.** One review is 0.58% of the corpus — which the spec's bands already label an "Emerging signal". Every headline share below carries a **Wilson 95% confidence interval** so the reader can see how wide these estimates really are.
3. **The corpus is 18.4% of the rating base.** 937 people rated this app across the same 43 storefronts; 172 wrote text. The 765 silent raters are not represented here.
4. **The text corpus is more negative than the rating base.** Corpus mean 4.599★ vs a ratings-weighted public mean of **4.781★** across the same storefronts. Treat the corpus as skewed slightly negative, not as a population estimate.
5. **This app solicits reviews in-app, and two reviewers rated it 1★ *for that reason*.** Ratings here are not an unprompted sample. See §1.8.
6. **The store's version history only reaches back to v1.8.0 (2025-07-03).** 99 of 172 reviews (57.56%) predate it. No release attribution is possible for the first ~11 months.
7. **The paid tier changed shape and price during the window, and the corpus is the only record of it.** No archived price list was available. §2.6 states exactly which reviews establish which price on which date, and says plainly what cannot be established.
8. **Two reviews were sourced by a giveaway** (`12352054623`, `14185235911` — the second discloses it unprompted). Both are retained; both are flagged.
9. **Nothing here is a conversion rate.** Eleven reviews carry purchase evidence. That is enough to describe buyers, and nowhere near enough to estimate what share of users buy. No conversion, renewal, refund or churn rate appears in this report.

- `13106720476` (2025-09-06) — "and **best of all** — it's a one time purchase!!"

Today's listing (captured 13 Sep 2026) sells **five SKUs**: Powerpack $9.99, **Ripples Plus Lifetime $24.99**, **Plus Monthly $2.99**, **Plus Yearly $9.99**, Powerpack Family $17.99. And the corpus records the change arriving:

- `13669961043` (2026-01-24, 5★, ru) — "I really like the app and **plan to purchase a subscription**" — the first review in 18 months to name a subscription as the thing to buy.

**The differentiator that 12 reviewers named as the reason they chose this app has been removed.** That is the most consequential single fact in this corpus, and it is corroborated by the live store listing rather than inferred.

What it does say is that a paywall two users describe this differently in the same fortnight is a **disclosure problem**, and §0.8 shows why.

## 0.8 The store listing says nothing about money at all

The full description was captured on 13 September 2026. It runs five headed sections — *SEE THE PATTERN*, *TRACK MORE THAN CHECKMARKS*, *STAY CONSISTENT WITH LESS FRICTION*, *UNDERSTAND YOUR PROGRESS*, *PRIVATE BY DESIGN* — and **contains no monetization paragraph whatsoever**. No free-tier limit. No mention of Plus. No mention of a subscription. No price.

Meanwhile the In-App Purchases box lists **five SKUs under three different product names** — *Powerpack*, *Ripples Plus Lifetime*, *Plus Monthly*, *Plus Yearly*, *Powerpack Family* — and, as Apple renders it, **shows no billing period against any of them**. A shopper sees "Plus Monthly $2.99" and "Ripples Plus Lifetime $24.99" side by side with no statement of what either unlocks or how they relate.

That is the structural cause of every confusion in §0.4 and §0.2:
- `11575210211` calls it "the premium **subscription** … a one time 10 dollar payment" — two models in one sentence, in 2024.
- `12104500362` calls it "**$10 for a lifetime subscription**" — the same collision.
- `14422900213` reaches for "**bait and switch**" (`MON_BAIT_SWITCH`) and asks the developer to "just charge for the download instead".

**Two reviewers say in as many words that the surprise, not the amount, is the complaint** (`MON_UNEXPECTED`, n=2, mean 2.00★): `14422900213` and `14483046633` ("after a while **it turns out** I have to pay").

## 0.9 The asset that is quietly eroding is the developer relationship

This app was, for its first year, partly a *person*. **`U:developer_equity`** — praise for the developer, for the indie-ness, for the public roadmap, or naming YouTube/Reddit as the acquisition channel — is **36 reviews (20.93%, mean 4.94★)**. Seven reviews name the creator-led channel directly: `11575210211` and `11622746398` (Reddit), `11839901182`, `11978848977`, `12104500362`, `13426922120`, `13439873787` (YouTube).

It is falling: **E1 29/110 (26.4%) → E2 2/21 (9.5%) → E3 5/41 (12.2%)**.

Two sub-signals are sharper than the union:
- **`PRAISE_INDIE` (n=9, mean 5.00★) has no entry after 2025-02-28.** Eighteen months, zero reviews praising this as an indie product, after nine in the first seven.
- **`CH_YOUTUBE` (n=5) has no entry after 2025-11-25.**

And the one review that reports being ignored is a paying customer: `13582314702` — "**Developer doesn't respond.** Waste of time." (`SUP_NO_RESPONSE`, n=1). Set against **14 reviews praising developer responsiveness** (`PRAISE_DEV`, 8.14%, mean 4.93★, last entry `14162555466` 2026-06-09), the pattern is not that support got bad — it is that the *audience changed*. The people arriving now did not come from a YouTube channel and have no relationship to spend.

## 0.11 Fifteen claims tested and not found

Every one of the following was searched for across all 172 reviews in all 13 text languages, with word-boundary patterns, and **returned zero hits**: advertising complaints, privacy or data-selling complaints, battery drain, accessibility needs, cancelling a subscription, a refund actually obtained, a support ticket or unanswered email, Android/web/cross-platform demand, Family Sharing, ADHD or clinical framing, AI features, social/friends/leaderboard features, a first-launch paywall, forced account creation, and inability to export data.

Two of those blanks are load-bearing:
- **Nobody in this corpus ever reports cancelling or getting a refund.** One review *threatens* a refund (`14173657564`). The entire churn picture rests on 3 reviews (`U:competitive_loss`, 1.74%).
- **Zero privacy complaints, against 3 reviews that volunteer privacy as a reason they chose it** (`11832301736`, `12160259550`, `14185235911`). "Private by design" is working.

## 0.12 The eight decisions this corpus supports

1. **Fix purchase delivery before anything else.** 4 of 11 one-star reviews, 33% of stated payers, mean 1.00★, corroborated by the developer's own v2.7.0 release note. (§0.6, §5.4, §8.1)
2. **Write the monetization paragraph the description does not have.** Two reviewers say the surprise is the complaint; one reaches for "bait and switch"; two describe the free tier incompatibly two weeks apart. (§0.8, §8.1)
3. **Put a billing period on every SKU, and reduce three product names to one.** (§2.4, §8.1)
4. **Reconsider gating the widget.** 27 reviews praise it, mean 5.00★, none negative; three reviews complain specifically that it is now paid. (§0.4, §8.4)
5. **Decide, and then say, whether this is a subscription product.** The corpus contains 12 reviews that chose it *because* it was not. (§0.2, §8.4)
6. **Treat post-v2.1.0 reliability as a regression, not as noise.** 0.9% → 19.5%, including three data-loss reports. (§0.7, §8.2)
7. **Ship the Watch app or say it is not coming.** Three voices, two of them explicitly withholding a star. (§0.10, §8.3)
8. **Turn off, or heavily throttle, the in-app review prompt.** It has directly produced two 1★ reviews from people who say the app is good. (§1.8, §8.2)

## 1.2 Schema actually present

Thirteen fields, all populated on all 172 records, zero nulls and zero empty strings:

`review_id`, `app_id`, `app_name`, `country`, `country_name`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `author`, `date`, `vote_count`, `vote_sum`, `is_edited`.

Notes on the fields that matter:
- **`app_id`** is `6502667826` on all 172; **`app_name`** is `Habit Tracker - Ripples` on all 172 — the title at extraction time (2026-09-08), not the current US title.
- **There is no app-version field.** Nothing in this corpus attributes a review to a build. All release attribution in Part 8 is made by **date against the published version history**, and is labelled as coincidence-in-time, never as cause.
- `is_edited` is true on **10 records (5.81%)**: #84, #89, #94, #100, #102, #122, #139, #141, #149, #152. Two of these carry visible edit history in the body (`14173657564` has three timestamps in one text; `14118758406` appends "Edit - so happy to have compact boards now!"). **An edited review's date is its last-edit date**, which is a known limitation for §8.
- `vote_count`/`vote_sum` are non-zero on only 5 records. Too sparse to use; not used.
- Bodies contain HTML entities (`&#39;`, `&quot;`). All text was HTML-unescaped before reading and before every pattern test.

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Dump** (`79-dump.py`) — all 172 records, HTML-unescaped, sorted by `(date, review_id)`, into a single reading file plus an index table. Working index **#1…#172** is assigned here and is used throughout.
2. **Read** — every review read individually, in its original language, in date order, start to finish. No keyword filtering, no sampling, no stopping at a context limit. Reading in date order is deliberate: it is what makes §0.3 and §0.4 possible, because the price and the free tier only make sense as a sequence.
3. **Hand-classify** (`79-cls/*.txt`) — one line per review: index, codes, and a note quoting the words that drove the judgement. Written while reading, not afterwards. This file is the expensive artifact; everything else regenerates from it.
4. **Resolve** (`79-build-classification.py`) — index → `review_id`, after re-deriving the ordering from `reviews.jsonl` and asserting it field-by-field against the index table. Emits `79-review-classification.py`.
5. **Validate** (`79-validate.py`) — **37 checks, all passing**. See §1.9.
6. **Aggregate** (`79-aggregate.py`, `79-extra.py`, `79-extra2.py`) — counts, unions, Wilson intervals, per-star profiles, storefronts, eras, quarters, months, market groups, integrity checks, absence tests.
7. **External capture** — iTunes Lookup, the US product page, the 25-entry version history, and per-storefront ratings for all 43 storefronts. All saved to disk on 13 September 2026 so every external claim is re-checkable offline.
8. **Generate and verify** (`79-tables.py`, `79-verify-report.py`) — every table in this report is generated from the classification, and the finished report is then re-checked against it.

**The classification is entirely human judgement.** No automated classifier, embedding model or keyword rule assigned any code. The known error risk is therefore *reader* error, and §9.C lists the judgement calls where a different reader could reasonably disagree.

## 1.6 Limitations — read these before citing any figure

**1.6.1 No storefront qualifies for standalone analysis.** The spec's threshold is 50. The largest storefront here is the US at **38**. Germany is 17, the UK 12, India 10. **Part 7 makes no standalone country claim.** Where a storefront is discussed, it is labelled limited evidence and the count is shown.

**1.6.2 The signal bands cannot discriminate at n = 172.** One review is 0.58%, which the band table calls an "Emerging signal". Three reviews is 1.74%, a "Meaningful signal". The bands are applied as the spec requires, but **the Wilson 95% interval is the honest number** and is printed beside every headline share. Example: `MON_PAYWALL_BLOCKS` is 4 reviews = 2.33%, band "Meaningful signal", **CI 0.9–5.8%**. Do not plan against the point estimate.

**1.6.3 Selection bias, quantified.** 937 public ratings across the same 43 storefronts; 172 text reviews. **Text reviews are 18.4% of the rating base.** Ratings-weighted public mean **4.781★** vs corpus mean **4.599★**. The text corpus is the more negative sample by about 0.18★. France is the extreme case: **57 public ratings, 2 text reviews.**

**1.6.4 Solicitation is confirmed, not suspected.** See §1.8.

**1.6.5 No version field, and a version history that starts late.** The published history begins at v1.8.0 (2025-07-03); **99 of 172 reviews (57.56%) predate it**. For those, only dates exist.

**1.6.6 Price history is reconstructed from reviews, not from an archive.** No historical price list was available. §0.3 and §2.6 state which review establishes which price on which date. What the corpus **cannot** establish: when exactly the subscription SKUs were introduced, whether the Powerpack and Plus lines coexist or supersede one another, or what each SKU unlocks.

**1.6.7 Edited reviews carry their last-edit date.** 10 records. `14173657564` is the clearest: its text contains an original complaint, a same-week correction, and a "2yrs later" edit, all under the date 2026-06-12. Time-trend statements that depend on a single edited review are flagged where they occur.

**1.6.8 Language identification is the analyst's, not the file's.** There is no language field. The 13 buckets in §9.K are hand-assigned from the text; three records are bilingual and are tagged as such (`en/de`, `en/uk`).

**1.6.9 Storefront is not nationality and not language.** Germany's 17 reviews include both German and English texts; `14192253561` is a Spanish-language review on the **US** storefront; `14187027057` is Spanish on the **Mexican** storefront but says nothing about the app at all.

Both retained, both flagged; removing them moves the corpus mean by less than 0.02★.

## 1.8 Review integrity — solicitation, bursts, contradictions

**Solicitation is confirmed by the corpus itself.** Two reviewers describe an in-app rating prompt, and **both rated the app 1★ specifically because of it**:

- `13728350332` (2026-02-08, us) — "Great app, but I always leave one star reviews for apps that **harass me with 'enjoying the app? Please leave a review' popups**. It's gross. Change that behavior and I'll update this to 5 stars."
- `14173657564` (2026-06-12, ph) — "i already rated this highly, but now **I keep getting the review prompt after every check in**. Lowering to 1 stars until fixed."

Two consequences. First, **the rating distribution in this corpus is prompted, not spontaneous** — the 83.14% 5★ share should be read as what a satisfied user gives when asked at a good moment. Second, the prompt is itself **producing** one-star reviews: 2 of 11, which is 18.2% of the app's worst ratings caused by a growth mechanic rather than by the product.

**Bursts.** 140 distinct review days across a 770-day window; maximum 5 reviews on any day (2024-12-25); maximum 9 in any rolling 7-day window (from 2024-08-04, the fortnight after launch). No repeat authors anywhere in the corpus. **There is no evidence of a coordinated or purchased review campaign.**

## 5.3 What causes people to buy

Four mechanisms appear, in descending order of evidence.

**1. Hitting the board cap — the only explicit trigger in the corpus.**
`14201744091` (2026-06-19, nl, 5★): *"**upgraded for more boards** and to support the developer :)"*. One review, so a weak signal on its own — but it is the only review that names what it bought and why, and it matches the free-tier structure that three other reviewers describe (§2.5).

**2. Supporting the developer.** `MON_SUPPORT_DEV` = 3 (`11832301736` "because I like your approach", `13854797898`, `14201744091`). This is a *donation* motive attached to a purchase, and it is the motive the §0.9 erosion threatens most directly. `13854797898` is the cautionary case: the support motive was present and the price defeated it.

**3. The one-time model itself.** Seven of the 12 `MON_ONETIME_PRAISE` reviews frame the pricing as a *reason to choose this app over rivals* (`11737999553` "in comparison to other habit trackers that are subscription based"; `13106720476` "best of all — it's a one time purchase"). **4. Roadmap confidence.** `11602935317` (ru) — *"what I personally miss (notes and custom units) is **already planned by the developer**! So I'll probably buy the full version soon"*. Purchase intent conditioned on a published roadmap. `12071906839` bought and *then* said they were waiting on the roadmap.

**Purchase intent** (`MON_WILL_BUY`) = 7 reviews, **mean 5.00★**, spanning 2024-07-28 → 2026-04-24. ## 5.5 The three-board cap as a conversion instrument

The cap is the conversion mechanism, and the corpus shows it working and failing in the same evidence:

- **Working:** `14201744091` upgraded *for more boards*.
- **Tolerated:** `12899474787` (2025-07-16, 5★) — "the free version allows for three habits … so you can use it for free **without huge limitations**". `11832301736` (2024-10-14, 5★) — free "for widgets and 3 habits **without limitations**". `13426922120` (2025-11-22, 5★) — "Man kann aber auch gut ohne Leben."
- **Failing:** `13681657252` (2026-01-27, 3★) — "met 3 boards ben je **totaal niks**".

The number never changed. What changed is that in 2024–25 the free tier included widgets and the cap read as a fair limit; by 2026 the free tier had lost the widget and the same cap read as a wall. **The cap is not the lever that broke; the bundle around it is.**

## 5.6 Refunds, cancellations and renewals — an explicit blank

- **Zero** reviews report obtaining a refund.
- **Zero** reviews report cancelling a subscription.
- **Zero** reviews mention a renewal.
- **One** review threatens a refund: `14173657564` — "I'll refund my subscription if this doesn't get fixed soon" — and its own later edit says the underlying problem resolved.

All four statements were tested with word-boundary patterns across all 172 reviews in all 13 languages (§9.F). **The post-purchase churn picture in this report therefore rests on three reviews** (`U:competitive_loss`), and no statement about renewal or cancellation behaviour is made anywhere.

## 7.2 T1 — Ratings fell monotonically across every boundary — *worsening, and the strongest trend in the corpus*

Mean **4.89 → 4.19 → 4.02**. 5★ share **95.5% → 71.4% → 56.1%**. Reviews carrying any negative code **4.5% → 23.8% → 41.5%**.

Cross-checks that this is not an artefact of one bad month:
- First 50 reviews (through 2024-12-25): **4.88★**. Last 50 (from 2025-12-31): **3.98★**.
- First 12 months: 105 reviews, **4.89★**. Last 12 months: 65 reviews, **4.12★**.
- Quarterly: 2024 Q3 **4.93** → 2025 Q2 **5.00** → 2026 Q1 **3.82** → 2026 Q3 **3.71**.
- One-star reviews: **2 in the first 15 months, 9 in the last 11**.

**What this is not:** it is not a collapse in how people regard the product. `U:craft_praise` is still 58.5% in E3, `COMP_BEST` is still being awarded in the final review of the corpus, and 23 of the 41 E3 reviews are 5★. The decline is additive — the same praise, now with something attached to it.

## 7.3 T2 — Monetization complaints appeared and stayed — *worsening*

`U:money_negative`: **3/110 (2.7%, mean 1.67★) → 4/21 (19.0%) → 7/41 (17.1%, mean 2.29★)**.

The composition changed as much as the volume. In E1 the three money complaints are: one price objection (`11570370725`, 2024-08-04), and two failed purchases (`11921754194`, `12352054623`). **There is not a single complaint about the paywall's *shape* in the first 15 months** — `U:paywall_shape` is **0/110 in E1**, then 9.5% and 9.8%.

And the positive side did not vanish; it thinned and then partly recovered. `MON_ONETIME_PRAISE`: **E1 9 → E2 1 → E3 2**. `MON_PRICE_FAIR`: **E1 6 → E2 0 → E3 4**. Four reviewers still volunteer that the price is fair *after* it rose, the last of them in the final review of the corpus (`14501661045`, 2026-09-02: "der Preis für die Vollversion sehr fair").

**Reading:** this is not a market rejecting a price. It is a market that was sold a specific promise — one payment, no subscription — encountering a different one, with no listing text to mediate the change.

## 7.4 T3 — Defects went from zero to a fifth of the corpus — *worsening, and newly*

`U:defect_any`: **1/110 (0.9%) → 0/21 (0.0%) → 8/41 (19.5%, mean 2.50★)**.

Eight of the nine defect reports in the corpus fall after **v2.1.0 (2026-02-11)**, the release whose own notes warn that widgets and custom shortcuts must be re-created, and which was followed within four days by **v2.1.1/2.1.2** adding a *"Deduplicate data"* tool "as some people reported duplicated boards and check-ins after the previous release".

Within E3 the sub-classes are traceable:
- **Crash** — `13767244783` (2026-02-19), fixed by name in **v2.2.0, 11 days later**.
- **UI bugs** — `13836767975` (2026-03-11), one month after v2.1.0, from a self-described daily user who says the app is now "unusable".
- **Notifications** — `13929651995` (2026-04-06) and `13962890919` (2026-04-16), both arriving **five and six weeks after v2.2.0 shipped multiple reminders**. A feature three reviewers asked for in 2024 shipped and then generated two reliability complaints.
- **Data loss** — `14173657564` (2026-06-12), `14293610339` (2026-07-12), `14397430419` (2026-08-07). The last matches **v2.6.2 (2026-07-20)**, a stability fix for updates from year-old builds.

**Causation is not claimed.** What is claimed is the coincidence in time, which is tight, repeated, and corroborated by the developer's own notes acknowledging each class of problem.

## 7.5 T4 — The paywall tightened in the second half — *emerging in E2, persistent in E3*

Every `U:paywall_shape` review in the corpus (6) falls after 2025-10-25. The free-tier evidence sequence (§0.4) puts the change between **2025-07-16** (`12899474787`: free tier usable, no huge limitations) and **2025-10-25** (`13311025859`: can't even place a widget). That is a **101-day window** in which the free tier's most-praised component appears to have moved behind the wall.

Counter-evidence is kept in view: `14482128335` (2026-08-28, 5★, es) still describes the free tier as "súper útil con analíticas y todo" — consistent with **v2.4.0 (2026-04-27)** de-gating advanced analytics. **The paywall moved in both directions during E3**, and this report does not present it as a one-way tightening.

## 7.6 T5 — Developer equity thinned — *worsening, moderate confidence*

`U:developer_equity`: **26.4% → 9.5% → 12.2%**.

Two sharper sub-signals:
- **`PRAISE_INDIE` (n=9, mean 5.00★) has no entry after 2025-02-28.** Nine reviews in the first seven months; zero in the following eighteen.
- **`CH_YOUTUBE` (n=5) has no entry after 2025-11-25.**

`PRAISE_DEV` itself holds up better — 14 reviews, last one 2026-06-09 (`14162555466`: "Appreciate the updates. Keeps improving") — so this is not a story about support getting worse. It is a story about **who is arriving**: the early corpus is full of people who followed a developer; the recent corpus is full of people who found an app.

**Confidence caveat:** with 5–9 reviews per sub-signal and only 65 reviews in the last twelve months, an absence of 18 months is suggestive rather than conclusive. It is flagged as a trend to watch, not as an established one.

## 7.7 T6 — Requests stayed constant and got structurally harder — *persistent*

`U:request_any` is flat: **16.4% → 19.0% → 19.5%**. What changed is what is being asked for.

| Era | Typical request |
|---|---|
| E1 | notes, reminders, smaller widgets, start-of-day, multi-board view — all **incremental**, and all shipped |
| E3 | Apple Watch, iPad, data import, categories/search, photo attachments — all **structural**, none shipped |

Three E1-era requests shipped and were visibly acknowledged: `14118758406` returned to edit its review — *"so happy to have compact boards now!"* — and `14162555466` opens its E3 update with "Appreciate the updates. Keeps improving." **The request pipeline works.** The backlog that remains is the part a point release cannot clear.

## 7.8 What persisted unchanged across all 770 days

- **Craft praise.** `PRAISE_DESIGN` appears in every quarter of the corpus without exception.
- **The comparative win.** `COMP_BEST` runs 2024-09-18 → 2026-09-02 and is present in the final review.
- **Widgets at 5.00★.** 27 mentions, 2024-08-05 → 2026-06-22, not one below five stars.
- **Zero complaints about ads, privacy, battery, accessibility or forced accounts** — across all three eras (§9.F).
- **The three-board free cap.** Stated as a number in 2024-10, 2025-07 and 2026-01. It never moved.

## 7.9 What this corpus cannot say about time

- **It cannot date the pricing change.** The corpus brackets it; the store does not publish price history; §2.6 states the bracket and stops.
- **It cannot attribute the rating decline to any single cause.** The pricing change, the widget gating, the v2.1.0 regression and the review prompt are all live in the same window and cannot be separated with 41 reviews in E3.
- **It cannot rule out a composition shift.** E1's reviewers include a self-selected audience arriving from YouTube and Reddit; E3's do not, as far as the corpus shows. A drop from 4.89 to 4.02 is consistent with a *harsher audience* as well as with a *worse experience*, and this corpus cannot separate those two.
- **It cannot measure volume trend reliably.** Monthly counts range 1–18 with no seasonality visible, and review volume is not install volume.

# PART 8 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation names the findings it rests on. Immediate fixes, experiments and open research questions are kept apart, because the evidence for each is different in kind.

## 8.1 Do first — fix delivery and disclosure. Cost: days, not sprints.

**8.1.1 Make the purchase work, and make restore work.** *(from §0.6, §3.4-N2, §5.4)*
4 of 11 one-star reviews, **mean 1.00★**, **33.3% of stated payers**, spanning 2024-11-07 to 2026-06-17 — so this is not a single bad build, it is a *recurring* class. Three distinct failure modes appear: entitlement not applied after payment (`11921754194`), restore-purchases failing (`11921754194`, `13582314702`), and a paid entitlement not usable on a second device (`14192253561`). The developer's own **v2.7.0** note — *"Fixed an issue where Ripples+ features would sometimes become locked while the device was offline"* — confirms the class is real. Add: an in-app "my purchases" state that shows what is owned and when it was verified, an offline grace period for verified entitlements, and a visible support path from the failure screen. `13582314702` is the worst outcome available here: a paying customer who says "**Developer doesn't respond**" in a public 1★.

**8.1.2 Write the monetization paragraph the description does not have.** *(from §0.8, §2.4, §3.4-N1)*
The store description contains **no monetization text at all**. Two reviewers say the *surprise* is the complaint (`14422900213`, `14483046633`); one reaches for "bait and switch"; two describe the free tier incompatibly fifteen days apart. Four sentences would close this: what the free tier includes, that the board limit is three, what Plus adds, and that both a subscription and a one-time option exist. **This is the cheapest high-value change in the report.**

**8.1.3 Put a billing period on every SKU and collapse three product names to one.** *(from §2.3, §2.4)*
The listing sells *Powerpack*, *Ripples Plus Lifetime*, *Plus Monthly*, *Plus Yearly* and *Powerpack Family* with no period shown against any of them. Nine reviews use six different names for the paid tier and two incompatible billing models — including one review that calls it "the premium subscription … a one time 10 dollar payment" in a single sentence. A customer who cannot tell whether they are buying a subscription cannot consent to buying one.

**8.1.4 Turn the review prompt down.** *(from §1.8, §3.4-N5)*
Two 1★ reviews, **mean 1.00★**, **18.2% of the app's worst ratings**, both from people who say the app is good. `14173657564` is explicit: *"i already rated this highly, but now I keep getting the review prompt after every check in. Lowering to 1 stars until fixed."* Cap it: once per user, never after a check-in, never after a user has already rated. Expected effect is immediate and measurable in the rating stream.

**8.2.1 Treat post-v2.1.0 defects as a regression, not as background noise.** *(from §0.7, §7.4)*

| 1 | **Data / board loss** | `14173657564`, `14293610339`, `14397430419` | For a habit tracker the record *is* the product. One user attributes the loss to the rename; one recovered via iCloud; one is asking for boards back. |
| 2 | **Notification reliability** | `13929651995` (3★), `13962890919` (4★) | Both **after** v2.2.0 shipped reminders. `13962890919` ends "maybe is a bug **I would like a anwser**". |
| 3 | **Board reordering / layout freeze** | `14501661045` (5★, 2026-09-02) | Reported **after** the v2.4.2/v2.5.0 drag-and-drop rework, by the corpus's final reviewer. |
| 4 | Crash on board creation | `13767244783` | **Already fixed** in v2.2.0 — listed to close the loop, not to reopen it. |

I had a nice streak going" — a 4★ from someone who lost their record and is still being polite. An archive/restore feature shipped in v2.3.0; nothing in the corpus suggests users know it exists or that it covers this case.

## 8.4 Positioning and monetization strategy

**8.4.1 Reconsider gating the widget.** *(from §0.4, §3.3-P3, §5.5)*
`PRAISE_WIDGET` is 27 reviews at **mean 5.00★ with zero negative mentions** — the only feature in the corpus with a perfect record. Two reviewers complain specifically that it is now paid, one of them in the app's angriest review. A third proposes the compromise himself: `14373214557` — *"make 2 widgets available in free version, one the bigger one and one the minimalist one."* A widget on a home screen is a **daily impression of the product**; removing it from the free tier removes the strongest retention and word-of-mouth surface the app has, in exchange for pressure on a cap that was already working (`14201744091` upgraded *for more boards*, not for widgets).

**8.4.2 Decide whether this is a subscription product, then say so plainly.** *(from §0.2, §7.3)*
Twelve reviews chose this app partly because it was not a subscription. That audience is not hypothetical — it is 6.98% of everyone who wrote, at a mean of 4.67★, and it includes the corpus's only "used to be great" review (`13836767975`), which lists the absence of a subscription among the things it has lost. If the subscription stays, the one-time Lifetime SKU must be prominent enough that the original promise is visibly honoured; if it goes, say that too. The current state — five SKUs, three names, no periods, no description text — is the worst of both.

**8.4.3 Price level is not the barrier. Do not lead with a discount.** *(from §3.3-P7, §3.4-N3, §6.8)*
Ten reviews volunteer that the price is good (**mean 5.00★**), four of them written *after* the increase, against five that object to the amount. The five objectors are Italian, Slovak, German, German and Czech — **not** a low-income-market pattern. The elasticity evidence points at *what you get* and *whether you knew*, not at *how much*. A price cut would buy less than four sentences of disclosure.

**8.4.4 Rebuild the acquisition story, or accept a harsher audience.** *(from §0.9, §7.6)*
Seven reviews name YouTube or Reddit as the channel; nine praise the indie-ness; none of the latter appears after 2025-02-28 and none of the former after 2025-11-25. The E1 corpus rated 4.89★ partly because it arrived predisposed. Whatever replaces that channel will not be predisposed, which makes §8.1 and §8.2 more urgent, not less.

## 8.5 Do not do

- **Do not add gamification, streak pressure, social feeds or AI.** `PRAISE_NO_GAMIFICATION` is explicit (`11832349033`: "Minimal incentive to follow some arbitrary set of rules"; `13882115210`: "no extra gamification, no ads, no BS"), and `U:anti_bloat` is 38.37% at mean 4.98★. Zero reviews ask for social features or AI (§9.F). The product's position **is** restraint.
- **Do not add ads.** Five reviews praise their absence; zero ask for a cheaper ad-supported tier.
- **Do not weaken the privacy posture.** Three reviews cite it as a reason for choosing the app; zero complain about it.
- **Do not rewrite the UI.** 68.02% craft praise, and the one review that says the app became "unusable" blames *bugs introduced by an internal overhaul*, not the design.
- **Do not treat the 4.599★ corpus mean as the app's rating.** The public base is 937 ratings at **4.781★** (§1.6.3).

## 8.6 Experiments the corpus raises but cannot settle

1. **Free tier with widgets, cap at 3 boards vs current.** Hypothesis from §8.4.1: widget access drives retention and word-of-mouth more than it drives free-riding. Measure trial→paid and D30 retention, not just conversion.
2. **Disclosure A/B on the store listing.** Add the monetization paragraph (§8.1.2) and measure the 1★ rate and the `MON_UNEXPECTED`-style complaint rate, not installs.
3. **Review-prompt throttle.** §8.1.4. Cheapest experiment in the report; expected to move the 1★ rate directly.
4. **Weekly-target boards.** §8.3. Two reviewers have pre-committed a star; the question is whether non-daily habits are a niche or a large unserved segment.
5. **Lifetime-forward paywall.** Show Ripples Plus Lifetime first, subscription second, and measure whether the "no subscription" audience converts at a higher rate than the current ordering.

## 8.7 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

- **What share of users ever hit the three-board cap?** Eleven purchase-evidence reviews cannot tell you. Instrumentation can.
- **How many of the 765 silent raters are on the free tier?** Unknown and unknowable here.
- **Did existing one-time buyers get grandfathered when the subscription arrived?** `13582314702` is consistent with a migration failure but is one review (§2.6).
- **Is the rating decline an experience change or an audience change?** §7.9 states plainly that this corpus cannot separate them.
- **Why did France produce 57 ratings and 2 text reviews?** The most under-written market in the corpus.
- **Does the Watch app actually convert, or is it a stated preference?** Three voices, two with pre-committed stars — enough to justify the experiment, not enough to justify the roadmap slot on its own.

## 8.8 If only three things get done

1. **Make purchases and restores work, everywhere, including offline.** *(4 one-star reviews, mean 1.00★, a third of all stated payers, corroborated by the developer's own v2.7.0 note.)*
2. **Write four sentences of monetization disclosure into the store listing, and put a billing period on every SKU.** *(Six paywall-shape complaints, all after Oct 2025, none before; two reviewers naming surprise as the grievance; two reviewers describing the free tier incompatibly two weeks apart.)*
3. **Put the widget back in the free tier — or replace it with an equally visible free surface.** *(27 mentions, mean 5.00★, zero negative; the only free-tier component whose removal is separately attested by three reviewers.)*

## 9.A Counting rules

1. **Denominator.** 172 for every global figure, unless the sentence names a narrower scope.
2. **Non-exclusive.** A review carrying `PRAISE_DESIGN` and `MON_PRICE_LEVEL` is counted in both. 172 reviews carry **563** assignments (3.27 per review).
3. **Unions count once.** `U:` codes count a review once regardless of how many member codes it carries.
4. **Direction is a property of the code, not of the review.** `MON_PRICE_LEVEL` is a negative code and appears on a 5★ review (`13426922120`). §4.8 handles this explicitly.
5. **Segment rates are labelled and paired.** Every segment rate appears with both its segment denominator and its global count (§5.7).
6. **Signal bands** are applied exactly as specified, at the global level. Because n=172 makes them coarse (§1.6.2), a **Wilson 95% interval** accompanies every headline share.
7. **Eras** are assigned by calendar date against three published release dates. Every review falls in exactly one era; the assignment is asserted in validation check 12.
8. **Language** is hand-assigned from the text (no language field exists). Bilingual reviews get a compound tag.
9. **Nothing was excluded.** Low-information, off-topic, giveaway-sourced and edited reviews all sit in every denominator. §1.7 gives the sensitivity analysis.

## 9.C Judgement calls, borderline decisions, and known error risk

The classification is entirely human. The risk is reader error, so the calls a different reader could reasonably make differently are listed here.

**Three errors caught by the text-derivation checks, and corrected before any number was computed:**

1. **`14201744091` carried `MON_CAP_STATED`.** The check requires the literal number three next to "boards"/"habits"; this review says only "upgraded for **more boards**". The code was removed. It still carries `MON_PAID` and remains the corpus's clearest purchase trigger — but it does not attest the cap's *value*, and now says so.
2. **`13137552157` failed the `PRAISE_GITHUB_GRID` text check.** It describes the grid ("when you see the little boxes fill up day by day") without naming GitHub. Rather than drop the review, the code's **definition was widened** to "named as GitHub, or described as the grid of boxes filling in", and the check widened with it. Both are recorded here so the widening is not invisible.
3. **`12547828773` was coded `INT_LOW_INFO`** on a misread of a line-wrapped body. The full text ends "**Can't recommend highly enough ✨**". Recoded to `OUT_RECOMMEND`. This is the one place where a display truncation, not a judgement, produced an error — which is why check 31 (low-information reviews must be short) exists.

**Borderline decisions a different reader might make differently:**

- **`INT_LOW_INFO` threshold.** Defined as "names no feature, outcome, defect, request, price fact or use case". Under that rule `12059967532` ("Beautiful and simple" / "Aaaah!") is **not** low-information, because "simple" and "beautiful" are product attributes. A stricter reader would include it, moving the code from 7 to 8. §1.7 shows the whole sensitivity band is under 3 percentage points either way.
- **`MON_PAID_IMPLIED` (n=2).** `12226286073` ("worth every penny") and `12537644066` ("für einen so geringen einmaligen Preis") are read as owners. Neither says "I bought it". They are kept separate from `MON_PAID` precisely so a reader can discard them; every payer figure in Part 5 is quoted against the **9** stated payers, not the 11.
- **`INT_RATING_CONTRADICT` (n=5).** Applied where the text's dominant content opposes the star. `14173657564` is arguable — a 1★ protest rating with a stated reason is not strictly a contradiction — but it is flagged because the spec requires rating-vs-text conflicts to be disclosed rather than smoothed.
- **`COMP_TRIED_MANY` vs a rhetorical comparison.** Applied only where the reviewer says they tried or searched. `11987758860` ("None of the overhead of these overly complicated trackers") implies it and is **not** counted. A looser reading would add 2–3 reviews.
- **`MON_DECLINED_PURCHASE` on `13426922120`.** A 5★ review that says the price is "etwas teuer" and "man kann auch gut ohne leben". Read as a declined purchase. A different reader could read it as neutral.
- **`CHURN_ABANDON` on `14483046633`.** The reviewer recommends a competitor but does not say they uninstalled. Read as churn on the strength of "For anyone poor, I recommend 'Blossom'".
- **`14290676887`'s `MON_PAYWALL_BLOCKS`.** "You don t let me modifie my habits whitout paying" is the corpus's only claim that *editing* is gated. Retained at n=1 with no weight placed on it beyond §4.6.
- **Era boundaries.** Chosen as release events, not as data splits. Moving E3's boundary to v2.2.0 (2026-03-01) would move 6 reviews and does not change the direction of any trend in Part 7.

**What the classification does not attempt:** sentiment scoring, intensity weighting, or inferring a rating from text. Codes record what a review *says*, and the star rating is carried separately.
