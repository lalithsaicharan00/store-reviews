**App:** Blossom Habit Tracker (Fern) — *Build habits. Grow every day*
**Developer of record:** Frances Ling · **Bundle:** `com.fernhabit.app`
**Corpus:** 91 written reviews · 29 storefronts · 10 June 2026 → 4 September 2026
**Corpus mean rating:** 4.956 · **Extracted:** 8 September 2026 (`itunes MZStore userReviewsRow`)
**Analysis date:** 12 September 2026
**Business model (review-derived):** Free download, **no subscription, no paywall, no free-tier habit cap, no ads**, with a **voluntary in-app tip** as the only payment surface observed in the corpus (§2.2). **Zero reviewers in this corpus report paying for this app.**

## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Every claim traces to an exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`. **§9.6 is a complete 91-row index** — every review in the corpus, with ID, storefront, star rating, date, verbatim title and assigned themes. Nothing in this report is unreachable from that table.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several. All 91 records carry at least one theme (mean 3.40 themes per review, max 10).
- Signal thresholds: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority. Denominator for every global percentage is **91**. **With n = 91, one review is 1.10%** — the threshold table's lowest three bands are unreachable, and a single review lands in "meaningful". This is stated again wherever it matters.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.

### ⚠️ Nine warnings before any number here is used

1. **This corpus is tiny (91) and the rating distribution is degenerate.** 89 of 91 reviews are 5★ (97.80%) and 2 are 3★ (2.20%). **There is not a single 4★, 2★ or 1★ review in the corpus.** A rating distribution with two occupied buckets out of five cannot support a ratings analysis in the usual sense, and Part 4 says so explicitly rather than manufacturing one.
2. **The corpus is 87 days long.** First review 10 June 2026, last 4 September 2026. This is a launch-window corpus for a brand-new app, not a product history. **No long-run trend claim is possible and none is made.** Part 7 compares two 45/46-record halves and labels every observation with its confidence.
3. **This corpus measures first impressions, not retention.** The median body is 140 characters; 25 of 91 bodies (27.47%) are 60 characters or shorter; two reviewers state they had only just downloaded the app (`14385677429`, `14424966134`). **Nobody in this corpus has used the product for more than 87 days, and most have used it for days.** Every praise theme here should be read as "what makes people adopt", not "what makes people stay".
4. **The absence of negative reviews is a finding about the corpus, not a proof of product quality.** A 97.80% five-star rate in a habit-tracker corpus is an extreme outlier — comparable App Store habit-tracker corpora in this dataset run 60–75% five-star. The most likely explanations are (a) the app genuinely removes the single biggest source of anger in this category, the paywall (§3.1), and (b) the app is very young, so nobody has yet hit a renewal, a data loss, or a year boundary. **Both can be true. Neither is evidence that no defects exist** — 14 of 91 reviewers (15.38%) volunteered a request or a defect *inside a five-star review*, and those are the only quality signal this corpus contains (§3.4).
5. **No review burst, no vote manipulation, and no evidence of solicited reviews was found.** Maximum reviews on any single day is 4 (four days reach it: 2026-06-29 is 3; 2026-07-09, 2026-07-14, 2026-07-25 and 2026-09-01 reach 4); 91 reviews spread over 56 distinct days. **Every record in the corpus has `vote_count` = 0 and `vote_sum` = 0, and `is_edited` is false on all 91** — consistent with a listing too new for anyone to have voted on a review. No reviewer mentions being asked for a review. **Interpretation: the corpus is organically distributed. The positivity is not manufactured — but see warning 3 for why it is also not durable evidence.**
6. **Creator-led acquisition is visible in the text and is a confound.** **8 reviewers (8.79%, high-priority) state they found the app through TikTok**, naming the creator directly. An audience arriving from a creator's own video is pre-disposed to rate favourably and is not a representative sample of App Store search traffic. **Interpretation: part of the 4.956 mean is audience selection, not product quality.**
7. **Only one storefront clears the 50-review bar — and it does not.** The United States is the largest storefront at **n = 26 (28.57%)**, still half the 50-review threshold. **Therefore this report makes no standalone country claim at all.** Every storefront section in Part 6 is explicitly labelled limited evidence, and country-level percentages are shown for shape only.
8. **Classification was hand-curated, not automated.** All 91 records were read individually and in full, in their original language (English, plus Spanish `14262715380`, Taglish `14276142725` / `14342658101`). 52 themes, every one a hand-assigned ID list. No regex or clustering assigned any theme; keyword searches were used afterwards only as an audit of the hand map, and the three discrepancies found are documented at §9.1. The map is saved at `temp/58-review-classification.py` and validated programmatically (§1.5).
9. **External verification is weak and is used only where labelled.** The App Store listing (`apps.apple.com`) and the developer's site (`fernhabit.com`) are both unreachable from this environment (egress-blocked); only search-engine summaries were obtainable. They **corroborate** the review-derived model — free, unlimited habits, no subscription, no ads, no paywall — but they are summaries, not primary sources. **No finding in Parts 3–8 rests on an external source.** See §2.3 and §9.5.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. "It is free and it does not cap my habits" is not a feature of this product — it is the product.**
**52 of 91 reviewers (57.14%, high-priority, mean 4.962) praise the monetization model itself** — the app being free (42, 46.15%), having no habit cap or premium gating (14, 15.38%), carrying no ads (8, 8.79%), or requiring no earned points (1). That is a larger share of the corpus than praise for simplicity, ease of use or design. *"Finding a app that you don't have to pay for is like finding diamonds"* (`14417648063`, CA); *"For this app to be free with everything you get feels like a crime"* (`14393257431`, US); *"No hidden payments or subscriptions"* (`14507313209`, GB). **Interpretation: the pricing decision is doing the work that features normally do. Every other finding in this report is downstream of it.**

**2. Six reviewers name the competitive wound precisely: rival habit trackers charge for the act of tracking habits.**
**6 reviews (6.59%, high-priority, mean 5.000) explicitly attack competitors' paywalls**, and two of them name the exact mechanic. *"Finally a habit tracker app where you can add more than 5 habits for free!"* (`14305681444`, HR — a competitor's 5-habit free cap); *"all the other apps like tell me I have to pay to put more things on the checklist"* (`14300463758`, SA); *"when we normalised that a habit tracker app, doesn't allow us to track habits? Fern demolishes this tendency"* (`14166656933`, UY). **Interpretation: this is a positioning bullseye, and it is stated in the reviewers' own words, unprompted, from four continents. The category has trained users to expect a cap, and removing it reads as generosity rather than as a missing feature.**

**3. The displacement evidence is the strongest quality signal in the corpus, because it is comparative rather than absolute.**
**35 reviewers (38.46%, high-priority, mean 5.000) position this app against alternatives they personally tried** — 24 (26.37%) call it the best habit tracker they have used, 8 (8.79%) say rivals were overwhelming or complicated, 6 (6.59%) say rivals were paywalled, and 5 (5.49%) describe a long prior search. *"I have spent the last 3 years trying out all the different habit tracking apps but was never satisfied"* (`14423852732`, ZA); *"I've tried many"* (`14333652382`, US); *"I used finch before and it was really great when I was younger, but now it was just overwhelming"* (`14385677429`, DE — the only competitor named by name). **Interpretation: praise from a first-time tracker user is weak evidence. Praise from someone who has churned through the category is strong evidence, and a quarter of this corpus is the latter.**

**4. There is no monetization to analyse — and that is the report's central business risk.**
**Zero reviewers state they paid, subscribed, started a trial, renewed, or requested a refund.** A keyword audit for purchase language across all 91 records returns 6 hits, and **every one of them is about the app *not* charging** (`14245487733`, `14342658101`, `14355206933`, `14360994698`, `14401530018`, `14507313209`). The only payment surface any reviewer mentions is a **voluntary tip**: *"there are no ads! But you can leave a tip, which is very nice ✨"* (`14277295245`, DE, 1 review, 1.10%). **Interpretation: the corpus contains complete evidence of product-market fit and zero evidence of revenue. The evidence that the model works as acquisition is overwhelming; the evidence that it can be converted is absent. This is the single most important gap in the dataset, not a finding that revenue is impossible.**

**5. Users are pre-emptively defending the free model, which tells you the conversion window is narrow and emotionally charged.**
**8 reviewers (8.79%, high-priority) give a forward-looking monetization signal**: 4 (4.40%) plead that the app stay free, 2 (2.20%) offer or urge voluntary support, and 4 (4.40%) self-identify as budget-constrained. *"Please dont put any subscription on it. This really helped my ADHD"* (`14342658101`, PH); *"I wish and hope and pray it will continue to be free and to those who can, please support the developers!"* (`14410658467`, PH — the same reviewer does both); *"please be free forever 🙏"* (`14510494540`, PH); *"I appreciate this app an hope that it will continue its 'legacy' by staying ✨FREE🥲"* (`14305573901`, SA). **Interpretation: `14410658467` is the template the evidence supports — voluntary support framed as patronage of a creator, not a paywall. Introducing a habit cap on this user base would invert the single theme that produced 57% of the corpus's praise.**

**6. Simplicity is the second product, and it is defined negatively — by what the app refuses to do.**
**58 reviewers (63.74%, high-priority, mean 5.000) praise the UX**: simplicity (35, 38.46%), ease of use (30, 32.97%), aesthetic design (28, 30.77%). The phrasing is consistently about restraint: *"not overcrowded and overwhelming"* (`14324333814`, DE); *"doesn't have all the complicated tracking mechanics other apps have to get all caught up in"* (`14467544215`, US); *"no visual noise"* (`14166656933`, UY); *"Simple yet effective with an aesthetic finish"* (`14308111831`, IE). **Interpretation: every feature request in Part 3.4 must be weighed against this. The corpus's largest praise theme is the absence of features.**

**7. The ADHD and neurodivergent segment is self-identifying, unprompted, and unanimous.**
**8 reviewers (8.79%, high-priority, mean 5.000) describe themselves as ADHD or neurodivergent**, and 5 of the 8 tie their praise directly to simplicity. *"ADHD HEAVEN … Not overwhelming or complicated. Focused and easy. Doesn't feel like a chore to set up or use. I love it, my brain is happy"* (`14323412379`, ZA); *"Having a neurodivergent brain makes it so hard to start new habits. Setting up is simple. No extras"* (`14285772400`, US); *"i'm neurodivergent and this app is an absolute blast … also THE WIDGET that is clickable?? the best ever"* (`14361894176`, UA); *"Helped SO much with my ADHD, helped me get back on track"* (`14445278992`, US). **Note that the app's own store positioning does not appear to target ADHD** (§2.3) — **this segment found the product on its own. Interpretation: an under-claimed, high-conviction audience.**

**8. TikTok is a named, measurable acquisition channel — and the only one anyone names.**
**8 reviewers (8.79%, high-priority, mean 5.000) attribute discovery to TikTok**, several to the creator personally: *"I found them on tiktok and immediately had to download"* (`14217435167`, AU); *"I saw the creator talking about her app on TikTok and I'm so happy!!"* (`14328125592`, CA); *"thanks to that tik tok girl i forgot her name lol"* (`14309581453`, JO); *"if the creator of this app wants to know. I heard of it from TikTok when you advertised it"* (`14305573901`, SA). **No reviewer names App Store search, a friend, an ad, or any other channel.** One reviewer notes the listing's thinness: *"I can't believe ang onti ng reviews pero ang ganda ng app!"* / "I can't believe how few reviews there are but how beautiful the app is" (`14276142725`, PH). **Interpretation: acquisition is single-channel and founder-dependent. That is a concentration risk and, separately, the confound in warning 6.**

**9. The entire defect and unmet-need backlog is 14 reviews, and it fits on one page.**
**14 of 91 reviewers (15.38%, high-priority, mean 4.714) volunteered a request or a defect — all but two inside a five-star review.** In descending order: more icons/emojis (3, 3.30%), more frequency options (3, 3.30% — times-per-day, biweekly, monthly), more colours/themes (2, 2.20%), cross-device sync (2, 2.20%), and one each for habit categories, specific-weekday scheduling, a custom reward system, celebration feedback, mood/journal logging, widget polish, a progress-display tweak, and social/friends. **One functional defect is reported in the whole corpus**: widget habit icons rendering as white boxes in clear mode (`14468763471`, US, 5★). **Interpretation: this is a roadmap, not a bug list. The fact that a full-corpus defect sweep returns one rendering bug is itself the most positive reliability signal available — and also a direct consequence of warning 3.**

**10. Two reviewers point at the same structural risk, and it arrives on 1 January 2027.**
`14483338239` (GB, 5★): *"I'm also wondering if we can view habit consistency from previous years I noticed it only shows 2026 so I'm a bit nervous for when we go into the new year I hope all of my 2026 is not removed but time will tell!"* Against that, **5 reviewers (5.49%, high-priority) chose this app specifically for its unrestricted history**: *"you can see all of your history not just a month back"* (`14360994698`, US); *"see progress through a year"* (`14331162486`, CA); *"look back on my weeks and months that I have tracked"* (`14333652382`, US). **Interpretation: the corpus ends four months before the app's first year boundary. If year-rollover behaviour is wrong or merely unclear, it will break the promise that five reviewers say they adopted the product for — and the first evidence of it will arrive in a review corpus, not in a bug report.**

**11. Both three-star reviews are requests, not complaints — and both come from the same storefront.**
The only two non-five-star reviews in the corpus are Saudi Arabian: `14348044475` (25 Jul) asks for more colours, more emojis and a times-per-day frequency — *"there isn't like enough colors and like I feel like the app is depressed"* — while still saying *"overall it's free and you can customize and stuff but I'll keep the app as a daily use"*; and `14502988614` (2 Sep) asks for one thing only — *"I wish that we can connect this great habit tracker to another device so I can work in two different places"*. **Interpretation: the corpus's entire downside consists of two people asking for more product. Note that SA's mean (4.200, n=5) is the only storefront below 5.000, and that this is 2 reviews — it is a coincidence of a tiny sample, not a market finding (§6.8).**

**12. The cheapest high-value moves, in evidence order:** confirm and publicise year-rollover behaviour before 1 January 2027 (finding 10; 1 explicit worry against 5 reviewers who adopted for unlimited history) → decide the revenue model now, while goodwill is at its peak, and model it on `14410658467`'s patronage framing rather than a cap (findings 4–5; 8 reviewers) → ship the icon/emoji and colour/theme expansion, which is the only request with repeat demand and is the one thing both 3★ reviewers ask for (`14483065068`, `14348044475`, `14389332832`) → add per-weekday and times-per-day frequency (`14281923322`, `14348044475`, `14431546099`, `14333652382`) → fix the clear-mode widget icon rendering (`14468763471`) → scope cross-device sync, the only request that is structural rather than cosmetic (`14502988614`, `14300350854`) → diversify acquisition beyond TikTok (finding 8) → claim the ADHD/neurodivergent positioning the corpus already gave you for free (finding 7).

## 1.2 Schema

Fields present on every one of the 91 records: `review_id`, `app_id`, `app_name`, `country`, `country_name`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `author`, `date`, `vote_count`, `vote_sum`, `is_edited`.

**Fields used in this analysis:** `review_id`, `country`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `date`.
**Fields inspected and set aside:** `author` (not analysed; no reviewer is named in this report), `app_id` / `app_name` / `country_name` (constant or derivable from `country`).
**Fields inspected and used only as integrity checks:**
- `vote_count` / `vote_sum` — **zero on all 91 records.** No review in this corpus has ever been voted on. This is consistent with a listing under three months old and means vote data cannot be used to detect inflated clusters; the burst checks in warning 5 rely on date distribution alone.
- `is_edited` — **false on all 91 records.** No review has been revised, so no reviewer's opinion is observed changing over time.

**Text handling:** HTML entities (`&#39;`, `&#34;`, `&amp;`) are present in the raw `title` and `body` fields and were unescaped before reading and classification. This affects string rendering only. Body length: median **140** characters, mean 172.6, min **2** (`14200381141`, PH — body is two emoji; the signal is entirely in its title, *"I love this so muchhhhh, as a broke student !!!"*), max **1030** (`14389332832`, CA, a single-paragraph theme-design wishlist).

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Parse and reconcile.** All 91 records parsed from `reviews.jsonl`; reconciled against `by_country/*.jsonl`, `manifest.json` and `_state.json` (§1.3). Script: `temp/58-reconcile.py`.
2. **Full-text dump.** Every record written out in full, HTML-unescaped, sorted by storefront then date, with ID, country, rating, date, vote counts and edit flag. Script: `temp/58-dump.py` → `temp/58-all-reviews.txt`.
3. **Individual reading.** All 91 reviews read one by one, in original language. No sampling, no keyword filtering, no truncation, no stopping at a context limit. Non-English and mixed-language records were read in the original: `14262715380` (Spanish, MX), `14276142725` and `14342658101` (Taglish, PH). Translations in this report are the analyst's and are marked with `/`.
4. **Hand-curated classification.** A theme → review-ID map built by hand, **52 themes**, saved at `temp/58-review-classification.py`. No regex or automated clustering was used to assign any theme.
5. **Programmatic validation** (`temp/58-validate.py`), run to completion before any number in this report was written:

| Validation check | Result |
|---|---|
| Review IDs in the map that do not exist in `reviews.jsonl` | **0 ✓** |
| Duplicate IDs within any single theme | **0 ✓** |
| Records in the corpus with no theme assigned | **0 ✓** |
| Themes with an empty ID list | **0 ✓** |
| Records carrying ≥1 theme | **91 / 91 ✓** |
| Themes per review | min 1 · max 10 · mean 3.40 |

6. **Keyword audit of the hand map.** After classification, keyword searches were run *against* the hand map to find disagreements — not to build it. Three were found and each was adjudicated by re-reading the record; all three are documented at §9.1 (two were regex false positives, one was a genuine boundary correction that was applied).
7. **Aggregation.** All counts, percentages, means, rating splits, country cuts, month/half-corpus buckets and family unions computed from the validated map (`temp/58-stats.py`, `temp/58-stats2.py`, `temp/58-stats3.py`). Appendix tables §9.2, §9.3 and §9.6 are machine-generated from the same map (`temp/58-appendix.py`), so **no review ID in this report was transcribed by hand**.

**Known error risk.** The judgement calls with the widest error bars are: (a) `simplicity` vs `ease_of_use` — the two overlap heavily and are reported both separately and as a union with `aesthetic_design` (§3.3), so no finding depends on the split; (b) `free_praise` vs `no_paywall_limits` — praising "free" and praising "no cap" are distinct claims but many reviewers make both in one sentence, and the boundary rule is at §9.1; (c) `comparative_best` requires the reviewer to reference alternatives, so a bare *"Best habit app ever!!!"* (`14241077207`) is included while a bare *"Amazing!!!"* (`14450651496`) is not — this is the most contestable boundary in the map and it is enumerated by ID at §9.3.

## 1.6 Limitations and known biases

- **Written reviews are not users.** 91 written reviews from a 3-month-old listing are a small, self-selected slice. Nothing here estimates install base, retention or conversion.
- **This is a launch corpus.** Warning 3. The oldest possible tenure in this corpus is 87 days. No statement about retention, renewal, long-term data integrity or year-two behaviour can be supported.
- **The rating distribution has two occupied buckets.** Warning 1. Part 4 is presented as a structural observation rather than a conventional ratings analysis, because 89/91 records carry the same value.
- **Creator-channel selection bias.** Warning 6. At least 8.79% of the corpus arrived from the founder's own TikTok audience; the true share is probably higher, since attribution is only captured when a reviewer volunteers it.
- **No storefront reaches n = 50.** Warning 7. Part 6 makes no standalone country claim.
- **No version field exists in this schema**, so no finding can be tied to an app version, and no reviewer in this corpus references an update or a regression.
- **Vote and edit fields are uniformly empty**, so two of the usual integrity checks (vote-inflated clusters, opinion revision) are unavailable.
- **External verification is summary-only** (warning 9). §2.3 corroborates but does not confirm.
- **One review is 1.10%.** Every "meaningful" label attached to a single-review theme in §9.2 should be read as "one person said this", and each is enumerated by ID.

# PART 7 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 7.1 Method, and what it cannot do

**The corpus spans 87 days.** That is too short for launch-vs-mature comparison, too short for seasonality, and too short to observe any release cycle (no `version` field exists, and no reviewer mentions an update).

Two documented cuts are used, and **neither is treated as a trend on its own**:
1. **Calendar month** — 2026-06 (n = 12, partial), 2026-07 (n = 41), 2026-08 (n = 31), 2026-09 (n = 7, partial). Two of the four buckets are partial months, so month-over-month rate comparison is unsound and is used only for stability checks.
2. **Corpus halves by date** — first 45 records (10 Jun → 25 Jul) vs last 46 (25 Jul → 4 Sep). **This is the primary cut**, because it splits the corpus into two equal-sized, contiguous windows of 45 and 41 days.

**Confidence labels used below:** *high* = the direction holds in both cuts and the counts are ≥10 on each side; *medium* = direction holds but one side is thin; *low* = observed, reported, not relied upon.

## 7.2 Trend 1 — The monetization theme is flat, not decaying · **persistent, high confidence**

| Window | `free_praise` | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| First half (45) | 21 | 46.7% |
| Second half (46) | 21 | 45.7% |
| 2026-06 | 6/12 | 50% |
| 2026-07 | 19/41 | 46% |
| 2026-08 | 13/31 | 42% |
| 2026-09 | 4/7 | 57% |

**Fact:** the corpus's largest theme is within 1 percentage point across the two halves and within 8 points across all four months.
**Interpretation:** "free" is not a launch-novelty talking point that fades as word-of-mouth broadens the audience. Three months in, it is still the first thing new reviewers write about. **This is the strongest stability result in the corpus.**

## 7.3 Trend 2 — Comparative/displacement language more than halved · **changing, medium confidence**

| Window | `comparative_best` | Rate |
|---|---|---|
| First half (45) | 17 | 37.8% |
| Second half (46) | 7 | 15.2% |

| 2026-08 | 5/31 | 16% |

**Fact:** the decline is monotonic across all four months and holds in the half-split. **No review in either half ranks the app below a competitor** — the theme is not inverting, it is disappearing.
**Interpretation (medium confidence): this is a change in who is reviewing, not in what the product is.** Early reviewers were category shoppers who arrived after actively evaluating alternatives, and they compare. Later reviewers increasingly arrive without that frame and review the app on its own terms. **If correct, this predicts that displacement language will keep falling as the audience broadens, and that it should not be read as competitive erosion.** The corpus cannot confirm the mechanism; it can only show that the rate fell while satisfaction did not.

## 7.4 Trend 3 — Feature requests roughly doubled · **emerging, medium confidence**

|---|---|---|

**Interpretation (medium confidence — 4 vs 9 is a small absolute change):** this is the ordinary maturation pattern. As tenure accumulates, users move from "does this exist" to "does this go deep enough". **It is also the leading indicator worth monitoring**: if the trajectory continues, the next 90 days should produce the corpus's first sub-5★ reviews that are genuinely complaints rather than requests.

`aesthetic_design` runs 16/45 (35.6%) in the first half against 12/46 (26.1%) in the second. **A 9-point move on 28 records is well inside sampling noise at this corpus size, and no mechanism is proposed.** Recorded so a future analyst with more data can check it.

`tiktok_discovery` runs 6/45 (13.3%) in the first half against 2/46 (4.3%) in the second. **Two readings are equally consistent with 8 records:** (a) the acquisition mix is genuinely diversifying away from the founder's TikTok, or (b) the novelty of naming the channel wears off as the app becomes established. **The corpus cannot distinguish them.** This is worth instrumenting outside the review corpus, because executive finding 8 depends on it.

First half mean **5.000** (45/45 five-star); second half mean **4.913** (44/46 five-star). Weekly means are 5.00 in every week except 20–26 July (4.86) and 31 Aug–6 Sep (4.75), each containing one 3★ review.
**Fact:** the rating distribution is stable and there is no deterioration to explain.
**Interpretation:** combined with §7.4, the honest summary of this corpus's time dimension is **nothing has gone wrong yet** — which is the expected state at 87 days and is not evidence that nothing will.

## 7.8 What the corpus cannot tell you about time

- **Retention.** No reviewer describes returning after a lapse, and none describes churn.
- **Release impact.** No version field, no reviewer mentions an update, and no theme shifts around any identifiable date.
- **Seasonality.** Habit-tracker corpora are January-dominated; this corpus contains no January.
- **The year boundary.** The corpus ends **118 days before 1 January 2027**, the date `14483338239` is worried about (§3.4). **The single most consequential time-based question raised by this corpus falls outside the corpus.**

## 8.2 Structural decisions — direction is clear, magnitude is not

**8.2.1 Decide the revenue model now, while goodwill is at its maximum — and do not use a habit cap.**

The corpus is unusually clear about shape and silent about magnitude (§5.4).

- **What the evidence rules out:** a free-tier habit cap. **15 reviewers say a cap or a paywall is what they left another app over** (`no_paywall_limits` ∪ `competitor_paywall_frustration`), and 52 (57.14%) praise the absence of charging. A cap would invert the corpus's largest theme family into its opposite.
- **What the evidence rules out:** advertising. 8 reviewers (8.79%) explicitly praise its absence; none tolerate it.
- **What the evidence supports:** patronage. `14410658467` (PH) holds both positions in one sentence — *"I wish and hope and pray it will continue to be free and to those who can, please support the developers!"* — and `14277295245` (DE) confirms a tip already ships and is received warmly. **The framing that works is "support the creator", not "unlock the feature".**
- **What the evidence permits, with care:** paid cosmetic depth. 5 reviewers already ask for theme/icon content that is not core tracking (§8.1 item 3). **The hazard is that `14348044475` (3★) already treats the current customization set as a shortfall** — selling what a 3★ reviewer says is missing would read as a paywall on a deficiency, not as a premium extra. **Ship the baseline expansion free first; only then consider paid packs on top.**
- **What the evidence cannot tell you:** any price, any conversion rate, any revenue figure. **No review names a price for this app.** Nothing in §5 should be turned into a number.

**8.2.2 Cross-device sync is the one structural request that changes the product's risk profile.**

2 reviewers ask for it (`14502988614` — a **3★** whose entire review is this request; `14300350854`). It is also the most expensive item in the report: sync requires accounts and servers, and **2 reviewers chose this product partly because it has neither** — *"you don't have to sign in with an email or create an account. It makes it quick and easy to start using"* (`14215578061`, NO); *"I love that you don't need to make an account to use it!"* (`14333652382`, US).

**Interpretation:** this is a genuine 2-vs-2 tension, not a backlog item. **If sync ships, it must be optional and must not become a precondition for first use** — and note (§4.5) that accounts and servers are exactly the machinery that generates the 1★ classes this corpus is currently free of.

**8.2.3 Claim the ADHD / neurodivergent positioning the corpus has already given you.**

8 reviewers (8.79%) self-identify unprompted, across 6 storefronts, and 5 tie their praise directly to the app's restraint (§3.3.6). **No external summary of the store listing mentions ADHD** (§2.3). Competing trackers in this category market to this audience explicitly. **The evidence for the positioning is stronger than the evidence most apps have when they adopt it — and it was produced by users, not by marketing.** The caution is `14406981588` (GB), the one segment member who wants *more* stimulation, not less (§8.3.1).

**8.2.4 Diversify acquisition beyond the founder's TikTok.**

8 reviewers (8.79%) name TikTok; **no reviewer names any other channel** — not App Store search, not a friend, not an ad. `14276142725` (PH) notes the listing's thinness directly: *"I can't believe ang onti ng reviews pero ang ganda ng app!"* / "I can't believe how few reviews there are but how beautiful the app is". **Interpretation: acquisition is single-channel and founder-dependent, which is both a business risk and the confound behind the 4.956 mean (warning 6).** §7.6 shows TikTok attribution falling between the corpus halves, but on 8 records that cannot distinguish diversification from attribution fatigue — instrument it outside the review corpus.

## 8.3 Experiments worth running

**8.3.1 The celebration tension.** 7 of 8 neurodivergent reviewers praise restraint; `14406981588` (GB) asks for *"bit more of celebration for those who like dopamine hits due to neurodivergent"*, and `14355206933` (US) asks for a points-and-rewards layer. **Do not resolve this by argument — make it optional and measure it.** An off-by-default celebration setting satisfies both sides and tests whether the 35-reviewer simplicity theme is about the default or about the ceiling.

**8.3.2 Habit categories vs. simplicity.** `14483338239` (GB) asks for user-defined sections. This is the request most likely to threaten §3.3.3 — grouping adds a navigation layer to an app praised for having none. **Test it as an opt-in that is invisible until a user creates the first section.**

**8.3.3 Does the "free" message still convert once it is not novel?** `free_praise` is flat across the corpus halves (§7.2), which suggests it does. **But every reviewer in this corpus arrived while the app was new.** Worth a listing-copy test that leads with simplicity instead of price in one market — the US subset already leads with simplicity unprompted (§6.2).

**8.3.4 Ask for reviews, carefully.** This corpus shows **no evidence of a solicited-review prompt** (warning 5), and it also shows an unusually low contentless-praise rate (3.30%, §4.2). That is a rare, clean evidence base. **If a prompt is ever added, gate it behind a real usage milestone** — the corpus's quality as a product-discovery instrument is itself an asset, and prompt-driven corpora lose it.

## 8.4 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

1. **Retention.** Nothing here distinguishes a user who tracked for 80 days from one who tracked for 3. Needs product analytics.
2. **Conversion and willingness to pay in currency.** §5 establishes shape only. Needs a priced test.
3. **Does the free model actually acquire, or does the creator?** Warning 6 and §8.2.4. Needs channel attribution outside reviews.
4. **Is the US "simplicity over price" divergence real?** §6.2, §6.12 — the largest composition difference in the corpus, on 26 records. Needs n ≥ 50 per storefront.
5. **What happens at the year boundary?** §8.1 item 1. **Answerable by testing the app, today, without any new data.**
6. **Why did 37 storefronts return zero reviews?** Distribution, localisation, or simple youth — the corpus cannot say.

## 8.5 What not to change

- **Do not add a habit cap.** §8.2.1. 15 reviewers left another app over exactly this.
- **Do not add ads.** 8 reviewers praise their absence; none would tolerate them.
- **Do not require an account to start.** 2 reviewers named it as an adoption reason (§8.2.2).
- **Do not add structure to the main surface.** 35 reviewers praise simplicity and 8 explicitly contrast it with rivals' complexity. Every addition in §8.1 and §8.3 is deliberately either cosmetic or opt-in for this reason.
- **Do not read 4.956 as a quality score.** Warnings 1, 3 and 6. The correct reading is "no one has hit a failure mode yet, and the audience is partly self-selected".
