## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label and review IDs**. - Theme counts are **non-exclusive**: one review can carry several themes.
- Signal thresholds: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority.

### ⚠️ Seven warnings before any number here is used

1. **The corpus is small and saturated with 5★.** 591 of 743 reviews (79.5%) are 5★. Only **48 reviews (6.5%) are 1–2★**. Every negative theme therefore rests on small counts. Read the IDs, not just the percentages.
2. **The signal thresholds are easy to clear at this size.** One review is 0.13% of 743 (already "weak"); four reviews are 0.54% ("emerging"). In the US (N = 265), three reviews are 1.13% ("meaningful"). The labels are applied as the prompt requires, but **treat any theme with n < 5 as anecdotal**, whatever its label.
3. **Some high ratings were probably promotion-driven.** Between **28 and 30 June 2023, 27 reviews arrived in three days** from 11 storefronts (the corpus averages about one review every four days). Their mean is 4.81, 25 of them are 5★, and **55.6% have a body of 25 characters or fewer**. Four mention the app being free: "Freeeee" (`10083799946`), "This App free" (`10085535827`), "It let's me add as much habits as I want" (`10083139099`), and "Scam of free life time subscription" (`10087293260`). A later Chinese review asks 啥时候还有限免呐？ *"When will there be another limited-time free offer?"* (`12597410321`). **Interpretation:** a limited-time free-lifetime promotion ran around 29 June 2023. Without these 27 reviews the corpus mean is 4.571.
4. **The app asks for a rating in-app**, sometimes early: "you want me to rate it before I even finish setting it up" (`13586879451`), "App asks for 5 stars review" (`9821511278`), "lovely review pop up" (`9613494929`). Prompted reviews skew positive and short.
5. **No `version` field exists.** Release events come only from what reviewers write (e.g. "the 2024.4 update", `11147450348`; "after the April 10th 2024 update", `11145404810`). Dates of feature launches are bracketed by the first and last reviews that mention them.
6. **"Payer" evidence is self-selected.** 51 reviewers (6.86%) say in the first person that they paid, subscribed or own a lifetime licence. They are not a sample of customers, and no conversion rate can be derived.
7. **Single-rater coding.** One analyst read and coded all 743 reviews against a codebook of 95 hand-applied codes, one regex-derived code (`KEVIN`) and 10 derived unions (Part 11.2). The keyword recall sweep corrected 10 codes (Part 11.3). Borderline calls are disclosed; no second rater validated them.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. The product wins on one idea: see a whole year of a habit at a glance, with almost no effort.**
**Core praise appears in 507 reviews (68.24%, high-priority), mean 4.87.** What is praised:
- **Simplicity**, 275 (37.01%; 273 of them rated 4–5★; zero 1★);
- **Design**, 166 (22.34%);
- **"Best / the only one that stuck"**, 107 (14.40%, no 1★);
- **The year grid / month calendar / "GitHub-like" heatmap**, 105 (14.13%, no 1★) — the one feature reviewers say rivals lack: "the only habit tracker I could find with a whole year view" (`10791779600`); "I have never seen another app doing this" (`9730846797`); "The year in pixels completely sold me" (`9667437355`); "the dev is a fan of the GitHub commit tracker because that's basically what I was looking for" (`14284351778`).
- **This app is where people land after trying others:** 98 reviews (13.19%) name a competitor or say they tried many; only one of them is 1★. "I downloaded all the habit trackers, this is the only one I kept" (`10184986574`); "downloaded like 30 different apps" (`13649480321`); "tried over a dozen apps and always come back to this one" (`6713570145`).

**2. The 2-habit free tier is the main cause of low ratings.**
**Monetisation friction: 78 reviews (10.50%, high-priority), mean 2.68. It is in 28 of the 37 1★ reviews (75.7%) and 9 of the 11 2★ reviews.** Within it:
- **The habit cap: 39 (5.25%, high-priority), mean 2.44; 15 of them 1★.** "Only 2 habits for free" (`9839460397`); "Two is really stingy — with three I'd have kept it for decency; with five I'd have started using it, got hooked, and then bought" (`9899413299`, Russian, translated); "Other apps usually have 5 free habits" (`10904298957`).
- **Other gated features: 11 (1.48%), mean 1.55 — the worst-rated theme with n ≥ 5.** Widgets (`12760639157` "Cant use widgets without subscription"), notes (`13605658579`), colours (`13629525960`), backup (`12046497872`).
- **Friction rose sharply after 2024:** monetisation friction was 5.7% of E1 and 8.4% of E2, then **16.4% of E4 (2024) and 14.3% of E5 (2025–26)**. Cap complaints went from 2.8% of E2 to **9.7% of E4**.

**3. The objection is mostly about *evaluation and disclosure*, not only the price level.** Reviewers want to judge the app before paying:
- **not told about the limit before downloading** (3): "Waits until I made two habits to tell me I needed to pay" (`11348148556`); "only to find out AFTER downloading the app that there is a fee" (`12233797625`);
- **no trial or too short** (7): "I refuse to pay the min. $9 just to find out if I like it" (`11939700468`); "the free trial lasts only 3 days" (`12738794859`);
- **price level** (25, mean 3.16): "$40 … An indie game developed over years … will cost 5 to 25 dollars" (`11554013205`).

**4. Ratings are drifting down, slowly.** Era means: **4.71 (E2) → 4.63 (E3) → 4.51 (E4) → 4.40 (E5)**. The 1–2★ share rose from 3.3% of E3 to **7.5% of E4 and 11.0% of E5 (17 of 154)**. 2026 so far: mean 4.26, 15.4% 1–2★ (n = 39, small). The rise is driven by the paywall (above), not by reliability.

**5. Widgets were the #1 request for four years; shipping them created the top new praise, and a new ask.**
- **Widget requests: 30 (4.04%, very strong)**, peaking at **11.1% of E3 (Jun 2023 – Mar 2024)**: "I'll take one star off for a widget … I promise … to put it back" (`10552434405`).
- **Widgets shipped around mid-March 2024** ("Thanks to the devs for adding the widgets!", `11062493236`). **Widget praise: 24 (3.23%), 11.2% of E4.** "I use the widget religiously" (`12172218323`).
- **The new ask is interactive widgets: 7 (0.94%), all after the launch** — "Los widgets siguen sin ser interactivos" *"The widgets still aren't interactive"* (`13731564617`, Feb 2026). And widgets are **behind the paywall** for some users (`12760639157`, `12738794859`).

**6. 2025–26 brought trust events that are rare but high-consequence.** Each rests on one or two reviews:
- **CSV export disabled:** "Out of nowhere, the developer decided to disable CSV exports … years of data are stuck in this app" (`13731751322`, 1★, Feb 2026);
- **Backup required (and paid) before deleting:** `12046497872` (1★);
- **Lifetime option removed or not honoured:** "After updating the app this option is gone" (`13392990758`); "After a year or two, your 'lifetime' subscription wont be working anymore" (`14138883204`);
- **Reminder time picker broken** for a paying user (`13505537432`, Dec 2025; `13868583107`, Mar 2026).

**7. Paying users are satisfied.** **51 explicit payers (6.86%), mean 4.63, 42 of them 5★; only 3 rate 1–2★.** They buy **after the 2 free habits prove the method** ("purely because it has been so effective in reinforcing the two habits … and I needed to add more", `13242549122`), because a **lifetime / one-time option exists** (`11286612018`, `11583264179`), and **to support a solo developer** (`4497735688`, `11737212105`, `13919822858`). What goes wrong after paying is functional (reminders, a widget, the lifetime entitlement), not price.

**8. The developer relationship is an asset, and it is thinning.** **Developer praise: 52 reviews (7.00%), mean 4.96.** Reviewers report features built on request ("He listened to the community and implemented weekly habits", `3954949955`; "has even implemented one of mine", `8400091910`, German) and an in-app "in progress" roadmap (`13247369835`, `13882752696`). Its share fell from **13.2% of E1 to 5.2% of E5**. One reviewer reports no email reply (`10904298957`).

**9. United States (265 reviews, mean 4.577) is the comparison-shopping market.** Relative to the other 478 reviews, US reviewers are **twice as likely to name competitors (19.62% vs 9.62%)**, more likely to say "best" (18.87% vs 11.92%), to be explicit payers (9.06% vs 5.65%) and to praise the price (6.79% vs 3.35%). They report **less monetisation friction (7.92% vs 11.92%)**, but more **explicit churn (9 reviews, 3.40%, vs 1.05%)**.

**10. Cheapest wins, in evidence order:**
1. State the free limit on the store page and in onboarding, and delay the rating prompt until after setup.
2. Let people evaluate before paying: a real trial, or 3–5 free habits.
3. Never gate data exit: restore CSV export for everyone and make backup/delete free.
4. Honour and publish what "lifetime" includes; say clearly which plans are sold.
5. Fix the reminder time picker and the widget rendering bugs.
6. Ship interactive widgets and quantity (partial-completion) habits.

## 1.2 Schema

Every record carries: `review_id`, `app_id`, `app_name`, `country`, `country_name`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `author`, `date` (ISO 8601 UTC), `vote_count`, `vote_sum`, `is_edited`. There are no nulls.

- **Fields ignored:** `author` (personal data, no analytical value); `app_id` and `app_name` (constant).
- **Votes are too sparse to weight.** `vote_count` is non-zero on 31 records. The most-voted is `4310058011` (8 votes, 5★, US, 2019 — a diabetic reviewer's weight-loss story); no other review has more than 2.

**Every one of the 743 records was read in full**, in date order, in 6 batches of about 125 reviews. Nothing was sampled or keyword-selected. Every language was read and translated by the analyst: English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Dutch, Swedish, Danish, Polish, Russian, Ukrainian, Chinese (simplified and traditional) and Korean. One-word reviews, emoji-only bodies and gibberish were read, coded (`GEN+`, `GEN0`, `JUNK`) and counted.

## 1.4 Processing method

1. **Parse and clean.** Parse all 743 records programmatically; double HTML-unescape titles and bodies; collapse whitespace.
2. **Read and hand-code everything.** Emit the corpus as 6 readable batches (`id|country|rating|date|edited|title :: body`, date order). For each review, assign **one or more codes** from a codebook built inductively while reading (Part 11.2).
3. **Verify coverage.** A script checked that every `review_id` received codes exactly once and that every code is in the codebook. First run: 743/743, no errors.
4. **Recall sweep.** 29 keyword patterns (English plus Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Russian, Ukrainian and Chinese forms) listed every review that matched a theme's vocabulary but did not carry that theme. **All candidates were read.** Most were already correct ("add" is not ADHD; "year" and "subscription" often appear in praise). **10 corrections were made** (Part 11.3). One objective code (`KEVIN`, the reviewer names the developer) was derived by regex.
5. **Aggregate.** Counts, shares, means and rating profiles by theme, star band, year, era, US vs non-US, payer segment and event windows (the June 2023 burst).

## 1.5 Known limitations

- **One analyst, no second rater.** Borderline calls are listed in Part 11.4.
- **Rating is not a feature preference.** 5 reviews carry a star rating that contradicts their text (`MISRATE`), e.g. 5★ "Lost all data" (`4289434524`), 5★ "Greedy developer" (`9342516160`), 5★ "doesn't work after the … update" (`11145404810`), 5★ "I have to open the app 20-30 times to get it to load" (`13744408241`). Positive themes are also reported as "rated 4–5★" subsets.
- **Storefront ≠ nationality ≠ language.** The US storefront contains reviews in Russian (`11213904536`) and Korean (`13580872804`) and a request for Chinese (`10120710103`).
- **One review may be misattributed.** `7611242355` (Canada, July 2021) praises a "days since" counter "displayed attractively on a widget", but every other reviewer says the app had no widget until March 2024. It is kept and coded as written.
- **Reviewers contradict each other on plans** (whether a trial exists, whether widgets are free, whether lifetime is still sold, 2 vs 4 free habits). The contradictions are reported, not resolved; they may reflect storefront, version or A/B differences.
- **No download, revenue, retention, churn or refund-outcome data exists** in this corpus.
- **No external source was consulted** for product facts. Every feature, price and event below is attested by at least one review ID.
- **672 of the 743 records (90.4%) carry at least one specific theme.** The remaining 71 (9.6%) are generic ("Great app", "Love it"); their mean is 4.82.

**Interpretation.** A lifetime purchase is a bet on continuity. # PART 9 — WHAT A COMPETITOR LEARNS FROM THIS CORPUS FOR FREE

1. **A year-at-a-glance grid is a differentiator in its own right.** 104 reviews rated 4–5★ praise it, and several say no other app does it (`10791779600`, `9730846797`).
2. **Restraint is a feature.** Simplicity is praised in 37% of reviews; reviewers name "hokey games", "cutesy interfaces" and "decision fatigue" as reasons they left rivals.
3. **A 2-habit free tier converts believers and repels evaluators.** It is both the top purchase trigger and 40.5% of 1★ reviews.
4. **Never surprise users with the wall.** Disclosure complaints are few but come from people who searched for "free".
5. **Offer lifetime, then keep the promise.** Lifetime is a reason to buy (`11286612018`, `11583264179`); withdrawing it is a reason for 2★ (`14138883204`).
6. **Local-first privacy wins praise, but add optional sync.** Otherwise data loss and single-device use follow.
7. **Ship the most-requested feature and it becomes the most-praised one** (widgets, E3 → E4).

# PART 11 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 11.1 Counting rules

- **Denominators.**
  - Every global percentage uses **743** (all records, no removals). The substantive series uses **494** (body > 60 characters).
  - Era denominators: E1 = 53, E2 = 249, E3 = 153, E4 = 134, E5 = 154. Year denominators: 2018 24 · 2019 59 · 2020 89 · 2021 49 · 2022 35 · 2023 144 · 2024 189 · 2025 115 · 2026 39.
  - US = 265; non-US = 478.
  - Segment denominators are always labelled as such: explicit payers 51; 5★ 591; 4★ 77; 3★ 27; 2★ 11; 1★ 37.
- **Themes are non-exclusive.** Every review received ≥ 1 code.
- **Unions** are computed on distinct review IDs from their components:
  - `U_CORE_PRAISE` = USEFUL SIMPLE DESIGN VIZ+ STATS+ STREAK+ MOTIV OUTCOME BEST.
  - `U_MON_FRICTION` = CAP PRICE- SUB- PAYWALL UPSELL DISCLOSE TRIAL AFFORD ONETIMEGONE LOCKIN.
  - `U_MON_POSITIVE` = PRICE+ ONETIME+ FREE+ CAPOK.
  - `U_RELIABILITY` = DATALOSS SYNC- CRASH NOTIF- WIDGET- ICONBUG BUG.
  - `U_UNMET` = all `R_*` codes.
  - `U_WIDGET_DEMAND` = R_WIDGET R_IWIDGET.
  - `U_PLATFORM_DEMAND` = R_WATCH R_PLATFORM R_SYNC.
  - `U_DATA_TRUST` = DATALOSS SYNC- LOCKIN ENT ONETIMEGONE.
  - `U_REGRESSION` = UPDATE- REMOVED.
  - `U_GENERIC_ONLY` = reviews whose only codes are GEN+/GEN-/GEN0/JUNK/MISRATE.
- **"Payer"** requires a first-person statement of payment, subscription or lifetime ownership in any language. Conditional statements ("I'd buy if…") are coded `BUYIF`.
- **`CAP` vs `CAPOK`.** `CAP` is the habit limit expressed as friction; `CAPOK` is the limit accepted or defended. Reviews using the cap as their reason to buy are coded `PAYER` without `CAP`.
- **`KEVIN`** is derived by regex (`\bkev(in)?\b`, case-insensitive) on title + body; it is objective, not a judgement.
- **Rounding.** Percentages are rounded to 2 dp globally and 1 dp in era and band tables; means to 2 dp.
- **Qualitative splits** (Part 3.3) are labelled as such and are **not** additional quantified themes.

## 11.2 Theme definitions (codebook)

Each code is applied by the analyst on reading the full review. The labels in 11.6 give each code's one-line definition. The rules that needed judgement:

| Code | Rule |
|---|---|
| `GEN+` / `GEN-` / `GEN0` | Affective content only, no specific theme ("Great app", "Love it", "so far so good") |
| `USEFUL` | States the app helps them build/track habits, with no more specific praise |
| `OUTCOME` | A concrete change in the reviewer's life (a habit acquired, weight lost, quit smoking) or an explicit "changed my life" claim |
| `VIZ+` vs `STATS+` | `VIZ+`: the year grid, month calendar, heatmap or long-term overview. `STATS+`: percentages, numbers, data |
| `BEST` / `COMP` | `BEST`: best tracker, or the only one that stuck. `COMP`: names a competitor, says they tried several, or switched from another app |
| `DEV+` vs `SUPPORT+` vs `KEVIN` | `DEV+`: the developer listens, builds requests, improves the app, or is praised as an indie / team. `SUPPORT+`: a direct support exchange was good. `KEVIN`: the name appears (derived) |
| `CAP` / `PAYWALL` | Habit-count limit / any other gated feature or "have to pay" |
| `SUB-` vs `PRICE-` | `SUB-`: objects to subscriptions as a model. `PRICE-`: objects to the level |
| `DISCLOSE` | Says the limit or price was not visible before download or setup |
| `TRIAL` | No trial, trial too short, or cannot preview Premium before paying |
| `LOCKIN` | Cannot take data out or delete without paying / export disabled |
| `ONETIMEGONE` / `ENT` | The one-time option was withdrawn or a lifetime licence stopped working / a paid entitlement was lost |
| `PROMO` | Mentions the app being free in a way that implies a promotion (June 2023 burst) or asks when a free offer returns |
| `R_WIDGET` vs `R_IWIDGET` | Any widget request (before launch, or "make widgets better") vs specifically ticking habits from the widget |
| `R_*` | A request for a capability that did not exist *for that reviewer at that time* |
| `MISRATE` | The star rating clearly contradicts the text |
| `SOLICIT` | Mentions the in-app review prompt |
| `JUNK` | Gibberish or off-topic text |
