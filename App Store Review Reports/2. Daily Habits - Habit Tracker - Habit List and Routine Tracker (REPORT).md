# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST: THIS IS AN ABANDONED APP, AND THE RATING LIES ABOUT IT

**Executive summary in one line:** a well-liked, genuinely differentiated free habit tracker was killed by data loss, crashes and five years of silence — and its *headline rating went up while it died*.

**1. Development stopped in May 2021. Apple's own listing confirms it.**
Version 1.5.1, shipped **18 May 2021**, is still the current build as of September 2026 — **5 years 4 months without an update.** Reviewers noticed years before that: `5229271933` (2019, *"the developers seemed to have stopped supporting the app"*), `5080536455` (2019, DE, *"where is the next update? apparently the app is no longer being developed"*), `6185785453` (2020, NI, *"1 year without new updates"*), `10872403505` (2024, GB, *"Abandonware?"*), `13788341591` (2026, IT, *"it seems it hasn't been updated in a long time"*). Total explicit abandonment complaints: **9 (1.74%, mean 2.89)**.

**2. The corpus collapsed, and the rating rose as it collapsed. This is survivorship, not recovery.**

| Year | n | Mean | 1–2★ | 5★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016 | 65 | 4.32 | 10.8% | 66.2% |
| **2017** | 99 | **3.74** | **25.3%** | 48.5% |
| 2018 | 61 | 3.87 | 18.0% | 45.9% |
| 2019 | 100 | 4.39 | 10.0% | 68.0% |
| 2020 | 62 | 4.10 | 16.1% | 59.7% |
| 2021 | 45 | 4.38 | 11.1% | 75.6% |
| 2022 | 32 | 4.25 | 15.6% | 68.8% |
| 2023 | 20 | 4.20 | 15.0% | 65.0% |
| 2024 | 15 | 4.27 | 6.7% | 46.7% |
| 2025 | 14 | 4.50 | 7.1% | 78.6% |
| 2026 | 4 | 4.75 | 0.0% | 75.0% |

Review volume fell **100 → 4 per year (2019 → 2026), a 96% decline**. The mean climbed from 4.19 (2018–19) to 4.31 (2022–26) purely because everyone who could be disappointed had already left. **Do not read the rising average as the product getting better — nothing shipped after May 2021.**

**3. The US — the largest and richest market here — is the *worst-rated* one.**
US mean **3.80** vs global 4.15, with **23.5% one-and-two-star** vs 15.1% globally. Japan is worse still at **3.30** [limited evidence, n=20]. The markets that rate it highest (Brazil 4.65, GB 4.45) are the ones writing the shortest reviews.

**4. A quarter of the corpus carries almost no product information.**
**132 reviews (25.5%) have bodies under 40 characters**, and they average **4.52** — well above the 4.15 corpus mean. Short reviews inflate the rating. Vietnam is the clearest case: **14 reviews, 13 of them 5★, mean 4.93**, and most are literally "Good", "Ok", "Nice" (`4088257905`, `4173137456`, `4707702065`, `5720578554`, `1634520759`).

**5. The launch window looks partly seeded — flagged with caution.**
Nov–Dec 2016 = 65 reviews at mean 4.32 / 66.2% five-star, against 4.12 / 60.0% for the rest of the corpus. Many are generic, non-native English praise posted within days of the 1 Nov 2016 release (`1478551952`, `1478933026`, `1485661227`, `1478389236`, `1478560873`, `1479031508`, `1478508225`, `1478967168`, `1478263216`). **Two reviewers called this out at the time**: `1479044283` (JP, *"the obviously machine-translated 5-star reviews actually make a bad impression"*) and `1484864417` (RU, *"the positive reviews are LIES"*). There was also a **free-Pro giveaway running at launch** (`1483240079`, `1482736956`, `1484186051`), which independently inflates early sentiment. This is **inference from writing style and timing, not proof** — but the first two months should be discounted, not treated as a clean baseline.

**What this means for you:** the honest read on this product is **~3.8–4.0 among engaged Western users**, and its decline is entirely self-inflicted through reliability, not competition.

# PART 1 — MONETIZATION (the most important section)

## 1.1 The model

- **Free download, ad-supported, with an unusually generous free tier — unlimited habits.** This is the app's single strongest differentiator and it is given away.
- **Pro is a one-time in-app purchase**, not a subscription. Price points reviewers actually paid: **US $3.99** (`1484186051`, 2016), **~$4 CAD** (`9175173155`), **€1** (`5727003954`, IT 2020 — likely a promo), and repeatedly described as "minuscule" (`10949884121`, GB 2024) or "저렴한 가격 / cheap" (`2911210521`, KR).
- One reviewer describes it as a subscription (`7418712220`, CO: *"I payed the subscription a year ago"*) — **isolated, and contradicted by the App Store listing and by 18 other reviewers who explicitly praise it as a one-time fee.** Treat as reviewer error.

**8 reviews (1.55%) describe people who tried to pay and could not**, or were confused out of it:

`4025884406` is the whole monetization problem in one sentence: from the user's seat, Pro reads as an ad-removal fee, because its three real features don't work.

## 1.6 Monetization mistakes, in order

1. **Every paid feature is broken.** Sync, calendar, stats — the entire Pro bundle. 6.77% of all reviews.
2. **Paying makes people angrier, not happier.** Payers rate 3.10 vs 4.15.
3. **The free tier is too good relative to a broken Pro.** Unlimited habits free is the top praise theme (5.80%); Pro adds nothing that reliably works.
4. **The purchase and signup flow itself leaks buyers** (1.55%), including a user who says outright he *wants* to pay.
5. **Cross-app integration was implied and never delivered** — it drove at least two purchases and both ended in 1★ (`2109039905`, `7232398007`, plus `8731669029`, `6467272815`, `9974150902` asking for it).

## What this actually says

**Fixed:** the off-by-one date bug — **6.7% → 1.9% → 0.0%**, gone after 2019 and never mentioned again. It is the one clear engineering win in the dataset.

**Got worse, then went quiet:** crashes (6.7% → 3.7% → **8.4%**) and data loss (6.1% → 1.9% → **7.5%**) both spiked in 2020–21, coinciding with iOS 14 / 14.5 (`7232398007`, `7580228017`, `7271771390`, `6734756659`). The drop to ~2% in era D is **not a fix — it is the sound of users having left.** Era D has 85 reviews where era A had 164, and the last code change was May 2021.

**Grew as everything else shrank:** the two pricing themes. Unlimited-free (3.0% → 8.2%) and cheap-one-time (0.6% → 8.2%) are now the *only* reasons anyone writes about this app. The 2022–26 cohort is a small group of long-term users who value the free tier and the buy-once price and have made peace with the rest: `13629753462` (BR, 2026), `12145789866` (GB, 2025), `13550599009` (CA, 2025), `13955224346` (US, 2026 — a user updating the same review since 2017).

**Discovery-stage competitive scrutiny collapsed:** 11.0% → 3.5%. The app stopped being evaluated by new shoppers around 2018.

**Support flipped.** Every positive support mention is from 2016–2018 (`1490152355`, `1490259621`, `1488986466`, `1520975020` — *"kept the promise of responding in 48 hours"*, `3504776374`, `6682336849` — *"support persisted with me and it worked"*). Every negative one is later or unanswered (`1650678342`, `5403701988`, `6467272815` — *"I click on App Support, nothing happens"*).

**No AI-related expectation appears anywhere in the corpus.** Zero mentions across all 517 reviews and all 11 years. Do not infer demand for it here.

# PART 8 — WHAT TO BUILD (if you're designing the best habit tracker)

Every item ties back to a numbered finding above.

### Non-negotiable — these determine whether you have a product at all
1. **Local-first, durable storage with a restore path.** Data loss is 4.26% of the corpus at mean 1.95 and it is what turned paying customers into refund requests (Part 1.4, Part 4). No feature matters more.
2. **Never let the app freeze on check-off.** Crashes/freezes are the #1 cause of 1★ (14 of 47). Tap latency must not grow with habit count — Japan's payers waited 5+ seconds (`1639153995`, `1664490322`, `3488095225`).
3. **Get the date right, in every surface, in every timezone.** Widget, Watch and app must agree (Part 4, 2.71%). This is solvable — this developer solved it once.
4. **If you ship an Apple Watch app, it must actually sync.** 12 of 15 Watch mentions are failures at mean 2.33, and it was a stated purchase reason.
5. **Never write to the user's calendar without exact, reversible, correctly-sized events.** `1650678342` needed Apple support to repair their phone.

### Pricing — this is what makes money here
6. **Keep the one-time purchase.** 19 reviews at mean 4.84 name it as the reason to buy; it is the app's only durable competitive weapon against subscription rivals.
7. **Do not gate the habit cap.** Unlimited-free is the highest-rated theme in the corpus (mean 4.97) and is what earns the word-of-mouth that a tiny marketing budget cannot buy.
8. **Then make the paid tier things that actually work.** The lesson here is brutal and precise: this app charged for sync, calendar and stats, shipped all three broken, and paying users ended up a full star below free ones. **Ship the paid feature before you sell it.**
9. **Fix the purchase flow.** 1.55% of all reviews are people who tried to give money and couldn't — including one who says so explicitly (`5819047278`).

### Product — this converts 3–4★ into 5★
10. **"X times per week" scheduling** — 2.32%, the single most-requested model change, and a churn cause on its own (`8509313946`).
11. **Manual habit reordering** — 1.74% at mean 4.33. Trivial to build, requested for eight straight years.
12. **Customisable, louder reminder sounds** — 1.93% at mean 4.10. The cheapest 5★ upgrade available.
13. **Weekly / monthly / yearly review screens** — 2.51%; the top 4★ blocker.
14. **Keep personal photos on habits.** Rare, loved, and no competitor in this corpus offers it.
15. **Onboarding** — 6.12% of US reviews. A skippable, replayable tour of swipe-to-complete and the break-a-habit toggle.

### Positioning
16. **Lead with "unlimited habits, free, no subscription."** It is what every advocate in this corpus says unprompted.
17. **If you have sibling apps, integrate them or say plainly that you don't.** Assumed integration drove purchases that ended in 1★ (`2109039905`, `7232398007`).

### Do not do
18. **Do not ship and walk away.** Abandonment is visible to users within about a year (`6185785453`) and the app becomes an iOS-release liability (iOS 14 broke it for paying users who had no recourse).
19. **Do not spam rating prompts or cross-promote to paying customers** (`1484050413`, `3342902073`, `7598926605`).
20. **Do not seed launch reviews.** Two users spotted it in week one and said so publicly (`1479044283`, `1484864417`), and it permanently contaminates your own analytics baseline — as it does this one.

## Appendix — method, taxonomy and limitations

**Files used.** `app store review/2. Daily Habits - Habit Tracker - Habit List and Routine Tracker/reviews.jsonl` (517 records), the 61 files in `by_country/`, `manifest.json`, and `_state.json`.

**Schema.** Each record: `review_id`, `app_id`, `app_name`, `country`, `country_name`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `author`, `date` (ISO-8601 UTC), `vote_count`, `vote_sum`, `is_edited`. All 13 fields are present on all 517 records; no field is null and no body is empty.

**Coverage and reconciliation.** 517 lines read; **517 unique `review_id`s — zero duplicates, so no deduplication was applied and 517 is the denominator for every global percentage.** The 61 `by_country/*.jsonl` files hold 517 records whose ID set is **exactly identical** to the merged file. Both match `manifest.json` (`total_reviews: 517`, 61 storefronts). The rating distribution (5★ 314, 4★ 89, 3★ 36, 2★ 31, 1★ 47) and mean (4.145) reproduce the manifest exactly. **Coverage is 100%.**

**Date range.** 2016-11-02 → 2026-08-17. `is_edited` is true on 4 records; the visible text is the edited text.

**Processing method.** Records were parsed programmatically, then **all 517 reviews were read in full, in their original languages** (English, Spanish, German, Portuguese, Japanese, Russian, Ukrainian, French, Italian, Korean, Chinese, Thai, Turkish, Vietnamese, Finnish, Norwegian, Czech). A multilingual regex pass generated candidate sets per theme; every candidate was then **manually inspected and the final theme membership hand-curated as explicit ID lists**. This was done because automated matching produced clear false positives at this corpus size — for example `\badd\b` matching the verb "add" rather than ADHD, and "productive" matching "be productive" rather than the competitor app *Productive*. All counts, percentages and means in this report are computed from the hand-curated ID lists, not from the raw regex output.

**Counting rules.** Theme membership is **non-exclusive** — a single review can and often does count toward several themes (`1816542296` appears under data loss, Apple Watch, broken Pro feature, confirmed purchase and refund). Percentages therefore sum to well over 100% and must not be added. Every percentage uses 517 as its denominator unless the row explicitly says "% of US" (denominator 98) or "of payers" (denominator 30), which are **labelled segment rates and are not substituted for the global band classification**.

**External source.** Apple iTunes Lookup API for id 1133119692, US storefront, **accessed 2026-09-09**: current version 1.5.1, `currentVersionReleaseDate` 2021-05-18, `releaseDate` 2016-11-01, price $0.00, minimum iOS 11.0, 299 US ratings at 4.398, and the Pro feature list quoted in Part 1.2. **This is the only externally sourced claim in the report**; every other statement derives from review text. The store's 299 US ratings vs this corpus's 98 US written reviews is the expected gap between raters and reviewers, not a discrepancy.

**Theme definitions.** *Data loss* = user reports habits, check-off history or notes vanishing or reverting. *Crash/freeze* = hard failure (crash, force-close, permanent freeze, won't open); *slow/lag* is counted separately. *Confirmed purchase* = the reviewer states in the first person that they bought, paid for, or upgraded to Pro — intent to buy and price commentary are excluded. *Broken Pro feature* = a complaint naming one of the four capabilities Apple's listing places behind Pro. *Abandonware* = an explicit statement that the app is no longer updated or supported; separate from *iOS regression*, which reports a specific OS-version break.

**Limitations and known error risk.**
- **n = 517 is small.** One review = 0.19%. Themes in the 1–3% band rest on 5–15 reviews; a handful of judgement calls could move any of them one band. Treat the ordering as more reliable than the exact percentages.
- **Only one storefront (US) clears the 50-review threshold.** Every other country claim is limited evidence and is labelled as such.
- **Selection bias runs in both directions.** 25.5% of reviews are under 40 characters and average 4.52, inflating the mean; and reviewers skew toward the delighted and the furious in any corpus.
- **The launch-window seeding concern in Part 0 is inference**, based on writing style, timing and two contemporaneous user accusations. It is not established fact and is not used in any quantified finding — the launch window is included in all global counts.
- **The "high-spend markets" grouping in Part 6.3 is qualitative only.** No spend or download data was available, and review volume is used solely as a disclosed engagement proxy.
- **Time-trend eras have unequal and shrinking samples** (164 / 161 / 107 / 85). The era-D declines in crash and data-loss share are consistent with audience attrition rather than repair, and are read that way; the corroborating fact is that no code shipped after May 2021.
- **Rating is not read as a feature preference**, and no correlation in this report is stated as causation. Where a causal path is asserted — paying → disappointment — it rests on reviewers explicitly narrating that sequence themselves (Part 1.4), not on the correlation alone.
