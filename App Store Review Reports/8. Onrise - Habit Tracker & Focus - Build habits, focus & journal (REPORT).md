### ⚠️ Read this before anything else: Onrise has no monetization at all

Onrise is **free, ad-free, and has no in-app purchases, no subscription, no premium tier, and no donation mechanism**. This is confirmed three ways: the listing price is Free **[external]**; **zero of 850 reviews** contain any statement of having paid, subscribed, upgraded, restored a purchase, or requested a refund; and 27 reviews mention "premium/pro/upgrade" — every one of them either describes a *competitor's* paywall or explicitly notes that Onrise has none.

**This changes what this report is.** There is no purchase-trigger analysis, no refund analysis, no paywall-reaction analysis, because there is nothing to buy. What Part 1 analyses instead is the thing that is actually decision-relevant here: **a large, articulate, unmonetized willingness-to-pay signal, and the acquisition engine that "free" is currently buying.**

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

## 1. "Free" is not a pricing decision here — it is the product's entire market position

**285 reviews (33.53%, HIGH-PRIORITY)** spontaneously praise the app for being free / having no paywall / no subscription / no in-app purchases. Mean rating **4.81**, **88.4% are 5★**. Counting the wider money-adjacent set (free praise + willingness to pay + donation requests + fear of monetization + explicit competitor-paywall escape), **308 reviews (36.24%)** of the corpus talk about money in some form.

To put that in proportion: money is the **second-largest theme in the entire corpus**, behind only simplicity (42.71%) and ahead of design (26.94%). Among 5★ reviewers it is even more dominant — **274 of 601 five-star reviews (45.6% segment rate)** mention money.

Reviewers do not say "it's cheap". They say the *absence of a paywall is the reason they chose it*:

- `11533284738`(VN,5★) *"The app is completely free, I can't even find its premium version."*
- `12534773715`(CA,5★) *"(1) solves a problem, (2) completely free, (3) doesn't push you to pay for a premium version since it doesn't exist!"*
- `10972915619`(TR,5★, most-voted review in the corpus at 6 helpful votes) *"I was used to an app being 'free' and asking for extreme amounts of money so I created a few quick habits expecting to see that pop up after 5 habits but it never did… I wasn't sure if there was a catch because it looked too good to be true."*
- `12502921315`(US,5★) *"A FREE, Zero-AD, Non-Subscription, Premium-Designed app in the productivity tools niche. 'too good to be true'… is now TRUE! As a dev myself I have nothing but respect."*

> The strategic read: Onrise's differentiator is not a feature. It is a **business-model position** in a category where every competitor gates 3–5 habits behind a subscription. That position is doing all the acquisition work, and it is the single most fragile asset in the product.

## 2. Not one of the 42 one- and two-star reviews is a price complaint — and that is the headline finding

Across **42 reviews rated 1★ or 2★ (4.94% of the corpus)**, read individually, the reasons break down as:

| 1–2★ reason | n | of 42 |
|---|---|---|
| App is functionally broken (won't open, can't create, data lost, can't log, widget dead, crashes/lag) | 18 | 42.9% |
| Missing capability (multi-daily check-in, stats, frequency options, custom habits, export) | 13 | 31.0% |
| UI/UX confusion or friction (forced reminders, notification nagging, layout) | 7 | 16.7% |
| Off-topic / philosophical / rating-text mismatch | 3 | 7.1% |
| **Price / paywall objection** | **0** | **0.0%** |
| Paywall *misconception* (believes a free cap exists that does not) | 1 | 2.4% |

The one apparent exception is `13650696629`(HR,1★, Jan 2026) — *"Zato što trebate nam dopustiti da radimo više routina besplatno"* (["Because you should let us make more routines for free"]) — a reviewer who assumed a free cap that Onrise does not have. That is not a pricing complaint; it is a **positioning failure**: the category's norms are so strong that a user assumed the paywall was there.

**Interpretation.** In the seven other apps analysed in this set, monetization is the dominant source of rating damage. Remove the paywall and the rating damage does not disappear — it **relocates entirely to reliability and capability**. Onrise is the natural experiment that shows what a habit tracker's review profile looks like when price is off the table.

## 1.4 Monetization friction that already exists, without a paywall

Three frictions are already costing this product something despite there being no price:

1. **No way to give money.** Seven reviewers went out of their way to ask for a donate button. `13440800887`(CA,5★) *"Would consider donating a few bucks if option existed on the app."* Zero engineering risk, zero rating risk.
2. **The free position invites suspicion.** `10309724588`(PL,4★) *"I'm aware that you need to get money from somewhere since this app is 100% free (for which I'm very glad), but I wish there was more transparency on your side"* — asked directly whether journal entries are part of the collected data and could not find the answer in the privacy policy. `11504152895`(PH,5★) *"i dont know how creators of this app make money."* `14220350355`(GB,1★, Jun 2026) escalated the same instinct into a battery/tracking accusation: *"it is constantly on and running watching my every move."*
3. **The free position creates abandonment anxiety.** The seven `abandonment_concern` reviews are the direct product of a free app with no visible revenue: users correctly infer there is no business model sustaining it.

## 1.5 Upsell pressure is zero — and reviewers name that as a feature

There is no nag, no interstitial, no "upgrade" prompt anywhere in 850 reviews. Reviewers cite this explicitly and favourably: `14281675824`(US,5★) *"it doesn't buy me everyday to 'upgrade to premium'"*; `10908785407`(CA,5★) *"no in-app purchases or 'premium' version nag pop ups"*; `12223448511`(IN,5★) *"they don't [nag] you with their premium plans."*

The only nag in the corpus is **not** commercial — it is the widget-onboarding overlay (§4.6), which trapped four users inside an unclosable full-screen prompt.

# PART 8 — WHAT CHANGED OVER TIME

## 8.1 Method

Reviews are bucketed by calendar year of the `date` field (2021 n=25, 2022 n=51, 2023 n=162, 2024 n=229, 2025 n=237, 2026 Jan–Sep n=146). Quarterly buckets are used only for 2024 onward, where n per quarter is 34–84. **2021 and 2022 have small n (25 and 51); percentage swings there are not reliable and are reported as counts.** September 2026 is a partial month (4 reviews) and no conclusion rests on it. There is no `version` field in the corpus, so all release attribution comes from dated reviewer reports plus the current release notes **[external]**.

## 8.2 Trend 1 — a real quality regression in 2025, and a real recovery in 2026

Established in §0.3. Restated as the causal chain the evidence supports:

**Development stalled (last update ~mid-2024) → iOS moved on → widget, create flow, day-boundary and launch paths broke → functional-failure reports rose to 7.2% of 2025 → 2025Q3 mean fell to 4.184 with 12.2% 1–2★ → development resumed → functional failures fell to 2.7% of 2026, the lowest rate in five years.**

Corroboration: `abandonment_concern` appears only in 2024–2025 (7 reviews) and is **absent from all 146 reviews of 2026**.

## 8.3 Trend 2 — shipping against reviews demonstrably works here

| Fix | Complaint rate before | Complaint rate after |
|---|---|---|
| Optional (not forced) reminders, ~mid-2023 | **17.6% of 2022** (9/51) | 0.6% of 2023 → **0.0% of 2025–26** (0/383) |
| Monthly / yearly summaries, ~Jan 2026 | 6.8% of 2025 | 3.4% of 2026 |
| Custom habit names / colours / ordering, ~late 2025 | 2.5% of 2025 | 0.7% of 2026 |
| Widget tap-to-tick, ~mid-2026 | 4.6% of 2025 | 2.1% of 2026 |

The forced-reminder result is the strongest: a theme that was **the app's single largest friction in 2022 was eliminated to zero and has stayed at zero for two years and 383 reviews.**

## 8.4 Trend 3 — the free position has held constant and never eroded

`praise_free_nopaywall` by year: 4.0% (2021, n=25) → 29.4% → 32.1% → 36.7% → 32.1% → **39.0% (2026)**. Five years in, the free position is being praised at the highest rate in the app's history. There is no fatigue effect and no sign that it has stopped being remarkable to users.

Meanwhile `fear_monetization` appeared only from 2023 and peaked in 2025 (2.5%) — users worrying about the model *because* the app looked abandoned.

## 8.5 Trend 4 — the residual complaint is now capability, not reliability

| Theme | 2025 rate | 2026 rate | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Functional failures (aggregate) | 7.2% | **2.7%** | ✅ resolved |
| want_stats_analytics | 6.8% | 3.4% | ✅ improving |
| custom_habit_blocked | 2.5% | 0.7% | ✅ resolved |
| want_backfill_past | 5.5% | 2.7% | ✅ improving |
| want_sync_backup_account | 4.6% | 2.7% | ↔ still open |
| focus_timer_issue | 4.2% | 1.4% | ✅ improving |
| **want_multi_daily_checkin** | **3.0%** | **8.2%** | ❌ **worsening** |
| praise_design | 19.4% | 15.1% | ❌ declining |
| praise_simplicity | 40.9% | 34.2% | ❌ declining |

**Two things to watch.** First, multi-daily check-in is now the last big structural gap and it is the only complaint theme rising. Second, praise for design and simplicity has been declining monotonically since 2021 (design 60.0% → 15.1%; simplicity peaked 48.8% in 2023 → 34.2%). Some of that is novelty decay and reviewer-base broadening, but the app also added a monthly summary, custom colours, ordering and an iOS 26 redesign in this window. **There is no design *backlash* in the corpus — zero reviews complain about the Aug 2026 Liquid Glass refresh — but the design is no longer the thing people lead with.**

## 8.6 Trend 5 — New Year seasonality is real and large

January review counts: 2021: 7 · 2022: 1 · 2023: 20 · 2024: 21 · **2025: 49** · 2026: 31. January 2025 alone is **20.7% of all 2025 reviews**. The resolution cohort arrives in January, and their reviews are written in the first days of use — `13581401872`(US,4★) *"I'm only on my second day"*; `12138019597`(US,5★) *"I have never written a review before this one"*.

**Product consequence:** the January cohort judges onboarding, not retention. Every onboarding defect in this report — the create flow, the preset-habit picker, the can't-log-today bug, the widget overlay — is amplified ~2× in January. The can't-log-today cluster is itself concentrated in **January 2025 (3 of 11 reviews in a 14-day window)**.

# PART 9 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation below cites the finding that supports it. Immediate fixes are separated from experiments and open questions.

## Immediate — fix before anything else (days, not sprints)

1. **Fix the day-boundary / "can't log today" defect.** §0.7, §2 (1★ band). 11 reviews, mean 2.73, 45.5% 1–2★, still reported April 2026, concentrated in January. This breaks the product's only core action. Highest severity in the corpus.
2. **Fix the monthly-summary off-by-one.** §4.2. `13742512516`(US,1★) `14018076544`(US,4★) — two independent reports three months apart that the last day of the month is excluded. This turned the flagship 2026 feature into a 1★. Almost certainly a one-line date-range bug.
3. **Fix the widget placeholder-string leak.** §4.6. 13 reviews across three years; the symptom ("no data / mo datos / keine daten") is a localized placeholder shipping instead of data. Reported as recently as `13609169075`(MD, Jan 2026).
4. **Make the focus-timer alarm fire when backgrounded or silenced.** §4.5. 7 reviews Jan 2024 → Aug 2026, including `14397732532`(IN,2★) reporting the timer *resets* on backgrounding. This is an audio-session / notification-category configuration, not a feature.
5. **Audit the reminder time picker for 12/24-hour locales.** §4.8. `13724690797`(US,1★) could not set AM. A locale bug that costs a full star from an enthusiastic user.
6. **Answer `13311871983`(EG).** §3.1. A user asked for their data in March 2025 and was still unanswered in October. Whatever the export roadmap is, an unanswered data-access request is a reputational and regulatory exposure that costs nothing to close.

## Product — converts 4★ into 5★

7. **Ship intra-day completion: tap the checkbox N times to fill N/N.** §0.6, §6. 36 reviews (4.24%), mean 3.94, **rising to 8.2% in 2026**, #2 blocker in both the 4★ and 3★ bands, one stated uninstall. Reviewers have designed it for you: `13440800887`(CA) *"the habit checkbox would increment with each click until you hit your target of x."* Pair it with quantity logging (§4.1 sibling cluster, 9 reviews).
8. **Show circles only on days a habit is actually scheduled.** §4.4. 25 reviews, mean 3.76. This is a rendering change, not a model change — the underlying flexible-frequency logic is *praised* by `14254399815` and `13813008711`. Fixing the display keeps the flexibility and removes the confusion.
9. **Extend backfill from ~7 days to an unbounded calendar.** §4.1. 27 reviews, mean 3.52 — the worst mean of any request theme. Users say the 7-day wall makes the app feel like it "starts over" (`13237495965` `13182431485`).
10. **Ship a multi-habit widget and lock-screen widgets.** §4.6, §6. 41 reviews, **zero 1–2★**, above-corpus mean — pure upside with no simplicity risk. It is the single most-repeated widget ask, going back to `6862625618`(US, Jan 2021).
11. **Local encrypted backup + iCloud Drive document sync — no account, no server.** §4.3. 37 sync requests + 12 export requests + 9 data-loss reports. India 14.10%. This preserves the no-account, no-server privacy posture that `9853721563`(DE) and `10309724588`(PL) value, while closing the corpus's largest reliability anxiety. `11943323917`(PH) explicitly assumes it is unaffordable — proving them wrong is cheap goodwill.
12. **Let users change a habit's colour after creation.** §6. 20 reviews. `12470711816`(AU,4★): today you must delete the habit and lose all its history to recolour it. That is data loss disguised as a settings limitation.

## Monetization — the permitted moves, and the forbidden one

13. **Add a tip jar / "buy the developer a coffee" one-time IAP.** §0.8, §1.4. 7 reviewers asked for exactly this, unprompted, all 5★. 13 more said they would pay. **Zero rating risk** — it gates nothing. It also directly answers the abandonment anxiety in §0.4 by giving the product a visible reason to exist.
14. **If a paid tier is ever built, make it a one-time purchase or an additive add-on — never a gate on anything that is free today.** §0.8. Six of 13 WTP statements name a one-time purchase or add-on; exactly one names a subscription. Twelve 5★ reviewers explicitly warned against monetization. `13920667278`(UA,5★) states the whole strategy in one line: *"If there would be some subs I wouldn't use it, but I could buy it for 5-10 bucks if it provided the full experience forever."*
15. **The Apple Watch app is the best paid-add-on candidate in the corpus.** §6, §1.3. 18 requests, mean 4.61, zero 1–2★ — and it is the *only* feature two reviewers independently volunteered to pay for (`8082446036`(DE) *"würde ich hierfür auch sehr gerne Geld zahlen"*; `10117160205`(US) *"You can make this a premium feature as I know this is not an easy task"*). It is additive, it does not touch the free tier, and the demand and the willingness to pay are attached to the same feature.
16. **Do not gate the widget, unlimited habits, the journal, or the focus timer. Ever.** §0.1, §0.10, §3. These four are 33.53%, 4.35%, 7.65% and 7.18% of the corpus's praise and the reason 32 reviewers left paid competitors. Gating any of them converts the corpus's strongest asset into its category's standard complaint.

## Positioning — free rating and revenue, no engineering

17. **Say "free forever, no subscription, no ads, no account" in the App Store subtitle and first description line.** §0.1, §0.2. The listing description **[external]** currently leads with behaviour-design language and never mentions the price model. Meanwhile 33.53% of reviewers discover it by *surprise* (`10972915619` *"I wasn't sure if there was a catch"*) and one reviewer (`13650696629`, HR, 1★) assumed a paywall that does not exist. **The single biggest differentiator is absent from the store listing.**
18. **Publish a one-line "how this app is funded / what data it collects" note in-app.** §1.4. Three reviewers speculated about the business model in public reviews and one escalated it to a 1★ tracking accusation (`14220350355`). A sentence closes it.
19. **Re-request reviews from the 2025 cohort.** §0.3, §8.2. The 2025 trough was caused by defects that are now fixed. The store aggregate (4.83, 7,184 ratings across 13 storefronts **[external]**) already masks it, but the written corpus — which is what prospective users read — still carries 17 functional-failure reports from a version that no longer exists.

## Research questions this corpus cannot answer

- **What is retention?** 55.6% of reviews are pure praise and a large share are written on day 1–3 (`13581401872` *"second day"*, `11638684551` *"my first day"*, `12146578302` *"After testing it out for 5mins"*). The corpus measures first impressions far better than it measures habit formation.
- **Would a tip jar actually convert?** 20 volunteers among 850 writers (who are 9.4% of raters) is a signal of *sentiment*, not a demand curve.
- **Is the "can't log today" bug timezone-related?** The corpus gives symptoms (SE, SG, HU, ID, AU, US, CA) but not devices, locales, or timezones. Needs instrumentation, not more reviews.
- **How many users hit the preset-habit picker and left without reviewing?** §4.7 shows 13 people who wrote about it. The silent version of that cohort is unmeasurable here.
- **Does the widget failure correlate with a specific iOS version?** No version field exists in the corpus.
- **What did the 2024–2025 development pause cost in installs?** Review volume actually *rose* in 2025 (237 vs 229), so the corpus cannot see it.

## 10.3 Method and known error risk

1. **Full read.** All 850 records were dumped chronologically in 8 batches of ~110 and read individually, in the original language, before any classification was written. Non-English reviews were read in-language; no machine translation was used. Ten reviews use non-Latin scripts (Cyrillic, CJK, Thai, Hebrew); ~91 are German; the remainder are English or Romance/Nordic/Slavic languages read directly.
2. **Taxonomy built from the read**, not from a pre-existing list. 62 themes.
3. **Classification is hybrid.** Fifteen broad praise themes (simplicity, design, no-ads, free/no-paywall, unlimited, all-in-one, journal, focus, developer, reminders, streak, outcome, privacy, no-account, widget-mention) use multilingual regex over `title + body` and were spot-checked against their 1–2★ members. **Every high-stakes theme** — all 27 request themes, all 10 bug/defect themes, the entire monetization set (WTP, donate, fear, switched-from-paid, misconception), plus ADHD and rating-text mismatch — was **hand-curated from an explicit review-ID list** after reading each candidate in context.
4. **Direction was assigned by hand, not by rating.** A 5★ review asking for sync is counted in `want_sync_backup_account`; a 2★ review praising the design is counted in `praise_design`. Seven of the 20 two-star reviews praise the design in the same breath as their complaint, and all seven are counted in both.
5. **The monetization set was audited line by line.** `wtp_explicit` requires a first-person statement of willingness to pay or buy; `want_donate` requires asking for a tip/support mechanism; the two overlap on one review (`10529311558`). "This app deserves more funding" (`11824366215`) was **excluded** as not a personal purchase statement. `12259538103`(SG) was excluded from `fear_monetization` as too ambiguous.
6. **Regex over-matches were removed by hand.** For example, the multi-daily-check-in regex returned 38 candidates of which only 22 were genuine; the theme was rebuilt from the full read and now stands at 36 hand-verified members. `9219455705`(RU,2★, *"UI and UX are complex"*) was explicitly excluded from `praise_simplicity`.
7. **HTML entities** (`&#39;`, `&amp;`, `&gt;`) were unescaped before matching and before quotation.

**Known error risk:** the fifteen regex-driven praise themes may carry roughly ±3–5% relative error from context misses (e.g. a review calling a *competitor* "simple"). Manual inspection of every 1–2★ member of the two largest (`praise_simplicity` n=363, `praise_design` n=229) found **one** genuine misclassification, which was removed — an observed error rate near 0.3% on the inspected subset. Because these themes are large and one-directional, this does not change any label or conclusion. **The hand-curated themes carry near-zero false-positive risk but may under-count** — a review expressing a theme obliquely, or in a language construction not read as such, can be missed. **All small-n themes should be read as floors, not point estimates.**

## 10.4 Known limitations and biases

- **Selection bias.** Written reviews are 9.4% of raters across the 13 storefronts checked (677 of 7,184) **[external]**. §0.9 quantifies the direction: written reviews sit 0.14–0.46 stars *below* tap-only ratings in 11 of 13 storefronts.
- **Recency-of-use bias.** A large share of reviews are written in the first days of use ("my first day", "second day", "5mins"). The corpus over-measures onboarding and under-measures retention.
- **No version field.** All release attributions come from *dated reviewer reports* plus the current release notes **[external]**. Timeline claims are therefore ordinal and approximate, and are flagged as such.
- **No device or OS field.** The 2025 functional-failure cluster cannot be attributed to a specific iOS version or device class. `12982290428` self-reports an iPhone 7; that is the only device datum in the corpus.
- **Small early cohorts.** 2021 (n=25) and 2022 (n=51) percentages swing on 1–2 reviews. The forced-reminder trend (§8.3) rests on 9 of 51 reviews in 2022 — a real signal, but with wide error bars on the percentage.
- **Partial final month.** September 2026 has 4 reviews. No conclusion rests on it.
- **Prompt-timing bias.** Any in-app review prompt shapes who writes. Its placement is unknown, and the very high 5★ share (70.71%) is consistent with prompting at a moment of completion.
- **No developer-response data.** The corpus shows what users wrote, not what was answered. Two reviews (`10827993637`, `13742512516`) quote a developer reply in an edit, proving replies happen — but reply text is not in the schema.
- **No churn measurement.** Only three reviews state an uninstall (`9386826805` `13501714015` `14021302236`). Silent churn is invisible here.
- **Market-group definitions are external judgements**, not corpus-derived. See §7.2 and §7.3.

## 10.5 Counting rules

- A review belongs to **all** themes it expresses. Counts are non-exclusive and do not sum to 850.
- **Global percentages always use n=850.** Rating-band percentages use that band's n (5★=601, 4★=165, 3★=42, 2★=20, 1★=22). Country percentages use that country's n (US=188, DE=114, CA=89, IN=78). Segment rates are labelled and carry both denominators.
- "Mean" is the arithmetic mean star rating of the reviews carrying that theme. Corpus mean is 4.533.
- "Rating penalty" is (theme mean − 4.533).
- A theme's **direction** is inferred from context, not from the star rating.
- `money_any` is the union of `praise_free_nopaywall`, `wtp_explicit`, `want_donate`, `fear_monetization`, `switched_from_paid`, `paywall_misconception`.
- `widget_positive_only` is `widget_any` minus `widget_broken`, `want_widget_better`, and `bug_widget_popup`.
- The "functional-failure cluster" (§0.3) is the union of `bug_cannot_create`, `bug_white_screen`, `widget_broken`, `data_loss`, `bug_cannot_log_today`, `perf_lag_crash` — 43 unique reviews.
- Signal labels are applied to the **global** share (n=850) and, separately, to each eligible country's own share.
