# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST: A GREAT PRODUCT THAT SHOT ITSELF IN JULY 2025

**Executive summary in one line:** the best-loved free habit counter on the App Store (mean 4.77 across 10,621 reviews) put its single most-praised feature — the widget — behind a paywall in **July 2025**, and its 1–2★ rate went from **1.75% to 8.64% in one quarter** and has not fully recovered fourteen months later.

### 1. The product itself is genuinely excellent and the praise is not thin

- **3,647 reviews (34.3%) praise simplicity / clean UI / ease of use** — mean 4.90. This is the largest single theme in the corpus by a wide margin.
- **920 reviews (8.7%) mention the widget.** It is the #2 theme overall.
- **495 (4.7%) explicitly praise the app being free** or having a usable free tier; **206 (1.9%) praise the absence of ads**; **244 (2.3%) praise unlimited/multiple counters**; **145 (1.4%) praise the privacy stack** (Face ID lock, no account, disguised app icon).
- Reviewers repeatedly describe it as indistinguishable from a first-party Apple app: `10404638991` (AE), `13283082126` (IN, *"The most Apple looking non-Apple app ever"*), `9406490807` (CA), `12367096941` (US), `8478010591` (ZA).

## 1.7 Monetization mistakes, in order

1. **Paywalling the widget.** 128 reviews, 1.21% of the corpus, mean 2.48, and a full-quarter rating collapse. It is the single most expensive product decision visible in this dataset.
2. **Not honouring entitlements.** Lifetime buyers told to subscribe (`14060380344`); paid subscribers who can't find what they bought (`9912775984`); premium members still shown upsells (`10764707061`, `12282678957`).
3. **Billing hygiene.** Double charges (`10263513150`, `11203632971`), unclear cancellation (`13964501187`, `10999894947`, `11248201618`), charges after trial cancellation (`11203632971`, `11722683268`).
4. **Nagging users who already told you no** — 176 reviews, and no "never ask again" control.
5. **No one-time / tip option at a sane price.** 18 explicit requests plus ~12 unsolicited offers to donate. The corpus contains people literally asking to give money in a way the app doesn't accept.
6. **No regional pricing** in IN, TR, UA, SA — markets where the review text explicitly says the price is the only blocker.

# PART 3 — WHAT PEOPLE PRAISE (and why it is defensible)

### 1. Radical simplicity — 3,647 reviews (34.34%), mean 4.90 · HIGH-PRIORITY STRENGTH
Users choose this app *after rejecting others for being too much*. `11351620542` (US, *"most miss the boat entirely or try to be sophisticated at the expense of being complex and useless… THE habit tracker to rule them all"*), `9725862564` (GB, *"I don't need to log all of my reasons and thoughts… I already know my reasons"*), `11549990017` (JP, *"Other developers would clog the interface with ads or force you to subscribe to Sobriety Plus"*), `8911015925` (CA), `12948824437` (IN), `9900639372` (CA, *"the only one of 3 apps I downloaded that didn't give me bible quotes every time I logged on"*).

### 2. The widget — 920 reviews (8.66%)
Not decoration. # PART 8 — WHAT TO BUILD (if you're designing the best quit-habit tracker)

Every item ties to a numbered finding above.

### Non-negotiable — these determine whether you have a product at all

1. **Never paywall the widget.** (Part 0.3, Part 1.2, Part 2) It is the intervention, not a convenience. 26% of all one-star reviews are about this one decision, and it cost a full quarter's rating. If the widget must be monetised, monetise *variants* (multi-counter widgets, custom art, goal-progress rings) and keep a single-counter widget free forever.
2. **Ship iCloud sync and automatic backup, free.** (Part 4C) Losing a 2-year sobriety streak on a phone upgrade is an unrecoverable brand event. 44 reviews, 9 confirmed data losses, and `13629061971`'s *"Back up is literally PREMIUM?"* is the line that will follow you.
3. **Make reset undoable and the widget reset button optional.** (Part 4B) 33 reviews, several from paying users, one of whom stayed on the free trial specifically to avoid the risk. Add a confirmation, an undo window, and a per-widget toggle.
4. **Honour entitlements the moment they're purchased.** (Part 1.4) Lifetime buyers being asked to subscribe is the most damaging bug class in the corpus.

### Pricing — this is where the money actually is

5. **Offer a cheap one-time purchase.** 18 explicit requests, ~12 unsolicited offers to donate with no mechanism to do so, and the single most-quoted objection is subscription-as-principle, not amount. `14060380344`: *"subscription fatigue is real."*
6. **Regional pricing for IN, TR, UA, BR, MX, SA.** India has 346 reviews, a 4.79 mean, near-zero monetization friction, and three separate reviews naming price as the only blocker. This is the largest untapped conversion pool visible.
7. **Add a "don't ask again" control for the upgrade prompt.** 176 reviews, 73% of them from 5★ users. This costs you nothing and buys back a percentage point of rating.
8. **Respect the iOS "no in-app review prompts" setting.** `10818110589` is a lifetime customer telling you you're violating a system preference.

### Product — this converts 3–4★ into 5★

9. **Pause / stop / archive a counter.** (Part 4A) 44 requests, mean 4.02, several explicit "this is the only thing keeping it at 4 stars."
10. **Milestone celebration and achievements.** (Part 4D) 235 requests over six years. v4.1.0 appears to address this — validate it against the 3–4★ band in the next corpus.
11. **A proper notes / journaling field.** 152 reviews. The current field is a single cramped line: `12151285522` (CA, paying), `14270251700` (GB, 4★, *"an important part of recovery is journaling to help identify common triggers… the current notes field makes this very difficult"*), `9673744206`, `12251429856`, `12662372114`, `10483620131`.
12. **Graphs and trend analytics.** 48 requests, mean 4.60. Specifically: streak length over time, resets per month, average-streak trend. `10193651577` (US) and `13974353363` (GB) both describe the exact chart they want.
13. **Countdown / "days until" mode.** 27 requests spanning 2020–2026. Users want one app, not two.
14. **Folders / categories.** 43 requests, mean 4.67, **zero 1–2★** — pure power-user demand from people who now track 20–100 counters.
15. **A "good habit" inverse mode.** 10 reviews describe using the app backwards. The chore/ADHD cohort is 4.17% of the corpus and growing; it deserves a first-class mode rather than a workaround.
16. **Money-saved counter.** 14 requests, mean 4.57. Standard in competing quit-smoking apps and repeatedly named as the one thing missing.

### Positioning

17. **Lead with "unlimited counters, free, private."** These are the three things advocates say unprompted, and all three are the reason people leave *I Am Sober* (30 direct comparisons, mean 4.47).
18. **Protect the privacy stack as a headline feature.** 145 reviews, mean 4.90, **zero negative**. Face ID, no account, neutral name, alternate icons. This is what makes the app usable by a 13-year-old tracking self-harm on a family phone.
19. **Keep the neutral, non-judgemental reset.** 130 reviews praise it. The competitive differentiation against every "sobriety coach" app is that this one *doesn't talk*.

### Do not do

20. **Do not remove a feature users already have.** The 2022 subscription launch was survivable because it added a tier. The 2025 widget change was not, because it took something away. `12887257900` (GB, 1★): *"I understand they have to make money. I just don't agree with taking away features that have been free for years."*
21. **Do not monetise the vulnerable-user surface.** 11 reviews accuse the developer of preying on addicts. In a category where users are tracking self-harm and suicide attempts, that framing spreads faster than any feature.
22. **Do not ship a paywall change silently.** `12882383970` (DK): *"No warning, no version update info about it."* If the July 2025 lock-out really was a bug, the absence of a release note is why nobody believed it.

## Appendix — method, taxonomy and limitations

**Files used.** `app store review/3. Days Since - Quit Habit Tracker - Sober Streak Day Counter/reviews.jsonl` (10,621 records), the 111 files in `by_country/`, `manifest.json`, and `_state.json`.

**Schema.** Each record: `review_id`, `app_id`, `app_name`, `country`, `country_name`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `author`, `date` (ISO-8601 UTC), `vote_count`, `vote_sum`, `is_edited`. All 13 fields present on all 10,621 records; **no field is null and no title or body is empty**.

**Coverage and reconciliation.** 10,621 lines read; **10,621 unique `review_id`s — zero duplicates, so no deduplication was applied and 10,621 is the denominator for every global percentage.** The 111 `by_country/*.jsonl` files hold 10,621 records whose ID set is **exactly identical** to the merged file. Both match `manifest.json` (`total_reviews: 10621`, `storefronts_with_reviews: 111`). The rating distribution (5★ 9,250 · 4★ 849 · 3★ 191 · 2★ 106 · 1★ 225) and the mean (4.7694) reproduce the manifest exactly. **Coverage is 100%.**

**Date range.** 2019-08-17 → 2026-09-06. `is_edited` is true on 121 records (1.14%); the visible text is the edited text. 187 records (1.76%) carry at least one helpfulness vote.

**Processing method.** Records were parsed programmatically, then **all 10,621 reviews were read in full** — the 1,371 reviews rated 1–4★ first, ordered by rating and date, then all 9,250 five-star reviews ordered by country and date. Reading covered every language present (English, Spanish, German, Portuguese, Russian, Ukrainian, French, Italian, Polish, Dutch, Swedish, Danish, Norwegian, Finnish, Czech, Slovak, Turkish, Arabic, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Hungarian, Romanian, Bulgarian, Serbian, Lithuanian, Latvian, Kazakh). A multilingual regex pass then generated candidate sets per theme; every consequential theme was **manually inspected and false positives removed** (for example, `\bexcel\b` was required so that "excellent" did not count as an export request; "no crashes" and "bug-free" were excluded from the crash theme; "widgets are free" praise was separated from widget-paywall complaints). The confirmed-payer list is **fully hand-curated from context snippets**, not regex output.

**Counting rules.** Theme membership is **non-exclusive** — a single review can count toward several themes (`14060380344` appears under confirmed payer, widget paywall, entitlement failure and 1★ analysis). Percentages sum to well over 100% and must not be added. Every percentage uses 10,621 as its denominator unless the row explicitly says otherwise; country rates use that country's own total, and "of payers" uses 45. Those are **labelled segment rates and are not substituted for the global band classification.**

**External sources.** Two, both accessed **2026-09-09**: (1) Apple iTunes Lookup API for id 1445348921, US storefront — version 4.1.0, `currentVersionReleaseDate` 2026-08-31, `releaseDate` 2019-08-17, price $0.00, minimum iOS 15.0, 19,073 US ratings at 4.821, release notes naming Achievements; (2) the public App Store product page for the same id — the in-app-purchase price list, "Languages: English", the device compatibility list, and the 12+ age rating. **Every other statement in this report derives from review text.** The store's 19,073 US ratings vs this corpus's 5,600 US written reviews is the expected gap between raters and reviewers, not a discrepancy.

**Theme definitions.** *Widget paywall* = the reviewer states a widget/Lock-Screen/Home-Screen feature requires payment; separated from *widget broken*, which reports a technical failure with no payment language. *Confirmed payer* = the reviewer states in the first person that they bought, paid for, subscribed to, or were charged for the app; stated intent to buy, price commentary, "worth every penny" idioms and free-trial mentions are excluded. *Data loss* = counters, history or notes reported as vanished. *Paywall general* = any statement that a capability requires payment, including but not limited to the widget. *Pause/archive* = a request to stop counting without deleting history. *Milestones* = any request for badges, achievements, celebration or reward at a threshold.

**Limitations and known error risk.**
- **The July 2025 widget-paywall event cannot be fully adjudicated from reviews alone.** Developer replies (visible only as reviewer quotations) framed it as a bug; two reviewers report a reversal; 31 reviewers dated 2026 still report it. The report states both readings and does not choose between them.
- **Selection bias runs in both directions.** 22.1% of bodies are under 40 characters and average 4.83, inflating the mean; and reviewers everywhere skew toward the delighted and the furious. The honest read on the free-tier experience is probably close to the 4.77 mean; the paid experience is materially worse than the corpus mean and is measured on only 45 records.
- **n = 45 confirmed payers is small.** The 3.91 payer mean rests on 45 reviews; a five-review swing moves it by roughly 0.1. The *direction* (payers rate lower than the corpus) is robust; the exact figure is not.
- **Only 23 storefronts clear the 50-review bar,** and eight of those sit between 50 and 71 reviews where a single review moves a rate by 1.4–2.0 points. Denmark's and Poland's elevated widget-paywall rates rest on 2 and 3 reviews respectively and are flagged as such.
- **Non-English markets are structurally under-represented.** The app is English-only; only 0.63% of bodies use a non-Latin script. Absence of localisation complaints is not evidence of localisation adequacy.
- **Review volume is used as an engagement proxy only.** No download, revenue or spend data was available. The "high-spend markets" grouping is defined qualitatively from Apple's consistently top-spending storefronts and is disclosed as such.
- **Rating is not read as a feature preference,** and no correlation here is asserted as causation. Where a causal path is claimed — the July 2025 widget paywall caused the Q3 2025 rating collapse — it rests on three independent lines of evidence: the monthly complaint spike (1 → 39 in a single month), the quarterly 1–2★ discontinuity (1.75% → 8.64%), and reviewers narrating the sequence themselves in the first person.
