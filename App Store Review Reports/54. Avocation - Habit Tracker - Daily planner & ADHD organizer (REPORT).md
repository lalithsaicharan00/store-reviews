# Avocation — Habit Tracker (App Store ID 1479581895) — Full Review Analysis

**App:** Avocation - Habit Tracker — *Daily planner & ADHD organizer*
**Developer of record:** David Joech · **Bundle:** `app.avocation.avocation`
**Corpus:** 369 written reviews · 43 storefronts · 21 December 2019 → 19 August 2026
**Corpus mean rating:** 3.981 · **Extracted:** 8 September 2026 (`itunes MZStore userReviewsRow`)
**Analysis date:** 12 September 2026
**Business model (review-derived):** Free download · **hard free-tier cap of 3 habits** (reported inconsistently — see §2.2) · **one-time "lifetime" Pro unlock**, no subscription, **no ads**. Observed Pro prices in review text span **$9.99–$15 / £9 / €9.99–17 / ¥68–98 / NT$490 / ฿399 / R$25**. **44 reviewers (11.92%) state outright that they paid.**

---

## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Every claim traces to an exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`. **§9.6 is a complete 369-row index** — every review in the corpus, with ID, storefront, star rating, date, verbatim title and assigned themes. Nothing in this report is unreachable from that table.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several. All 369 records carry at least one theme (mean 2.92 themes per review, max 11, 90 records carry exactly one).
- Signal thresholds: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority. **The denominator for every global percentage in this report is 369.** One review is 0.27%. Country-level and segment-level denominators are always stated beside the number.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such. Non-English reviews are quoted in the original with a labelled translation.

### ⚠️ Eight warnings before any number here is used

1. **Only one storefront clears the 50-review threshold.** The United States has **119 reviews (32.25%)**. The next largest is Germany at 36, then Great Britain at 31, China 24, Ukraine 18, Spain 17, Canada 16. **Part 6 therefore makes exactly one standalone country claim (US) and labels every other storefront limited evidence.** Country percentages for sub-50 storefronts are shown for shape only and must not be quoted as market findings.
2. **The corpus spans 6 years 8 months and the product changed inside it.** 2019: 1 review · 2020: 60 · 2021: 85 · 2022: 104 · 2023: 64 · 2024: 30 · 2025: 16 · 2026: 9. **Review volume peaked in 2022 and has fallen ~91% since.** Any theme percentage computed over the whole corpus averages across a period in which the free cap, the price, and the app's maintenance status all appear to have moved. Part 7 splits the corpus into three documented periods for exactly this reason, and period percentages should be preferred over whole-corpus percentages wherever a trend claim is being made.
3. **Falling review volume is itself ambiguous and is not treated as a finding about product quality.** A drop from 104 reviews (2022) to 9 (2026 partial) is consistent with declining installs, with Apple's review-prompt behaviour, with the developer no longer prompting, or with all three. **This corpus cannot distinguish them.** It is reported as a fact about the corpus in §1.4 and used nowhere as evidence.
4. **No review burst, no vote manipulation, and no duplicate authors were found.** 369 reviews across **324 distinct days**; the busiest day has 4 reviews (2021-01-23, 2022-01-21); the busiest month has 19 (January 2022). **No author name appears twice.** 53 reviews (14.36%) carry at least one helpfulness vote, maximum `vote_sum` 6. 3 reviews are flagged `is_edited` (`9061891888`, `6980535429`, `7624659981`) — one of them (`8986983542`, US) contains a visible "EDIT" block in its body though the flag is false, so **the `is_edited` field is unreliable and is not used for any finding.** No reviewer mentions being asked or paid for a review.
5. **This corpus over-weights the moment of first contact with the paywall.** The median body is 164 characters and 82 of 369 bodies (22.22%) are 60 characters or shorter. The single most common complaint — the 3-habit free cap — is by construction something a reviewer hits in the first session. **Interpretation: the corpus tells you a great deal about why people do and do not convert, and much less about why people stay. Retention evidence in this corpus is limited to the 9 reviewers (2.44%) who state a duration of use.**
6. **Classification was hand-curated, not automated.** All 369 records were read individually and in full, in their original language — English, German (36), Simplified Chinese (24), Traditional Chinese (11), Spanish (17 + MX/CO/EC/DO/PE/PY), Ukrainian and Russian (18), Portuguese (10), Italian (7), Turkish (7), Polish (4), Arabic (3), Thai (1), Vietnamese (4), French (1, in CA). **47 records (12.74%) are written in a non-Latin script.** 90 themes, every one a hand-assigned ID list; no regex or clustering assigned any theme. The map is saved at `temp/54-review-classification.py` and validated programmatically (§1.5).
7. **No external source could be used.** `apps.apple.com` and the iTunes lookup API are both blocked by this environment's network egress policy (verified 12 September 2026, §9.5). **Every statement in this report about the price, the free-tier cap, the feature set and the Pro contents is derived from review text only**, and is therefore a reconstruction of what users believed they were buying — which for a monetization analysis is arguably the more useful artefact, but is not the same as the listing. Where reviewers contradict each other about the model, the contradiction is reported rather than resolved (§2.2).
8. **Star rating is not treated as a feature preference.** The most design-praised theme in this corpus, `aesthetic_design` (128 reviews), appears in 9 two-star reviews. The corpus's angriest segment — confirmed payers — has a mean rating of **3.091 against a corpus mean of 3.981**. Rating and sentiment are reported separately throughout.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. This is a beautiful product with a monetization wound, and the two facts are the same size.**
**128 of 369 reviewers (34.69%, high-priority, mean ★ 4.36) praise the visual design** — the largest theme in the corpus by a factor of two. **85 of 369 (23.04%, high-priority, mean ★ 3.106) raise some form of paywall or pricing friction.** These are not separate populations: 9 of the 23 two-star reviews and a third of the three-star reviews praise the design in the same breath as they attack the paywall. *"Muito bonitinho porém … Só da para ter dois hábitos se quiser mais tem que pagar 25"* / "Very cute, but — you only get two habits, if you want more you have to pay 25" (`10137074917`, BR, 1★). **Interpretation: design is not the constraint on this business. The shape of the free tier is.**

**2. The 3-habit free cap is the single most-discussed product decision in the corpus, and it reads as a wall rather than a sample.**
**44 reviewers (11.92%, high-priority, mean ★ 3.16) complain specifically about the free habit limit** — and the anger is concentrated at the exact moment of collision: *"I was having fun setting up my habits until I maxed out my 3 free habits before being required to pay $15 … Deleted immediately"* (`6883715554`, US, 2★); *"免費習慣只有三個，太令人傻眼了… 早睡、早起、運動，就三個用掉了"* / "Only three free habits, that's shocking — early to bed, early to rise, exercise, and all three are used up" (`7892340081`, TW, 3★); *"真沒想到只能列三個習慣～直接刪掉"* / "Never imagined you could only list three habits. Deleted it straight away" (`7165608219`, TW, 1★). A further **11 reviewers (2.98%, meaningful, mean ★ 1.91) go further and call the free tier useless** — *"A paid app disguised as free"* (`10579933045`, US, 1★). **Interpretation: three habits is below the minimum viable habit set that users arrive with. The TW reviewer names it exactly — sleep, wake, exercise is three. The cap does not demonstrate the product; it terminates the trial.**

**3. Post-purchase failure is the most damaging thing in this corpus and it is nearly invisible in the aggregate.**
**12 reviewers (3.25%, very strong) report that a completed purchase did not work — and their mean rating is 1.083, the lowest of any theme in the report.** Two distinct failures: **Pro never unlocks** (6 reviews, 1.63%, mean ★ 1.17) and **Pro cannot be restored after reinstall or device change** (7 reviews, 1.90%, **mean ★ 1.000 — all seven are one-star**). *"I paid for the app and absolutely nothing. It's exactly as it was before. They didn't ask for any details so how do they know I've paid???"* (`9025109463`, AU, 1★); *"買了490的永久功能 反而一點都不能用還不讓人退費根本詐欺"* / "I bought the NT$490 permanent unlock and it doesn't work at all and they won't refund — this is simply fraud" (`6759577812`, TW, 1★); *"ซื้อ Membership Lifetime ในราคา 399 บาท … หลังจากที่ฉันลบแอปแล้วติดตั้งใหม่ พบว่า Membership แบบ Lifetime ไม่สามารถใช้งานได้"* / "I bought Lifetime Membership for ฿399 … after deleting and reinstalling, the Lifetime membership does not work" (`12201812878`, TH, 1★, quoting an order ID). **5 reviewers (1.36%) ask for a refund in the review body; all five are one-star.** **Interpretation: for a one-time-purchase product, restore-purchases is not a feature, it is the product's warranty. It is failing, it is failing across seven storefronts and six years (2021→2026), and every single instance costs a one-star review.**

**4. "Advanced Statistics" is a promise the Pro tier does not keep, and payers say so in the paid tier's own words.**
**12 reviewers (3.25%, very strong, mean ★ 2.58) describe a gap between what they paid for and what they got — 10 of the 12 are confirmed payers (22.7% of the payer segment).** Three of them quote the marketing phrase back: *"I bought this app bcs it stated 'advanced statistics' for paid version. But I can not call these statistics even basic"* (`12680052718`, AL, 2★); *"pro which I paid for touts &#34;Advanced Statistics&#34; but all I see is a calendar"* (`9622751603`, AU, 4★); *"Obwohl ich Pro gekauft habe, gibt es keine erweiterten Statistiken"* / "Although I bought Pro, there are no advanced statistics" (`10904975030`, DE, 2★). Separately, **8 reviewers (2.17%) call the statistics too basic** and **28 (7.59%, high-priority) request longer-range history**. **Interpretation: the single named benefit of the paid tier is the one that under-delivers. This is a copy-versus-product mismatch, and it converts buyers into detractors rather than churners — which is worse, because they review.**

**5. The confirmed-payer segment is the angriest cohort in the corpus.**
**44 reviewers (11.92%, high-priority) explicitly state they bought Pro. Their mean rating is 3.091 against a corpus mean of 3.981, and their distribution is 12×5★ / 10×4★ / 4×3★ / 6×2★ / 12×1★ — payers are 3.3× more likely than the corpus to leave one star** (27.3% vs 8.4%). Segment rates within those 44 (labelled as segment rates, not global signals): value gap 22.7%, basic statistics 13.6%, purchase never unlocked 13.6%, restore failure 13.6%, calendar/stats bugs 11.4%, refund requested 11.4%, support unresponsive 9.1%. **Interpretation: paying is currently a risk transfer from the developer to the user. The 22 payers who rated 1–3★ are not disappointed by price — they are disappointed by delivery.**

**6. Widgets are the most-requested missing capability in the corpus and demand is accelerating, not decaying.**
**41 reviewers (11.11%, high-priority, mean ★ 4.29) ask for a home-screen or lock-screen widget** — and the period split is **P1 7.5% → P2 8.3% → P3 17.6%**, the steepest rise of any theme measured. Crucially, the request is framed as a retention mechanism, not a nicety: *"with my adhd, once it's closed i never use it and the reminders are too easy to ignore"* (`8214584771`, CA, 4★); *"i love this app i use it everyday but NEED a widget option! i forget to click the app if i can't see it. i'm autistic and this helps me alot"* (`10104424067`, GB, 4★); *"The main reason why I won't be able to stick to it is because there are no widgets … the ones that have stuck with me are the ones that show my streak/progress on a widget"* (`11512811846`, PH, 4★). **And it blocks revenue: 5 reviewers (1.36%) say a missing capability is what is stopping them buying, and 3 of those 5 name the widget** — *"I'm not sure to buy the subscription yet since I've noticed this app doesn't have a widget"* (`10307040667`, CO, 3★); *"如果能放成桌面小工具的話，點開的頻率會大大增加！才會更願意購買"* / "If there were a home-screen widget I'd open it far more often — then I'd be more willing to buy" (`8179279036`, TW, 4★). **Interpretation: this is the rare request that is simultaneously the top retention ask, the top platform gap, and a stated purchase blocker. It has been asked for since October 2020 (`6534928843`, CN).**

**7. The "no streaks, no shame" design is a genuine differentiator and it is under-exploited.**
**12 reviewers (3.25%, very strong, mean ★ 4.75) praise the deliberate absence of streaks and punishment** and they are unusually articulate about why: *"This is the first habit tracking app I have stuck with, most likely because it does not track streaks (which my black and white brain reads as failure if I am not perfect) … I work in mental health and I appreciate the compassion. These are skills. Skills can be learned. I am not broken."* (`6534235105`, US, 5★); *"I have adhd and using streaks has always resulted in beating myself up at the end of the week"* (`8266460563`, US, 5★); *"Fear-based, don't break the chain or streak methods never worked for me"* (`6458113686`, US, 5★). One reviewer says this is why they downloaded it at all — *"I originally got this app bc I read a review saying that this app doesn't track streaks so you don't feel like you've blown it if you miss a day and that really resonated with me"* (`7597717527`, US, 5★). **Against that, 7 reviewers (1.90%) ask for streaks or a calendar of dots.** **Interpretation: do not add streaks. Add the *evidence of consistency* people are actually asking for (history, per-habit records) without the punishment semantics — the two are separable and the corpus proves users distinguish them.**

**8. The plant is the emotional engine, and when it breaks the product stops working.**
**59 reviewers (15.99%, high-priority) discuss the plant mechanic**: 35 (9.49%) are motivated by it, 10 (2.71%) find it underwhelming, 5 (1.36%) report it stuck or dead, 9 (2.44%) want a collection or garden, 9 (2.44%) want more game. When it works it is decisive — *"Growing my plant has 100% been the thing that has kept me on track"* (`5839377039`, US, 5★). When it stalls, users say it removes their reason to open the app: *"my plant says infinity days until next growth … It makes me want to stop using the app, since the little reward of growing the plant has been taken away"* (`8956070679`, US, 3★, a payer); *"la planta dejó de crecer después de 67 días de riego. ¿por qué hay este límite extraño?"* / "the plant stopped growing after 67 days of watering — why is there this strange limit?" (`10138813839`, ES, 3★); *"Leider geht das Wachstum der Pflanze nicht weiter … obwohl ich mir die Pro Version besorgt habe"* / "Sadly the plant's growth doesn't continue … even though I bought the Pro version" (`14161103908`, DE, 1★, the most recent German review in the corpus). **Interpretation: "infinity days until next growth" is a single defect string that appears in three separate reviews across three storefronts. It is the highest-leverage bug in the report.**

**9. Habits that are not daily cannot be modelled, and this is the largest functional gap after the paywall.**
**24 reviewers (6.50%, high-priority, mean ★ 3.79) need a frequency the app cannot express** — "X times a week without naming the days" dominates. *"I'd like to work out 3 days a week. I don't care or know when those days will be but this app makes you designate them … This simply Is not congruent with most of my goals making This app unusable for me"* (`9511531312`, US, 4★); *"Da mein Beruf und der Dienst den ich versehe jeden Tag unterschiedlich ist, kann ich nie sagen, an welchen Tagen ich es zum Sport schaffe … So ist die App für mich leider nicht zu gebrauchen"* / "Because my job and shifts differ every day I can never say which days I'll make it to sport … so the app is unfortunately unusable for me" (`6407176617`, DE, 3★). A related **15 reviewers (4.07%) cannot backfill a missed day** — *"Sometimes you need to mark yesterday habit as done if you forgot … Started to test it and deleted on the second day because of the issue"* (`10955907409`, UA, 2★). **Interpretation: two reviewers use the word "unusable" about a scheduling model, not about a bug. This gap converts otherwise-satisfied users into churn.**

**10. The app is visibly unmaintained, and users have started saying so out loud.**
**9 reviewers (2.44%, meaningful, mean ★ 2.33) state the app is no longer being updated, and 6 (1.63%, mean ★ 1.50) that support does not answer.** Both rise sharply in the recent period: abandonment **P1 0.9% → P2 1.4% → P3 5.0%**; support-unresponsive **P1 0.0% → P2 0.7% → P3 4.2%**. *"dass so ein Basic Fehler nach scheinbar 1 Jahr (letztes Update) immer noch nicht gefixt ist, geht echt nicht! Da fühlt man sich etwas verarscht"* / "that such a basic bug still isn't fixed after apparently a year (last update) is really not on — you feel a bit taken for a ride" (`12115176508`, DE, 2★); *"I know from Reddit that you aren't spending much time on this app anymore, but please fix this small necessary thing! I would pay for the premium version again in a heartbeat!"* (`10462417239`, US, 4★); *"Your support pages and email are down"* (`14383965056`, GR, 1★, August 2026). **Interpretation: the corpus's most recent months are dominated not by feature complaints but by abandonment signals. The 2026 mean rating is 3.111 with 4 of 9 reviews at one star. `10462417239` is the important one — a user volunteering to pay again, blocked by a maintenance state.**

**11. The one-time purchase model is a real asset and users defend it unprompted.**
**14 reviewers (3.79%, very strong, mean ★ 4.93) praise the lifetime/one-time model explicitly, and 16 (4.34%, mean ★ 4.69) call the price fair** — against 16 (4.34%, mean ★ 2.75) who call it too high. *"I love the honesty of a pricier lifetime membership versus the subscription model that so many 'extortionists' are using now"* (`8528259165`, US, 5★); *"Schön, schlank, kein Abo … Darüber hinaus mag ich keine Abos"* / "Pretty, lean, no subscription … beyond that I don't like subscriptions" (`8150960736`, DE, 5★); *"ich finde es gut dass es eine einmalige Zahlung ist denn Abos Stressen mich total"* / "I like that it's a one-off payment because subscriptions totally stress me out" (`11652337363`, DE, 5★). **4 reviewers (1.08%) say they bought specifically to support the developer.** **Interpretation: do not convert this to a subscription. The pricing complaint is about the *gate*, not the *model* — price objections cluster in lower-ARPU storefronts (CN, TW, PH, TR, CO: 9 of 16) while model praise clusters in US/DE.**

**12. The cheapest high-value moves, in evidence order:** fix restore-purchases and make it discoverable (finding 3; 12 reviewers, mean ★ 1.083, every one recoverable) → fix or remove the "infinity days until next growth" plant state (finding 8; `8956070679`, `10078139554`, `14161103908`, `7793589747`, `10138813839`) → either build the "Advanced Statistics" the paid tier advertises or stop advertising it (finding 4; 12 reviewers, 10 of them payers) → raise the free habit cap from 3 to 5–7, which is the range reviewers themselves propose (finding 2; `8285309663`, `8784039212`, `7824596230`, `6249084137` all name 5–8) → ship a widget (finding 6; 41 reviewers, rising, and a named purchase blocker) → add "X times per week" frequency and past-day backfill (finding 9; 37 unique reviewers between them, 10.03%) → publish a maintenance signal of any kind, because the corpus's most recent theme is abandonment (finding 10).

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Parse and reconcile.** `temp/54-stats.py` parsed `reviews.jsonl`, recomputed every manifest figure, and cross-checked all 43 country files. All checks in §1.3 passed before any reading began.
2. **Dump for reading.** `temp/54-dump.py` wrote all 369 records to `temp/54-all-reviews.txt`, sorted by storefront then date, one record per line, with ID, storefront, rating, date, votes, edit flag, verbatim title and verbatim body. **No field was dropped and no text was truncated.**
3. **Read every record.** All 369 were read in sequence, in the original language, in nine passes of ~40 records. Non-Latin-script records (47, 12.74%) were read in the original; translations in this report are the analyst's and are labelled.
4. **Hand-curate the map.** 90 themes were defined while reading and each was written out as an explicit list of record positions in `temp/54-review-classification.py`. **No regex, clustering, sentiment model or keyword rule assigned any theme to any review.**
5. **Validate programmatically.** Running `temp/54-review-classification.py` asserts, and this run passed: **(a)** zero unknown or out-of-range indices; **(b)** zero intra-theme duplicates; **(c)** every theme has a declared family; **(d)** **zero unassigned records** — all 369 carry ≥1 theme. Output: `VALIDATION PASSED — 369 records, 90 themes, 1079 theme-assignments, 0 unassigned`.
6. **Aggregate.** `temp/54-aggregate.py` and `temp/54-cuts.py` computed every count, percentage, mean rating, rating split, storefront split, period split, family union and segment rate reproduced in this report. No number in this report was typed by hand from memory.
7. **Audit the map after the fact.** `temp/54-price-cap.py` ran keyword sweeps for price tokens and free-tier cap statements across all 369 bodies **as a check on the hand map, not as a classifier**. Its output is reported directly in §2.2 and §5.2 and is the basis for the free-tier inconsistency finding.
8. **Verify every cited ID.** §9.7 re-reads the finished report, extracts every `review_id` mentioned anywhere in it, and checks each against `reviews.jsonl`.

**Known error risk.** Hand classification of 369 multilingual records carries judgement error concentrated at three boundaries, all of which are documented in §9.1: (a) *request* vs *complaint* — a reviewer who says "I wish there were widgets" is counted as `req_widget`, not as a defect; (b) *`paid_confirmed`* — counted only where the reviewer states a completed purchase; ambiguous phrasing such as "I highly recommend the paid version" (`8175155082`) was **excluded**, so this segment is a floor, not a ceiling; (c) translation nuance in Chinese, Thai, Ukrainian and Arabic records, where classification relied on the analyst's reading of the original.

## 1.6 Limitations and known biases

1. **Self-selection.** App Store reviewers are not users. This corpus over-represents people with something to say at a decision point — most often the paywall.
2. **One storefront only.** 42 of 43 storefronts are below the 50-review threshold. Everything in Part 6 outside the US section is shape, not finding.
3. **No external ground truth.** Price, free-tier size and Pro contents are reconstructed from reviewer claims and are internally inconsistent (§2.2). Egress to `apps.apple.com` was blocked (§9.5).
4. **No version field.** The corpus carries no app-version data. Bugs cannot be tied to builds, and "fixed" cannot be distinguished from "stopped being reported".
5. **`is_edited` is unreliable.** Flagged true on 3 records (`9061891888`, `6980535429`, `7624659981`), yet `8986983542` (US) contains a literal `EDIT` block in its body and is flagged false. **The field is not used for any finding.** The practical consequence: an unknown number of these reviews are revisions, so a rating may not correspond to the text's first draft.
6. **Six years is not one product.** Themes are averaged across a period in which the free cap, the price, and the app's maintenance state all appear to have changed. Prefer Part 7's period figures for any trend claim.
7. **Translation risk.** 47 records (12.74%) are non-Latin-script. Translations are the analyst's own; the originals are quoted alongside so any reader can check.
8. **Rating ≠ sentiment.** Nine 2★ reviews praise the design; eleven reviews of 4★ or better report a defect, a value gap or an abandonment signal (§4.6). Star ratings are never used alone as evidence of preference.

## 5.2 What triggers a purchase

Six distinct triggers appear in the text, ordered by how often payers describe them.

# PART 7 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 7.1 Method

**The corpus is split into three contiguous periods of comparable size**, chosen so that each holds roughly a third of the records rather than to fit any hypothesis:

| Period | Window | n | % of corpus | Mean ★ | 5/4/3/2/1 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **P1** | 2019-12-21 → 2021-06-30 | 106 | 28.73% | 4.038 | 51/27/15/7/6 |
| **P2** | 2021-07-01 → 2022-12-31 | 144 | 39.02% | **4.139** | 73/43/13/5/10 |
| **P3** | 2023-01-01 → 2026-08-19 | 119 | 32.25% | **3.739** | 49/31/13/11/15 |

**Limits of this method, stated before any trend:** (a) P3 covers 44 months against P1's 19 and P2's 18, so P3 averages over a much longer and thinner stretch; (b) the corpus has no version field, so no trend can be tied to a release; (c) at these sizes a 2-point percentage move is 2–3 reviews and is not a trend. **Every trend below is labelled with a confidence level, and the low-confidence ones are reported rather than relied on.**

## 7.2 Trend 1 — Widget demand more than doubled · **emerging, high confidence**

**P1 8 (7.5%) → P2 12 (8.3%) → P3 21 (17.6%).** The steepest rise of any theme in the report, and the only request theme that grows in both directions (absolute count rises in P3 despite P3 having fewer reviews per month than P2). **Interpretation: this tracks the platform, not the product — iOS 14 shipped home-screen widgets in September 2020 and lock-screen widgets arrived in iOS 16 (2022), and the first widget request in the corpus is 14 October 2020, one month after iOS 14. The demand is now a platform expectation, not a nice-to-have. High confidence because the direction, the magnitude and the external cause all agree.**

## 7.3 Trend 2 — The paid segment grew, then soured · **meaningful, high confidence**

**`paid_confirmed`: P1 5 (4.7%) → P2 19 (13.2%) → P3 20 (16.8%).** Payers become a steadily larger share of reviewers. **But over the same span:**

- `paywall_value_gap`: P1 **0 (0.0%)** → P2 5 (3.5%) → P3 7 (5.9%)
- `stats_too_basic`: P1 **0 (0.0%)** → P2 1 (0.7%) → P3 7 (5.9%)
- `bug_calendar_stats_wrong`: P1 **0 (0.0%)** → P2 3 (2.1%) → P3 6 (5.0%)
- `restore_purchase_failure`: P1 1 (0.9%) → P2 1 (0.7%) → P3 **5 (4.2%)**

**Interpretation, high confidence because four independent themes move together in the same direction:** as the installed base of payers grew, the failures that only payers can experience became a larger share of the review corpus. **P1 contains zero value-gap complaints and zero statistics complaints — these are not launch problems, they are accumulated-base problems.** The mean rating decline from 4.139 (P2) to 3.739 (P3) is largely this.

## 7.4 Trend 3 — Abandonment signals appeared and are now the defining recent theme · **meaningful, high confidence**

**`abandonment_no_updates`: P1 1 (0.9%) → P2 2 (1.4%) → P3 6 (5.0%). `support_unresponsive`: P1 0 (0.0%) → P2 1 (0.7%) → P3 5 (4.2%).**

The P3 instances escalate in specificity over time: 2023 *"I know from Reddit that you aren't spending much time on this app anymore"* (`10462417239`) → Dec 2024 *"nach scheinbar 1 Jahr (letztes Update)"* / "after apparently a year since the last update" (`12115176508`) → Jul 2025 *"I dont think they are updating the app anymore. I reported an error over email and heard nothing back"* (`12871248376`) → Jun 2026 *"der Anbieter regiert nicht bzw ist nicht zu erreichen"* / "the provider doesn't respond / can't be reached" (`14161103908`) → Aug 2026 *"Your support pages and email are down"* (`14383965056`). **Interpretation: reviewers moved from inferring neglect to reporting a closed support channel. In 2026 (n = 9) four reviews are one-star and the year's mean is 3.111 — the lowest of any year in the corpus.**

## 7.5 Trend 4 — Design praise is declining as a share, but not in kind · **persistent, medium confidence**

**`aesthetic_design`: P1 48 (45.3%) → P2 49 (34.0%) → P3 31 (26.1%).** A 19-point fall. **This is almost certainly composition, not deterioration:** the app's design did not change (no reviewer in P3 says it got worse; three say it is still excellent while rating 1–2★), but later reviewers have more to complain about, so design praise occupies less of each review. **Medium confidence** — the direction is unambiguous but the cause is inferred. **Nothing in P3 supports a claim that the design regressed.**

## 7.6 Trend 5 — The habit-cap complaint softened · **changing, medium confidence**

**`free_habit_limit_complaint`: P1 15 (14.2%) → P2 18 (12.5%) → P3 11 (9.2%).** And `price_too_high` fell from 7.5% → 2.8% → 3.4%. **Two candidate explanations, and the corpus cannot separate them:** (a) the US price fell from ~$15 (2021) to $9.99–10 (2022) — documented in §2.2 — which would reduce price anger; (b) later reviewers are disproportionately existing users who already resolved the paywall decision, so the cohort mix changed. **Medium confidence in the observation, low confidence in either explanation.** Note this trend runs *opposite* to the overall rating decline, which reinforces that the P3 rating drop is driven by post-purchase failure, not by pricing.

## 7.7 Trend 6 — Displacement wins are declining · **changing, medium confidence**

**`comparative_best`: P1 12 (11.3%) → P2 17 (11.8%) → P3 6 (5.0%).** The "I tried many apps and this is the best" review halved in P3. **Interpretation, cautious: this is consistent with a maturing category in which competitors have closed the design gap, and with a product that stopped shipping. It is 6 reviews in 44 months, so it is a signal to watch rather than to act on.** The same direction appears in `no_streak_shame_free` (3.8% → 4.2% → 1.7%) and `req_apple_watch` (1.9% → 4.2% → 0.8%).

## 7.8 Trend 7 — Outcome reports peaked in P2 · **persistent, medium confidence**

**`outcome_behavior_change`: P1 10 (9.4%) → P2 24 (16.7%) → P3 15 (12.6%).** Behaviour-change reports remain the third-largest theme in every period and never fall below 9%. **Interpretation: the core mechanism kept working throughout. Whatever is wrong with this product in P3, it is not that the app stopped helping people build habits.** This is the single most important stabilising fact in Part 7.

## 7.9 What the corpus cannot tell you about time

- **Nothing can be tied to a release.** No version field, no changelog, no external listing (§9.5).
- **Whether anything was ever fixed.** `bug_water_tracker` last appears in August 2020 and `bug_crash_lag` in February 2022. That is equally consistent with a fix and with nobody bothering to report it again.
- **Whether the 2026 rating collapse is real.** 2026 has 9 reviews. A mean of 3.111 on n = 9 is not a measurement; the 4 one-star reviews in it are individually meaningful (§6.8) and the average is not.
- **Why volume fell 91% from 2022 to 2026.** Stated as a corpus fact in §1.4 and used as evidence nowhere (warning 3).

## 8.2 Structural decisions — direction is clear, magnitude is not

### D1 · Raise the free habit cap to 5–8

**Evidence:** §3.5.1 — 44 cap complaints (11.92%, high-priority) + 11 "free version is useless" (2.98%, mean ★ 1.91) = **50 unique reviewers (13.55%)**. §5.4 — the cap is the top upgrade barrier. §2.2 — reviewers report the cap may already have been *reduced* from 5 to 3.

**Why 5–8:** the reviewers name the number themselves and they cluster: 5 (`7824596230`, `8784039212`), 5–7 (`8285309663`), 7–8 (`6249084137`), 8 (`12197119033`), 9 as 3×3 across the app's own time-of-day sections (`6039511469`), 10 (`7076973873`). **Nobody in this corpus asks for the app to be free.** They ask for the sample to be large enough to prove the product before paying for it.

**The counter-argument, stated fairly:** the cap is presumably the conversion mechanism, and 44 of the 44 complainants might convert rather than churn. **The corpus argues against that.** Of the 44, **23 rated 1–3★** (11×3★, 6×2★, 6×1★). Two state they deleted the app immediately on contact with the cap — *"直接刪掉"* / "deleted it straight away" (`7165608219`, TW) and *"Deleted immediately. Sad."* (`6883715554`, US) — and two more call the free tier unusable rather than limited (`7892340081` TW, `10772046188` GB). Meanwhile the strongest conversion stories in the corpus are people who got *enough* free room to be convinced: two weeks (`10801492320`), one week (`10851094567`), a few minutes but with the whole feature set visible (`8528259165`). **Interpretation: the cap currently terminates evaluation rather than gating it. Three habits is below the size of the habit set users arrive with — `7892340081` names it exactly: sleep, wake, exercise.**

**Do this as a measured experiment, not a belief.** Raise to 6 in a subset of storefronts and compare purchase rate and 30-day retention. The corpus can tell you the cap hurts; only an experiment can tell you what it earns.

### D2 · Keep the one-time purchase. Fix its price geography instead.

**Evidence:** §3.3.6 — 42 unique reviewers (11.38%) give a positive monetization signal; 14 praise the one-time model at mean ★ 4.93 and several name subscription fatigue explicitly. §5.4 and §6.9 — **price objections are 3.1× more frequent outside high-spend storefronts** (8.3% vs 2.7%), with 9 of 16 in CN/TW/PH/TR/CO.

**Recommendation: do not move to a subscription.** The model is an asset with named advocates. **Do introduce storefront-tiered pricing**, because `7157687565`'s objection is structural: a ¥98 one-time price competes at the point of purchase against a ¥3/month anchor. The same logic covers TW (NT$490), PH, TR and CO.

**Also fix the subscription confusion (§2.2):** 5 reviewers believe there is a subscription and one reports the *store listing itself* advertising a €2.50 subscription the app did not sell (`7477741748`, who says they would have bought it immediately). If a listing inconsistency of that kind still exists, it is both a lost sale and a trust cost.

### D3 · Ship a widget — it is the top request, it is accelerating, and it blocks revenue

**Evidence:** §3.4 rank 1 — 41 reviewers (11.11%, high-priority), **P1 7.5% → P2 8.3% → P3 17.6%** (§7.2), open since 14 Oct 2020. 19 of the 41 are five-star reviewers (§4.1). 3 of the 5 `missing_feature_blocks_purchase` reviewers name it (§3.5.1).

**Build it as a completion surface, not a display surface.** The corpus is specific: users want to *tap* the widget (`12792214076`: *"being able to tap the widget to update/complete a habit"*), and they want it because the app is otherwise out of sight (`8214584771`, `10104424067`, `11512811846` — all of whom tie it to ADHD, autism, or simply forgetting the app exists). **A read-only widget solves the discovery half and misses the logging half.**

### D4 · Build the consistency record — 48 reviewers (13.01%) are asking for the same thing

**Evidence:** §3.4(b) — `req_history_stats` 28 + `req_per_habit_stats` 16 + `req_streaks_calendar` 7 + `stats_too_basic` 8 = **48 unique reviewers, 13.01%, high-priority, mean ★ 3.83** — the second-largest product need in the corpus after the paywall.

**What they want, precisely:** to pick one habit and see how consistently they have done it over months, not days. *"I would love to be able to track my habits and look back on progress…. That seems to be a major point of tracking, right?"* (`10209487543`). **Not** streaks-with-punishment: §3.3.3 shows 12 reviewers chose this product *because* it refuses streaks, and one adopted it on that basis alone (`7597717527`).

**Design constraint from the corpus itself: the evidence of consistency and the punishment for breaking it are separable, and this user base wants the first without the second.** Colour-density calendars, per-habit completion counts and monthly totals deliver it; a streak counter with a reset does not.

### D5 · Let habits be non-daily

**Evidence:** §3.4 rank 3 — 24 reviewers (6.50%, high-priority, mean ★ 3.79) for "X times per week" without naming days; 15 (4.07%) for backfilling a missed day; **37 unique (10.03%) between them**. Two reviewers use the word "unusable" about the scheduling model, not about a bug (`9511531312` US, `6407176617` DE).

**Two mechanics, both small:** (a) a frequency type of "N times per week/month, any day"; (b) allow editing any past day, not just yesterday. **(b) is arguably a bug rather than a feature** — `10955907409` (UA) deleted the app on day two over it, and `8900579113` (US) shows how (a) and (b) compound: if you can only schedule Mon/Wed/Thu and you actually worked out on Tuesday, you cannot record it at all.

### D6 · Claim the shame-free and neurodivergent positioning you already have

**Evidence:** §3.3.3 — 12 reviewers (3.25%, very strong, mean ★ 4.75) praise the absence of streaks, 8 of them American (§6.2). §3.4/appendix — 7 reviewers (1.90%, meaningful) self-identify as ADHD, autistic or anxious, all 4–5★. The storefront subtitle recorded for this app in this repository is *"Daily planner & ADHD organizer"*, yet **no reviewer in the corpus mentions having been sold on that** — every one of the seven discovers the fit themselves. (The live listing could not be checked, §9.5.)

**Interpretation: the product's most defensible differentiator is a design philosophy that the corpus articulates better than any marketing copy could.** `6534235105`'s review — *"These are skills. Skills can be learned. I am not broken."* — written by someone who says they work in mental health, is the positioning statement.

## 8.3 Experiments worth running

| Experiment | Hypothesis from the corpus | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| **Free cap 3 → 6** in matched storefronts | The cap terminates evaluation rather than gating it (D1) | Purchase rate, D30 retention, 1★ rate |
| **Storefront-tiered pricing** in CN/TW/PH/TR/BR | Price objection is an anchor problem, not a willingness problem (D2, §6.5) | Purchase rate per storefront |
| **Interactive widget** vs read-only widget | Users want to log, not just look (D3) | DAU, completion rate, review mentions |
| **Explicit "Restore purchase" entry point** on the paywall | Some purchase failures are discovery failures, not entitlement failures (F1) | Support volume, 1★ rate |
| **Onboarding for the water jar and repotting** | 10 reviewers (2.71%) could not work out three specific mechanics (§3.5.5) | Feature engagement, `onboarding_confusion` rate in new reviews |

## 8.4 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

1. **What is the actual free-tier cap today, and has it changed?** §2.2 documents five incompatible answers across six years. Only the build can settle it.
2. **Are restore failures a regional entitlement bug or a behavioural artefact?** §6.9 finding 2 shows a 6× concentration outside high-spend storefronts, and the corpus cannot distinguish a StoreKit problem from users changing devices more often. **Receipts data settles this in an hour.**
3. **Do cap complainants convert or churn?** The corpus contains only the people who wrote a review. Both populations are invisible.
4. **Why did review volume fall 91% from 2022?** Installs, prompt behaviour, or both (warning 3).
5. **Did any of these bugs get fixed?** No version field, no changelog, no reachable listing (§7.9, §9.5).
6. **What does the App Store listing actually promise?** Egress-blocked (§9.5). Three separate findings — Advanced Statistics (F4), the €2.50 subscription (D2), the 5-habit free tier (§2.2) — are reviewers reporting the listing and the product disagreeing. **All three are unverifiable from here and all three are cheap to check.**

## 8.5 What not to change

- **Do not add streaks with penalties.** 12 reviewers chose this product for their absence; at least one adopted it on that basis alone (`7597717527`). Ship the consistency record (D4) instead.
- **Do not add features that are visible.** 60 reviewers (16.26%) praise the restraint, several by explicit contrast with competitors (*"Unlike some other apps which include too many unnecessary settings"*, `9624026814`). Every item in §8.2 must ship without the home screen getting busier.
- **Do not redesign.** 128 reviewers (34.69%) praise the design and **no 1★ review criticises it**. The design is the moat.
- **Do not move to a subscription.** §3.3.6, D2 — the one-time model has 14 explicit advocates at mean ★ 4.93 and zero detractors of the *model* (as opposed to the price).
- **Do not remove the plant.** 35 reviewers are motivated by it. Fix it (F2) and extend it (a garden or shelf: 9 requests) rather than replacing it.

# PART 9 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 9.1 Theme definitions and boundary rules

90 themes, grouped by family. A review may carry any number of them.

### Praise — UX
- **`aesthetic_design`** — praise for the visual design, illustrations, colours, "cute", "aesthetic", "beautiful". **Boundary:** praise for the *plant* or the *avocado* specifically goes to those themes; this theme is the general look.
- **`simplicity_minimalism`** — praise for restraint: uncluttered, not overwhelming, "just what you need", explicit contrast with feature-heavy competitors.
- **`ease_of_use`** — praise for being easy, intuitive, quick to set up or operate. **Boundary:** distinct from simplicity — a thing can be simple and still awkward (`8787273217` praises ease, `10309242210` reports the opposite).
- **`time_of_day_structure`** — names the morning/afternoon/evening/any-time grouping as a benefit.
- **`customization_praise`** — praise for being able to customise (names, colours, icons, days, times).
- **`reliability_praise`** — explicitly praises stability, usually by contrast with crashing competitors. n = 1.
- **`localization_praise`** — praises the app being available in the reviewer's language. n = 1.

### Praise — core mechanic
- **`plant_growth_motivation`** — the growing plant/tree is described as motivating. **Boundary:** requests for *more* plant go to `req_plant_variety_collection` or `req_gamification_more`; disappointment goes to `plant_underwhelming`; a stuck plant goes to `bug_plant_not_growing`. A review can carry both praise and a gap.
- **`avocado_mascot`** — names the avocado character specifically.
- **`no_streak_shame_free`** — praises the absence of streaks, chains, punishment or pressure.
- **`lessons_articles_praise`** — praises the in-app habit-science lessons/articles/videos.
- **`water_tracker_praise`** — praises the water jar or credits it with drinking more.
- **`reminders_praise`** — praises reminders/notifications as working or useful.

### Outcome, displacement, advocacy, acquisition
- **`outcome_behavior_change`** — reports a changed behaviour or a concrete result. **Boundary:** "I love it" is not an outcome; "it got me to drink more water" is.
- **`daily_sustained_use`** — states repeated or long-duration use (days/weeks/months named, or "every day", "I keep coming back").
- **`comparative_best`** — states they tried alternatives and this one won, or that it is the best they have used.
- **`advocacy_recommend`** — explicitly recommends it to others.
- **`no_ads`** — praises the absence of advertising.
- **`developer_responsive_praise`** — praises support or developer responsiveness.
- **`discovery_wom`** — names word of mouth (a friend, another review) as the discovery route.
- **`generic_praise_no_detail`** — positive with no attributable reason ("круто", "Top das Ding", "muy útil"). **Included so that every record is accounted for and so that undifferentiated praise is never miscounted as evidence for a feature.**

### Monetization — positive
- **`onetime_purchase_praise`** — praises the lifetime/one-time model, usually against subscriptions.
- **`price_fair_praise`** — calls the price fair, reasonable, affordable or worth it.
- **`free_tier_praise`** — says the free version is generous or sufficient.
- **`willing_to_support_dev`** — states they paid (or would) to support the developer rather than to unlock a feature.
- **`willing_to_pay_intent`** — states an intention to buy, not a completed purchase.
- **`missing_feature_blocks_purchase`** — states a specific capability whose absence is preventing purchase. **The highest-value monetization theme per review in this report.**

### Monetization — segment
- **`paid_confirmed`** — the reviewer states a completed purchase. **Excluded:** intent ("I'll buy it"), recommendation of the paid tier without a stated purchase (`8175155082`), and praise of the price without a purchase claim. **This makes the segment a floor.**

### Monetization — friction
- **`free_habit_limit_complaint`** — names the habit cap as a problem.
- **`free_version_useless`** — says the free tier is not usable as a product ("paid app disguised as free", "does literally nothing").
- **`price_too_high`** — objects to the amount. **Boundary:** distinct from the cap — a reviewer can accept the cap and reject the price, or vice versa.
- **`paywall_value_gap`** — paid, and the product did not deliver what the paid tier promised. **Boundary:** distinct from `purchase_not_unlocked` (which is a failed entitlement, not a disappointing one).
- **`no_trial_requested`** — asks for a trial or a refund window before committing.
- **`nonhabit_gating_complaint`** — objects to *non-habit* things being gated: colours, pots, icons, multiple reminders, statistics.
- **`paywall_objection_general`** — generic "it costs money" with no specific gate named.
- **`paywall_prompt_friction`** — the upgrade prompt is intrusive, or still appears after purchase.
- **`subscription_confusion`** — believes the product is or might be a subscription.
- **`listing_mismatch`** — reports the store listing and the app disagreeing.
- **`upgrade_flow_broken`** — told to upgrade but cannot find a way to. n = 1.

### Monetization — post-purchase failure
- **`purchase_not_unlocked`** — paid, and Pro never activated.
- **`restore_purchase_failure`** — had Pro, lost it after reinstall, device change or platform change, and cannot restore.
- **`refund_request`** — asks for money back in the review body.

### Requests
Twenty-four request themes, defined by the capability asked for: `req_widget`, `req_apple_watch`, `req_ipad_mac`, `req_dark_mode`, `req_sync_backup`, `req_health_integration`, `req_flexible_frequency`, `req_backfill_past`, `req_quit_bad_habits`, `req_onetime_tasks`, `req_undo_uncheck`, `req_habit_units_measure`, `req_timer`, `req_history_stats`, `req_per_habit_stats`, `req_streaks_calendar`, `req_reorder_habits`, `req_more_customization`, `req_plant_variety_collection`, `req_gamification_more`, `req_notes_journal`, `req_more_reminders`, `req_localization`, `req_social_sharing`, plus `lessons_content_gap` (more or updated lesson content).

**The governing boundary rule for this family: a request is a capability that does not exist. A defect is a capability that exists and misbehaves.** "I wish there were widgets" is `req_widget`; "the calendar shows the wrong data" is `bug_calendar_stats_wrong`. Where a reviewer is ambiguous, the review was classified as a request, which makes the bug counts in §3.5.2 conservative.

### Bugs
`bug_calendar_stats_wrong` (calendar or statistics show incorrect data), `bug_plant_not_growing` (plant stalls, "infinity days", stops at a day count), `bug_notifications` (reminders don't fire, wrong sound, no badge, fire after completion, or the reminder-setting UI fails), `bug_crash_lag` (crash, freeze, lag), `bug_data_loss` (habits, plants or history disappear), `bug_water_tracker` (the jar cannot be filled or does not respond), `bug_layout_text` (text clipped, not full-screen on tablet), `bug_habit_creation` (created habits don't appear), `bug_timezone`.

### Product friction
`stats_too_basic` (statistics exist but are too thin — distinct from requesting a new view), `limited_features_general` (unspecific "lacks features"), `plant_underwhelming` (the plant works but is boring or too slow — distinct from broken), `abandonment_no_updates`, `support_unresponsive`, `onboarding_confusion` (could not work out how a mechanic works), `reminders_ineffective` (reminders fire but are ignorable — distinct from broken), `ux_input_friction` (small interaction problems: time picker, unclear completion state), `accessibility_issues` (VoiceOver, Dynamic Type, light sensitivity), `language_barrier` (content is in a language the reviewer cannot read), `hard_to_use`, `churn_stated` (states they left or will leave), `low_information_negative` (negative with no stated reason — included so no record is unaccounted for).

### Segment
- **`adhd_neurodivergent`** — the reviewer self-identifies as ADHD, autistic or anxious. **Boundary:** metaphorical self-description without a named condition (`6534235105`'s *"my black and white brain"*) was **excluded**, making this segment a floor.

### Known boundary risks, disclosed
1. **Request vs defect** (above) — biases bug counts down, request counts up.
2. **`paid_confirmed`** — strict reading only; the true payer share among reviewers is higher than 11.92%.
3. **`adhd_neurodivergent`** — named conditions only; 7 is a floor.
4. **Translation judgement** in the 47 non-Latin-script records. Originals are quoted throughout so any reader can check the reading.
5. **`generic_praise_no_detail` and `low_information_negative`** exist so that the "zero unassigned records" guarantee is honest rather than achieved by stretching substantive themes.

## 9.4 Counting rules, denominators and reconciliation summary

**Counting rules**

1. **Non-exclusive themes.** One review may carry any number of themes. 369 records carry 1,079 theme-assignments — mean 2.92, max 11 (`8324458354`, DE), min 1 (90 records).
2. **One review, one vote per theme.** A reviewer who asks for widgets twice in one body counts once. Intra-theme duplicates are a validator error and the map contains none.
3. **Global denominator is always 369.** Never the number of reviews "relevant" to a theme, never the number that mention a topic area.
4. **Segment and country denominators are always stated inline** and never substituted for the global classification. A theme at 22.7% of payers is *not* a 22.7% signal.
5. **Signal labels** come from the global percentage only: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority. With n = 369, one review = 0.27% (weak) and two = 0.54% (emerging), so the "ignore by default" band is unreachable and no theme in this report falls into it.
6. **Below-threshold exceptions.** Two themes below 1% are promoted into the narrative under the safety/legal/data-loss exception: `accessibility_issues` (0.81%, §3.5.5) and `bug_data_loss` (0.81%, §3.5.2 and §6.8). Both are labelled with their true signal.
7. **Direction labels** (positive / negative / mixed / unmet need) are the analyst's reading of the theme, not a computed sentiment. Mean star rating is given beside every theme so a reader can disagree.
8. **No deduplication was performed** because there are no exact duplicates (§1.3). Percentages therefore need no adjusted denominator.
9. **No conversion rate, install count, revenue figure or download number appears anywhere in this report**, because review text cannot produce one.
