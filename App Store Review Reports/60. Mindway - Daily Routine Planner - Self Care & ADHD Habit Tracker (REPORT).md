**App:** Mindway: Daily Routine Planner · **Developer:** DEEP FLOW SOFTWARE SERVICES - FZCO · **Bundle:** `com.codespaceapps.routineplanner`
**Corpus:** 216 reviews · 35 storefronts · 2024-07-12 → 2026-08-01 (750 days) · mean ★ 3.856
**Source extracted:** 2026-09-08T11:57:31Z (iTunes MZStore `userReviewsRow`) · **Analysis date:** 2026-09-12
**Method:** every one of the 216 reviews read individually in its original language; hand-curated theme map at `temp/60-review-classification.py`; validated by `temp/60-validate.py` (PASS).

## How to read this

Every number below carries: count, percentage, denominator, scope, time window, signal label, and review IDs. Any figure resolves to exact reviews through §9.6 (theme → IDs) and §9.7 (all 216 reviews → their themes). Review IDs are the App Store's own `review_id` values and are the audit key.

### ⚠️ Eight warnings before any number here is used

1. **Nearly half the corpus says nothing about the product.** 92 / 216 reviews (42.59%) have a body that carries no product information at all — a first name, one word, an emoji, a greeting (`12484500703` "Samira routine", `12526421035` "Hej", `12487058725` "SLAY"). Their mean rating is **4.52**, well above the corpus mean of 3.86. **The star average is built substantially on reviews that contain no evidence.** Use theme counts, not the star average, to judge product health.
2. **The corpus has a 4-month burst that changes its composition.** 98 of 216 reviews (45.37%) fall in **2025-03 and 2025-04 alone**, mean ★ 4.12, drawn from 32 storefronts, 63 of them 5★. Before that burst the US was 85.3% of reviews; during 2025-01 → 2025-04 it was 26.8%. Disclosed and analysed in §1.4 and Part 7 — this is the single most distorting structural fact in the dataset.
3. **The corpus effectively stops after 2025-04-28.** Only **14 reviews (6.48%)** cover the following 15 months, including a 136-day gap (2025-10-20 → 2026-03-05). Any claim about the app's *current* state rests on those 14 reviews and is labelled as such.
4. **The negative surface is almost entirely about money, not about the product working.** 40 of the 52 reviews rated 1–2★ (76.92% of that segment) carry a monetization complaint. Only **2 reviews in 216 (0.93%) report a crash**, and no other functional defect is reported by more than one reviewer.
5. **Only 13 reviewers (6.02%) give direct evidence of having paid.** Their mean rating is **1.38**. Every conclusion about buyers rests on those 13 reviews; conversion rates cannot be derived from review text and none is claimed.
6. **Only the US storefront clears the 50-review threshold** (101 reviews). Every other storefront — including Norway (15), Turkey (14), Australia (11) — is below it and carries an explicit limited-evidence warning.
7. **Storefront ≠ nationality ≠ language.** 59 bodies (27.31%) are non-English, spread over 22 storefronts; some English bodies appear on non-English storefronts and vice versa (`12471622906` NL storefront, mixed Dutch/English). Country here means *App Store storefront*, nothing more.
8. **Reviewers are volunteers, and at least one says the rating was coerced.** `11910326853` (GB, 5★): *"Good but forcing me to rate😅🙄 / ITS FORCING MEE"*. Where a rating prompt fires matters: 28 reviews (12.96%) explicitly say the reviewer has only just started and has not yet used the app.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Mindway's problem is not its product. It is the shape of its paywall and the state of its billing.** Users who get past the money question describe real behaviour change; users who hit it describe a trap.

**The one decision this corpus forces:** Mindway is monetizing *before* it has demonstrated value, and its billing is generating trust failures at a rate (5.09%) that ordinarily attracts store-level scrutiny. The corpus contains no evidence that the price is the problem in itself — it contains evidence that **a 1–3 task cap plus an immediate paywall plus a hard-to-exit subscription converts interested users into 1★ reviewers**. Every one of the 13 confirmed payers wrote a negative review. Fixing the *order* of the funnel — let users build and run a real plan first, charge second, make cancellation trivial — is the highest-value change available, and it is not a feature change.

- **98 of 216 reviews (45.37%) fall in 2025-03 and 2025-04.** Mean ★ 4.12; 63 of the 98 are 5★.

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Schema inspection** — all 13 fields enumerated and null-checked before any analysis.
2. **Reconciliation** — merged corpus vs 35 country files vs `manifest.json` (§1.3), *before* reading.
3. **Full manual read** — all 216 reviews dumped date-ordered to `temp/60-all-reviews.txt` and read one by one, **in the original language**. Turkish (12), Norwegian (8), Spanish (6), Swedish (5), German (4), French (3), Danish (3), Ukrainian (2), Italian (2), Dutch (2) and single reviews in Portuguese, Hebrew, Romanian, Czech, Slovak, Finnish and Moldovan-Romanian were read as written and translated only at the point of quotation. No sampling, no keyword filtering, no truncation.
4. **Hand classification** — 62 themes assigned by hand, review by review, and recorded per review (`REVIEW_THEMES`) rather than per theme, so the theme → ID map is *derived* and the two can never disagree. **No automated classifier, clustering, keyword rule or LLM-batch labelling was used**, so there is no automated-classification error rate to disclose; the error mode here is single-analyst judgement, addressed by the published boundary rules in §9.1 and by the fact that every ID is listed so any assignment can be contested.
5. **Programmatic validation** — `temp/60-validate.py` asserts: zero unknown IDs, zero unassigned records, zero intra-theme duplicates, no repeated theme within a review, unique IDs, country-file reconciliation both directions, and manifest agreement on totals, per-country counts, rating distribution and mean. **Result: PASS.**
6. **Aggregation** — counts, percentages, signal labels, mean ratings, per-theme date windows, family unions (deduplicated to unique reviewers), rating cross-tabs, period cross-tabs, storefront cross-tabs and segment rates computed by script, never by hand.
7. **External corroboration** — App Store listing consulted for price and feature framing only (§2.4); direct fetch was blocked by this environment's network egress proxy (403 at the CONNECT stage for both `apps.apple.com` and `itunes.apple.com`), so external facts come from search-result snippets and are labelled lower-confidence throughout.
8. **Final verification** — every review ID appearing in this report re-checked against `reviews.jsonl` (§9.4).

## 1.6 Limitations and known biases

- **The burst dominates.** 45.37% of the corpus arrives in two months from a distribution that does not match the rest of the corpus. Global percentages are therefore weighted toward March–April 2025 and toward non-US storefronts. Part 7 reports every major theme by period so this can be corrected for.
- **Low information density.** 42.59% of bodies carry no product signal. The effective evidential corpus is **124 reviews**, and supplemental rates against that denominator are given where useful and always labelled.
- **Recency is thin, not absent.** Conclusions about the app as of 2026 rest on 14 reviews. Anything said about "current" state is labelled limited evidence.
- **No version field.** Regression or release-attribution analysis is impossible from the corpus.
- **No purchase field.** The paid segment is self-declared, n=13, and is almost certainly biased toward the dissatisfied: a satisfied subscriber has less reason to write. **Do not read "payers are unhappy" as "all payers are unhappy"** — read it as "the only payers who wrote are unhappy", which is a narrower and still serious claim.
- **Survivorship and prompt bias.** Users who abandoned in the first session mostly did not write; users prompted at a good moment did. 28 reviews (12.96%) are written before the app has been used at all.
- **Storefront ≠ market ≠ nationality.** See warning 7.
- **Translation risk.** 59 / 216 (27.31%) bodies are non-English. Nuance may be lost; originals are preserved in `reviews.jsonl` and quoted alongside translations.
- **Single-analyst classification.** Boundary calls (for example "not allowed to add tasks" → `M_PAYWALL_BLOCK` vs `R_CUSTOM_TASKS`) are judgement. §9.1 documents each rule; §9.6 exposes every ID for challenge.
- **External facts are lower-confidence.** apps.apple.com was egress-blocked; App Store facts here are second-hand (§2.4).

## 3.6 The low-information problem — 92 reviewers (42.59%), mean ★ 4.52

Counted as a theme rather than discarded, because dropping it silently would inflate every other percentage.

A low-information review is one whose body contains no statement about the product: a name (`12484500703` "Samira routine"), a greeting (`12526421035` "Hej"), an interjection (`12487058725` "SLAY", `12506298316` "Lets goooooo beby"), a single word (`11562267840` — body "Some"), a bare emoji (`11548752988` "🙅‍♀️"), or a fragment (`12502572609` "Utsikt men bare"). Praise with no reason — `P_GENERIC_ONLY`, 67 reviewers (31.02%) — is tracked separately and overlaps heavily.

Three consequences, all of which constrain this report:

1. **The star mean is not a product signal.** Low-information reviews average 4.52 versus 3.86 corpus-wide, and 71 of the 92 are 5★. Remove them and the remaining 124 reviews average **3.36**.
2. **Supplemental rate, labelled as such:** against the 124-review substantive denominator, monetization complaints are **48 / 124 (38.71%)** and substantive praise **55 / 124 (44.35%)**. These are segment rates and do not replace the global classification in §9.5.
3. **The low-information share is a period effect, not a constant** — 26.67% in P1, 52.76% in P2, 35.71% in P3 (§7.3). It is largely the burst.

## 4.7 How themes differ by rating

The spec requires checking that no theme is treated as exclusively positive or negative. Results:

- **`M_PAYWALL_BLOCK` is not purely a 1★ theme**: 13 × 1★, 2 × 2★, 3 × 3★, **1 × 5★**. A user can be blocked and still rate well (`12525758438`).
- **Price mentions are not uniformly negative**: `M_PRICE_TOO_HIGH` is 0 × 5★ and 0 × 4★, but `M_COST_NOTED` is 1 × 5★ and 2 × 4★. **How the cost is encountered, not the cost itself, determines the rating** — the three cost-noters all met it as an optional add-on, the thirteen price-rejecters met it as a block.
- **`M_PAID_CONFIRMED` is almost purely 1★** (11 of 13) but not entirely: `11604816252` rated 4★ and `12149341496` rated 3★.
- **Praise themes are not purely 5★**: `P_ORGANIZED` includes a 3★ and a 4★; `P_MOTIVATION` includes a 3★; `P_DESIGN` has a **mean of 3.00** because two of its three reviewers are attacking the price in the same sentence.
- **`P_WELLBEING`, `P_PRODUCTIVITY` and `P_GRATITUDE` are the only themes of size that are unanimously 5★.** They are also the themes with the least commercial exposure — nobody who feels calmer is arguing about the subscription.

## 5.2 Purchase triggers — what actually makes someone consider paying

Direct evidence is thin; it is separated from inference below.

**Direct (a reviewer states what they wanted the paid tier for):**

- **Capacity, in every case.** `13815532923`: *"I want to create as many tasks as I want without having to pay"*. `12741993650`: the third task. `12945940456`: more than one activity. `11602546791`: the fourth task. The paid trigger Mindway has actually built is **"you have run out of room"**.
- **Only one reviewer describes a positive pull toward the paid tier**: `11796162718` (5★) likes the app enough to keep using the free parts and names Pro as the thing they would want. No reviewer names a Pro *feature* they wanted.

**Inferred, clearly labelled as inference** — what the 55 substantive positive reviewers value, and therefore what a paid tier could plausibly be built on:

| Inferred trigger | Supporting evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Getting the day structured, reliably | `P_ORGANIZED` 15 (6.94%), mean 4.80 | Strongest — largest positive theme |
| Visible behaviour change in one named habit | `P_HABIT_OUTCOME` 12 (5.56%), mean 4.75 | Strong, and marketable verbatim |
| Feeling better / calmer / more energetic | `P_WELLBEING` 9 (4.17%), mean 5.00 | Strong, zero dissent |
| Guided content — the preset routines and self-care guide | `P_CONTENT_PACKS` 5 (2.31%), mean 4.80 | Moderate; this is the only *content* asset reviewers praise |
| ADHD-specific support | `P_ADHD_FIT` 3 (1.39%), mean 5.00 | Weak base, unanimous, and differentiated |

**None of these is what the paywall currently sells.** The paywall sells task slots. The value users report is outcomes, structure and guidance. That mismatch is the core monetization finding of the report (§8.2 D1).

## 5.4 Refund and churn drivers

**Refund requests: 4 (1.85%), all 1★** — `11862274231` (refused, uninstalled), `11939232936` (no route found), `12040812221` (demands it, threatens to report), `12274815966` (*"I wish I could get my money back"*). All four are US. All four follow an annual charge.

**Stated churn: 4 reviewers (1.85%, `M_CHURN_DELETE`) say they deleted or are deleting the app** — `11548752988` (asks how to remove it), `11862274231` (uninstalled after the $39.99 charge), `11920445220` (*"It costs too much. So I deleted it."*), `12009909717` (*"I'm not paying for it. So I'll just delete.. so lame"*).

Note the composition: **two of the four churned over price before paying anything, and one churned immediately after paying.** The only churn driver visible in this corpus is money. Not one reviewer says they stopped using Mindway because it was buggy, boring, or ineffective.

## 5.5 Issues that specifically affect paid users

1. **Cancellation.** Four reviewers could not find or complete it; a fifth cancelled and was charged anyway. This is the highest-severity item in the report.
2. **Trial mechanics.** Three separate patterns reported: a $0.99 paid trial converting to $39.99 within hours (`11862274231`), a "3 day free trial" that billed a week (`11604816252`), and no trial being offered at all before purchase (`11995687857`).
3. **Plan clarity at purchase.** `11939232936` believed they were buying monthly and were charged annually. `12274815966` understood the choice and still regretted it.
4. **Support.** Five of the thirteen payers used a 1★ public review as their support channel. One states no help came (`11583074924`); one waited politely and rated 3★ instead of 1★ (`12149341496`). **The one payer who found a support route rated the app two stars higher than the ones who didn't.**
5. **Post-purchase value delivery.** Five of thirteen say the paid product is a list, not a tracker. `11973575224` used it for one day; `12274815966` names the exact missing capability (repeat intervals beyond daily/weekly/monthly).

# PART 7 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 7.1 Method

Three periods, cut at natural breaks in the volume series (§1.4) rather than at equal intervals, because the corpus is not evenly distributed:

| Period | Window | n | % of 216 | Mean ★ | 5★ | 4★ | 3★ | 2★ | 1★ | US share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1 — launch half-year** | 2024-07-12 → 2024-12-23 | 75 | 34.72% | **3.51** | 39 | 6 | 5 | 4 | **21** | **85.3%** |
| **P2 — international burst** | 2025-01-02 → 2025-04-28 | 127 | 58.80% | **4.13** | 83 | 12 | 11 | 8 | 13 | 26.8% |
| **P3 — long tail** | 2025-05-08 → 2026-08-01 | 14 | 6.48% | **3.21** | 6 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 4 | 21.4% |

Every trend below states its period denominators. **P3 is 14 reviews.** No P3 claim is made at better than low confidence, and none is made at all where a single review would move the percentage by more than 7 points without corroboration elsewhere in the corpus.

## 7.2 Trend 1 — Review acquisition rose, spiked, then stopped · **the dominant trend · high confidence**

202 of 216 reviews (93.52%) arrive in the first 291 days; 14 (6.48%) in the following 459. Monthly volume runs 5 → 11 → 11 → 18 → 19 → 11 → 20 → 9 → **39 → 59** → 2 → 1 → 1 → 2 → 1 → 3 → 0 → 0 → 0 → 0 → 2 → 0 → 1 → 0 → 0 → 1.

The drop is not gradual. April 2025 carries 59 reviews; May 2025 carries 2. **Whatever produced the burst was switched off, and no organic review flow replaced it.** The corpus cannot say whether installs followed the same curve — review volume is not installs — but a habit app with a healthy active base normally produces a continuing review trickle, and Mindway produces roughly one review a month across 2025-05 → 2026-08, including two four-month silences.

## 7.3 Trend 2 — Review substance fell as volume rose · **high confidence**

| Measure | P1 (n=75) | P2 (n=127) | P3 (n=14) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low-information reviews | 20 (26.67%) | **67 (52.76%)** | 5 (35.71%) |
| Praise with no reason | 17 (22.67%) | 47 (37.01%) | 3 (21.43%) |
| Non-English bodies | 3 (4.00%) | **51 (40.16%)** | 5 (35.71%) |
| First-session reviews | 15 (20.00%) | 12 (9.45%) | 1 (7.14%) |
| Mean ★ | 3.51 | **4.13** | 3.21 |

**The rating improvement in P2 and the collapse in review substance are the same event.** P2 added 52 non-English reviews and 47 reviews of reason-free praise, and the mean rose 0.62 stars. When the burst stopped, the mean fell back to 3.21 — *below* the launch-period level. Any read of Mindway's rating history that treats 2025-03/04 as an improvement in the product is reading a marketing artefact.

## 7.4 Trend 3 — The paywall complaint is the one theme that grows across all three periods · **the most actionable finding in this Part · medium-high confidence**

| Period | `M_PAYWALL_BLOCK` | % of period |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | 4 / 75 | 5.33% |
| P2 | 11 / 127 | 8.66% |
| P3 | **4 / 14** | **28.57%** |

The P3 figure rests on four reviews and is directional only — but the direction is corroborated by content, not just by count: the four are `12741993650` (PL, 2025-06), `12945940456` (CA, 2025-07), `12643837955` (TR, 2025-05) and `13815532923` (US, 2026-03), spread across four storefronts and 10 months, each describing the same block. **Twenty months after the earliest report of it (`11602546791`, 2024-08-12), the cap was still stopping users.** Nothing in the corpus suggests it was ever changed except possibly downward (§2.2).

Price complaints follow the same shape: 6.67% (P1) → 4.72% (P2) → 14.29% (P3), and the last review in the corpus (`14373191293`, 2026-08-01) is a price complaint.

## 7.5 Trend 4 — Billing failures cluster hard in P1, then thin out · **medium confidence, two readings**

| Theme | P1 | P2 | P3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| `M_PAID_CONFIRMED` | 10 (13.33%) | 2 (1.57%) | 1 (7.14%) |
| `M_UNAUTHORIZED_CHARGE` | 4 (5.33%) | 0 | 0 |
| `M_CANCEL_DIFFICULTY` | 3 (4.00%) | 0 | 1 (7.14%) |
| `M_TRIAL_PROBLEM` | 3 (4.00%) | 0 | 0 |
| `M_REFUND_REQUEST` | 3 (4.00%) | 1 (0.79%) | 0 |
| `M_SCAM_ACCUSATION` | 4 (5.33%) | 1 (0.79%) | 0 |
| `M_MISLEADING_ADVERT` | 4 (5.33%) | 1 (0.79%) | 0 |

**Reading A (improvement):** the billing problems were real in the launch half-year, were addressed, and largely stopped.
**Reading B (composition):** P1 was 85.3% US and P2 was 26.8% US, and billing complaints are 10-of-11 US (§6.2). The apparent improvement may be the audience changing, not the billing.

**The corpus cannot separate these**, and the tiebreaker is unhelpful: `13251351092` (AU, 2025-10-11) is still asking how to cancel, 14 months after the first such report. The safe statement: **the billing complaint rate among US reviewers did not visibly improve; the US share of reviews fell.** Treat the problem as open until instrumented data says otherwise (§8.4 Q3).

## 7.6 Trend 5 — Benefit claims peak in P2 and vanish in P3 · **low-medium confidence**

`P_HABIT_OUTCOME` runs 4 (5.33%) → 8 (6.30%) → **0**; `P_MOTIVATION` 3 → 8 → **0**; `P_WELLBEING` 2 → 7 → **0**; `P_ORGANIZED` 4 (5.33%) → 10 (7.87%) → 1 (7.14%).

At 14 P3 reviews, zero is only weak evidence of absence. But the *combination* — no habit outcome, no motivation and no wellbeing claim anywhere after April 2025, while paywall complaints rose to 28.57% — is the corpus's least comfortable pattern. `P_PRODUCTIVITY` is the one positive theme with a genuine 2026 data point (`14073659974`, AU, 2026-05-17, *"This app is keeping me so focused"*).

## 7.7 Trend 6 — Requests are rare in every period and never cluster · **persistent · medium confidence**

The ten requests are spread 5 (P1) / 4 (P2) / 1 (P3) with no theme reported by more than three people over 750 days. **There is no rising feature demand in this corpus.** The one 2026 request (`13901287389`, programme pacing) is the only signal about how the app behaves today beyond the paywall.

## 7.8 Trend 7 — Crashes appear once and never return · **improving · low confidence**

Both crash reports are US and fall within five days of each other (`11789667295` 2024-10-02, `11802262343` 2024-10-06). Nothing comparable appears in the following 22 months. Most consistent with a specific bad build in early October 2024 that was fixed — but with no `version` field, this is inference, not attribution.

## 7.9 What the corpus cannot tell you about time

- **Nothing about the product after 2026-08-01**, and very little after 2025-04-28.
- **Nothing tied to a release.** No version field; no reviewer names a version.
- **Whether the free-tier cap changed.** Reviewers report 3 tasks (2024-08 and 2025-06), 2 tasks (2024-12 → 2025-03) and 1 task (2025-04, 2025-07). The sequence is not monotonic, so it cannot be read as a single change.
- **Whether anything was fixed.** No reviewer in the corpus reports a problem being resolved, and `is_edited` is false on all 216 records, so no one visibly revised an earlier verdict.

# PART 8 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation names the findings it rests on. Immediate fixes, structural decisions, experiments and open questions are separated.

## 8.1 Immediate fixes — the evidence is unambiguous

| # | Fix | Findings | Why now |
|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** | **Make cancellation one tap from the main screen and confirm it in writing.** | §3.3.3, §5.5.1 — 4 reviewers could not cancel, 1 cancelled and was charged anyway; mean ★ 1.00 | Every one of these became a public 1★ review. This is the cheapest star recovery available and it removes the most serious store risk in the report. |
| **F2** | **Audit the trial-to-charge flow end to end**, specifically: the $0.99 paid trial, the "3-day free trial" that billed a week, and the monthly-vs-annual selection screen. | §3.3.3, §5.5.2–3 — `11862274231`, `11604816252`, `11939232936` | Three distinct failure descriptions from three users is a flow problem, not three user errors. |
| **F3** | **Put an in-app support route in front of the review prompt**, and answer it. | §3.5 — 7 reviewers (3.24%) used a 1★ review as a help desk; the one who reached the developer rated 3★ instead of 1★ | Seven support tickets became permanent public 1★ reviews. |
| **F4** | **State the subscription price and the free-tier limit in the App Store listing and in the first ad frame.** | §3.3.4 — 5 misleading-advert reports, 2 ad-sourced reviewers both 1★; `11839657635` asks for exactly this | Removes the "click bait" / "it said it was free" class of review entirely. |
| **F5** | **Stop firing the upsell inside an in-progress task.** | §3.3.6 — `12011759865`, `12136204580`, `12502986885` | *"let me do my activities and move on with my life"* is a description of a broken interaction, not of a pricing disagreement. |
| **F6** | **Add AM/PM time entry.** | §3.7 — `11735164096` | One review, one line of work, and it is the only reported input-level usability defect in the corpus. |

## 8.2 Structural decisions

### D1 · Move the paywall behind first value, not in front of it. This is the decision the corpus forces.

**Evidence:** 19 reviewers (8.80%, mean ★ 1.63) blocked at a 1–3 task cap (§3.3.1); 13 (6.02%) rejecting the price outright (§3.3.2); 31 of 38 one-star reviews carrying a monetization complaint (§4.5); the theme still at 28.57% of the thin 2026 tail (§7.4).

The cap is set below the app's own minimum viable use. A "daily routine" of two tasks is not a routine, so the free tier cannot demonstrate the thing the paid tier sells. Meanwhile the value users actually report — structure (15), habit outcomes (12), wellbeing (9) — takes **days** to appear. The current design charges before any of it can happen.

**What the corpus supports:** let a user build and run a complete day, for long enough to experience one of the outcomes in §5.2, and gate on *depth* (multiple routines, history, advanced recurrence, the guided content) rather than on *count*. The three reviewers who met the cost as optional rather than as a wall averaged 4.33★ (§3.3.2); the thirteen who met it as a wall averaged 1.69.

### D2 · Sell the outcome, not the task slots.

**Evidence:** §5.2 — zero reviewers name a Pro feature they wanted; every stated purchase trigger is "I ran out of room". Meanwhile 43 reviewers (19.91%, mean ★ 4.79) describe concrete benefits.

A paywall that says *you have used your 2 free tasks* invites the comparison that 6 reviewers made explicitly — Reminders, Notes, Calendar, paper — because task slots are exactly what those free tools give away. A paywall that offers guided routines, progress history and the self-care library is not comparable to Apple Reminders. **The corpus shows Mindway losing a fight it chose.**

### D3 · Build the tracking layer. It is the largest non-pricing gap in the report.

**Evidence:** §3.3.5 — 10 reviewers (4.63%, mean ★ 1.50) say the product is a list, not a tracker, and 5 of them paid. `12364644988` names the expectation precisely: *"completion percentage - tracking consistency"*. `12410685351` adds *"encouragement"*. No reviewer anywhere in 216 describes a streak, chart, score or progress view.

This is also the cheapest way to make D2 true: completion history and consistency stats are exactly the kind of capability that justifies a subscription and cannot be replicated in Notes.

### D4 · Commit to the ADHD positioning, and say so in the listing.

**Evidence:** §3.4.4 — 4 mentions (1.85%), 3 endorsements, mean ★ 5.00, zero complaints, across US, TR and CH. `11988875832` (*"I don't think I have ADHD but it really helps"*) shows the positioning reaching beyond its target rather than narrowing the market.

Small base, so this is a positioning bet rather than a proven demand. But it is the only differentiated claim the corpus supports, it costs nothing to state, and the competitor set named in §3.3.2 (Reminders, Notes, Calendar) offers nothing comparable.

### D5 · Fix the recurrence model before adding anything else to the backlog.

**Evidence:** §3.7 — `R_REPEAT_OPTIONS` 3 (1.39%) and `R_CUSTOM_TASKS` 4 (1.85%) are the two largest requests, and both are flexibility complaints. Two reviewers independently enumerate the exact limitation (daily / weekly / monthly only) and both name real intervals they cannot express: every 2 weeks, every 6 months, hourly hydration, haircuts. `13901287389` adds fixed 28/38-day programme pacing in 2026.

Ten requesters is a small backlog, and §3.7 explains why it is small — but of the ten, **four are asking for the same thing in different words.**

### D6 · Decide what the review channel is for, and stop harvesting ratings from users who have not used the app.

**Evidence:** §1.4 and §7.3 — 28 reviewers (12.96%) rate before use, 92 (42.59%) write nothing substantive, one states the prompt was coercive (`11910326853`), and the 2025-03/04 burst raised the mean 0.62 stars while adding almost no information.

The burst bought a rating, not a reputation, and it decayed the moment it stopped (§7.2). A prompt fired after a user *completes a routine for the fifth consecutive day* would produce fewer reviews, better reviews, and — on this corpus's own evidence, where every substantive-benefit reviewer rates 4.79 — a more durable average.

## 8.3 Experiments worth running

| # | Experiment | Hypothesis from the corpus | Primary metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** | Raise the free cap to a full day's routine (8–10 tasks) for a randomized cohort | If §3.3.1 is right, 1★ rate falls sharply and trial starts rise, because users reach the value in §5.2 before the ask | 1★ review rate; trial start rate; D7 retention |
| **E2** | Replace the count-based paywall with a time-based one (full app, 7 days, no card) | Tests D1 without giving away capacity permanently; addresses `11995687857` (*"I wish it would of let me try it out first"*) | Trial→paid conversion; refund rate |
| **E3** | Offer a cheap one-time purchase or a low monthly tier alongside the weekly | 6 reviewers name a free substitute and 3 name the weekly price with outrage; nobody objects to paying *something* (`M_COST_NOTED`, n=3) | ARPU; price-objection review rate |
| **E4** | Ship a minimal consistency view (completion %, current streak, 30-day grid) | D3 — closes the "just a to-do list" gap for the exact users who paid | Review mentions of tracking; payer rating |
| **E5** | Delay the rating prompt to after five completed days | D6 — the burst reviews carry no information and decay | Share of reviews with substantive text; rating stability |
| **E6** | Add a "bulk remove pack" control and a first-run "pick one of three ready-made days" | `12149341496` (*"spend all day deleting tasks"*), `11971799940` (*"3 complete day schedules"*), `11892038325` | Time-to-first-completed-routine |

## 8.5 What not to change

- **Do not touch the core routine-planning model.** 43 reviewers (19.91%, mean ★ 4.79) report it working, in ten languages, and not one reviewer says the concept is wrong.
- **Do not redesign the visual identity on the strength of one complaint.** `11774398931` finds it *"too bubbly/cute"*, but `12469906657`, `12594894710` and `14373191293` praise the design — and two of those three are complaining about the *price* in the same breath.
- **Do not chase the feature backlog ahead of D1–D3.** Ten requesters across 750 days (§3.7) does not outrank 49 monetization complainers.
- **Do not read the 3.86 star average as the health measure.** It is 42.59% content-free reviews and a two-month burst (§3.6, §7.3). The substantive corpus averages 3.36, and the payer segment averages 1.38.

`R_CUSTOM_TASKS` (create own tasks/categories) · `R_REPEAT_OPTIONS` (intervals beyond daily/weekly/monthly) · `R_FOLDERS` (categorise reminders) · `R_TIME_FORMAT` (AM/PM) · `R_ONBOARD_TEMPLATES` (ready-made day schedules) · `R_HOWTO` (in-app guidance, bulk pack removal) · `R_PROGRESS_TRACKING` (completion %, consistency) · `R_ENCOURAGEMENT` (motivational feedback) · `R_SCHEDULE_CONTROL` (control over programme pacing)

`B_CRASH` (app closes, glitches, won't open) · `F_SETUP_OVERWHELM` · `F_PACK_DELETION` (laborious removal of preset tasks) · `F_PAYWALL_INTERRUPT` (upsell fires inside an in-progress task) · `F_ONBOARD_QUIZ` (questionnaire before value) · `F_FORCED_RATING` · `F_TONE_CUTE` (styling objection) · `F_REVIEW_AS_SUPPORT` (review used as a help ticket) · `F_SUPPORT_UNRESPONSIVE` · `F_UNINSTALL_CONFUSION`

`M_PAYWALL_BLOCK` (free-tier cap stops the core job) · `M_PRICE_TOO_HIGH` · `M_WISH_FREE` (softer: wishes it were free, no accusation) · `M_VALUE_NOT_WORTH` (product judged under-built for the money) · `M_COMPETITOR_SUBSTITUTE` (names a free alternative they already have) · `M_PAY_REFUSAL` (refuses to pay on principle) · `M_MISLEADING_ADVERT` (advertised as free / not as advertised / price not shown) · `M_SCAM_ACCUSATION` (calls it a scam or rip-off) · `M_ANTI_RECOMMEND` (tells other readers not to download) · `M_CANCEL_DIFFICULTY` · `M_UNAUTHORIZED_CHARGE` (charged after cancelling, or without subscribing) · `M_TRIAL_PROBLEM` · `M_BILLING_MISLEAD` (plan term not as understood) · `M_REFUND_REQUEST`

`M_PAID_CONFIRMED` (**direct** evidence of payment — a sum, a held subscription, a cancellation, a refusal of refund) · `M_CHURN_DELETE` (deleted it or states the intention to) · `S_NON_ENGLISH` · `S_LOW_INFO` (body carries no product statement) · `S_ONBOARDING_ONLY` (states they have just started / not used it yet) · `S_LONGTERM_USE` (states sustained use over weeks or more) · `S_RATING_TEXT_CONTRADICTION` · `S_SOLICITED_RATING` · `S_ADHD` (mentions ADHD, positively or otherwise) · `S_MINOR` (self-identifies as a child) · `S_AD_ACQUISITION` (says an ad brought them) · `S_REVIEW_MISUSE` (review box used for something other than a review) · `N_UNEXPLAINED_NEGATIVE` (1–2★ with no reason given)

### Known boundary risks, disclosed

1. **`M_PAYWALL_BLOCK` vs `R_CUSTOM_TASKS`.** `11774398931` (*"does not allow for many other tasks or for building your own tasks"*) could be either the cap or a customization limit. Assigned `R_CUSTOM_TASKS` only, because the reviewer never mentions payment. If read the other way, `M_PAYWALL_BLOCK` becomes 20 (9.26%) — the signal label does not change.
2. **`M_PAYWALL_BLOCK` vs `M_PRICE_TOO_HIGH`.** Assigned by what stopped the reviewer: being unable to proceed (block) versus judging the amount excessive (price). Six reviews carry both.
3. **`P_GENERIC_ONLY` vs a specific praise theme.** "It really helps me" is generic; "it helps me organise my day" is `P_ORGANIZED`. The dividing line is whether a *mechanism or outcome* is named.
4. **`S_LOW_INFO` vs `P_GENERIC_ONLY`.** Overlapping but distinct: "Love it" is both; "Samira routine" is only low-information; a long, warm, reason-free paragraph is only `P_GENERIC_ONLY`.
5. **`11892038325`** (*"Stop playing games and get to plan"*, 2★) reads like onboarding friction but names nothing. Assigned `N_UNEXPLAINED_NEGATIVE` only, **not** `F_ONBOARD_QUIZ`, to avoid inventing evidence. If read as onboarding friction, `F_ONBOARD_QUIZ` becomes 2 (0.93%).
6. **`M_TRIAL_PROBLEM` on `11995687857`** (*"I wish it would of let me try it out first"*) describes the *absence* of a trial rather than a broken one. Counted, and flagged here.
7. **`S_NON_ENGLISH` on very short bodies.** "Gn" (`12469367697`), "Kara" (`12374186384`) and "1000" (`12527540114`) are not attributable to a language; only `12527540114` (Russian title) was counted. Under-counting here is deliberate.
8. **`M_PAID_CONFIRMED` is deliberately strict.** `12501545031` (*"it makes us spend a lot of money"*) and `12011759865` (extensive complaints about payment prompts) are **not** counted as payers, because neither states a completed purchase. A looser rule would give 15 rather than 13.
