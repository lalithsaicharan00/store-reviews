# That Girl: Routine Planner (App Store ID 1597753705) — Full Review Analysis

**Target folder:** `app store review/12. That Girl - Routine Planner - Cute Daily Calendar Schedule`
**Product:** That Girl: Routine Planner — "Cute Daily Calendar Schedule" / daily routine planner
**Developer:** Solowei Group LTD (`danylosolowei-thatgirl`, artistId 1683543614)
**Category:** Productivity (secondary: Health & Fitness) — habit / routine / day planning
**Corpus:** 404 written reviews, 58 storefronts, 18 Dec 2021 → 16 Aug 2026
**Written-review mean rating:** 2.10 · **Store aggregate (US, 9 Sep 2026):** 4.83 across 5,825 ratings
**Decision this informs:** whether a hard-paywall, onboarding-heavy routine planner is a viable model, and what a competitor entering this space should build, price and avoid.

### ⚠️ Two warnings before any number in this report is used

**1. The star rating in this corpus is not a clean sentiment measure for this app.** 23 reviewers (5.69%) describe an in-app prompt that asks for a 5-star rating *during onboarding, before the app has been used*. Two of the 77 five-star reviews say in plain text that they rated 5 because they were told to (`13814071134`, `13279632399`). 14 of the 77 five-star reviews (18.18% of 5★) carry text that contradicts the rating. Treat 5★ counts here as an upper bound on satisfaction. See §5.1.

**2. Written reviews are ~2.4% of ratings.** Across the 13 storefronts where store metadata was fetched, there are 12,784 ratings and 309 written reviews in this corpus (2.42%). Written reviewers skew to people with something to complain about *and*, in this app's case, to people who escaped the onboarding rating prompt. The 4.83 store average and the 2.10 written mean describe two different populations. Neither alone describes the product.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

## 1. This is not a habit-tracker review corpus. It is a paywall corpus.

**188 of 404 reviews (46.53%) — high-priority signal** complain about the payment gate: either that the app cannot be used at all without paying (`paywall_block`, 162 / 40.10%) or that there is no way to try it first (`no_trial`, 62 / 15.35%). Mean rating of the `paywall_block` group: **1.30**. 134 of its 162 members are 1★.

**172 reviewers (42.57%)** hit the gate and did **not** pay — they never saw the product. Their mean rating is 1.31. Nearly half of everything written about this app was written by people who never used it.

This is not a discovery artefact of keyword search. Reading all 404 records, the paywall complaint is the single largest thing in the corpus in every one of the five time periods analysed, from Dec 2021 to Aug 2026 (§8).

Representative IDs: `8192594330` (gb, 1★, "Didn't even get to see the app. Told me I had to pay"), `8916870417` (us, 1★), `8933371666` (it, 1★), `9375776581` (de, 1★, "Steht hier als kostenloser Download drin" / "it's listed here as a free download"), `12185804645` (br, 1★), `12691620078` (ph, 1★), `13726769977` (nz, 3★), `14284089819` (us, 1★), `14337005478` (us, 1★).

**The listing does disclose it.** The US store description says verbatim: *"That Girl app is fully paid and purchase is required to access any content."* (accessed 9 Sep 2026). The price label still reads **Free**. 162 reviewers hit an expectation gap that the listing technically closes in body text — which makes this a **discovery and framing failure, not an honesty failure**, and it is fixable at the top of the funnel rather than in the product.

## 2. Every reviewer who says they paid is unhappy. Not most — every one.

**80 reviewers (19.80%) state directly that they paid, subscribed, were charged, or requested a refund.** Segment stats:

| Rating | Count | Segment share |
|---|---|---|
| 5★ | **0** | 0.00% |
| 4★ | 2 | 2.50% |
| 3★ | 7 | 8.75% |
| 2★ | 5 | 6.25% |
| 1★ | **66** | **82.50%** |

**Segment mean: 1.31.** Zero five-star reviews among 80 self-identified paying customers is the most consequential single fact in this corpus. Whatever drives this app's 4.83 store average, it is not paying customers writing about the product they bought.

Compare: the 22 reviewers who describe a *sustained outcome* from using the app (`praise_outcome`) have a mean of 4.95 — and **not one of them appears in the paid group**. Across the wider `praise` theme, only 8 of 91 reviewers (8.79%) also state they paid. The people who love this app and the people who say they bought it are, in this corpus, almost entirely different people.

## 3. The number-one thing paying customers report is that payment did not grant access

**33 reviewers (8.17%) — high-priority signal** describe an entitlement failure: they paid, and the app kept demanding payment, refused to log them in, or reported "no subscription found". Mean rating **1.15** (31 of 33 are 1★). 32 of the 33 also state directly that they paid, so as a segment rate this is **40.00% of the 80 direct-paid reviewers**.

## 4. Support is a dead end, and refunds are the second-order damage

**15 reviewers (3.71%) — very strong signal** report they could not reach support at all; five state explicitly that the listed support email address does not exist or bounces (`12162547099`, `12122344254`, `13194937352`, `13812177642`, `11839685862`). One of those is a **5★** review whose entire text is a plea for help (`12162547099`).

**21 reviewers (5.20%) — high-priority signal** raise refunds; mean rating **1.05** (20 of 21 are 1★) — the angriest cluster in the corpus. Segment rate: **25.00% of the 80 paid reviewers** (20 of the 21 also state they paid). Causes: refund denied by the developer or Apple (`12013949215`, `12192898307`), no route to ask (`11617090482`, `12122344254`), charged after cancelling (`8560736398`), charged without consent (`12630584133`), a minor's purchase (`12192898307`, and `14335809799` describes being a minor without a card).

The chain is mechanical and repeats across countries: **hard paywall → entitlement failure → no support → refund attempt → 1★ "scam".** 42 reviews (10.40%) use the word *scam* or a direct-translation equivalent (*truffa, golpe, estafa, Abzocke, oszustwo*).

## 5. The in-app 5-star prompt is a compliance and data-integrity risk, and it is getting more visible

**23 reviewers (5.69%) — high-priority signal** describe being asked to rate the app 5 stars during onboarding, before any use. It first appears in **2024** reviews and rises: 0% of 2022–2023 → 9.0% of 2024 → 5.5% of 2025 → **11.1% of 2026**.

The clearest evidence is inside the 5★ population itself:
- `13814071134` (gb, **5★**, 2026-03-04): *"It said I had to give them 5 starts so here I am xx"*
- `13279632399` (nl, **5★**, 2025-10-17): *"I had ticked in 5 stars because the begin was cool but then I wanted to go…"*

And reviewers are drawing the inference publicly:
- `11586279561` (us, 1★): *"During the set up process it makes you rate the app 5 stars which is probably why the ratings are good."*
- `13638785503` (mx, 1★): *"…it asked me to rate it 5 stars… which is probably how they have 4.8 stars."*
- `13096698945` (pl, 1★): *"Question is how apple showing 4.8 average when most positive rates are 1 star."*

Two independent, uncoordinated reviewers in different countries named the exact aggregate (4.8) that the store showed. **Notably, this theme is almost absent from the US (2 of 23) and concentrated in GB, PL, IT, CA, NL and MX** — see §7.4.

**This does not prove a guideline violation from review text alone.** It does establish that a material share of users perceive the rating request as coerced, that at least two 5★ ratings were given for that reason, and that the discrepancy between a 4.83 store average and a 2.10 written mean is publicly visible to prospects reading reviews before they download.

## 6. The onboarding is the best-loved and most-hated part of the product

The intro sequence — a narrated, unskippable explainer plus a personalisation questionnaire — generates praise and rage from the same feature:

- **Against: 21 reviewers (5.20%) — high-priority.** Unskippable, too long, forced audio narration. `11396873201` (us): *"Include whatever flowery explanation screens you want but making them unskippable is insane."* `10913036513` (gb): *"Super long onboarding intro with perky American reading everything out, you can't swipe through it faster."* Also `11500680241`, `11512469934`, `11511872399`, `11626061734`, `10923103793` (tw), `11193945325` (tw), `13096698945` (pl), `10907144601`, `14337005478`.
- **For:** `12809171419` (au, 5★) *"I love all the quetions"*; `12663975904` (gb, 5★) *"I love the way it has music at the start and it speaks!"*; `10978019411` (us, 5★) praises the narration effort; `11652586176` (us, 5★) *"so calming especially the beginning"*; `14118054672` (us, 5★) *"very personalized content"*.

**Nine of the 77 five-star reviews (11.69% of 5★) praise the onboarding while stating or implying they have not yet used the product** (`12990607438`, `12809171419`, `12663975904`, `10978019411`, `11538597964`, `11652586176`, `13366086371`, `13691510671`, `11513175352`).

That is the core strategic tension in this product: **the onboarding converts, the product does not retain.** The complaint appears verbatim: `11586279561` (us, 1★) — *"The intro before you purchase seems to have more thought put into it then the actual app itself."*

**The onboarding complaint peaked in 2024 (16 of its 21 members, 16.0% of 2024 reviews) and drops to 1 review in 2025.** That is the corpus's clearest evidence of a fix that landed.

## 7. When people do get in, the app is judged as a worse version of the calendar they already have

**13 reviewers (3.22%) — very strong signal** say the app offers nothing beyond Apple Calendar, Apple Reminders or Google Calendar. Mean 1.38.

- `8985632836` (ca): *"a glamourized version of the FREE Reminders app on iPhones."*
- `12236900050` (us): *"This app does nothing more than take over your iPhone calendar."*
- `13766147453` (de): *"Es gibt weniger Funktionen wie in der kostenlosen Kalender App von Apple."* [Fewer features than Apple's free Calendar app.]
- `13937741808` (us): *"There are no routines or trackers in the app. It's just a calendar/to do list. they charge you before you can see that."*
- Also `12558262311`, `8252268729`, `9975565509`, `9314712194`, `12033069615`, `14005091328`, `11586279561`, `12982453369`, `14329102917`.

This is the single most dangerous finding for the *paid* proposition. The paywall is defensible only if what is behind it is differentiated; 13 reviewers who got behind it say it is not.

**52 reviewers (12.87%) — high-priority signal** report crashes, freezes, glitches, being kicked out, or the app not opening. Trend: 11.4% (2022) → 3.8% (2023) → 9.0% (2024) → 16.5% (2025) → **17.5% (2026)**.

## 9. The clearest, cheapest unmet need is a widget

**9 reviewers (2.23%) — meaningful signal** ask for a home-screen widget, including from otherwise-happy paying users. Mean rating of the group is **2.44**, the highest of any complaint theme — these are retained users, not churned prospects.

- `13550565309` (us, **5★**): *"Love it but needs widgets… I forget all the time to use this app cause I don't have a widget :("*
- `12667337121` (us, 3★): *"as someone who spends like 0 time on their phone it's so much more handy and accountable to have a widget."*
- Also `11973399697`, `14357143421`, `12178849529`, `12722697888`, `12215752061`, `12341288794`, `12892920714`.

**Every widget request is dated 2024 or later** (1 in 2024, 7 in 2025, 1 in 2026). It is a live, current, un-met request from the paying cohort, and it names the exact retention mechanism the app lacks: a passive surface that reminds people the app exists.

## 10. What people genuinely love: being told what their day looks like

**91 reviewers (22.52%)** include a praise component; **22 (5.45%) — high-priority signal** describe a concrete sustained outcome (mean 4.95). The praised outcome is consistent and narrow: *the day is planned for me, so I stop losing track of it.*

# PART 8 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

**Method:** reviews bucketed by calendar year of `date` (2021 folded into 2022 as P1; 2026 is partial, ending 2026-08-16). Buckets are large enough for comparison except P1's 2021 tail (n=4) and P2 (n=26), which are flagged where they matter.

| Period | n | Mean ★ | 1★ | 5★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 2021-12 → 2022-12 | 88 | 1.68 | 60 (68.2%) | 6 (6.8%) |
| P2 2023 | 26 | 1.46 | 19 (73.1%) | 1 (3.8%) |
| P3 2024 | 100 | 2.29 | 57 (57.0%) | 24 (24.0%) |
| P4 2025 | 127 | 2.19 | 78 (61.4%) | 29 (22.8%) |
| P5 2026 (to Aug) | 63 | 2.48 | 35 (55.6%) | 17 (27.0%) |

## Trend 1 — The paywall complaint never goes away. Persistent, four years, nine months.

52.3% → 61.5% → 36.0% → 33.9% → **33.3%**. The share falls after 2023 but never drops below a third of all reviews in any period. This is the longest-running unresolved theme in the corpus, and it is unresolved by design — the store description confirms the model is intentional.

## Trend 2 — "No free trial" collapses after 2022; a trial appears and then breaks.

35.2% (P1) → 23.1% (P2) → 8.0% (P3) → 6.3% (P4) → 14.3% (P5).

The 2022 cohort is unanimous that no trial exists (`8944662701`, `8992840051`, `9380396109`, `8988433850`, `9086515342`). From 2025, reviewers describe a trial that exists but fails: `12608420898` (gb, 2025-05) *"I opted for the free trial. Although it shows that I've subscribed, the app doesn't move past the subscription screen"*; `12605461922` (us, 2025-05) *"once i got the 3 day free trial it just glitched and won't move forward"*; `12601413294` (mx) *"They gave a free trial but it doesn't work."*

**Interpretation: a trial was introduced somewhere between 2023 and 2025 and does not reliably grant access.** The complaint did not go away — it changed shape from "there is none" to "it doesn't work."

## Trend 3 — Entitlement failure is the growth defect. Worsening.

3.4% (P1) → 0% (P2) → 8.0% (P3) → **12.6% (P4)** → 7.9% (P5). In absolute terms: 3 → 0 → 8 → 16 → 5. 2025 is the worst year on record for paid users being locked out of what they bought, and the last shipped version is dated 2025-05-15 with release notes reading only "Bug fix."

## Trend 4 — The onboarding was fixed. This is the corpus's one clear win.

Onboarding-friction complaints: 0 (P1) → 1 (P2) → **16 (P3, 16.0% of 2024)** → 1 (P4, 0.8%) → 3 (P5, 4.8%).

A 16-review spike concentrated in Feb–Aug 2024, then near-silence. Something changed in the intro after mid-2024 — most likely a skip control. Note the cost of the delay: the spike coincides with the arrival of a large negative 2024 cohort.

## Trend 5 — The 5-star prompt appears in 2024 and is *more* discussed in 2026 than ever.

0 (P1) → 0 (P2) → 9 (P3, 9.0%) → 7 (P4, 5.5%) → **7 (P5, 11.1%)**. First occurrence: `10905116935` (ca, 2024-02-05). Most recent: `14210464046`/`13960253250` (gb, 2026-04) and `14138334719` (th, 2026-06).

The 5★ *count* also steps up in exactly the same period — 1 in 2023, then 24, 29, 17. **These two facts are correlated in time and it is tempting to read one as causing the other. The corpus cannot establish that.** Both are consistent with the prompt's introduction; both are also consistent with a genuine 2024 product improvement. What the corpus *does* establish is that the prompt exists, that it is placed pre-use, and that at least two 5★ ratings were given because of it.

## Trend 6 — Reliability is getting worse, not better.

Bugs: 11.4% → 3.8% → 9.0% → 16.5% → **17.5%**. Two sub-themes are new since 2024 and still open in 2026: calendar-sync failure (first 2024-12, `12021381513`; latest 2026-06, `13812177642`) and time-editing/format bugs (first 2024-12; latest 2026-07, `14329102917`).

## Trend 7 — The typo problem was fixed and has not returned.

12 text-quality complaints, **all dated between 2022-01 and 2023-02**, then zero across 259 subsequent reviews. `8200241804` (us, 2022-01) *"Everywhere in the app where it is meant to say lunch, it instead says launch"*; `9525710586` (us, 2023-01) *"'You gonna' 'gonna gonna gonna' I can't take it serious."* This is a genuine, completed remediation — and worth noting that it took roughly 13 months during which it cost the app 12 reviews at a mean of 1.58.

## Trend 8 — Widgets became a demand only after 2024.

0 requests before 2024-11; then 1 (2024), 7 (2025), 1 (2026). The demand is new, current, and comes from retained users (group mean 2.44, including one 5★).

## Trend 9 — The promo giveaway is a bounded, closed incident.

All 8 `promo_not_honored` reviews fall between 2024-07-20 and 2025-05-28, clustered around a lifetime-access giveaway (several name "AppAdvice"). Two distinct failure modes: the offer never appeared (`11514752409`), and the entitlement was granted then lost or charged (`12136171638`, `12060522005`). No occurrences after May 2025.

## 10.3 Method and its known error risk

1. **Full read.** All 404 records were read in full in their original language, ordered by country then date. The read-through index (1–404) is preserved in Part 11 and is the basis of every hand-assigned label.
2. **Hand assignment.** Theme membership was assigned per record during the read. A regex classifier was then run independently over the same corpus; where it disagreed with the manual pass, each disagreement was inspected and adjudicated manually. The regex under-detected the paywall theme by 48 records (114 vs 162) — mostly non-English and idiomatic phrasings ("asks u to pay", "requires purchase before viewing", "je bent er niks mee") — which is why the manual pass, not the classifier, is authoritative.
3. **Aggregation.** All counts, percentages, cross-tabs and the Part 11 index were computed programmatically from the hand-assigned ID lists, so every number in this report is reproducible by filtering Part 11.
4. **Known error risk.** Boundary judgments were required for: (a) whether a review criticising price should also count as `paywall_block` — resolved by requiring an explicit statement that access was blocked; (b) whether a 5★ review's praise refers to onboarding or to use — the 9-record `praise at onboarding stage` group in §5.1 is the least certain classification in this report and should be treated as ±3; (c) `paid_direct` membership requires an explicit statement of payment, so genuine paying customers who did not say so are undercounted. **80 is a floor, not an estimate.**
5. **Translation.** Non-English reviews were read in the original. Where quoted, the original text is preserved and an English gloss is given in square brackets.

## 10.4 Known limitations and biases

- **Selection bias, twice over.** Written reviews are 2.42% of ratings in the 13 sampled storefronts, and this app additionally solicits ratings pre-use — so the written corpus over-represents people who bypassed or resented the prompt.
- **Survivorship.** Satisfied long-term users are systematically under-represented; 22 sustained-outcome reviews out of 404 is a floor on satisfaction, not a measure of it.
- **No version field.** The corpus has no per-review app version. All version attribution in Part 8 is inferred from dates against the externally known release date (v4.3.8, 2025-05-15) and is labelled as inference.
- **2026 is partial** (Jan–Aug). P5 comparisons are to a partial year.
- **Only one country clears n=50.** All non-US country statements are group-level or explicitly flagged as limited evidence.
- **`vote_count`/`vote_sum` unused.** 94 votes across 404 reviews is too sparse to weight by helpfulness.
- **The high-spend group membership is external and judgment-based**, not corpus-derived; it is stated so it can be challenged.

## 10.5 Counting rules

- **Non-exclusive.** A review carrying 6 labels counts once in each of 6 themes. Theme percentages therefore sum to well over 100%.
- **Global denominator = 404** for every theme percentage in Parts 0, 3, 5 and 8.
- **Segment rates** (e.g. "41.25% of paid reviewers") always name their denominator and are never substituted for the threshold label, which is always computed against 404.
- **Country denominators** are that country's total records (US = 135).
- **`paid_direct`** requires an explicit statement of payment, charge, subscription, trial purchase, or refund request. Inferred purchase is excluded.
- **`paywall_block`** requires a statement that the app could not be used/accessed without payment, or an objection to payment being required. A bare "too expensive" with no access claim is `price_objection` only.
- **`praise`** requires any positive statement about the product. `praise_outcome` additionally requires a described result from use over time.
- **Direction** (+/−/mixed) describes the theme, not the review; a review can carry both `praise` and `paywall_block` (16 do).
