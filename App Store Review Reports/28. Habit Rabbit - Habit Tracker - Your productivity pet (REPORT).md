# Habit Rabbit: Habit Tracker (App Store ID 1522121879) — Full Review Analysis

**App:** Habit Rabbit: Habit Tracker — *Your productivity pet*
**Developer of record:** Justin Patrick Silang · **Bundle:** `com.superbyte.habitrabbit`
**Corpus:** 821 written reviews · 62 storefronts · 15 September 2021 → 6 September 2026
**Corpus mean rating:** 4.030 · **Extracted:** 8 September 2026 (`itunes MZStore userReviewsRow`)
**Analysis date:** 11 September 2026
**Business model:** Free download, **consumable in-app currency ("carrots")** that doubles as the premium unlock. Store listing (accessed 11 September 2026) shows five consumable tiers: 1,000 carrots $1.99 · 2,500 $3.99 · 6,000 $9.99 · 10,000 $14.99 · 70,000 $59.99. No subscription is listed. Free tier is ad-supported with **forced full-screen interstitials fired on habit check-off** — this single mechanic is the dominant fact of the entire corpus.

---

## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Every claim traces to an exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several themes. 687 of 821 records (83.7%) carry at least one theme; the remaining 134 are short generic praise (median 38 characters, 120 of 134 are 5★) and are counted in every denominator.
- Signal thresholds: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.

### ⚠️ Seven warnings before any number here is used

1. **One theme dominates to an unusual degree.** 164 of 821 reviews (19.98%) complain about advertising. No other negative theme exceeds 5.5%. Any reading of this corpus that treats the ad load as one problem among many misreads it: it is *the* problem, and it is four times larger than the next one.
2. **Only two storefronts clear the 50-review bar** — US (357) and GB (77). Every other country observation in Part 7 is explicitly labelled limited evidence. 51 of 62 storefronts have fewer than 10 reviews.
3. **The corpus is US-heavy: 43.5% of records.** Global percentages are effectively US-weighted. Where a finding could plausibly differ by market, the US and GB rates are shown separately.
4. **Classification was hand-audited, not blind-automated.** All 821 records were read in full. Regex candidate sets were then generated and every candidate list was manually inspected; 12 false positives were removed from the ad theme (English "add" used as a verb) and one false negative added (Dutch `reclame`, `12271048837`). Boundary rules are in Part 10.1. Residual error risk is highest in the broad `motivation` and `cute` themes, which are reported as context, not as decision drivers.
5. **Paid-user evidence is thin and skewed to complaints.** Only 24 reviewers (2.92%) give direct evidence of having paid. Reviews are a complaint channel; satisfied payers are under-represented. **No conversion rate can be inferred from this corpus and none is claimed.**
6. **The store listing's last version date (5.12, 18 November 2025, accessed 11 September 2026) is ~10 months before the newest review in the corpus.** Treat this as one data point, not a proven release cadence — but it is consistent with 2026 complaints about freezing on current iOS (`14264271258`).
7. **A review-solicitation prompt is visible in the data and is getting worse.** 7 reviewers (0.85%) complain about being nagged to rate; 5 of the 7 are from 2026. Rating inflation from prompting is possible, unquantifiable, and likely non-trivial given 134 records of contentless 5★ praise.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. The forced interstitial after each habit check-off is the single largest product problem in the corpus, and it is actively destroying the app's rating.**
**164 reviews (19.98%, high-priority, mean rating 2.73)** complain about ads. The comparison that matters: **reviews that complain about ads average 2.73 stars; every other review averages 4.35.** Ad complaints account for **52.4% of all 1★ reviews (44/84)**, **50.0% of 2★ (25/50)** and **46.9% of 3★ (45/96)**. Evidence: `7937662501`, `8227676097`, `8969536208`, `9432095925`, `10285562204`, `10864657096`, `11343479903`, `11436179186`, `11927347262`, `12141354840`, `12237563623`, `12684556656`, `12937375914`, `13331183751`, `13813598207`, `14167531729`, `14489106892`, `14517986953`. **Interpretation: this is not ad fatigue in general — it is one specific placement. The interstitial fires on the exact tap the whole product exists to reward.**

**2. Reviewers name the mechanic precisely, and they name it as a punishment.**
**33 reviews (4.02%, very strong, mean 2.55)** explicitly describe an ad firing per check-off or per tap. The recurring framing is inversion of the reward: *"Ads after each habit tracked punishes this behaviour"* (`10285562204`, CA, 1★), *"it feels like a punishment for doing what you were meant to do"* (`9512039822`, CA, 4★), *"It's like receiving a punishment for doing what you're supposed to be doing"* (`12798782665`, TW, 1★), *"instead here you will get a forced ad popping up — replacing 'the feel-good' with this uncanny worry"* (`10603295980`, SE, 2★), *"at least let me tick them all off and then show me an ad"* (`13331183751`, AU, 2★). Also `8227676097`, `8500066140`, `9016620744`, `9525968834`, `11317318079`, `11925342130`, `12224909805`, `13215106051`. **Interpretation: users are not asking for zero ads. They are asking for the interstitial to move off the reward tap. Several propose the fix themselves.**

**3. Ads are costing installs that had already converted emotionally — 30 reviewers say the app became unusable.**
**30 reviews (3.65%, very strong, mean 2.17)** state the ad volume made the app unusable or caused them to stop; **19 (2.31%, meaningful, mean 2.21)** use explicit churn language (deleted / uninstalled / switching). *"I've just stopped using the app altogether"* (`12937375914`), *"No longer usable due to amount of ads"* (`12684556656`), *"Used to be great, now unusable due to ads"* (`13565718990`), *"Viel zu viel Werbung. gelöscht."* / "Far too much advertising. Deleted." (`14402457033`, DE, 1★), *"Desinstalada"* (`12237563623`, ES, 1★), *"Ad bombs have not been fixed after two years… Sad to say goodbye"* (`12683376685`). **Interpretation: the churn is concentrated among people who first liked the app — the phrase "used to love this app" recurs (`9174047672`, `11108270607`, `12413507566`, `12646697365`, `13565718990`).**

**4. Nineteen 5-star reviewers complain about the ads anyway. That is the strongest possible signal that the product is good and the ad policy is not.**
IDs: `8045917070`, `8222753363`, `8374013533`, `8638052150`, `10129690561`, `10513148812`, `10861065889`, `11156342874`, `11266972332`, `11571616630`, `11872296553`, `11921666440`, `12291106258`, `12334583336`, `12340932114`, `12368196957`, `13644706658`, `13698391268`, `14057599797`. Representative: *"Probably the best free app I have | Except for the ads…"* (`11921666440`), *"Müq ama oyun reklam vermeseydi silmiyecektim"* / "Great, but if the game didn't show ads I wouldn't delete it" (`10129690561`, TR, 5★), *"Pls remove add with a offer to remove adds please"* (`13644706658`, GB, 5★).

**5. The ad load measurably worsened in the second half of 2024 and has only partially recovered.**
Ad-complaint share by half-year: **2021H2 3.5% → 2022H1 11.9% → 2022H2 7.4% → 2023H1 11.8% → 2023H2 13.2% → 2024H1 22.0% → 2024H2 39.6% → 2025H1 34.4% → 2025H2 32.6% → 2026H1 18.8% → 2026H2 16.7%.** Corpus mean rating tracks it: 4.729 (2021) → 4.180 (2022) → 3.877 (2023) → **3.810 (2024)** → 3.846 (2025) → 4.048 (2026). Users noticed the change and said so: *"Since then, the amount of ads has increased HEAVILY"* (`11585029310`, AU, 1★, 14 months of use), *"The developers got too greedy I guess"* (`11028566814`, TR, 2★), *"Seems like every update just brings more paywall and more forced advertisements"* (`9649621368`, US, 3★), *"It was great but way too many ads now… I wish they would go back to like 1 or 2 ads max"* (`11108270607`). **Interpretation: 2024H2 is the inflection point. The 2026 improvement is real but leaves the rate ~5× the 2021 baseline.**

**6. The core product works, and works unusually well. Nothing in the negative data touches the concept.**
**244 reviews (29.72%, high-priority, mean 4.54)** describe a concrete behaviour change or motivational benefit; **203 (24.73%, high-priority, mean 4.30)** praise the art/cuteness; **60 (7.31%, high-priority, mean 4.25)** specifically praise the clean-the-room loop; **40 (4.87%, very strong, mean 4.15)** describe emotional attachment to the rabbit. *"cleaning this stupid bunny's stupid room is what finally got me to stick to one"* (`9623080855`), *"I have tried other habit trackers, but Habit Rabbit has done what others could not do… make me feel guilty. :)"* (`11750623010`), *"Habit rabbit made me lose 80 pounds"* (`8975166340`), *"I take my iron pill just to check it off my list"* (`12752487585`). **Interpretation: the gamification loop is a genuine differentiator. It is being spent on ad inventory.**

**7. Study Bunny is the acquisition engine — and it is drying up.**
**87 reviews (10.60%, high-priority, mean 4.37)** reference the developer's companion app Study Bunny (or Fluffle). Era rates: **31.8% of 2021 → 9.0% (2022) → 16.0% (2023) → 8.5% (2024) → 3.7% (2025) → 3.8% (2026)**. Evidence: `7810155283`, `7824183159`, `7956860419`, `8968448221`, `9022749465`, `10479769940`, `11017662366`, `12020792664`, `13539715588`, `14499820512`. **Interpretation: cross-promotion from the sibling app drove launch and still drives a tenth of written reviews, but its share has fallen by eight-tenths. Habit Rabbit is increasingly acquiring on its own — which makes its store rating, and therefore the ad policy, commercially decisive.**

**8. The free-tier limits are a real but second-order friction — and they are structurally confusing.**
**43 reviews (5.24%, high-priority, mean 3.60)** hit a free-tier gate: the habit-count cap (**17 reviews, 2.07%**), the priority-slot cap of 1 red / 2 orange (**15, 1.83%**), paid moods (**10, 1.22%**), cloud sync behind purchase (**3**), and the leaderboard (**1**). The priority cap generates the sharpest anger because it forces users to misrepresent their own lives: *"it feels disingenuous to put things like 'exercise' as low priority just so that i can put washing my face… as medium"* (`8348335779`), *"I need to take my pills everyday and I need to create a habit of going to school everyday"* (`8245454882`), *"if I put hygiene as yellow it's just wrong"* (`9199521271`, SG). **Interpretation: capping *count* is a normal freemium lever. Capping *semantics* — how important a user is allowed to say something is — reads as the app overruling the user.**

**9. Purchase fulfilment is broken often enough to be a trust problem, and the segment rate is alarming.**
**10 reviews (1.22% global, meaningful, mean 2.60)** report a purchase that did not deliver. Against the 24 reviewers who give direct evidence of paying, that is a **segment rate of 10/24 = 41.7%** (segment denominator 24; global count 10 — this is a supplemental segment rate, not a threshold classification, and it is biased upward because unhappy payers are far likelier to write). Evidence: `7850790235` (SG, "My Purchase Can't Be Found", $6.98 skin), `8283139377` (US, "We bought 9.99$ pack and it never showed up… There is no Support button in the app"), `8433565154` (US, "$10… it hasn't given me my carrots"), `9449359473` (US, "I paid 10$ to remove ads but I still get ads"), `9493479602` (DE), `10852008291` (MX, *"No compren nada de la app… jamás las recibes"* / "Don't buy anything in the app… you never receive it"), `11266972332` (US, 5★, "purchased the full version and I am still getting ads"), `11875558557` (US, 1★, "I paid the 14.99 to unlock the full app 2 days ago and it's still saying I need to purchase"), `12064766837` (CA, restore-purchase failure on new device), `12752487585` (AU). **Interpretation: every one of these is a paying customer having a bad time in public. This is the highest-value-per-review defect in the corpus.**

**10. Five reviewers wanted to pay and were stopped by the absence of a trial.**
**5 reviews (0.61%, emerging, mean 2.20)**: *"I'd recommend the devs offer a premium trial, even just three days… I wouldn't have purchased until I was sure I liked the app, and it was impossible to like the app when it was a slew of ads"* (`11631739795`); *"Unlike the creator's other app, Study Bunny, there is no $2.00 30-day trial… if there's not even a dirt cheap 30-day trial, that's a full STOP sign"* (`11925342130`); *"I cannot yet afford to pay for the full app… but won't pay for an app that I have to force quit"* (`11782177772`); *"Kann so leider nicht beurteilen ob es wert wäre zu kaufen"* / "I can't judge whether it would be worth buying" (`12094245435`, DE); `9884921571`. **Interpretation: the ad load is simultaneously the reason to buy and the reason people never get far enough to want to. The sibling app reportedly solves this with a cheap trial.**

**11. Onboarding loses people before the loop can work.**
**38 reviews (4.63%, very strong, mean 3.24)** report confusion; the most common specific gap is that the tutorial never shows how to add, edit or delete a habit. *"The tutorial is pointless as they don't show you how to create a task"* (`11007342459`), *"the most important one: how to add and delete habits. I spent a good 15 minutes"* (`10747867482`), *"I have been sitting here just trying to figure out what I'm supposed to do"* (`10689805061`), *"how do i add a habit it wont let me"* (`10324612061`, 5★). US rate 8.12% vs GB 6.49%. A separate 11 reviews (1.34%, mean 2.36) report the edit/add controls genuinely not working — `7926829108`, `8453947308`, `8654318762`, `10651750730`, `10754209207`, `11641578754`, `13974442185`. **Interpretation: "can't add a habit" arrives as both a UX complaint and a bug report, and the app cannot tell which it is receiving. Both readings cost 1★ reviews.**

**12. Data loss is rare but catastrophic, and it is entangled with the game's punishment mechanic.**
**12 reviews (1.46%, meaningful, mean 2.42)** report lost progress; **10 (1.22%, mean 3.00)** complain about health/level decay. The two overlap: *"If you're on challenge mode and you run out of health it resets ALL of your progress… level 278… resets you to level 1"* (`11271378008`, 1★), *"I forgot about for a week… all my hard work was GONE"* (`8412783427`), *"It is the worst app you clean and if your not on it for a week you start over"* (`8650393283` — note: 5★), plus `9086097905` (FR), `9107107990` (LV), `9153972697`, `9457128262`, `9901658531` (ES), `11558759287` (DE, "My Account is gone, I was at a high Level"), `11980405124`. **Interpretation: an intentional decay mechanic and an unintentional data-loss bug are indistinguishable to the user. Both read as "the app deleted my progress".**

**13. The cheapest high-value fixes, in evidence order:** move the interstitial off the check-off tap (164 reviews) → fix purchase delivery and add an in-app support/restore path (10 payer reports + 6 support complaints) → ship a short cheap premium trial (5) → teach add/edit/delete in onboarding (38 + 11) → uncap priority slots or rename them (15) → ship a widget (14, mean 4.64, zero negatives) → add monthly / multi-per-day / every-other-week frequencies (15) → localize Spanish, Turkish, Russian, Chinese, Japanese (21) → separate decay from data loss and make backups visible (12 + 10) → add more rooms/late-game content (16).

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Full read.** All 821 records were dumped chronologically into nine files of ~95 records and read end to end, in the original language. No sampling, no keyword-only shortcut, no truncation.
2. **Candidate generation.** Multilingual regex patterns produced candidate sets per theme (English, Spanish, Portuguese, Turkish, German, French, Dutch, Vietnamese, Russian, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Arabic, plus accent-folded Latin variants).
3. **Manual audit of every candidate list.** Each candidate was reviewed with its surrounding text. Concrete corrections applied:
   - The English verb "add"/"adds" collided with the noun "ads"/"adds". **12 records were removed** from the ad theme on inspection (`7907655847`, `8066734047`, `8654318762`, `9351876515`, `9483570776`, `10695719769`, `10747867482`, `11190208726`, `12135189688`, `12309079639`, `13974442185`, `14362430505`).
   - **1 record was added** after a targeted sweep of other-language ad words found Dutch `reclame` uncovered (`12271048837`, BE, 2★).
   - 17 ad-mentioning records were separated out as praise or neutral rather than complaint (Part 4.1).
   - 20+ further removals across `priority_limit`, `content_exhaustion`, `health_decay`, `price_objection`, `bug_launch`, `localization` where the regex matched a positive description or an unrelated sense.
4. **Small/ambiguous themes were assembled by hand** from the full read rather than by regex (`bug_launch`, `bug_rabbit_offscreen`, `bug_cant_edit`, `data_loss`, `localization`, `content_exhaustion`, `frequency_flex`, `sync_cloud`, `iap_broken`, `ui_bloat_regression`, `game_not_tracker`, `pet_interaction_request`). These have no classifier error rate but do carry assignment judgement; the boundary rules are in Part 10.1 so a second reader can reproduce or dispute each call.
5. **Cross-tabulation** by rating, storefront, calendar year and half-year.

## 1.6 Limitations and known biases

- **Written reviews only.** Star-only ratings are absent. The App Store shows far more ratings than written reviews, so this corpus over-represents people with something specific to say — which systematically over-weights complaints and enthusiastic praise against the quiet middle.
- **US dominance (43.5%).** Global rates approximate US rates. Country splits are given wherever the difference could matter.
- **Only 2 storefronts reach n=50.** No standalone claim is made for any other country.
- **Language coverage of the classifier.** 93 records contain Latin diacritics and 23 contain non-Latin scripts (13 CJK, 4 Cyrillic, 2 Arabic, 2 Hangul, 1 Thai, 1 Georgian). All were read; the ad and localization themes were explicitly swept in those languages. Fine-grained themes (e.g. `onboarding`) may still under-count non-English records — the English-language rate should be assumed the more reliable one.
- **Review prompting.** 7 reviewers report being nagged to rate. This plausibly inflates the 5★ count and specifically the 134 contentless-praise records.
- **Version attribution is impossible.** The corpus has no app-version field. Statements like "the 2024H2 ad increase" are inferred from review dates and reviewer testimony, not from release notes.
- **No purchase data.** Payer identification relies entirely on self-report. No conversion, ARPU or refund rate can be or is computed.
- **Correlation, not causation.** Ad-complaining reviews average 2.73 vs 4.35 for the rest. That is a difference in who writes what, and is consistent with ads causing low ratings, but the corpus cannot prove causation on its own.

# PART 8 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

**Method.** Reviews are bucketed by `date` into calendar years and calendar half-years. No version field exists, so every "change" below is inferred from date plus reviewer testimony and is labelled as inference. Buckets below n=40 (2023H2, 2025H2, 2026H2) are flagged where used.

## 8.1 The headline series

| Period | n | Mean ★ | 5★ | 1★ | Ad complaint | Study Bunny | Bugs | Onboarding | Paywall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 (Sep–Dec) | 85 | 4.729 | 83.5% | 0.0% | 3.5% | 31.8% | 5.9% | 5.9% | 3.5% |
| 2022 | 178 | 4.180 | 62.9% | 9.0% | 9.6% | 9.0% | 10.7% | 2.8% | 6.7% |
| 2023 | 106 | 3.877 | 50.9% | 12.3% | 12.3% | 16.0% | 11.3% | 6.6% | 8.5% |
| 2024 | 211 | 3.810 | 50.7% | 14.2% | **31.3%** | 8.5% | 1.4% | 6.6% | 5.2% |
| 2025 | 136 | 3.846 | 50.0% | 11.8% | 33.1% | 3.7% | 0.7% | 2.2% | 2.9% |
| 2026 (to 6 Sep) | 105 | 4.048 | 58.1% | 8.6% | 18.1% | 3.8% | 4.8% | 3.8% | 3.8% |

## 8.2 Trend 1 — Ads: emerged 2021, escalated 2024H2, partially retreated 2026 · **worsening then improving, high confidence**

Covered in Part 4.5. Rate by half-year: 3.5% → 11.9% → 7.4% → 11.8% → 13.2% → 22.0% → **39.6%** → 34.4% → 32.6% → 18.8% → 16.7%. Five independent longitudinal reviewers report a real increase (`9016620744`, `11585029310`, `12646697365`, `12891214594`, `13565718990`). The 2026 decline is supported by 105 records across both halves and is the clearest good news in the corpus.

## 8.3 Trend 2 — The launch honeymoon ended in 2022 and the app never regained it · **worsening, high confidence**

**2021 had 85 reviews, 83.5% five-star, and zero one-star reviews.** By 2024 the 5★ share had fallen to 50.7% and 1★ to 14.2%. The launch cohort was overwhelmingly Study Bunny loyalists arriving on cross-promotion (`7810155283`, `7814100388`, `7819707744`, `7820368483`, `7822994215`, `7824183159`, `7825110155`, `7825681921`) and explicitly forgiving because the app was in beta (`7991549510`: *"Best beta app ever"*; `7963282358`: *"the fact that it is still in beta means it will probably just get better"*; `7825681921`: *"it is in beta currently so some feedback would be…"*). That grace period is gone.

## 8.4 Trend 3 — Study Bunny cross-promotion is fading fast · **worsening, high confidence**

**31.8% (2021) → 9.0% → 16.0% → 8.5% → 3.7% → 3.8% (2026).** The 2023 bump (16.0%, n=106) is within noise of 2022 and not treated as a reversal. **Interpretation: Habit Rabbit is now acquiring largely on its own store presence. That raises the commercial stakes of the rating and therefore of the ad policy — the cushion that absorbed the 2022–2024 rating decline is thinner than it was.** Cross-app requests users still make: single sign-on and shared bunny across both apps (`10309270138`), and a third app (`10883208523` proposes "self care hare"; `9082534470` proposes "sleep sheep"; `9242932170` wants one about hares).

## 8.5 Trend 4 — Bug reports collapsed after 2023, then ticked back up in 2026 · **improving then watch-list, medium confidence**

**5.9% (2021) → 10.7% (2022) → 11.3% (2023) → 1.4% (2024) → 0.7% (2025) → 4.8% (2026).** The 2022–23 era is dominated by three defects that essentially vanish afterwards: the launch/orange-screen failure (8 of 15 launch reports are 2022), the rabbit-off-screen bug (10 of 12 are 2021–2023), and the can't-clean-the-room bug (all 7 are 2021–2022). **Interpretation: the app was stabilised across 2023–24 and the improvement is genuine.** The 2026 uptick is small (5 reports) but its content is specific and consistent with a stale build: `14264271258` (*"lately it's been freezing every time I open the app, I think it needs an update to keep up with ios"*), `13994061660` (*"sometimes it randomly crashes"*), `14197625518` (*"It is always glitching"*), `14260811378` (login broken), `13974442185` (can't add habits). **Given the listing's last version date of 18 November 2025 (accessed 11 September 2026), the most likely explanation is OS drift on an unreleased build. Medium confidence — n=5.**

## 8.6 Trend 5 — Onboarding confusion is persistent, not improving · **persistent, medium confidence**

5.9% → 2.8% → 6.6% → 6.6% → 2.2% → 3.8%. No monotonic direction, and the *content* is unchanged across five years: the tutorial does not teach habit CRUD. Compare `7827681816` (Sep 2021: *"I opened the app, went through the tutorial and then my room was dirty and I'm not sure how to clean it"*) with `13859274524` (Mar 2026: *"I know the purpose and am I suppose to do the task that's in the game? And then click on the task in the game I'm soooo confused"*). **Four and a half years, same complaint.**

## 8.7 Trend 6 — Free-tier caps peaked as a topic in 2023 and have declined · **improving, medium confidence**

8.5% (2023) → 5.2% (2024) → 2.9% (2025) → 3.8% (2026). **Interpretation, labelled as inference: this decline may not mean the caps got better. In 2024–25 the ad load so dominated the review conversation that it plausibly crowded out every other complaint. The caps are still named in 2026 (`14047483354`, `14362430505`, `14445631071`, `14518675611`) at a steady low rate.**

## 8.8 Trend 7 — Review-prompt nagging is new in 2026 · **emerging, low-medium confidence**

5 of 7 complaints are 2026 (`14304401769`, `14376864779`, `14404977610`, `14489106892`, `14495169235`), against 1 in 2022 and 1 in 2024. n=7 overall; treat as an early signal, but note it includes a 1★ given **solely** for the prompt (`14495169235`).

## 8.9 What did not change

- **Cuteness praise is flat across all five years** (27.1% → 21.3% → 25.5% → 28.0% → 22.1% → 24.8%). The art has never stopped working.
- **The room-cleaning loop is praised in every year** from `7824183159` (2021) to `14374523863` (2026).
- **The widget request runs continuously** from April 2022 to September 2026 with no sign of being fulfilled.
- **No reviewer in any year mentions AI features, AI coaching, or AI-generated content as something they want from this app.** The only AI mention in 821 reviews is a complaint about AI homework-cheating *ads* being served inside it (`10481431006`). **Stated explicitly because it is a real negative finding: there is no evidence of AI demand in this audience.**

# PART 9 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation cites the findings that support it. Immediate fixes are separated from experiments and open research questions.

## 9.1 Immediate fixes (evidence is unambiguous; no further research needed)

**F1. Move the interstitial off the habit check-off tap.**
*Evidence:* Part 4 — 164 reviews (19.98%), 52.4% of all 1★, mean rating 2.73 vs 4.35, 33 reviews naming the per-action mechanic, 30 calling the app unusable, 19 churning, 19 five-star reviewers still complaining.
*Specifics users themselves propose:* batch it — *"at least let me tick them all off and then show me an ad"* (`13331183751`); cap it — *"Maybe max one every time you open the app"* (`8256803229`); reduce frequency — *"I wish they would go back to like 1 or 2 ads max"* (`11108270607`).
*Expected effect:* the 3★ and 4★ bands are 214 reviews of which 76 carry ad complaints; this is where rating recovery lives.

**F2. Make every ad dismissible and verify the "decline the rewarded ad, get a forced ad anyway" path.**
*Evidence:* `11782177772` (*"no way to exit the ad's pop-up window. I must force quit"*), `13141273505` (TR, *"when the ad appears you can't get out of it"*), `9174047672` (*"can't close after 15 seconds"*), and `13918308778`'s specific report that declining the rewarded double still serves an unrewarded ad. Three independent reports of non-dismissible ads is a store-guidelines risk as well as a UX one.

**F3. Fix purchase delivery and add an in-app "Restore purchases" + "Contact support" entry point.**
*Evidence:* Part 6.4 — 10 purchase failures (41.7% of identified payers, segment denominator 24), `8283139377` (*"There is no Support button in the app. Nor on developer's website"*), `12064766837` (restore fails on a new device), `10852008291` (emails unanswered). Counter-evidence that this works: `10450143353` upgraded **1★ → 5★** after a single support email.
*Also audit:* whether the consumable-only architecture is the root cause of restore failures (Part 2.2), and whether "no ads" should be a non-consumable product.

**F4. Teach habit add / edit / delete in onboarding, and make the tutorial skippable.**
*Evidence:* 38 onboarding complaints (4.63%, mean 3.24) + 11 CRUD-failure reports (1.34%, mean 2.36); the two are indistinguishable from the outside. `11007342459`, `10747867482`, `10689805061`, `11190208726`, `10475830356` all name the same gap; `11655072177` (1★) wants to skip it entirely.

**F5. Ship the widget.**
*Evidence:* 14 requests (1.71%), **mean 4.64, zero negative ratings**, continuous April 2022 → September 2026. Desired behaviour is specified: tap the widget to check a habit done (`14517374247`).

**F6. Uncap or rename the priority slots.**
*Evidence:* 15 reviews (1.83%, mean 3.40). The complaint is never "I want more features" — it is that the cap forces a false statement about the user's own life (`8348335779`, `8245454882`, `9199521271`, `8968448221`, `9124737122`). If the cap must stay for monetization, rename the tiers so they read as labels rather than as the app's judgement of importance, or let users choose the colours (`9199521271`: *"just let us choose the colour ourself and not give us a max amount"*).

**F7. Verify the app on current iOS and ship an update.**
*Evidence:* listing version 5.12 dated 18 November 2025 (accessed 11 September 2026); 2026 bug reports `14264271258` (freezing, *"needs an update to keep up with ios"*), `13994061660`, `14197625518`, `14260811378`. Medium confidence, n=5, but the cost of checking is near zero.

**F8. Fix the Chinese localization defects and audit CJK font fallback + string truncation.**
*Evidence:* `13744289117` (1★, Japanese glyphs rendered for Chinese text; habit names silently truncated), `12296652523` (5★, large amounts of untranslated English with Chinese selected), plus the historical precedent of `9179812608` (every "r" rendered as a black square after a localization update). `13582227455` (PT) reports the same partial-translation problem.

**F9. Dial back the rating prompt.**
*Evidence:* 7 complaints, 5 of them in 2026, including a 1★ given only for the prompt (`14495169235`) and a 5★ asking it to stop (`14404977610`). Prompting into an ad-frustrated session is also actively harvesting negative ratings.

## 9.2 Experiments (clear hypothesis, uncertain magnitude)

**E1. A cheap, short premium trial — mirroring Study Bunny's reported $2 / 30-day model.**
*Hypothesis:* the current funnel requires users to endure the worst version of the product before they can buy the good one; a trial inverts that.
*Evidence:* 5 reviews explicitly blocked by the absence of a trial (`11631739795`, `11925342130`, `11782177772`, `12094245435`, `9884921571`) plus 5 payers who converted only after extended free use (`8230449667`, `11264169679`, `11156342874`, `14057599797`, `14155124146`).
*Measure:* trial-start rate, trial→purchase rate, and change in the 1–3★ ad-complaint share.

**E2. Unbundle the purchase, or at minimum make what each purchase unlocks explicit.**
*Hypothesis:* an opaque consumable bundle suppresses purchases and generates support load.
*Evidence:* `9089433219` (*"There should be an option to just pay like two dollars to just get unlimited tasks"*), `10878892150` (wants moods sold separately), `12148758031` (*"I now realize just buying anything gives u cloud access"* — discovered by accident), `9407269123` (paid $10 and was then charged again for a new paid feature).

**E3. Late-game content: add rooms, or ship a "prestige"/reset loop.**
*Hypothesis:* completion of the house is a hard retention cliff.
*Evidence:* 16 reviews (1.95%), including a paying customer leaving over it (`8703335659`) and a fully specified design from a user (`9623080855`: reset the house, count resets as a progress metric). Also `9177007332`, `9891058432`, `12416426978`, `11918683339`, `8852905838`.

**E4. Flexible habit frequency: monthly, every-other-week, multiple-times-per-day, specific weekdays.**
*Hypothesis:* the weekly-only model wastes limited habit slots and blocks purchase.
*Evidence:* 15 reviews (1.83%), one explicitly blocking a purchase — *"I'd love for this to be my constant app, but I can't see myself purchasing without being able to make these distinctions"* (`11136830862`). Also `7868151028`, `8222834153`, `8282086026`, `11146992358`, `11264169679`, `14099704149`.

**E5. Localize Spanish, Turkish, Russian, and complete Chinese.**
*Hypothesis:* rest-of-world markets already rate the app higher (4.181 vs 3.956) and are blocked mainly by language (5.56% vs 1.09% localization demand).
*Evidence:* Part 7.4 and 7.5. Two reviewers name language as their reason for leaving (`12403260326`, `11644145389`); one says the opposite — *"i don't even want to look for another tracker"* if it were localized (`11946017120`).

**E6. Separate the decay mechanic from the data model, and say so.**
*Hypothesis:* users cannot distinguish intentional punishment from a bug, and both produce the same 1★.
*Evidence:* 12 data-loss reports + 10 decay complaints, overlapping in `8412783427`, `8650393283`, `9564128000`, `11271378008`, `11980405124`. A visible backup/restore state and a gentler or optional decay would separate the two. Note the counter-argument from a defender (`11960853599`) — some users value the stakes, so make it a setting rather than removing it.

**E7. Reward the to-do list, not just habits.**
*Evidence:* 9 requests (1.10%, mean 4.44) — *"we should be able to earn energy / rewards for getting your todo list done"* (`10705887680`), *"Please allow the timer to be used for todo list tasks with carrot rewards… these are usually the more irksome and time consuming tasks that you procrastinate on"* (`13365326137`), also `10029940699`, `11112031723`, `12752487585`.

## 9.3 Research questions the corpus raises but cannot answer

1. **Is ad frequency tenure-scaled?** `9016620744` suspects it; several long-tenured reviewers report escalation (`11585029310`, `12891214594`, `13565718990`). Server-side configuration would answer this in minutes and it materially changes the fix.
2. **What is the actual conversion rate, and how much revenue does the check-off interstitial earn versus the ratings it costs?** The corpus can size the damage but not the benefit. This is the central trade-off and it cannot be decided from reviews.
3. **Do CN storefront users see ads at all?** 12 CN reviews, zero ad complaints (Part 7.7). If not, CN is a natural control group for what the ad-free product's complaints look like.
4. **How much of the 2026 5★ volume is prompt-driven?** 134 contentless praise records plus a 2026 spike in prompt complaints suggests the recent rating recovery may be partly solicited rather than earned.
5. **Is the sibling-app funnel recoverable?** Study Bunny references fell from 31.8% to 3.8%. Whether that is a cross-promotion change, a Study Bunny audience change, or simple maturation is not answerable from this corpus.
6. **What is the retention curve after house completion?** 16 reviews say the motivation stops; the corpus cannot say when, for how many, or whether the reset idea would actually work.

## 9.4 What not to change

- **Do not simplify away the gamification.** It is the differentiator (Parts 3.2, 6.7). Two reviewers explicitly prefer it to the plainer competition, and one prefers Habit Rabbit precisely because it is *less* feature-heavy than Finch (`14075378286`).
- **Do not add AI features on the strength of category trend.** Zero of 821 reviews request them (Part 8.9).
- **Do not raise the price.** 16 price objections already, and the winning competitive position is "more affordable alternative" (`11488297687`).
- **Do not remove the rabbit's emotional stakes entirely.** 40 reviews describe attachment as the mechanism that works; the decay complaint is about calibration and recoverability, not about the concept (`11960853599`).

## 10.4 Counting rules

1. **Denominator.** All global percentages use 821. Country percentages use that country's own total (US 357, GB 77). Rating-band percentages use that band's total (5★ 473, 4★ 118, 3★ 96, 2★ 50, 1★ 84). Segment rates (e.g. purchase failures among identified payers) state both numerator and segment denominator and are labelled segment rates; they never substitute for the threshold classification.
2. **Non-exclusive membership.** A review can belong to any number of themes. Theme counts do not sum to 821.
3. **No deduplication.** There are no duplicate review IDs and no exact duplicate (title, body) pairs. Two reviews from different storefronts share near-identical Spanish text (`8012787229`, ES, Nov 2021 and `10892633195`, ES, Feb 2024) — these are distinct records with distinct IDs and dates and are both counted.
4. **Mean rating per theme** is the arithmetic mean of the `rating` field over that theme's members.
5. **Signal labels** follow the prescribed table applied to the global or country share as stated.
6. **Quotations** are verbatim, trimmed only at sentence boundaries, with HTML entities decoded. Non-English quotes are given in the original with a labelled English translation.
7. **Translations** in this report were produced by the analyst and are marked with `/`. They are functional, not certified.
