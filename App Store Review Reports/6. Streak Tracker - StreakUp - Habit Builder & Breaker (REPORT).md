# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

**Executive summary in one line:** a 13-month-old, genuinely liked, genuinely differentiated streak counter is converting its own good will into one-star reviews, because **the free tier caps at 2 streaks, the upsell is loud, the price ladder is incoherent, and at least two people were billed an amount they did not agree to** — while the one thing users unanimously love, the widget, is free and barely mentioned in the store listing.

## 1. Nearly half the written corpus is about money, and it is where all the damage is

**21 of 44 reviews (47.73%) touch monetization** in some form. **16 of 44 (36.36%) are negative about it**, and those 16 average **2.44 stars** against a corpus mean of 3.89.

The concentration is total at the bottom of the rating scale:

- **6 of the 8 one-star reviews (75%) are monetization complaints** — `13185604622`, `13531820409`, `13557096013`, `13628759209`, `13771926913`, `13969396065`, plus `14491149862` whose entire body is *"You gotta pay a subscription😭"*. That is 7 of 8 if you count the last one.
- **All 5 three-star reviews are monetization complaints.** Every single one: `13939159292` (double-charged), `14205874183` (*"make it free / pls"*), `14267106974` (*"You can add only two habits"*), `14307898374` (UA, price), `14346310474` (2-streak cap).
- **There are zero two-star reviews.** The rating distribution is 24 / 7 / 5 / 0 / 8. People are either happy or they are angry about the paywall; there is almost no middle.

**Interpretation:** this app does not have a product problem at its core. It has a **packaging and billing problem** sitting on top of a product people like. Fix the packaging and the 3★ band (5 reviews) converts almost mechanically, because every one of those five reviewers said something positive about the app in the same breath.

## 2. The 2-streak free cap is the single most-named thing in the corpus

**7 of 44 reviews (15.91%, mean 3.00) name the free streak limit explicitly** — `13185604622`, `13969396065`, `14214912053`, `14267106974`, `14346310474`, `14372026009`, `14477924333`.

What makes this the most important finding in the report is **who is complaining**. Look at the ratings: 1★, 1★, 4★, 3★, 3★, **5★**, 4★. The median cap-complainer *likes the app*:

- `14477924333` (US, **4★**): *"Love the app, just wish i could have more than 2 streaks without paying."*
- `14372026009` (CA, **5★**): *"it's got all it needs to have u can only have 2 streaks on the free plan tho"*
- `14214912053` (US, **4★**): *"App never has any glitches and works perfectly! The only problem I have is that streak freezes and unlimited streaks is only available if you pay… I'm personally a go-getter so I would like to have more than 2 streaks. But app is awesome otherwise."*
- `14346310474` (US, 3★): *"Great if you want a simple streak app but only lets you have 2 streaks for free."*

**This is a conversion problem being paid for in stars.** These are not people rejecting the product; they are people who hit the wall, did not buy, and rated on the way past. The cap is doing two jobs at once and failing at both: it is too tight to demonstrate value (a person tracking "gym + water + no doomscrolling" is already over budget at habit three), and it is loud enough to generate public complaint.

**The cap moved once already, and it did not solve the problem.** The earliest cap complaint, `13185604622` (26 Sep 2025), says **"only one streak"**. Every cap complaint from 18 Apr 2026 onward says **two** (`13969396065`, `14267106974`, `14346310474`, `14372026009`, `14477924333`). So the free allowance was raised 1 → 2 somewhere between Sep 2025 and Apr 2026 — and the complaint rate went *up*, not down: 1 cap complaint in the first 9 reviews, 5 cap complaints in the last 23. Going from 1 to 2 was not enough. There is no evidence that 3 would be either; the ask in the text is for **unlimited or near-unlimited**.

## 3. Two people were charged amounts they did not agree to — and this is the highest-severity finding

**2 of 44 (4.55%) report a billing-integrity failure.** At this sample size that is 2 people, but both are specific, both name figures, and both are unresolved in the text.

- `13771926913` (US, **1★**, 21 Feb 2026) — *"Said it was $1.99/month and charged my card $20.99?? I want my money back, this is not right."*
- `13939159292` (US, **3★**, 9 Apr 2026) — *"I was on the free trial, and decided to purchase the 14.99 forever choice. But come to find out they charge the 11.99 yearly and the 14.99. So I paid 25.98 and can't figure out how to get the 11.99 back."*

The second is the more diagnostic of the two. It describes a **specific, reproducible failure mode: buying the lifetime unlock does not cancel the annual subscription**, so the user pays both. That is not a misunderstanding — the reviewer names both SKUs and the correct sum ($11.99 + $14.99 = $25.98). If the lifetime purchase flow does not cancel or refund an in-flight subscription, every trial user who upgrades to lifetime is double-billed.

Note that this reviewer gave **3 stars while reporting being over-billed by $11.99** and led with *"I like the app."* That is a person who wanted to stay.

**Both reviews are also unanswered support tickets posted into the App Store**, which is what people do when they cannot find an in-app refund path.

Assembled from what reviewers say they were quoted or charged:

| **~$5** | subscription (UAH/USD, reviewer says "5 баксов") | `14307898374` | Jul 2026 |

Five different numbers in eleven months, from six reviewers, in a corpus of 44. **This is the mechanism behind the "greed" theme** (4 reviews, 9.09%, **mean 1.00** — `13185604622`, `13531820409`, `13628759209`, `13969396065`): users are not objecting to *a* price, they are objecting to not knowing *the* price. `13628759209` is the clearest statement of it — the reviewer does the value math out loud (*"I can set a reminder list on in my iPhone reminders for $0. What is the $11.99 for?"*) and concludes the app is a scam.

## 5. A willing buyer could not give the company money

`14203637124` (IN, **5★**, 20 Jun 2026), entire body: *"In app purchases are not available. I want to buy the life time subscription for this app."*

**This is a five-star review from a customer with wallet in hand who was blocked by a broken IAP flow.** One review, 2.27% — but it is the single highest-value defect in the corpus by expected revenue, and it costs nothing to check. It may be an India-storefront IAP configuration issue, an age/region gate, or a client bug. It needs a same-day answer.

## 6. What people actually love: the widget, the flexibility, and the fact that it does almost nothing

The positive corpus is unusually coherent for a young app. **9 of 44 (20.45%) report a concrete behaviour-change outcome, and every one of the nine is 5★** (mean 5.00, zero exceptions): `13093308789`, `13672386764`, `13795256017`, `13837160367`, `13845360166`, `13891333657`, `14093501015`, `14349062008`, `14389534229`.

Three specific strengths recur:

**a) The widget (7 mentions, 15.91%, mean 5.00).** `13085620460`, `13093308789`, `13767758923`, `13795256017`, `13845360166`, `14372026009`, and `14389534229`. Six are unqualified praise; `14372026009` (CA) says *"I lwk js use for the widget"* — the widget **is** the product for that user. `13795256017` (IN) explains why it works: *"I added this to my widgets which is really helping me to do my works irrespective of my mood."* The widget is free, is the most-loved feature, and is one bullet at the bottom of the store description.

**b) No daily check-in required (2 mentions, but structurally important).** `13085620460` (US, 5★, the very first review in the corpus): *"I really like that you don't need daily check-ins, but rather only declare if you broke your streak."* `13767758923`: *"you don't have to check it in the app since they have widgets."* This is the **Streak Counter** mode from the listing, and it is the app's actual differentiator against every check-off habit tracker on the store.

**c) The user defines what "a day" means.** `13672386764` (BR, 5★) is the most detailed review in the corpus and worth reading in full. Translated: *"Every app is locked to clock hours and I like to count my day based on when I wake up and when I go to sleep — so it can be the small hours. Traditional apps make me lose my goal when I actually didn't lose it! Here I can log the truth of what I live, not what they decide is the only right way."* This reviewer switched from competitors **specifically because of the midnight-boundary problem**, which is a known killer in this category. That is a positioning asset nobody at Explora appears to be marketing.

## 7. The same "no check-in" design is also the top source of confusion and 1★ churn

The differentiator cuts both ways, and this is the second-most-actionable product finding.

- `13628759209` (US, **1★**): *"I was hoping for more of a checklist where I could actually check off when I had completed my task… I set my streaks, set a reminder if I'd like, and that's it. I don't really ever need to open the app again unless I break my streak. Am I missing something?"*
- `13994050917` (NG, **1★**): *"What data are you using to track streaks? The streaks keep moving whether I enter data or not."*

Both reviewers are describing **Streak Counter mode working exactly as designed** and reading it as a bug. `13994050917` in particular is a person who thinks the app is fabricating their progress — that is a trust failure, not a UX nit.

Meanwhile `14292633754` (US, 5★) proves the fix already exists in the product: *"An automatic tracker and manual tracker both are good."* **Both modes ship. The onboarding is not making users choose consciously, so a subset lands in the wrong mode and rates 1★.** `13085620460` asks for the manual mode as a *feature request* while sitting in the automatic one — more evidence the mode choice is invisible.

## 8. Two reviewers call it a clone, and both are 1★

`13531820409` (GB, 1★): *"Terrible app a clone of streaks"* — naming **Streaks** by Crunchy Bagel. `13557096013` (CA, 1★): *"This is literally a copy of the app Days Since but it's both more expensive and has less features, let alone the horrible UI and lack of customizability."*

Two reviews (4.55%) is not a trend. It matters for three reasons: both are 1★ (mean 1.00), both name a specific competitor, and **"Days Since" is app #3 in this same analysis set** — these products are being directly compared by real users. The comparison StreakUp loses on, per `13557096013`, is **customization and price**, not core function.

## 9. The public rating is 0.7 stars higher than what people write

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Written corpus, all 13 storefronts (n = 44) | **3.89** |
| Written corpus, US only (n = 23) | **3.96** |
| **US App Store displayed rating (188 ratings, 9 Sep 2026)** | **4.65** |

Roughly **12% of US raters wrote anything** (23 written / 188 total taps). The written mean sits **0.69 stars below** the tap-only mean. This is the normal direction — written reviews skew to people with a grievance — but the size of the gap here matters for one reason: **the 4.65 badge is not telling the team about the paywall problem.** 36% of the people who bothered to type are complaining about money, and none of that reaches the dashboard.

Length confirms it. **12 of 44 bodies (27.3%) are under 40 characters and average 4.17 stars**; the 32 substantive reviews average **3.78 with 21.9% one-and-two-star**. Short reviews inflate; the app's real feedback lives in the long ones.

## 4.1 The one real feature gap: multiple logs per day

Two reviewers, from different angles, describe the same missing capability — and one of them **paid to get it and did not**:

- `13390634274` (CA, 4★): *"Does NOT allow for multiple streaks for one task, within a day. I purchased the Pro Version in hopes it would have."* → cancelled.
- `14471219702` (CA, 4★, teeth-brushing twice a day): *"Only app downside is you have to log everyday, not necessarily when goal is accomplished daily."*

**The paywall implied a capability the product does not have.** That is the difference between a feature request and a refund cause, and this one is on the refund side. Two reviews is thin — but one of them is a paying customer who churned over it, which raises its weight considerably.

## 4.2 What is *not* in this corpus

Worth stating, because in comparable apps these dominate and here they are absent:

- **No crash reports.** Zero. `14214912053` volunteers the opposite: *"App never has any glitches and works perfectly!"*

Second, and more important: **negative-monetization share did not improve at all** — 44.4% → 33.3% → **34.8%**. |---|---|---|
| Sep–Dec 2025 | 1 | 11.1% |
| Jan–Apr 2026 | 1 | 8.3% |

## 10.3 Method and its known error risk

Because the corpus is 44 records, **no automated classification was used.** Every review was read individually and assigned one or more theme labels by hand; counts were then computed programmatically from that hand-coded table so the arithmetic is reproducible and the label assignment is auditable (Part 11 shows every review's labels).

**Known error risks, disclosed:**
- **Coder judgement is single-pass.** There was no second coder, so borderline assignments (e.g. whether `13531820409`'s *"App doesn't work"* is a functional defect or rhetorical dismissal — it is coded as both) rest on one reading.
- **Low-information reviews.** 12 of 44 bodies are under 40 characters. `14414628073` (*"Max verstappen"*) carries no product signal at all and is coded `noise_no_signal`; it is retained in all denominators.
- **`is_edited` looks unreliable.** `14389534229` contains a literal *"Edit"* section in its body but has `is_edited: false`; `14417731278` has `is_edited: true` with no visible edit. The field is not used for any finding.
- **Theme membership is non-exclusive.** A review can carry up to six labels (`13628759209` carries six). **Percentages must never be summed.**

## 10.4 Known limitations and biases

1. **n = 44 is too small for rate estimation.** Every percentage in this report has a confidence interval wide enough to change its band label. Read the counts.
2. **No country clears 50 reviews**, so §7 makes no standalone country claims.
3. **Written reviews are ~12% of US raters** (23 written vs 188 rated) and average **0.69 stars lower** than the displayed rating. The corpus over-represents grievance, monetization grievance most of all. That is the correct lens for a fix list and the wrong lens for measuring overall satisfaction.
4. **No version field**, so nothing is attributable to a specific release, and §8's trends may reflect audience mix as much as product change.
5. **Only 4 reviewers give direct purchase evidence.** All four had a bad experience. This is a *qualitative* finding about the checkout path; it is emphatically **not** a claim that 100% of purchases fail, and no conversion rate is claimed anywhere in this report.
6. **The 1→2 free-cap change is inferred from review text**, not from a changelog. The inference rests on `13185604622` (Sep 2025, "one streak") vs five later reviews all saying "two." No release note confirms it.
7. **Star rating is never read as a feature preference**, and no correlation here is stated as causation. Where a causal path is asserted (paywall → low rating), it rests on reviewers narrating that sequence themselves.

## 10.5 Counting rules

- A review counts once per theme regardless of how many times it mentions it.
- `paywall_streak_cap` requires an explicit reference to the number of free streaks/habits. General "it costs money" complaints are coded `price_objection` instead.
- `paid_user` requires an explicit statement of a purchase, a charge, a trial, or a cancellation — not an intent or an opinion about price.
- `PAID_EVIDENCE` (n=4) is the union of purchase, charge, trial, cancellation and blocked-purchase evidence. `14214912053` is **excluded** — it approves of the lifetime SKU without claiming to have bought it.
- `OUTCOME_ANY` requires the reviewer to describe a real-world result (quit something, got fitter, wrote more), not merely to praise the app.
- Segment rates (e.g. "6 of 8 one-star reviews") are always labelled as segment rates and always shown with the global count alongside.
