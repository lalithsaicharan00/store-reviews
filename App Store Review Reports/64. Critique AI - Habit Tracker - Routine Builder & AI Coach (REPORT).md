### ⚠️ Eight warnings before any number here is used

1. **The written corpus is not the rating base, and the gap here is the largest single fact in this report.** 419 written reviews against **18,785 public ratings** = **2.23% coverage**. Public average **4.68**; written mean **2.589**. That is a **2.09-star gap**, and it is not a normal negativity skew — it is roughly double what the other reports in this set show. §1.1 argues the gap is structural, not stylistic: there is direct review evidence (`14032722731`) that the rating prompt fires *before* the paywall.
2. **This corpus overwhelmingly describes people who never got into the product.** 346 of 419 reviews (82.58%) contain no evidence the writer ever saw the app past onboarding. Only 69 (16.47%) describe in-app experience, and only 13 (3.10%) state they paid. **This is a report about a purchase funnel, not mostly about a habit tracker.**
3. **One theme is 28.40% of the corpus.** `PAY_HARDWALL` — "you cannot use the app at all without paying" — appears in 119 reviews. Nothing else in the product is discussed at anything like this volume. Feature-level findings sit on denominators of 4–18 reviews and are labelled accordingly.
4. **Sub-50-country rule.** Only the **US (180)** clears the 50-review bar. Every other storefront — including GB (40), CA (28), AU (26), DE (14) — is reported as **[limited evidence]** and no standalone conclusion is drawn from it.
5. **22 of 419 reviews (5.25%) are not in English** (6 German, 5 Italian, 2 Norwegian, 2 Hebrew, 1 each Korean, Dutch, Spanish-MX, Spanish-ES, Spanish-GT, Spanish-CR, Czech-mixed). All were read in the original; translations here are labelled. Their mean is **2.00** — below the corpus mean.
6. **11 reviews (2.63%) contradict their own star rating**, ten of them 5★ reviews whose text is negative or transactional — including `13870793043` (*"im only doing 5 star just for you to take the payment subscription away"*) and `14003739567` (*"I only gave 5 stars so you can see this"*). They are not silently dropped; see §5.6.
7. **39.8% of the 5★ band is not a product endorsement.** 45 of 113 five-star reviews are pre-use, one-word, or self-contradicting. Only **68 five-star reviews (16.23% of corpus)** contain substantive positive content.
8. **No review burst distorts this corpus.** The busiest calendar day is 6 reviews (2025-11-24, 2026-01-05) across 275 distinct days; no single day exceeds 1.4% of the corpus, and no window shows a coordinated defect wave. Trends in Part 8 are therefore genuine drift, not one event. Three reviews share identical text (`Good`/`Good`) but are distinct records from distinct authors in distinct storefronts and are **not** deduplicated (§1.4).

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

**Executive summary in one line:** Critique AI has a demand problem it has already solved and a conversion problem it has not — a creator-driven audience arrives in volume and *wants* the product, then hits a **hard paywall with no free tier, no usable trial, and a dismiss button that a tenth of the corpus reports cannot be found**, and 82.58% of everyone who writes a review never sees the product at all. The 17% who do get in describe a genuinely differentiated app (focus-mode app-blocking, XP/leaderboards, AI physique/outfit/food/voice analysis) whose AI layer — the thing the name sells — is the single most complained-about feature among people who paid.

## 1. The paywall is the product, as far as this corpus is concerned

**166 of 419 reviews (39.62%, high-priority, mean 1.52) describe being stopped by the paywall** — the union of five separable mechanics:

| Mechanic | Code | n | % of 419 | Band | Mean ★ | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No free use at all | `PAY_HARDWALL` | 119 | 28.40% | High-priority | 1.48 | "You cannot use the app without paying" |
| Paywall won't close | `PAY_SOFTLOCK` | 39 | 9.31% | High-priority | 1.72 | No X / cancel / skip; returns on relaunch |
| Believed it was free | `PAY_BAIT` | 30 | 7.16% | High-priority | **1.23** | Told free by creator, friend, or listing |
| Quiz/photo taken first | `PAY_QUIZWALL` | 23 | 5.49% | High-priority | 1.35 | Onboarding completed, *then* the wall |
| A second paywall | `PAY_2WALL` | 8 | 1.91% | Meaningful | 1.25 | "One-time offer" after dismissing the first |

**These are not the same complaint and they do not have the same fix.** A hard paywall is a pricing decision. A paywall that cannot be dismissed is a bug — or a dark pattern — and it is the one that produces the angriest, lowest-rated reviews and the strongest legal exposure.

**The softlock is reported with unusual mechanical precision, which is what makes it credible.** Reviewers independently describe the same missing control across 17 months and 20 storefronts:

## 2. The audience arrives believing the app is free, and that belief comes from the marketing channel

**42 of 419 reviews (10.02%, high-priority, mean 2.38) name a creator, YouTube, TikTok, or a friend as the reason they downloaded.** 13 name Pierre Dalati directly. **30 reviews (7.16%, high-priority, mean 1.23 — the lowest mean of any theme in this report) state they believed the app was free.** The overlap is explicit and repeated:

- `13443337238` (de, 1★): DE *"Ich wurde über Socialmedia angelockt. Das es kostenlos sei. Ist es nicht. Kompletter Clickbait."* — "I was lured in via social media, that it was free. It isn't. Complete clickbait."
- `14126245313` (de, 1★): DE *"Ich wurde von nem YouTuber angelockt das es kostenlos sei aber es ist so ein scam"* — same claim, different reviewer, 12 months later.
- `13600059562` (us, 1★): *"There were this one TikTok and it says it's for free but it's not."*
- `14113188077` (us, 1★): *"advertised on a YouTubers channel and he never mentioned anything about a paid subscription."*
- `14072700352` (co, 1★): *"Say that in the videos. Just disrespectful."*
- `14248303022` (se, 1★): *"I needed to pay while it was free for my friends and other that recommended this app to me."*

**This is the highest-leverage finding in the report that costs nothing to fix.** The product is not mispriced relative to what buyers who get in say (§6.4 — 11 reviewers actively defend $40/yr). It is **mis-expected**. Every reviewer in the `PAY_BAIT` set was acquired at full marketing cost and converted into a permanent 1★ on the listing, because nobody told them the price before the App Store did. Mean 1.23 means this cohort barely uses the 2★ and 3★ options at all.

## 3. 82.58% of reviewers never got inside the product

| Segment | n | % of 419 | Mean ★ | 1★ share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Never got in (no evidence of post-paywall use) | 346 | 82.58% | 2.35 | 52.0% |
| Got in (`INSIDE_APP`, payment not stated) | 69 | 16.47% | **3.87** | 15.9% |
| Stated they paid (`PAID_EXPLICIT`) | 13 | 3.10% | **1.85** | 76.9% |

Read those three rows together. **Access more than doubles satisfaction (2.35 → 3.87), and then stating that you paid halves it again (3.87 → 1.85).** The second step is the alarming one and it is dissected in Part 6: of the 13 reviewers who say outright that they paid, **10 gave 1★**, and 6 of the 13 are billing disputes.

The 13 paying reviewers are too few to carry a global rate — this is a **segment rate on n=13** and is labelled as such everywhere it appears. But the direction is unambiguous and it is corroborated by the larger `INSIDE_APP` set, where the AI features (§4.3) and plan rigidity (§4.4) are the named complaints.

## 4. The single most common constructive request is "let me in somehow"

**95 reviews (22.67%, high-priority, mean 1.75) propose a specific way to be admitted** — a free tier, ads, a longer trial, or a cheaper tier. This is not the same as "make it free"; a large part of it is people offering the developer money or attention on different terms:

**12 reviewers volunteer ads as an acceptable price** (`12932976917`, `13352317135`, `13437522598`, `13552984105`, `13596302986`, `13596863767`, `14018545521`, `14223602930`, `14238660669`, `14297447085`, `14312390685`, `14454537950`) — and exactly one paying reviewer objects to ads (`14208933476`, us, 5★: *"please don't add ads. I'd rather pay than see ads"*). Meanwhile `14436616950` (gb, 4★) reports **already seeing ads while paying £40/yr**, which if accurate is the worst of both models.

The most developed proposals are worth reading in full:

## 5. The audience is younger than the price assumes

**20 reviews (4.77%, very strong, mean 1.90) state the reviewer is a minor or argue the price fails a teen audience.** Self-stated ages in the corpus: 12 (`13947967990`), 13 (`12608609019`, `13507719273`, `14032722731`, `14387194180`), 14 (`13497049323`, `13552586437`, `13591712211`, `13851683780`), 15 (`12802076240`, `13882060214`). A parent buying for a 12-year-old asks for a refund (`14290822522`). A reviewer who *likes* the app notes the demographic unprompted: `13975991354` (us, 4★) — *"everyone on it seems to be like 12-15 years old which is kind of weird."*

Two paying adults independently make the same structural argument from the other side: `14045355654` (us, 2★) asks for *"a cheaper version for people below 18"*; `14391271895` (us, 5★) defends the price precisely by conceding the problem — *"I understand his main audience is teens, who will have trouble paying for this."*

**The commercial implication is concrete:** a ~$40/yr annual-upfront subscription requires a payment instrument that a material share of the acquisition channel's audience does not control. `13552586437` (us, 1★, **9 votes — the most-upvoted review in the corpus**) spells out the requested remedy: an adult tier and a free teen tier.

## 6. The AI is the brand and, among people who used it, the weakest part

**19 reviews (4.53%, very strong, mean 2.21) touch the AI analysis features**; **8 of those (1.91%, meaningful, mean 2.00) report the output is wrong, generic, or fabricated.** Among the 13 reviewers who state they paid, **3 (segment rate 3/13)** are AI-quality complaints — and they are the most specific reviews in the entire corpus:

- `14117563141` (ca, 1★, **paid $50**): *"The voice coach just repeats the exact same prompts over and over; I turned it on and off immediately without saying a word, and it still gave me the same generic feedback. It doesn't actually listen."*
- `14252106354` (us, 2★, **$12.99/mo**): *"When I said nothing to the mic for a speaking exercise, it gave me a '79' score."*
- `13706381217` (us, 1★, **paid $25**): *"take a black photo don't talk it just gives you things that were good and stuff to work on."*
- `13227562707` (gb, 1★): *"No matter what photo you upload, it gives the same results."*
- `14016792944` (us, 3★): *"the AI checkers like outfit and posture are hallucinating a bit."*

**Four independent reviewers, in four storefronts, over ten months, ran the same adversarial test — empty input — and all four got a confident score back.** That is not a hallucination complaint; it is a claim that at least one analysis path does not consume its input. It is the highest-severity product finding in this report and the only one where a single day of engineering work could be verified against a named reproduction step.

## 7. What actually works: three features nobody asks to have removed

Among the 69 reviewers who got inside, praise concentrates on things that are not the AI:

| Feature | Code | n | % of 419 | % of `INSIDE_APP` (69) | Mean ★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Focus mode / block apps, earn them back with exercise | `FEAT_FOCUS` | 10 | 2.39% | 11.6% | 3.40 |
| XP, quests, leaderboards | `FEAT_GAMIFY` | 8 | 1.91% | 10.1% | 4.00 |
| Habit / routine / calorie tracking | `FEAT_TRACK` | 10 | 2.39% | 11.6% | 4.20 |
| Community | `FEAT_COMMUNITY` | 4 | 0.95% | 5.8% | 3.75 |

**Focus mode is the most distinctive thing in the corpus and it is also the most broken.** 10 reviews name it; **6 of those 10 report it failing** (`FOCUS_BROKEN`, 1.43%, meaningful) — the unlock exercise does nothing (`14061007904`), the app stays locked after the push-ups (`14452220984`), apps are blocked permanently (`13706381217`), there is no in-between state (`13580337718`), and silent mode breaks the unlock notification (`14391271895` — diagnosed by a **5★ defender of the app**, which is why it is credible). One reviewer downloaded *specifically* for this feature and never reached it: `13051237656` (us, 1★) — *"I was only looking cause I wanted to be forced to do pushups before I opened an app."*

**Gamification is unambiguously liked** (`14047773348`, `14141948204`, `14233567561`, `14277884922`, `14359992073`, `14436616950`) — and `14018545521` proposes it as the *free* hook. One dissent, and it is a sharp one: `14493510323` (us, 2★) — *"the tasks (even when tailored) are kind of vague/randomized for each day and seem more like XP grabs than anything else."*

## 8. The three decisions this corpus actually supports

1. **Ship a free tier, or stop paying for creator acquisition.** 39.62% of reviewers are blocked; 22.67% name a way in they would accept; 10.02% arrived from a creator and 7.16% arrived believing it was free. The current funnel converts paid marketing into 1★ reviews at scale. The narrowest version that the evidence supports is `14018545521`'s: keep tracking + XP/leaderboard free, gate AI analysis and focus mode.
2. **Make the paywall dismissible in a way a 13-year-old can find in two seconds, and disclose the price before the quiz.** 39 softlock reports (9.31%) and 23 quiz-then-wall reports (5.49%) are, unlike the pricing question, uncontroversial fixes.
3. **Make the AI consume its input, or stop selling it as the headline.** The empty-input finding (§0.6) is reproducible from four named reviews and is the thing paying customers ask for refunds over.

## 1.4 Deduplication rule

**No records were removed.** Exactly one exact title/body collision exists in the corpus: three reviews whose title and body are both `Good` — `13283129495` (gb, 4★, 18 Oct 2025), `13436840309` (tn, 5★, 24 Nov 2025) and `13634194777` (us, 4★, 14 Jan 2026). These are distinct `review_id`s from distinct authors in distinct storefronts on distinct dates — coincidence, not technical duplication — and all three are retained. All percentages therefore use **419** as the global denominator with no adjustment.

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Parse** all 419 records programmatically; verify schema, uniqueness, and reconciliation against `by_country/` and `manifest.json`.
2. **Read every review individually**, in chronological order, in its original language. No sampling, no keyword filtering, no truncation at a context boundary. Reviews were read in five sequential passes over the full corpus dump (`temp/64-all-reviews.txt`), covering records 1–419 with no gaps.
3. **Hand-assign themes** from a 47-code taxonomy (Part 9.1) built inductively from the reading, recorded per review in `temp/64-review-classification.py`. Assignment is non-exclusive.
4. **Validate programmatically** (`temp/64-validate.py`): zero unknown IDs, zero unassigned records, zero unknown codes, zero intra-theme duplicates, zero unused codes, `LOWINFO` exclusivity enforced.
5. **Aggregate** (`temp/64-aggregate.py`, `-2.py`, `-3.py`): global counts, theme×rating, per-country, per-period, segment unions, market-tier cuts, public-vs-written comparison, burst detection.
6. **Re-read borderline and high-impact clusters manually** — specifically every `PAID_EXPLICIT` review, every `CONTRA` review, every `AI_QUALITY` review, every `FOCUS_BROKEN` review, and the full `PAY_SOFTLOCK` set — before writing any finding about them.

**No automated classifier was used.** Every theme assignment in this report is a human reading of the full review text. The error risk is therefore judgement error on ambiguous short reviews, not model error. The two judgement calls most likely to move a number are disclosed in §9.3.

## 1.6 Limitations — read these before citing any figure

1. **No live store data.** `itunes.apple.com` is blocked by this environment's egress proxy (`connect_rejected`, organization policy). **Every price, SKU, trial length, and feature name in this report is reconstructed from review text** and carries the reviewers' own errors and currency confusions. Where reviewers contradict each other on price, the contradiction is reported, not resolved (§3.2).
2. **The one external source used** is this repository's own snapshot `habit-apps-ranked.json`, which is undated in-file. It supplies public rating counts, public averages, storefront ranks, and a **market-tier classification (`rich` / `volume` / `other`) that this report adopts as its high-spend group definition** (§7.3) rather than inventing one.
3. **Selection bias is extreme and one-directional.** 2.23% of raters wrote. The written mean is 2.09 stars below the public average. This corpus is a near-pure sample of *friction*, and §1.7 argues it is not even a representative sample of friction.
4. **Only one country clears 50 reviews.** All non-US country statements are `[limited evidence]`.
5. **Small denominators on every product finding.** Outside the monetization cluster, the largest theme is `FEAT_AI` at 18 reviews. Treat Part 4's feature findings as *hypotheses with named witnesses*, not rates.
6. **No version field.** The corpus cannot attribute anything to a specific build. One reviewer references "after the update" (`13874326932`, 22 Mar 2026) but no release timeline is available to anchor it.
7. **No developer responses** are present in the data, so support responsiveness is assessed only from the 3 reviews that mention contacting support.
8. **Currency and price figures are reviewer-reported.** The same year appears as $40, £40, £24, 40€, 44€, 45€, $39.99, $45, $50, $60, $100, $192, $200 and ₦69,900 across the corpus. Some of these are certainly reviewer error; some are storefront pricing; some are the discounted vs undiscounted ladder. The report treats the **modal** figure (~$40/yr, named in 29 reviews = 6.92%) as the reference point and flags the outliers.

## 1.7 The 2.09-star gap is structural, and one review explains it

This deserves its own subsection because it changes how every other number should be read.

| | Written corpus | Public ratings | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global | 419 @ **2.589** | 18,785 @ **4.68** | **−2.09** |
| US | 180 @ 2.66 | 9,557 @ 4.73 | −2.07 |
| GB | 40 @ 2.45 | 1,443 @ 4.61 | −2.16 |
| CA | 28 @ 2.21 | 1,421 @ 4.65 | −2.44 |
| AU | 26 @ 2.73 | 700 @ 4.57 | −1.84 |
| DE | 14 @ 2.14 | 550 @ 4.59 | −2.45 |
| SE | 6 @ 1.67 | 268 @ 4.59 | −2.92 |

The gap is stable across every storefront — it is not a US artefact. A 1–1.5 star written-review skew is normal. **2.09 is not**, and the corpus contains a direct mechanical explanation:

> `14032722731` (us, 1★, 6 May 2026): *"it got me to rate it before it charged me money so I gave it a 5 thinking that it was free because I am 13 and broke"*

If the in-app rating prompt fires during onboarding — before the paywall — then the 18,785-rating, 4.68-average public base is substantially composed of **pre-experience ratings**, and the written corpus is composed of the people who came back afterwards. Three independent lines of evidence in this corpus point the same way:

- **20 reviews (4.77%, very strong, mean 4.65) are explicitly pre-use** — "just downloaded", "excited to try", "hope it works" (`12508805566`, `12794468813`, `13292311335`, `13438164592`, `13506074887`, `13741399870`, `13959253116`, `14043988322`, `14083016258`, and 11 more). One is unmistakable: `13965811431` (us, **5★**) — *"I like it so far. If it has a subscription I'm uninstalling."*
- **26 reviews (6.21%, mean 4.42) are content-free** one-word or emoji ratings, 20 of them 5★.
- **11 reviews (2.63%) contradict their star rating**, 10 of them 5★ — including `13824995999` (us, **5★**): *"It doesn't let me use the app without paying"*, and `14467750032` (us, **5★**): *"i created a acct and it wants me to purchase a package what should i do."*

**Together, 57 reviews (13.60%, mean 4.54) are ratings given without a product experience behind them.** They inflate the written mean; the same mechanism at 45× the volume plausibly explains the public 4.68.

**Consequence for this report:** the public 4.68 should **not** be cited as user satisfaction, and the written 2.589 should **not** be cited as the product's quality. # PART 8 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 8.1 Method

The corpus spans **4 Apr 2025 → 4 Sep 2026 (519 days)**. With no version field available, periods are defined by **calendar quarters of roughly equal review volume**, disclosed here so the cuts can be reproduced:

| Period | Window | n | Mean ★ | 1★ share | 5★ share |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **P1 — launch** | 4 Apr 2025 → 31 Aug 2025 | 77 | **2.04** | 59.7% | 13.0% |
| **P2** | 1 Sep 2025 → 31 Jan 2026 | 114 | 2.78 | 43.0% | 31.6% |
| **P3** | 1 Feb 2026 → 31 May 2026 | 125 | **2.98** | 38.4% | 37.6% |
| **P4 — recent** | 1 Jun 2026 → 4 Sep 2026 | 103 | **2.32** | 50.5% | 19.4% |

Monthly volume ranges 3–44 (Sep 2026 is a partial month, 3 reviews). **No period is smaller than 77 reviews, so period-level percentages are usable; theme-level period figures below 5 reviews are not stated as rates.**

**No burst distorts any period.** Busiest day = 6 reviews (2025-11-24, 2026-01-05), across 275 distinct review days. The largest single day is 1.43% of the corpus. There is no defect wave and no solicitation spike visible in the timestamps.

## 8.2 The arc: recovery, then reversal

Mean rating runs **2.04 → 2.78 → 2.98 → 2.32**. The product improved through P2–P3 and then gave most of it back in P4.

**What improved (P1 → P3):**

| Theme | P1 | P2 | P3 | P4 | Reading |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| `PAY_HARDWALL` | **42.9%** | 26.3% | 23.2% | 26.2% | Sustained fall from launch, then flat |
| `PAY_SOFTLOCK` | **16.9%** | 8.8% | 7.2% | 6.8% | **Halved and stayed halved — the clearest improvement in the corpus** |
| `INSIDE_APP` | **5.2%** | 15.8% | 19.2% | **22.3%** | **Monotonic rise — more reviewers are getting into the product every period** |
| 1★ share | 59.7% | 43.0% | 38.4% | 50.5% | Improved, then reversed |

**`PAY_SOFTLOCK` falling from 16.9% to 6.8% and staying there across three consecutive periods is a real, well-supported trend** (13 → 10 → 9 → 7 reviews on denominators of 77/114/125/103). Something was improved in paywall dismissal after the launch quarter. It was not fixed — 7 reports in the most recent period, including `14317192032`, `14233984114`, `14274686879` and `14284939336` — but the rate is down 60%.

**`INSIDE_APP` rising monotonically from 5.2% to 22.3%** is the other genuine trend, and it means the reviewing population is shifting from "people who bounced off the wall" toward "people who use the product". That is the shape of a maturing user base.

**What got worse (P3 → P4):**

| Theme | P3 | P4 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `PAY_PRICE` | 19.2% | **34.0%** | **+14.8 points — the largest single-theme swing in the corpus** |
| `PAY_FREEREQ` | 13.6% | 20.4% | +6.8 |
| `PAY_QUIZWALL` | 3.2% | 8.7% | +5.5 |
| `PAY_DEFEND` | 0.8% | 8.7% | +7.9 |
| 5★ share | 37.6% | 19.4% | −18.2 |

**The P4 reversal is a price story, not a quality story.** `PAY_PRICE` jumps to 34.0% of all recent reviews — a third of everyone writing — while defect themes stay flat and `INSIDE_APP` keeps rising. Three readings are consistent with the data and cannot be separated without pricing history:

1. **A price rise or a discount withdrawal.** The evidence is suggestive but not conclusive: `13525806908` (Dec 2025) says *"at least bring back the free trial"*, implying a trial existed and was removed; P4 contains the highest-quoted figures in the corpus (`14286509974` $100/yr, `14451479544` $50, `14284939336` 50€/mo).
2. **Audience widening past willingness-to-pay.** P4 also has the highest `PAY_QUIZWALL` rate (8.7%), i.e. more people completing onboarding before hitting the wall.
3. **A visible public argument about price.** `PAY_DEFEND` goes from ~1% to 8.7% in P4 — reviewers start arguing *with each other* about whether $40 is fair (`14364538522` explicitly rebuts an earlier reviewer; `14391271895` addresses *"all the people getting mad about the paywall"*). Contested pricing became a topic on the listing itself.

**This report does not choose between the three.** Reading 3 is directly observable in the text; readings 1 and 2 are inferences that pricing history would settle immediately.

# PART 9.1 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## A. Counting rules

1. **Denominator.** Every global percentage divides by **419** — the complete corpus, with no exclusions and no deduplication (§1.4). Segment rates divide by the stated segment size and are always labelled as segment rates with the global count shown alongside.
2. **Non-exclusive themes.** A review may carry any number of themes; 931 assignments over 419 reviews (2.22 per review). Theme counts therefore do not sum to 419 and must never be added together — use the composite unions in §3.2, where each review is counted once.
3. **`LOWINFO` is exclusive** — enforced by validation. A review with no analysable content carries that code and nothing else.
4. **`PAID_EXPLICIT` requires a first-person statement** of payment, charge, subscription, refund, or restore. Inferred payment is never counted here.
5. **`INSIDE_APP` requires** a description of post-paywall product behaviour or a personal outcome. It does not assert payment.

## D. Judgement calls and known error risk

Theme assignment was done by hand, review by review. The following calls are the ones most likely to move a headline number, and each is disclosed so a reader can reverse it:

1. **`13874326932` (in, 3★) counted as `PAID_EXPLICIT`.** The reviewer never says "I paid"; they say *"I expect better from an app which costs like 30 dollars"* while describing the coach tab crashing — which requires access. Counting it makes the paid segment 13 rather than 12 and lowers the segment mean slightly. Reversing it does not change any conclusion in Part 6.
2. **`13570161518` (us, 3★) counted as `INSIDE_APP` but *not* `PAID_EXPLICIT`.** *"sure it gives you some simple tools"* and *"I use it"* imply access; no payment is stated. This is the most borderline access call in the corpus.
3. **`12750820602` (us, 1★) — *"When I tried to submit a picture or even take one it doesn't do anything"* — counted as `BUG_FEATURE` + `FEAT_AI` but **not** `INSIDE_APP`, because the physique photo appears to sit before the paywall (§2.3). If the photo is post-paywall, this review belongs in the access segment.
4. **`PAY_HARDWALL` vs `PAY_PRICE` on short reviews.** *"Koster penger"* (`12627079017`, no — "costs money") is coded `PAY_PRICE`; *"You have to pay"* (`13039790691`) is coded `PAY_HARDWALL`. Both readings are defensible for several one-line reviews. This boundary affects the relative size of the two largest themes by a handful of reviews each; it does not affect the blocked-at-the-paywall union (§3.2), which is the figure the report actually relies on.
5. **`CONTRA` requires an explicit contradiction in the body**, not merely a mismatch of tone. `13657837093` (5★, *"It won't let me in"*) qualifies; a terse 5★ with no content is coded `LOWINFO`, not `CONTRA`.
6. **`INFLUENCER` counts named creators, YouTube/TikTok, *and* friend recommendations.** Splitting friends out would reduce it by 3 (`13626787196`, `14248303022`, `12995844721`).
7. **`13262675194` (it, 1★) — *"uguale a Marco Febbrai"* — coded `LOWINFO`.** The reference is opaque without Italian creator context; it may be an `INFLUENCER` review.

**Known error risk overall:** the monetization cluster is unambiguous — 119 `PAY_HARDWALL` reviews say some version of "you cannot use this without paying" in plain words, and the classification risk there is near zero. The risk concentrates in (a) the access/paid boundary described above, affecting segment sizes in Part 6, and (b) one-line reviews where intent is genuinely underdetermined. **No finding in this report rests on a single ambiguous review**; the smallest theme used for a recommendation is `FOCUS_BROKEN` at 6 reviews, all of which quote the failure explicitly.
