## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Every claim traces to an exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`. **§9.7 is a complete 638-row index** — every review in the corpus with its ID, storefront, star rating, date, verbatim title and assigned themes. Nothing in this report is unreachable from that table.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several themes. All 638 records carry at least one theme (48 carry only `low_info`).
- Signal thresholds: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority. Denominator for every global percentage is **638**; for the one eligible country (United States) it is **409**.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.

### ⚠️ Eight warnings before any number here is used

1. **This corpus is positive and top-heavy, and that is the opposite of the usual shape.** 373 of 638 reviews are 5★ (58.46%) and 94 are 4★ (14.73%) — **73.2% of written reviews are 4★ or 5★**. Only 117 reviews (18.34%) are 1★ or 2★. Unlike most review corpora in this series, the negative pole is the minority here. Findings about what is *wrong* with this product therefore rest on smaller absolute counts than findings about what is *right*, and are flagged accordingly.
2. **The corpus mean (4.014) is below the store's displayed aggregate (4.7 from 8.1K ratings, accessed 11 September 2026), but only modestly.** Written reviews are a ~7.9% sliver of all ratings. The gap of 0.69 stars is the ordinary writing-bias gap, not evidence of a hidden problem. **Do not report 4.014 as this app's rating.**
3. **One solicited-looking review burst exists and is excluded from market conclusions.** Thirteen US reviews were posted within **24 minutes of each other on 10 October 2016 (18:15–18:39 UTC)** — `1464510394`, `1464510888`, `1464511771`, `1464512380`, `1464514000`, `1464514406`, `1464514777`, `1464515896`, `1464516366`, `1464516987`, `1464517402`, `1464517827`, `1464518182`. All are 5★, all carry short generic non-native-English praise ("Use!!", "Best!!", "Helful", "Thanks to"), and they appear three days after the app's first review. Removing them moves the global mean from 4.014 to **3.994 (n=625)** and 2016's mean from 4.58 to **4.45 (n=40)**. They are retained in every denominator and flagged as theme `us_2016_burst`. **Interpretation: the effect is small because the corpus is large — but 2016's apparent strength is partly manufactured.**
4. **Only one storefront clears the 50-review bar: the United States (n=409, 64.11%).** Every other storefront is ≤28 reviews. The United Kingdom (28), Canada (27), Australia (21) and India (19) are presented as **limited evidence** only, and no standalone conclusion is drawn about any storefront below 50.
5. **The corpus is overwhelmingly one market and one language.** 64.11% of records are US; 82.45% are from English-primary storefronts. Non-English reviews exist in German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, Polish, Russian, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Turkish, Czech, Croatian, Arabic and Danish, and all were read in their original language — but they total 133 records across 46 storefronts. **Any statement in this report about "global" behaviour is, in practice, mostly a statement about US iPhone users.**
6. **Classification was hand-curated, not blind-automated.** All 638 records were read individually, in full, in their original languages, in date order. Every theme is a hand-assigned ID list, not regex output. No sampling. 110 themes across 8 families. Boundary rules are in §9.1.
7. **Five records have a star rating that contradicts their own text (0.78%):** `1666138250` (NL, **5★**, *"Unusable since update, crashes immediately after pressing done"*), `5241266296` (PH, **1★**, body and title both *"OK"*), `6174597189` (US, **1★**, text opens *"This a great app… I'm very impressed"* then an appended update reports total failure), `6825601567` (DE, **5★**, *"Year change broke the app… My streaks got reset"*), `7755890511` (VN, **1★**, *"best app I can find"*). They are retained in every denominator; rating-only analysis of this corpus carries roughly 1% noise.
8. **No conversion rate can be inferred from review text and none is claimed.** 48 reviewers (7.52%) give first-person evidence of having paid. That is a self-selected sample of writers, not of buyers.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. This product's central monetization problem was solved, and the corpus records exactly when.**
The free-tier cap complaint — *"you only get 3 habits, then you pay"* — is the largest negative theme in the corpus at **66 reviews (10.34%, high-priority, mean 2.36)**. But it is **not a current problem**. Its yearly incidence runs 7.5% (2016) → 11.5% → 13.3% → 12.8% → 13.8% → **14.1% (2021)** → 6.7% → 3.6% → **0.0% (2024)** → 3.3% → **0.0% (2026)**. The entire union of monetization friction follows the same curve: **23.1% of 2020 reviews, 0.0% of 2024 and 2026 reviews**. From 2022 onward reviewers describe the opposite situation — *"It has none of that premium membership biz while also including records over time… can have everything the app has to offer without purchasing anything"* (`8554147668`, US, 5★, Apr 2022); *"it's so hard to find an app that'll let you track unlimited amounts of habits for free but this one does it 🤩"* (`10869613654`, DK, 5★, Jan 2024); *"No need to pay to repeat everyday routine"* (`11799865019`, MY, 5★, Oct 2024); *"this is completely free!"* (`12410220634`, IN, 5★, Mar 2025). **Interpretation: the free tier was materially loosened somewhere around 2021–2022. This is the single clearest before/after in the corpus, and it is the reference case for what raising a free cap does to review sentiment.**

**2. Reliability replaced monetization as the thing that produces bad reviews, and the crossover is unambiguous.**
Reliability complaints run in the exact opposite direction: **0.0% of 2016 reviews, 10.0% of 2020, 23.9% of 2021, 21.4% of 2023, 26.1% of 2026.** Across the three eras the two lines cross cleanly:

| Era | n | mean ★ | Monetization friction | Reliability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2016–2018 | 176 | 4.34 | 23 (**13.07%**) | 6 (**3.41%**) |
| 2019–2021 | 308 | 3.90 | 58 (**18.83%**) | 41 (**13.31%**) |
| 2022–2026 | 154 | 3.88 | 6 (**3.90%**) | 27 (**17.53%**) |

The reliability union is **74 reviews (11.60%, high-priority, mean 2.35)** and it now accounts for **41 of the 117 one- and two-star reviews (35.0%)**, against 51 of 117 (43.6%) for all monetization friction combined — and almost all of that monetization share is historical. **Interpretation: in 2026 terms, fixing bugs is the whole job. There is essentially no live pricing objection left to fix.**

**3. Paying customers rate this app worse than non-payers, and the gap is caused by delivery failures, not by price.**
**48 reviewers (7.52%, high-priority) give direct first-person evidence of having paid.** Their mean rating is **3.58**, against **4.05** for the 590 non-payers. The damage is concentrated: **11 of 48 payers (22.9% segment rate; 1.72% of the global corpus) were hit by a reliability defect after paying** — `2945436722`, `5259644670`, `6302864374`, `6371120408`, `7226835619`, `7652035829`, `7984884909`, `9023682666`, `10019289476`, `10145156296`, `13278821225`. And **4 of 48 (8.3% segment rate) say the purchase simply did not deliver the feature they bought** — `1544580960` (US, 4★, *"I paid to upgrade to get the edit function, but no changes happened… Something broken in the backend??"*), `3395577909` (CA, 4★, *"I bought the premium but I haven't gotten any extra features"*), `5849652810` (US, 2★, *"Purchase premium but still getting free trial capabilities"*), `6371120408` (PL, 1★, *"I upgraded app and nothing happens, just took my money"*). Only **2 reviewers in 638 request a refund** (`6302864374`, `6537988680`) — a strikingly low rate for this series, and a direct consequence of the low one-time price. **Interpretation: the purchase is cheap enough that people do not demand money back — they just leave a bad review and move on. That makes review damage, not refund volume, the real cost of a broken unlock.**

**4. The one-time price is a first-class differentiator and reviewers say so in competitive terms, unprompted.**
**40 reviews (6.27%, high-priority, mean 4.60) praise the one-time purchase specifically as against subscriptions**, and **57 (8.93%, high-priority, mean 4.89) praise the price/value**. Together with `praise_free_tier` and `praise_no_ads`, the monetization-praise union is **89 reviews (13.95%, mean 4.70)** — **larger than the entire monetization-friction union (87)** and rated two full stars higher. Representative: *"I've tried 3 other apps that ranged from $25 to $60 per year. This one is simple, inexpensive and does everything you need"* (`7618584736`, CA, 5★); *"Others have less features yet will charge double the one time payment for this as a monthly subscription!"* (`5751872904`, GB, 5★); *"E perefetta e non si paga in modo mensile… Ti fanno pagare 2,99 pee solo una volta"* / "It's perfect and you don't pay monthly… they charge you 2.99 just once" (`7938484525`, IT, 5★); *"Thank you for making it accessible to super low income people like myself, Without having to have a subscription"* (`8208737898`, US, 5★); *"No subscription 🥳🎉 · Very reasonable one-time purchase price · Great data privacy (no data collected) · No ads"* (`7316654754`, DE, 5★). **Interpretation: this is a defensible market position, not an accident. Any move to subscription would attack the most-praised attribute of the product.**

**5. The signature feature is the "nag until done" repeating reminder, and nothing else in the corpus is described as unavailable elsewhere.**
**22 reviews (3.45%, very strong, mean 4.86) praise the nag feature by name**, and a further **54 (8.46%, high-priority, mean 4.76) praise reminders generally**. Reviewers repeatedly frame nagging as unique: *"Best feature by far is the 'nag me until Im done' option, which is something no other app has offered thus far"* (`6222322942`, US, 5★); *"HabitHub relies on good old-fashioned nagging. It's simple and effective"* (`2510076061`, US, 5★); *"The first time in my life I gave money to something to NAG me 😐"* (`5232097891`, US, 5★); *"I love the way you can set it to nag you as often as you want and don't have to stop what you are doing to turn off an alarm"* (`11268226195`, US, 4★). **Interpretation: the nag loop is the product's moat. It is also the mechanism most exposed to the notification bugs in finding 7 — when notifications break, the differentiator breaks.**

**6. Data loss is the most severe defect in the corpus, it is accelerating, and the most recent instances are users losing four years of history.**
**12 reviews (1.88%, meaningful, mean 2.08) report habits, streaks or history disappearing.** The pattern is 0 reviews in 2016–2018, 5 in 2019–2021, **7 in 2022–2026** — 4.55% of the recent era. The 2026 cluster is the worst: `13950149136` (US, 1★, Apr 2026, *"I have done 3 updates for this app (2 yesterday and one this morning) and now I can no longer access the app. It no longer opens. I have lost all of my reminders… All the I spent created things are lost😡"*), `14273363206` (DE, 4★, 7 Jul 2026, *"I used HabitHub for four years and after an update my locally stored data was gone"*), `14291899176` (DE, 4★, 11 Jul 2026, *"This morning I opened the app, the UI has changed and all my data of four years is gone"*). Both German reviewers say support replied immediately and both had already migrated to a competitor. One names the root cause directly: *"If I could change anything, I would sync my data to the iCloud to be sure"* (`14273363206`). **Interpretation: local-only storage with no enforced cloud backup is losing the app its longest-tenured users at the exact moment it ships its biggest redesign. This is the highest-severity finding in the report.**

**7. Notification failures are a recurring, version-linked defect that attacks the product's core differentiator.**
**15 reviews (2.35%, meaningful, mean 2.60) report notifications firing twice, firing after completion, or not firing at all.** There is a clear iOS 15 cluster in Oct–Nov 2021 — `7910377516` (13 Oct, 1★, *"Notifications continue even after marking task as complete or muting the task since iOS update"*), `7942724644` (22 Oct, 1★, *"App is now giving double notifications"*), `7954475421` (26 Oct, 3★, *"Others have also reported this, and I don't think the solution is to have us individually email tech support. It's clearly a bug"*), `7984884909` (3 Nov, 3★, payer, *"It's been over a month and a half since the new update came out and there hasn't been a bug fix"*), `8009348865` (10 Nov, 5★, support told them to reinstall and that worked). Two later reviews report **ghost habits** — a deleted habit that still fires: `11762923278` (US, 1★, *"It doesn't show up in the app, but keep firing at midnight… Aside from nuking this app, I don't see any way to interact with that habit"*), `13362043458` (US, 3★, *"old reminders keep popping up… I deleted and readded the app, I've changed phones and nothing seems to allow me to cancel"*). **Interpretation: a five-week public regression on the app's signature feature, with the published remedy being "email support", is the single most avoidable reputational event in this corpus.**

**8. The October 2025 redesign is the first change in the corpus that split reviewers, and the split is about modal popups.**
Two reviews one day apart attack the same new behaviour: `13278821225` (US, 1★, 17 Oct 2025, long-time payer, *"The latest update really is just bloatware. Long time paid user and pop up journal feature should have option to TURN OFF. Bad design to have to do 'skip' every time. Furthermore the pop up is obscured by my keyboard so it has destroyed the functionality"*) and `13280430575` (US, 2★, 18 Oct 2025, *"Latest update added a bunch of distracting popups (prompts to write a note when skipping a habit, a weird calendar header that takes up too much space)… Really ruined the UI. Wish we could get the older version"*). Against them, three reviews praise the same redesign: `13284708645` (US, 5★, 19 Oct 2025, *"The new design is amazing"*), `13415356335` (IN, 5★, Nov 2025, *"The ability to program your day windows and reorder items in time windows is fantastic"*), `14436400155` (HU, 5★, Aug 2026, *"After these changes this app is great again"*). **Interpretation: the redesign shipped a blocking journal modal with no opt-out. A settings toggle would neutralise both negative reviews — the cheapest fix in this report — and note that `13415356335` confirms the redesign also shipped the drag-to-reorder capability that seven earlier reviews had asked for.**

**9. Reviewers are asking for scheduling shapes the product does not support — and, in several cases, for shapes it already supports but they could not find.**
**29 reviews (4.55%, very strong, mean 3.38) name a missing scheduling capability.** Genuinely absent: intervals longer than one month (*"There are no options for tasks that occur beyond every 1 month… Wish I'd known that before I paid for it"* — `7289065763`, US, 2★, payer), Nth-weekday-of-month (*"you can't schedule a task for a 1st or 2nd weekday, such as '1st Tuesday of each month'"* — `6569265970`, US, 5★), "X times per week" without fixing the days (`2431436613`, `2885013412`, `7539356424`), every-other-week (`1522226211`, `5812621934`), and a due date that rolls from last completion (`10280711337`). But at least three reviews ask for something the app demonstrably has: *"You should be able to customize the days you want… Monday and Wednesday's"* (`5432803445`, US, 2★), *"you can't set a set amount of time for a task"* (`6246525358`, GB, 1★, the app has multi-step timers), *"I wonder if I can create a task without a time restriction?"* (`9662306542`, TR, 3★). **Interpretation: roughly a tenth of this theme is a discoverability problem, not a feature gap — and it lands as 1★ and 2★ reviews. See finding 10.**

**10. Onboarding is the cheapest unaddressed problem, and it has been named for nine straight years.**
**9 reviews (1.41%, meaningful, mean 2.78) say there is no guide, tutorial, FAQ or help screen**, and **14 (2.19%, meaningful, mean 3.36) describe a learning curve**. The union is **20 reviews (3.13%, very strong)**. It spans the entire corpus: `1553177362` (PK, 2★, Feb 2017, *"There must be some tips or suggestions in start"*) through `14407854273` (US, 1★, Aug 2026, *"I don't understand nothing"*). The most specific: *"how-to-use instructions are missing… There is no help option in settings which makes at least one action I want to take impossible: I want to create a habit that includes a timer. I can't find instructions for this in the app, on YouTube, or through a Google search"* (`6810959323`, US, 3★); *"I find the lack of set up instructions and lack of FAQ maddening. I have spent lots of time on trial and error… I have had to contact support twice on things that could have explained up front"* (`8251773389`, US, 3★); *"it took me an hour or so to figure out how to make it work and I'm still not sure about some of the logic"* (`13416844344`, US, 4★, Nov 2025, who adds *"I'd pay for this if it had some adjustments in usability"*). **Interpretation: this product is repeatedly described as both powerful and opaque. An in-app help screen is cheaper than any feature in this report and would recover part of finding 9 as well.**

**11. Support is a genuine asset and is named as a reason people stay — but it fails exactly where it is most needed.**
**24 reviews (3.76%, very strong, mean 4.67) praise support by name**, often describing a fix within hours: *"the support team contacted me and helped me fix the minor issue"* (`1626042608`); *"I recently requested a small new feature, got a response, and it was added a few days later"* (`13581758283`, GB, 5★, Jan 2026); *"I had problems with this app, but the developer quickly came to the rescue"* (`1958553531`); `3705163026` (US) is a **2★ review upgraded to 5★** after the team shipped the requested fix. Against that, **7 reviews (1.10%, meaningful, mean 1.29 — the lowest mean of any theme in this report) say support did not respond**: `3226700626`, `6537988680`, `7984884909`, `10019289476`, `11111402633`, `11605150337`, `11762923278`. Four more (`2331157968`, `4910574623`, `6294511871`, `7954475421`) report that the **in-app feedback channel itself is broken** — *"Feedback email can not send"*, *"They have a 'report bug' feature, but that also doesn't work"*, *"I wasn't able to email due to the configuration"*. **Interpretation: support quality is bimodal because the intake is unreliable. Fixing the in-app feedback path is a support-capacity improvement disguised as a bug fix.**

**12. ADHD and executive-function use is a growing, almost exclusively US segment, and it is the app's warmest constituency.**
**18 reviews (2.82%, meaningful, mean 4.61) name ADHD/ADD explicitly** and **12 more (1.88%, meaningful, mean 4.75) describe another accessibility use case** — autism, depression, chronic illness, head injury, ASD, disability, anxiety. **17 of the 18 ADHD reviews and 12 of 12 accessibility reviews are US.** The theme grows from **0.0% of 2016–2018 reviews to 5.84% of 2022–2026 reviews**. Representative: *"I was not diagnosed with ADHD until last year at age 25… Habit Hub is a surprisingly simple but infinitely helpful app. It has significantly improved my relationship with my fiancée"* (`5460506563`, US, 5★); *"I have ASD and some executive functioning issues… I love that it's a widget! I can see what I need to do right from the home screen… Thank you for %'s and grades"* (`7010047564`, US, 5★, 16 helpful votes); *"I have severe ADHD and I use this app to complete tasks that would otherwise take hours on end — cooking, cleaning, and even applying makeup"* (`9164282595`, US, 5★); *"I have severe depression paired with adhd, and this has so far motivated me to shower for the first time in a week"* (`8151253624`, US, 5★); *"I'm autistic and have ADHD and this app has helped me get my life under control"* (`11268226195`, US, 4★). **Interpretation: the store listing already says "ADHD-friendly interface". The corpus supports that claim with 25 first-person accounts (the union of the two themes; 5 reviews carry both) — and shows the segment is growing without the product having targeted it.**

**13. Localization is the only clearly non-US product gap, it is growing, and the store listing supports five languages.**
**10 reviews (1.57%, meaningful, mean 3.20) ask for a missing language; all 10 are non-US (4.37% of the 229 non-US records).** Arabic is requested three times in fourteen months — `12565071834` (SA, 3★, Apr 2025, *"هذي التطبيق يدعم العربيه ولا لا .؟"* / "Does this app support Arabic or not?"), `12752447582` (OM, 3★, Jun 2025), `12767914274` (SA, 3★, Jun 2025, *"جميل البرنامج لكن ينقصه دعم اللغة العربية لذلك يستحق ⭐️⭐️⭐️"* / "Nice app but it lacks Arabic support, so it deserves 3 stars"). Spanish is requested three times (`8182517804` ES 2★ *"La app solo esta en ingles"*, `11496905396` CL 3★, `11605150337` ID 1★), Portuguese twice (`5849454883`, `6230329628`, both BR 5★, both pleading), Polish once (`10158814839`), plus a general request (`4318358329`). The store listing (accessed 11 September 2026) lists **English, Japanese, Korean, Portuguese, Spanish** — so the Portuguese and Spanish requests appear to have since been answered, and the Arabic requests have not. One review also reports localization *quality* failure: *"The app is a mess, full of spelling mistakes"* (`10905816260`, GB, 1★). **Interpretation: Arabic is the only unanswered language cluster in the corpus, and it is producing exactly 3★ ratings — capped satisfaction from users who otherwise like the product.**

**14. Twelve reviewers asked for Apple Watch over five years; the store listing now says it ships. That request queue is closed and the next one is Mac.**
**12 reviews (1.88%, meaningful, mean 4.00) ask for Apple Watch support**, running May 2019 (`4161687730`, US, 5★, *"Not using your app due to lack of iwatch… Let us know when iwatch comes out!"*) to January 2024 (`10783651409`). Two reviewers report it already working via notifications (`1998324046` GB 2017, `6885418251` EE 2021). The listing (accessed 11 September 2026) lists Apple Watch, Mac (M1+), Vision and TV as supported platforms, and **no Apple Watch request appears after January 2024** — consistent with the gap having been closed. The remaining platform ask is a Mac/desktop app: `3869364986` (FR, 5★, *"il ne manque que l'application macOS"*), `6506840284` (FR, 4★, *"manque export des données ou application mac"*), `10116531918` (UA, 5★, *"MacOS desktop app"*). **Interpretation: this developer does ship requested features. The corpus contains at least four verified cases — Apple Watch, drag-to-reorder, the timer music fix in `3705163026`, and the feature in `13581758283` added "a few days later".**

**15. The cheapest high-value moves, in evidence order:** enforce iCloud backup and add a visible restore path (12 data-loss reviews; the 2026 German pair lost four years each and both churned) → make the new journal/skip popup optional in settings (2 reviews, 1★ and 2★, both Oct 2025, cheapest fix here) → add an in-app help/FAQ screen and first-run tour (20 reviews across nine years; also recovers part of the scheduling-gap theme) → fix the in-app feedback/bug-report channel (4 reviews report it broken; it is the intake for the 7 unresponsive-support reviews) → regression-test notification scheduling on every iOS major (15 notification-bug reviews clustered on OS updates; this is the product's differentiator) → add scheduling shapes beyond one month, Nth-weekday, and "X times per week" (29 reviews) → ship Arabic (3 reviews in 14 months, all 3★, all capped by exactly this) → **do not introduce a subscription** (89 reviews praise the one-time model, mean 4.70).

## 1.2 Schema

Fields present on every one of the 638 records: `review_id`, `app_id`, `app_name`, `country`, `country_name`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `author`, `date`, `vote_count`, `vote_sum`, `is_edited`.

**Fields used in this analysis:** `review_id`, `country`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `date`.
**Fields inspected and set aside:** `author` (not analysed; no reviewer is named in this report), `vote_sum` (net helpful score; it equals `vote_count` on 611 of 638 records and differs on 27 (4.23%), always by a small number of down-votes — no finding in this report uses it), `app_id` / `app_name` / `country_name` (constant or derivable).
**Fields inspected and used only as an integrity check:**
- `vote_count` — 572 of 638 records (89.66%) have zero votes; the non-zero median is 1 and the maximum is 17. The two outliers are both long, specific, 5★ US reviews: `5307496014` (17 votes, Dec 2019, *"Complete life changer"*) and `7010047564` (16 votes, Feb 2021, ASD/executive-function account). Unlike report 42's corpus, **votes did not surface the review burst here** — all 13 burst reviews have zero votes. The burst was detected by timestamp clustering instead (Warning 3).
- `is_edited` — true on 9 records (1.41%): `3705163026`, `4268975961`, `6506840284`, `6736154930`, `7401092364`, `7910377516`, `14258304700`, `14291899176`, `14436400155`. Four of these are materially important because the edit *changed the verdict*: `3705163026` (2★→5★ after a fix shipped), `6736154930` ("EDIT- Upgraded review"), `14291899176` (data loss, then support contact), `14436400155` ("After these changes this app is great again"). All are noted in-line where they matter.

**Text handling:** HTML entities (`&#39;`, `&#34;`, `&amp;`) are present in the raw `title` and `body` fields and were unescaped before reading and classification. This affects nothing but string rendering. Body length: median **139 characters**, mean 213, min 1 (`1720363940`, body `"."`), max 2235 (`6276587317`, US, a 5★ record of pure keyboard-mash which is classified `low_info`).

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Parse.** All 638 JSON lines parsed; schema verified field-by-field; HTML entities unescaped; records sorted by `date` ascending into a single flat working file (`temp/43-all-reviews.txt`).
2. **Reconcile.** Main file cross-checked against all 55 `by_country/*.jsonl` files and against `manifest.json` on totals, per-country counts and rating distribution. All checks exact (§1.3).
3. **Read.** Every record read individually and in full, in date order, in its original language — English, German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, Polish, Russian, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Turkish, Czech, Croatian, Arabic, Danish. No sampling, no keyword pre-filtering, no truncation, no stopping at a context limit.
4. **Classify.** Themes hand-assigned per record while reading, recorded as an explicit `review_id → [themes]` map in `temp/43-review-classification.py`. Non-exclusive: a review carries every theme it supports.
5. **Validate.** Programmatic checks over the map: zero unknown review IDs, zero unassigned records, zero records with no theme, zero duplicated themes within a record, zero themes outside the family taxonomy, zero themes in more than one family. All passed (§1.3).
6. **Aggregate.** Counts, percentages, means, rating cross-tabs, family unions, per-country cuts, per-era cuts and per-year cuts computed from the validated map. Every number in this report is derived from that one artifact.
7. **Verify.** Every review ID cited anywhere in this report was checked to exist in `reviews.jsonl` (§9.4).

## 1.6 Limitations and known biases

- **Single-market dominance.** 64.11% of the corpus is US. Global figures are, in practice, weighted US figures. Stated wherever it matters.
- **One eligible country.** Only the US clears the 50-review threshold. The UK (28), Canada (27), Australia (21) and India (19) get limited-evidence treatment only; 50 storefronts have ≤11 reviews each.
- **Selection bias toward writers.** Written reviews are ~7.9% of the store's 8.1K ratings. The corpus mean (4.014) is 0.69 below the displayed aggregate (4.7).
- **Solicited-burst contamination in 2016.** Thirteen records (Warning 3). Retained in denominators, flagged, and excluded from market conclusions.
- **The free-tier boundary is not directly observable.** Reviewers describe a 3-item cap from 2016 to early 2023 and describe *no cap* from 2022 onward. The corpus cannot say exactly what changed, only that the complaint stopped and the opposite claim started. The store listing today shows one $4.99 unlock. Treated as an inference throughout and labelled as such.
- **Feature-presence claims are review-derived unless labelled.** Where a reviewer asks for a feature the app appears to have, this report says so and treats it as a discoverability signal rather than silently discarding the review.
- **Version data is absent from the schema.** No `version` field exists on any record, so defect clusters are dated, not versioned. "Post-update" attributions come from reviewer wording only.
- **Recent-era sample sizes are small.** 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 hold 28, 28, 30 and 23 records. Era-level trends (Part 7) use three multi-year buckets to avoid over-reading single years; single-year rates in that range are directional only.
- **Sub-1% themes.** 42 of the 110 themes have 1–4 supporting reviews. They are reported at their honest signal label (weak or emerging) and never promoted.

3. **A missing capability, named.** `5606572349` and `13416844344` both state a specific condition for purchase.

## 5.7 Competitive position as reviewers describe it

50 reviews describe choosing this app over alternatives (§3.3.4); 22 name an alternative directly. The stated basis for choosing it is consistent: **simplicity + one-time price + the nag feature.** The stated basis for leaving is: **built-in free alternatives** (Apple Reminders, the phone calendar, paper) when the paywall blocked them, and **reliability** when it broke. No reviewer in 638 leaves for a named paid competitor.

# PART 7 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 7.1 Method

Three documented cuts, all over the same 638 records:

1. **By calendar year** (§1.4) — highest resolution, but 2023–2026 hold only 23–30 records each, so single-year rates in that range are directional only.
2. **By three multi-year eras** — 2016–2018 (n=176), 2019–2021 (n=308), 2022–2026 (n=154). Chosen so each bucket holds ≥150 records. This is the primary trend instrument.
3. **By halves of the corpus, split at the median record** — H1 = 7 Oct 2016 → 5 Jun 2020 (n=319), H2 = 5 Jun 2020 → 16 Aug 2026 (n=319). Equal-n control on the era cut.

A trend is only labelled if it holds in at least two of the three cuts. Percentages are always within-period rates, never shares of the theme.

## 7.2 Trend 1 — Monetization friction collapsed · **improving, high confidence**

| Cut | Early | Middle | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eras | 13.07% (23/176) | 18.83% (58/308) | **3.90% (6/154)** |
| Halves | 15.99% (51/319) | — | 11.29% (36/319) |
| Years | peak **14.1%** free-cap in 2021 | — | **0.0%** in 2024 and 2026 |

The halves cut understates the change because the split point (Jun 2020) sits in the middle of the paywall era. The era cut and the year cut agree: the theme peaked in 2020–2021 and is gone by 2024. `deceptive_free` has **zero instances after 21 October 2021**. **Supporting evidence in the opposite direction:** `praise_free_tier` appears in 2024–2025 with language that is incompatible with a 3-item cap (`10869613654`, `11799865019`, `12410220634`).

## 7.3 Trend 2 — Reliability complaints rose steadily and are now the dominant negative · **worsening, high confidence**

| Cut | Early | Middle | Late |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eras | 3.41% (6/176) | 13.31% (41/308) | **17.53% (27/154)** |
| Halves | 5.02% (16/319) | — | **18.18% (58/319)** |
| Years | 0.0% (2016) | 23.9% (2021) | **26.1% (2026)** |

Both cuts agree and the magnitude is large — a **3.6× increase** across halves. Within the family, the sub-themes that grew are `data_loss` (0.31% → 3.45% across halves), `regression_after_update` (1.25% → 3.76%), `crash_wont_open` (1.88% → 4.39%) and `notification_bug` (1.25% → 3.45%). `sync_icloud_error` did not grow and has no instance after Aug 2022.

`monetization_praise` union: **7.39% (2016–2018) → 16.88% (2019–2021) → 15.58% (2022–2026)**; halves 11.60% → 16.30%. `praise_value_cheap` specifically: 3.98% → 11.04% → 10.39%. **Interpretation: as competitors moved to subscription over the late 2010s, this app's unchanged one-time model became a stated reason to choose it. The praise is not about the app getting cheaper — the price went up from $2.99 to $4.99. It is about the alternatives getting more expensive.**

## 7.6 Trend 5 — ADHD and accessibility use grew from nothing · **emerging, high confidence in direction**

`praise_adhd`: 0.00% (2016–2018) → 2.92% → **5.84% (2022–2026)**. `accessibility_use_case`: 0.00% → 2.27% → 3.25%. Halves: 1.88% → 3.76% and 1.25% → 2.51%. Every cut agrees. The first ADHD mention in the corpus is April 2019 (`4047899872`, AU). **Interpretation: this tracks the broader rise in adult ADHD diagnosis and in ADHD-app marketing over the same period. The product did not change to attract this segment; the segment arrived.**

## 7.7 Trend 6 — Localization requests grew and moved from "any language" to named languages · **worsening, medium confidence**

0.00% (2016–2018) → 1.30% (2019–2021) → **3.90% (2022–2026)**. The character of the request also changed: the earliest is generic (*"Need more language"* — `4318358329`, 2019), the 2020 ones name Portuguese, and everything from 2024 onward names Spanish, Polish or Arabic. **Medium confidence: n=10 total.** The store listing's current five languages are consistent with the Portuguese and Spanish requests having been answered.

## 7.8 Trend 7 — Support quality became bimodal · **worsening, medium confidence**

`praise_support` is flat (4.55% → 3.25% → 3.90%; halves 3.45% → 4.08%). `support_unresponsive` is not: **0.57% → 0.65% → 2.60%**; halves 0.31% → 1.88%. Six of the seven unresponsive reports are 2020 or later; four are 2023 or later. **Medium confidence: n=7.** **Interpretation: the praise did not fall, but the tail got worse — consistent with a single-developer support operation that scales well until volume or a broken intake channel (§3.3.5, four reports of the in-app feedback form failing) causes messages to be missed entirely.**

## 7.9 Trend 8 — The 2025–2026 redesign is the most concentrated event in the corpus · **active, medium confidence**

Of the 23 reviews dated 2026 and the 30 dated 2025, **nine reference the redesign or a regression from it**: `13278821225` (1★, journal popup), `13280430575` (2★, popups + calendar header), `13284708645` (5★, *"The new design is amazing"*), `13415356335` (5★, reordering shipped), `13418583733` (4★, *"since the new redesign all habits scheduled for 'every 2/3 weeks' now show up every week"*), `13539509871` (1★, freezes + black screen), `13742967030` (2★, pause button removed), `13950149136` (1★, three updates then total data loss), `14436400155` (5★, *"After these changes this app is great again"*). Plus the two German data-loss reviews (`14273363206`, `14291899176`, both July 2026) which describe the UI having changed at the same moment the data vanished.

**Split: 4 positive, 7 negative.** **Medium confidence — n=11 over ten months, and 2025–2026 together hold only 53 records.** **Interpretation: the redesign delivered at least two long-requested capabilities (reordering, and the restructured routine screen) and simultaneously introduced a blocking modal, a scheduling regression, a removed feature, and at least three data-loss events. It is not yet settled which way it nets out; the final review in the corpus is positive.**

## 7.10 What did not change

- **Simplicity praise is permanent.** 26.70% → 15.58% → 18.18% by era; it is the top theme in every single year of the corpus. The dip in the middle era coincides with the paywall era crowding the corpus with complaints, not with simplicity praise disappearing.
- **The nag feature is praised in every era** (`1486308038` 2016 → `13284708645` 2025) and never criticised except once, indirectly (`4277971819`, *"the endless notifications were frustrating for me"*).
- **Scheduling flexibility has been requested continuously for nine years** — 2.84% → 4.87% → 5.84%, first instance Jan 2017 (`1522226211`), last Jan 2025 (`12187998188`). It is the longest-running unaddressed request in the corpus.
- **Onboarding has been requested continuously for nine years** — first instance Feb 2017 (`1553177362`), last Aug 2026 (`14407854273`). Nothing in the corpus suggests it was ever addressed.
- **No subscription ever appeared**, across 638 reviews and ten years.

## 8.5 Research questions this corpus raises but cannot answer

1. **When exactly did the free cap change, and to what?** The corpus dates the complaint's disappearance to 2022–2023 and shows contradictory post-2022 claims ("completely free" vs a $4.99 listing IAP), but no reviewer describes the current boundary. **Resolvable from release notes and IAP history, not from reviews.**
2. **Is the US crash concentration real?** 18 of 20 crash reports are US (4.40% vs 0.87% non-US). This could be device mix, iOS-version mix, or simply that US reviewers in this corpus write more diagnostic reviews. **Resolvable only from crash telemetry.**
3. **What is the actual conversion rate, and did it move when the cap changed?** 48 reviewers say they paid. That is a sample of writers, not of buyers. **No conversion claim is made anywhere in this report.**
4. **Did the July 2026 German data-loss pair share one root cause?** Two reviews, four days apart, same storefront, same reported symptom (four years of local data gone after an update), same reported support response. **Resolvable from crash/migration logs.**
5. **Does the ADHD segment convert differently?** 25 reviews identify as ADHD or accessibility users at a 4.64 mean; only 2 of the 25 are confirmed payers (`4047899872`, `4384478235`). **The corpus cannot distinguish "this segment does not pay" from "this segment does not mention paying."**
6. **Why is the payer rate higher outside the US** (10.04% vs 6.11%)? The difference is 9 records and may be noise. **Resolvable from per-storefront IAP data.**

`praise_one_time_price` (praises the one-time model, usually explicitly against subscriptions) · `praise_value_cheap` (praises the price or value for money) · `praise_free_tier` (praises what is available without paying) · `praise_no_ads` (praises absence of advertising) · `praise_privacy` (praises no-data-collection / no-account).

### Reliability (12 themes)

`crash_wont_open` (crashes, freezes, or will not launch) · `data_loss` (habits, streaks, history or notes disappear) · `notification_bug` (reminders duplicate, fire after completion, or do not fire) · `sync_icloud_error` (iCloud sign-in or sync errors) · `save_edit_bug` (settings or new items do not save) · `stats_bug` (statistics compute wrongly) · `timer_bug` (timer pauses/deletes incorrectly) · `ghost_habit` (a deleted habit still fires) · `widget_issue` (widget absent or not updating) · `scheduling_bug` (a schedule fires at the wrong interval) · `regression_after_update` (the review attributes the fault to a specific update) · `generic_buggy` (unspecified bugginess with no nameable mechanism).

**Boundary rule:** `regression_after_update` is additive — a review reporting a crash after an update carries both `crash_wont_open` and `regression_after_update`. `generic_buggy` is assigned only when no specific mechanism is nameable.

### Feature gaps and requests (29 themes)

`scheduling_flexibility_missing` · `apple_watch_missing` · `localization_missing` · `notification_improvement_request` · `reorder_missing` · `backfill_missing` · `grouping_missing` · `calendar_view_missing` · `feature_request_colors` · `bad_habit_missing` · `mac_desktop_missing` · `export_missing` · `shared_household_missing` · `health_integration_missing` · `pause_missing` · `lock_missing` · `location_reminder_missing` · `shortcuts_integration_missing` · `import_missing` · `undo_missing` · `bulk_edit_missing` · `progress_granularity_missing` · `social_sharing_missing` · `stats_insufficient` · `dark_mode_request` · `feature_request_themes` · `feature_request_other` · `too_limited` · `local_only_storage`.

**Boundary rule:** a *missing* capability is a feature gap; a capability that exists and fails is `reliability`. Where a reviewer asks for something the app demonstrably has, the theme is still assigned (the review is real feedback) and the discoverability implication is stated in §3.5 and Part 8 rather than the record being discarded. `too_limited` is the catch-all for "not enough functionality" with no specific ask.

### UX friction (13 themes)

`ui_dated_ugly` (visual design criticised as dated, bland, ugly, low-resolution) · `learning_curve` (the reviewer found it hard to learn) · `onboarding_missing` (explicitly asks for instructions, FAQ, tutorial or help) · `ux_confusion` (a specific interaction was not understood) · `ui_cluttered` (too much information or chrome on screen) · `swipe_done_ux` (the Done/Info/swipe interaction specifically) · `review_prompt_nag` · `review_prompt_bug` · `popup_journal_2025` (the Oct-2025 skip-journal modal and calendar header) · `text_size_accessibility` · `notification_annoyance` (reminders felt excessive — distinct from `notification_bug`) · `storage_complaint` · `localization_quality` (existing text is wrong, not missing).

### Support (4 themes)

`praise_support` · `support_unresponsive` (wrote and got no reply) · `support_channel_broken` (the in-app feedback/bug path itself fails) · `support_canned_response`.

### Core praise (28 themes)

`praise_simplicity` · `praise_behaviour_change` (describes an actual outcome or changed behaviour) · `praise_generic` (positive with substance but no named driver) · `praise_stats` · `praise_reminders` · `praise_best_of_many` (states they compared alternatives and chose this) · `praise_customization` · `praise_design` · `praise_nag` (the repeat-until-done feature by name) · `praise_timer_routines` · `praise_scheduling_flexibility` · `praise_multi_count` · `praise_adhd` (names ADHD/ADD/executive function) · `accessibility_use_case` (names another disability or health condition) · `praise_journal` · `praise_widget` · `praise_categories` · `praise_siri` · `praise_sync` · `praise_reliability` · `praise_updates` · `praise_redesign_2025` · `praise_reorder` · `praise_sound` · `praise_dark_mode` · `apple_watch_praise` · `accessibility_voiceover_praise` · `use_case_kids`.

**Boundary rule between `praise_generic` and `low_info`:** `praise_generic` requires a use case, a comparison, or a stated benefit. `low_info` is assigned when the review carries no recoverable content at all ("Ok", "Good", ".", gibberish). A review can carry both only if a substantive clause sits beside a contentless one.

### Meta (8 themes)

`low_info` · `paid_direct` (first-person evidence of having purchased) · `purchase_intent` (states an intention or condition to purchase, not yet done) · `competitor_mention` (names an alternative product) · `churn_exit` (states they deleted the app or moved on) · `churn_risk` (threatens to, or describes repeatedly abandoning it) · `rating_text_contradiction` · `us_2016_burst`.

**`paid_direct` boundary:** assigned only for first-person evidence — "I paid", "I bought", "I have the pro version", "worth the upgrade" in the past tense, naming a price paid, or reporting a post-purchase failure. **Not** assigned for recommending that others pay, or for describing the price in the third person. This is a deliberately conservative rule; the true payer count in the corpus is certainly higher than 48.

## 9.4 Counting rules, denominators and reconciliation summary

- **Global denominator is always 638.** The only other denominators used are **409** (United States, the one eligible storefront), **229** (non-US aggregate), **48** (payer segment), **117** (1–2★ reviews), **176 / 308 / 154** (eras), **319 / 319** (halves), and per-year counts as given in §1.4. Every segment rate is labelled as a segment rate and is shown alongside its global equivalent.
- **Theme counts are non-exclusive.** Family aggregates in §3.2 are unions, computed by set union over review IDs, not by summing theme counts.
- **No deduplication was performed** and none was required: zero exact duplicate (title, body) pairs exist.
- **No review was excluded from any denominator**, including the 13 burst reviews, the 59 `low_info` reviews and the 5 rating/text contradictions. Where those records would distort a figure, the figure is given both with and without them.
- **Verification of every cited ID:** all review IDs appearing anywhere in this report were checked against `reviews.jsonl`. **Result: every cited ID exists; zero unknown IDs.** The classification map was independently checked for zero unknown IDs, zero unassigned records, zero records with no theme, zero intra-record duplicate themes, and zero themes outside the family taxonomy — all passed (§1.3).
- **Reconciliation:** 638 records in `reviews.jsonl` = 638 records summed across 55 `by_country/*.jsonl` files = 638 in `manifest.json`; per-country counts, rating distribution and mean all reproduce exactly.
