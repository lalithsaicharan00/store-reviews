# Streaks — The habit-forming to-do list (App Store ID 963034692) — Full Review Analysis

**App:** Streaks — The habit-forming to-do list
**Developer of record in the corpus metadata:** Crunchy Bagel Pty Ltd (Adelaide, Australia) · **Bundle:** `com.streaksapp.streak`
**Corpus:** 7,270 written reviews · 93 storefronts · 1 June 2015 → 6 September 2026
**Corpus mean rating:** 4.194 · **Extracted:** 8 September 2026 (`itunes MZStore userReviewsRow`)
**Analysis date:** 10 September 2026
**Business model:** paid-up-front, one-time purchase. No subscription, no IAP, no ads (confirmed repeatedly in-corpus).

---

## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Any claim traces back to the exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several themes.
- Signal thresholds used throughout: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.

### ⚠️ Six warnings before any number here is used

1. **This is a healthy corpus, not a complaint corpus.** 63.08% of reviews are 5★ and the mean is 4.19. Unlike most apps in this category set, Streaks is not primarily a billing-dispute or dark-pattern story. The interesting findings are about *product limits* and *reliability regression*, not monetisation abuse.
2. **The corpus is front-weighted.** 2015–2017 supplies 3,400 of 7,270 reviews (46.8%). Only 401 reviews (5.5%) come from 2025–2026. Recent-period findings are real but rest on small samples; every one is labelled.
3. **Theme rates are not equally comparable across languages.** The classifier is regex-based with multilingual patterns, but coverage is thinner in Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Thai and Russian than in English. Where a language's coverage is thin, that theme is *under*-counted, never over-counted. Country comparisons lead with **rating distributions**, which are language-neutral.
4. **The app has changed shape three times.** Task capacity went 6 → 12 (v3.0, late July 2017) → 24 (v7, late July 2021). A finding from 2016 about "only 6 tasks" and one from 2024 about "only 24 tasks" are the *same* complaint against three different products. Part 8 splits the corpus by era so this is visible.
5. **No `version` field exists in this corpus.** Release-level attribution is inferred from review dates and from what reviewers explicitly say about "the last update." It is never asserted from metadata.
6. **"Paid evidence" ≠ "paying customers."** Every reviewer here paid — the app has never been free except through promotions. What Part 7 isolates is reviewers who *talk about the transaction*, which is a self-selecting group skewed toward regret and toward enthusiasm about the pricing model.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. The single largest product request in twelve years is capacity, and raising the cap twice did not settle it.**
**620 reviews (8.53%, high-priority)** ask for more tasks, pages or screens. It is the most-requested change in every large market and in every year of the corpus: 74 in 2015, 118 in 2016, 60 in 2021, 27 in 2024. The developer raised the cap from 6 to 12 (July 2017) and 12 to 24 (July 2021), and the request simply re-anchored on the new number. Against it, **118 reviews (1.62%)** actively defend the limit as the reason the app works. Evidence: `1210218319`, `1435204171`, `2118562647`, `3652626817`, `5996825854`, `6094260232`, `9405934613`, `12880039039`, `13458083376`; defenders: `1223740791`, `1465200157`, `6819955202`, `10779334259`, `13400151593`.

**2. Simplicity is the product's moat, and it is the same property that generates the capacity complaint.**
**1,780 reviews (24.48%, high-priority)** praise simplicity/minimalism, mean rating **4.58**, 76.9% of them 5★ — the highest-volume positive theme by a wide margin. The two findings are one finding: the constraint is the value proposition *and* the top objection. Any capacity change has to be an opt-in that leaves the default untouched.

**3. Reliability, not pricing, is what turned the ratings curve down — and it is a post-2021 story.**
Annual mean falls **4.58 (2016) → 4.02 (2021) → 3.92 (2022) → 3.81 (2024) → 3.71 (2025) → 3.29 (2026)**. The theme that tracks that fall is sync: sync-complaint reviews run at **1.4% of 2015–2017 reviews** but **7.60% of the 1,776 reviews from January 2022 onward** (135 of 1,776, mean rating **2.90**). Data-loss reports follow the same curve: **81 total (1.11%)**, but **60 of them (3.38% of reviews since Jan 2022, mean 2.03)** fall after the iCloud sync rewrite. Evidence: `8479907454`, `8646756790`, `8851969812`, `10125490424`, `11174051475`, `11217881769`, `11764467723`, `12257280896`, `13219722364`, `14156559364`.

**4. Apple Watch is the app's strongest differentiator and its most fragile surface.**
**626 reviews (8.61%, high-priority)** mention the Watch. Of those, **241 (38.5% of Watch mentions) carry explicit negative language**, and that subgroup's mean is **3.13** against **4.35** for the rest. In Japan (13.3% of that storefront's reviews) and Korea (13.5%) the Watch is the dominant topic. Watch sync/complication failure is the most commonly cited reason a five-year user leaves. Evidence: `1352113408`, `3355577958`, `4640524404`, `7946379003`, `8798302019`, `10510343633`, `10741780773`, `11748040853`, `12008652692`, `13235319229`.

**5. The interface wins design awards and loses users.**
**250 reviews (3.44%, very strong)** describe the UI as unintuitive, confusing, or undiscoverable. That theme has the **worst rating profile in the corpus: mean 2.66, 31.6% 1★**. Three specific actions recur: deleting a task, editing a task, and undoing an accidental completion. "Shake to undo" is named as unacceptable across a dozen markets. Evidence: `1946127158`, `3241868349`, `3801615694`, `5374792981`, `9143311516`, `10130810601`, `11129306077`, `12074352723`, `13091898748`, `13176889519`.

**6. The one-time price is a genuine, repeatedly-stated purchase reason — and the price objection is small and shrinking as a share of a shrinking corpus.**
**398 reviews (5.47%, high-priority)** explicitly praise "no subscription / one-time purchase," mean **4.43**. That theme *grows* over time: 3.61% of the pre-2017 era → **8.00% of the post-2021 era**. Against it, **123 reviews (1.69%, meaningful)** object to the price, mean **2.21**, 50.4% of them 1★. Named prices rise from **$3.99 (2015–16) → ~$5 (2017–21) → $7.99–$8 (2019–24) → $9.99–$10 (2024–26)**, roughly 2.5× over eleven years, and the objection rate did *not* rise with it (1.75% in E1 → 2.21% in E3).

**7. There is a specific, unbuilt feature the corpus asks for in seven languages: record effort beyond the goal.**
**77 reviews (1.06%, meaningful)** want to log over-achievement or partial progress — "I read 90 minutes, my goal was 30, and it stops counting"; "I did 4 of my 8 glasses and the calendar shows an ✗." The binary model is named as demotivating and as the reason people keep a second app. Evidence: `1230386714`, `9145507788`, `9592238918`, `11936906855`, `12209604326`, `13566507177`, `11968580277`, `14106848070`.

**8. Widget capability regressed and users noticed for four years.**
**318 reviews (4.37%)** mention the widget; **134 of those (42%) use negative language**, mean **3.40**. The specific event is the loss of the interactive Today-view widget around iOS 14 (Sept 2020), repeatedly asked for back through 2026. Evidence: `6448871007`, `6472011768`, `6540711760`, `6935238940`, `7824788952`, `9100003341`, `10006367703`, `11748639117`, `13580820001`, `13909346620`.

**9. Neurodivergent users are a growing, high-satisfaction segment the app was not marketed to.**
**73 reviews (1.00%, meaningful)** self-identify ADHD, autism, executive dysfunction, depression, memory injury or PTSD, mean **4.45**, 68.5% 5★. The rate nearly triples across eras: **0.66% (E1) → 0.70% (E2) → 1.90% (E3)**. Several arrive on a therapist's or doctor's recommendation (28 reviews mention a therapist/coach/doctor/physio, mean 4.50). Evidence: `8577993480`, `8266328218`, `9514806382`, `10779334259`, `11121463145`, `11285333857`, `12845331532`, `13274948716`, `13528928858`.

**10. Cheapest unshipped wins, in evidence order:** fix iCloud/Watch sync integrity → optional capacity beyond 24 → an undo button that is not a shake gesture → record-beyond-goal / partial progress → restore an interactive widget → a plain-language onboarding for delete/edit → longer history and a year view.

## 1.4 Processing method

1. Parsed all 7,270 records programmatically; double HTML-unescaped titles and bodies (the source double-encodes entities such as `&#39;`); collapsed whitespace.
2. Emitted the full corpus as 30 human-readable batches (`id|country|rating|date|title :: body`) and read all 30 in full to build the theme taxonomy inductively from what reviewers actually wrote.
3. Built a multilingual regex classifier for 50 themes (definitions in Part 12.2), applied to `title + body` lowercased.
4. **Validated and corrected.** Three initial patterns were found to over-match and were tightened before any number in this report was used:
   - `crash` initially matched 138 records because the fragment `hang(ing|s)` matched inside "c**hang**ing" — capturing 5★ "life changing" reviews. Corrected to word-bounded: **46 true crash/freeze complaints (0.63%)**.
   - `price` initially matched 153 records including neutral phrases like "for the price." Tightened to explicit objection wording: **123 (1.69%)**.
   - `limit` initially matched 724 records, mixing complaints with people *defending* the limit. Split into **620 asking for more (8.53%)** and **118 defending it (1.62%)**.
5. Split ambiguous surface themes (`watch`, `widget`, `sync`) into mention / negative-framed / positive-framed subsets rather than reporting a single misleading number.
6. Aggregated by rating, country, year and era; produced representative ID lists for every theme spread evenly across the corpus timeline.

## 1.5 Known limitations

- **Regex classification has error in both directions.** Manual reading of all 7,270 records is the control; the three corrections in 1.4 are the disclosed outcome of that control. Residual error is expected to be highest for CJK, Thai and Russian text, where the classifier under-counts.
- **Rating is not a feature preference.** A 5★ review containing a capacity complaint (457 of the 620 capacity requests are 5★ or 4★) is a satisfied user asking for more, not a detractor.
- **Review-selection bias.** People review after a delight moment (a long streak) or a rupture (data loss). Neither is representative of the silent majority.
- **Storefront ≠ nationality ≠ language.** Some `es` reviews are Catalan; some `ar` (Argentina) reviews are Spanish; several `hr`/`pl` reviews are Russian or Ukrainian. Country findings are storefront findings.
- **No download, revenue, retention or refund-outcome data exists in this corpus.** Review volume is used only as a disclosed proxy and is never called downloads.

# PART 4 — WHAT THE CORPUS SAYS ABOUT THE PRODUCT'S CORE DESIGN BET

## 4.1 The capacity limit is the defining tension of this product

**620 reviews (8.53%, high-priority) ask for more capacity. 118 (1.62%, meaningful) defend the limit.** Ratio 5.3 : 1.

**By year (denominator = that year's reviews):**

| Year | asks-for-more | that year's n | rate | the cap at the time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2015 | 74 | 868 | 8.53% | 6 |
| 2016 | 118 | 1,347 | 8.76% | 6 |
| 2017 | 60 | 1,185 | 5.06% | 6 → 12 (Jul) |
| 2018 | 57 | 468 | 12.18% | 12 |
| 2019 | 55 | 491 | 11.20% | 12 |
| 2020 | 72 | 631 | 11.41% | 12 |
| 2021 | 60 | 504 | 11.90% | 12 → 24 (Jul) |
| 2022 | 30 | 487 | 6.16% | 24 |
| 2023 | 36 | 469 | 7.68% | 24 |
| 2024 | 27 | 419 | 6.44% | 24 |
| 2025 | 23 | 283 | 8.13% | 24 |
| 2026 | 8 | 118 | 6.78% | 24 |

**Reading:** each capacity increase produces a visible drop in the complaint rate (8.76% → 5.06% around the 12-task release; 11.90% → 6.16% around the 24-task release) that then partially rebounds. The complaint never goes below ~6%. Raising the cap works and is never sufficient.

**What people actually want the extra slots for** — read directly from the reviews, this is the useful part:

1. **Time-of-day segmentation.** Morning / work / evening / bedtime routines, one page each. `1420887296`, `2210300778`, `3628077362`, `5451202886`, `6744050161`, `8299170626`, `10431684079`, `13631874444`, `14265126334`.
2. **Life-domain segmentation.** Health / work / relationships / creative. `1276552134`, `3401507494`, `4845782428`, `5860788478`, `9592334909`, `10655113870`.
3. **Graduated habits.** People want to *keep tracking* a habit that is now automatic, while starting a new one. This is the single most persuasive argument in the corpus and it is made repeatedly: `1417118522`, `4011854670`, `6522763838`, `10527629911`.
4. **Non-daily items eat daily slots.** A once-a-month or once-a-week habit consumes one of 24. `1424093667`, `3480265806`, `5230479351`, `6050921044`, `10096479510`.
5. **Family/multi-person use.** Tracking a child's chores alongside your own. `1784849811`, `11003956267`, `11316685590`.

**Counter-evidence (118 reviews) is substantive, not noise.** The defenders' argument is that the cap is the reason the app works: `1223740791` ("Limiting to only six reviews [sic — tasks] is a feature, not an area for improvement"), `1262758739`, `1324612441`, `1421300604`, `1509068759`, `3229553941`, `6819955202`, `10779334259` (an unusually well-argued 5★), `13400151593`, `13312978008`. Note `10779334259` explicitly credits the *four-page structure* with segmenting the day.

**Product implication (interpretation):** the request and the defence are compatible. Nobody in the 620 asks for the *default* to change. They ask for the *ceiling* to move. See Part 11.2.

## 4.2 Simplicity is measurable, and it degrades

Simplicity praise as a share of that period's reviews: **E1 32.30% → E2 20.80% → E3 18.36%.** Meanwhile UI-confusion complaints go **E1 0.69% → E2 4.93% → E3 5.33%**.

Reviewers name the inflection precisely. The v3.0 update of late July 2017 added negative tasks, a second page, sounds, themes and statistics, and the corpus reaction splits the same week:

- Delighted: `1698329281`, `1700895077`, `1702216092`, `1708201423`, `1723164061`, `1762432110`.
- Alarmed at the loss of simplicity: `1699860194` ("The new UI is confusing, annoying, inefficient… all in the service of aesthetics"), `1704290647` ("The great thing used to be having to pare down"), `1706842643`, `1708307755`, `1244333793` (an earlier version of the same complaint), `3554375156` ("They laid a whole layer of functionality that violates the original assumption… It feels like they dipped a pickle in chocolate").

Later waves repeat it: `8872999798` (2022, "Feature overload ruined core functionality"), `9132739356` (2022, "Please keep it simple, it got a little cluttered recently"), `9522443552` (2023, "Falling prey to adding complexity for the sake of complexity"), `9974958402` (2023), `12133468213` (2025, "Recent updates have made this app so confusing").

**Interpretation:** this is the clearest strategic warning in the corpus. The property that produced 1,780 positive reviews is being spent, one feature at a time, to answer requests from a minority.

## 4.3 Three specific interactions carry the entire UX complaint

Reading all 250 UI-confusion reviews, the complaint is not diffuse. It resolves into three actions:

| Action | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Deleting a task** | `1428576781`, `1736489826`, `1873...`, `3627060299`, `3813274742`, `3934184360`, `3969877398`, `4219370308`, `5503321016`, `5453294830`, `9212927813`, `13176889519` |
| **Editing / renaming / moving a task** | `1347730758`, `3772828822`, `3821585068`, `3931904391`, `4515004865`, `8435327387`, `9524...`, `12670297933`, `13127255768`, `14243139067` |
| **Undoing an accidental completion** | 77 reviews (1.06%), see below |

**The undo problem, specifically.** The only undo is a shake gesture available immediately after the action. Reviewers across 12 storefronts call this unacceptable: `1215117154`, `1303004020`, `3934671711`, `5528374045`, `5652418303`, `6499306517`, `6879...`, `7060772966`, `7273743439`, `8879991437`, `9212927813`, `9893956395`, `10535886439`, `10912008189`, `12894788718`, `13171890468`, `14245262334`, `14391501375`. **Interpretation:** these three actions are the entire gap between a 4.19 mean and something higher. They are not architectural. A single "Edit / Delete / Undo" affordance and an in-app text explanation would address the majority of 250 reviews carrying a 2.66 mean.

## 4.4 The binary completion model discards information users want

**77 reviews (1.06%, meaningful)** ask to record effort beyond, or short of, the goal. Two of the three most-upvoted reviews in the entire corpus are this request (`9145507788`, 313 net votes; `8642992805`, 126 net votes).

The complaint has two symmetrical halves:

**Over-achievement is discarded.** "My goal is 30 minutes of reading; I read 90 and it stops at 30." `1230386714`, `2749205687`, `6753878706`, `7249290170`, `7353753973`, `7622168499`, `8341383590`, `8847760481`, `10504293132`, `11851969749`, `11936906855`, `12190462519`, `12209604326`, `12750955556`, `13211973709`, `14106848070`, `14472714750`.

The most articulate statement of why this matters psychologically is `11968580277` (dk, 2★, Nov 2024): a habit stated as "do 30 push-ups" breaks on a 25-push-up day, whereas "push-ups per day" would have recorded a 25 and preserved momentum. Also `9592238918` (jp, 5★) which sets out five specific sub-requests in English and Japanese.

## 4.5 Frequency and history gaps

**Frequency (101 reviews, 1.39%).** Shipped over time: times-per-week (late 2015, `1295254379`), times-per-day (2016, `1450537959`), times-per-month, every-N-days. **Still absent in the last review in the corpus:** yearly goals (`5358836760`, `9436431103`, `10397702118`, `11891384654`, `12537226420`, `13735945699`), arbitrary intervals such as every 6 weeks / quarterly (`11315859157`, `14320549167`, `9989040594` — a Korean shift worker on a 4–5 day rotation), and specific dates of the month (`8577016754`, `9939580410`, `6914988991`).

## 6.1 Sync — the single largest reliability finding

# PART 7 — PAID-USER AND UPGRADE ANALYSIS

## 7.1 Framing this correctly

Every reviewer in this corpus paid (or redeemed a promo). There is no free tier, no trial, no upgrade path, and no subscription. So the useful segmentation is not "payers vs non-payers" — it is **reviewers who explicitly discuss the transaction (735, 10.11%, mean 3.52)** versus everyone else (6,535, mean 4.27).

**Interpretation:** talking about money in a review of a paid app is a signal of *dissonance* — either unusually strong satisfaction with the value, or regret. The 0.75-point mean gap is that dissonance, not a payment problem.

## 7.4 Price objection, examined

**123 reviews (1.69%, meaningful), mean 2.21, 50.4% 1★.**
Distribution: us 41, au 18, ca 12, de 12, gb 12, cn 10, nz 3, es/ru/se 2 each.
Rate against that storefront: **au 5.7%** (highest of any 50+ storefront), ca 3.6%, de 3.8%, cn 2.5%, gb 2.6%, us 1.3%.

**By era:** E1 (2015–Jul 2017) 1.75% → E2 2.41% → E3 2.21%. **Flat, despite a ~2.5× nominal price rise.**

## 7.5 Refunds and billing

**79 reviews (1.09%), mean 1.75, 70.9% 1★.** Two sources:

Total identified accidental-purchase reviews: 6 by strict regex, ~19 by reading.
2. **Expectation mismatch** — buyer discovers the cap or the "it's a checklist" reality and wants the money back: `9540311476`, `10238906445`, `11450516665`, `11474891367`, `11478493003`, `12595616102`, `12277830068`, `12729571609`.

There is also a small, notable cluster of **charge-amount confusion** — the reviewer says the price shown and the amount debited differed: `1821681612` (au), `2863100772` (ru), `3565187204` (ru), `4550475560` (tr), `5452308394` (tr), `12692528270` (au), `14197653464` (mx). **`[weak signal, n=7, 0.10%]`** — most likely tax/VAT or currency conversion, not developer behaviour, but it is a recurring, avoidable trust event.

**Nothing in this corpus supports a claim of deceptive billing.** The developer takes one payment through Apple. The refund friction reviewers describe is Apple's process, and several reviewers say so.

## 8.3 Germany — the corpus's worst-performing large market

**n = 319, mean 3.62 (global 4.19), 16.6% 1★ (global 8.94%), 43.6% 5★ (global 63.1%).**

2. **Bad German localisation.** Named explicitly and repeatedly: `1272924985` ("Die Übersetzung ins Deutsche ist total fehlgeschlagen"), `1273715314` ("die Übersetzung der Anleitung ist einfach nur gruselig"), `3626095782` ("Grausame Übersetzung"), `1670054290` (ch, "ich glaube Google hätte die besser hingekriegt"). ## 9.2 Trend 1 — The rating decline is real, dated, and driven by reliability `[very strong]`

Mean falls 4.56 → 4.08 → 3.83 across three eras with n ≥ 1,950 in each. The themes that rise in lockstep are sync (1.42% → 10.15%, **7.1×**), data loss (0.33% → 4.62%, **14×**) and widget bugs (0.04% → 2.10%, **52×**). The themes that do *not* explain it: price (flat), capacity (falls after each release).

**Confounder acknowledged:** the App Store review-prompt API changed in 2017, which mechanically reduced the volume of low-effort 5★ reviews across all apps. Corpus volume drops 1,185 (2017) → 468 (2018), consistent with that. So *part* of the 2017→2018 step is a platform artefact. **But the 2021→2026 continuation is not** — volume is roughly flat 2021–2023 while the mean keeps falling and sync/data-loss keeps rising.

## 9.3 Trend 2 — The iCloud sync migration is the sharpest single event `[very strong]`

Sync complaints: 25 (2021) → **60 (2022)** → 40 (2023) → 24 (2024). Rate against period reviews: 1.42% (2015–17) → 7.60% (Jan 2022 onward). Data loss: 6 (2021) → 17 (2022) → 22 (2024). The migration is described by a reviewer in `8482253962` (Mar 2022). The tail is still visible in 2026 (`13491181225`, `13319734610`, `14156559364`).

## 9.4 Trend 3 — Capacity complaints fall after each release, then rebound `[very strong]`

8.76% (2016, cap 6) → 5.06% (2017, cap raised) → 12.18% (2018, cap 12) → 11.90% (2021, cap raised) → 6.16% (2022, cap 24) → 8.13% (2025). Each release buys roughly three years.

## 9.5 Trend 4 — Simplicity praise is being spent `[very strong]`

32.30% → 20.80% → 18.36%, a 43% relative decline, while UI-confusion complaints rise nearly 8×. The corpus contains reviewers dating the loss precisely to the July 2017 feature expansion (9.2 evidence list in Part 4.2).

## 9.6 Trend 5 — The pricing model becomes a *stronger* differentiator over time `[very strong]`

"No subscription" praise: 3.61% → 5.55% → **8.00%**. As the category subscription-ised, an eleven-year-old one-time purchase became the reason to choose it. This is the one competitive position that strengthened.

## 9.7 Trend 6 — Neurodivergent adoption nearly triples `[meaningful]`

0.66% → 0.70% → **1.90%**. Concentrated in 2022–2026 (`8266328218`, `8428275405`, `8432447980`, `8577993480`, `9514806382`, `9822595740`, `9855154146`, `10779334259`, `11121463145`, `11141541374`, `11285333857`, `11390335993`, `12333316832`, `12845331532`, `13274948716`, `13528928858`, `14469956788`). Mean 4.45. Several of these reviews double as the *strongest* capacity complaints, because task decomposition is exactly what executive-function support requires (`5516647636` — an explicit accusation of ableism, `6888486242`, `8975182976`, `12523694361`, `13180845529`).

## 9.8 Trend 7 — The corpus is drying up `[very strong]`

Monthly review volume 2024–2026: 59 (Jan 24) → 25 (Dec 24) → 34 (Jan 25) → 12 (Dec 25) → 26 (Jan 26) → **6 (Sep 26, partial month)**. The trailing twelve months (Oct 2025 – Sep 2026) supply **156 reviews, 2.15% of the corpus**. Every 2025–2026 finding rests on that base and is labelled accordingly.

**2026 is the worst year on record: mean 3.29, 24.6% 1★, 36.4% 5★ (n=118).** `[meaningful but small — n=118 over eight months]`

## 9.9 Trends explicitly NOT claimed

- **No pricing-damage trend.** Price objections are flat across a 2.5× price rise.
- **No monetisation-abuse trend.** There is no subscription, no paywall shift, no entitlement revocation anywhere in 7,270 reviews.
- **No AI trend.** Exactly one review mentions an AI feature (`14069398191`, May 2026, asking for it to be removed). Not a trend.
- **No support-collapse trend.** Support praise (49) and support failure (`2168151281`, `3324443697`, `6513442179`, `7429308689`, `10450266476`, `11693785508`, `12008652692`) both appear across all three eras with no directional movement that the sample can support.
- **No competitor-displacement trend.** Competitor mentions are too sparse (≤14 each) to trend.

# PART 10 — WHAT A COMPETITOR LEARNS FROM THIS CORPUS FOR FREE

1. **A hard cap on tracked habits is a durable, defensible product position — and a permanent 6–12% complaint tax.** Both halves are true. Do not adopt the cap without a pressure valve.
2. **Press-and-hold beats tap.** Reviewers describe the deliberate friction as the source of satisfaction and as accident-proofing. Copying "tap to complete" loses something real.
3. **HealthKit auto-completion is the single highest-leverage integration in this category.** It removes the friction that killed every other habit app the reviewer tried. 277 reviews say so.
4. **Binary completion throws away the data users most want.** The two most-upvoted reviews in an 7,270-review corpus are both requests to record effort beyond the goal. Ship a floor-without-ceiling model and a partial-progress calendar state.
5. **The Apple Watch is a purchase driver, not a feature.** People buy for it and churn on it. If you ship a Watch app, its reliability budget is your main reliability budget.
6. **One-time pricing is a growing wedge, not a legacy handicap.** 398 reviews name it, and the rate more than doubled across eleven years.
7. **Loss-framed notifications ("you will lose your streak") draw complaints in four languages.** Frame gains.
8. **Never let sync be last-writer-wins.** `10815885619` spells out the correct architecture in a customer review.
9. **Undo must be a button.** Shake-to-undo produced 77 complaints, several of which end in deleting the app.
10. **Machine-translated help text is visible and expensive.** German, Chinese, Russian, Swedish, Korean and Taiwanese reviewers all name it, and it lands hardest in the markets already objecting to price.

## 11.5 Experiments worth running

1. **Capacity valve A/B:** unlock pages 5–8 behind a setting for a random cohort; measure 90-day retention and rating delta against the constrained cohort. The corpus predicts the *complaint* falls; it does not tell you whether *retention* falls.
2. **Onboarding rewrite:** measure day-3 deletion rate against the current icon-only tutorial. The corpus predicts a large effect on the 1★ rate.
3. **Notification copy:** loss-framed vs gain-framed, measure completion rate and notification-disable rate.
4. **Partial-progress rendering:** does replacing ✗ with a partial ring change 30-day retention after a missed goal?

## 12.3 Validation approach and known error risk

**What was validated and how:**
1. **Full manual read.** All 7,270 records were read. The taxonomy was built from that reading, not imposed on it.
2. **Three corrections made before publication** (documented in 1.4): `crash` 138 → 46, `price` 153 → 123, `limit` 724 → 620 + 118. Each was found by inspecting the theme's rating distribution for implausibility (a "crash" theme with 62% 5★ is a red flag) and then reading matched records.
3. **Ambiguous-surface splits.** `watch`, `widget` and `sync` were split into mention / negative / positive rather than reported as single numbers, because their raw means (3.88, 3.84, 3.51) were obviously mixing praise and complaint.
4. **Spot-checks of high-impact clusters.** The 620 capacity requests, the 250 UI complaints, the 81 data-loss reports, the 123 price objections and the 79 refund reviews were each re-read against their matched IDs.

**Known residual error:**
- **Under-counting in CJK, Thai, Hebrew and Arabic.** The classifier's non-Latin patterns are narrower than its English ones. Themes such as "record beyond goal" and "UI unintuitive" are almost certainly higher in `cn`, `jp`, `kr`, `tw` and `th` than the table shows. The Chinese UI-confusion rate of **0.0%** is not credible and is a coverage artefact — manual reading found at least `1752928108`, `10022946276`, `1713794915`, `9292610270`.
- **Over-counting risk on broad themes.** `P_simple` (24.48%) matches the word "simple" in any context including "too simple" criticism. Its 4.58 mean and 76.9% 5★ rate indicate the contamination is small, but it is not zero.
- **Themes that shipped mid-corpus.** Notes, multiple-per-day, backfill and negative tasks were all requests that were later built. Their counts therefore mix "please build this" (pre-ship) with "thanks for building this" (post-ship). Part 3.4 flags each.
- **The classifier cannot detect sarcasm, and this corpus has some** (`1861463137` title "バグだらけ。絶対買うな" is unambiguous; `13592437604` "Reed :: I despise this app" is not sarcastic but is content-free).

## 12.7 The one-paragraph version

Streaks is a genuinely well-liked product — 63% five-star across 7,270 reviews and eleven years — whose central design bet, a hard cap on tracked habits, is simultaneously its most-praised property (1,780 reviews praise the simplicity, mean 4.58) and its most-requested change (620 reviews ask for more capacity, and the cap has already been raised twice without settling it). Its rating has fallen steadily from 4.56 to 3.83 across three product eras, and the themes that track that fall are not pricing or monetisation — the one-time purchase is a *growing* competitive asset praised in 8% of recent reviews — but reliability: iCloud sync complaints rose 7× and data-loss reports 14× after a 2022 sync migration, and Apple Watch failures now churn the very customers who bought the app for the Watch. Three interface actions — delete, edit, undo — account for essentially all of the 250 reviews carrying the corpus's worst rating profile (mean 2.66). The cheapest wins are unambiguous and small: make sync merge instead of overwrite, surface the backup path, put Undo on a button, record effort beyond the goal, and let the ceiling move without moving the default.
