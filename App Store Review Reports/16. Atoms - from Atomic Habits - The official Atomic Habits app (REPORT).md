## How to read this

- Every percentage uses **1,168** as the denominator unless a different denominator is named on the line.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several themes. They do not sum to 1,168.
- Signal labels follow the standard band table:

| Share | Label |
|---|---|
| <0.1% | Ignore by default |
| 0.1–0.5% | Weak |
| 0.5–1% | Emerging |
| 1–3% | Meaningful |
| 3–5% | Very strong |
| >5% | High-priority |

- **Segment rates** (e.g. "59% of confirmed payers") are always labelled as such and always carry both the segment denominator and the global count.
- Only **four** storefronts clear the 50-review bar for standalone analysis: **US (542), GB (103), CA (73), AU (52)**. Everything else is folded into global figures; sub-50 claims are marked `[limited evidence]`.
- Facts taken from outside the review corpus are marked **[external]** and are never mixed into corpus percentages. **Live App Store lookup was blocked by this session's network policy** (`itunes.apple.com` returned 403 at the egress proxy). External store-rating figures therefore come from `habit-apps-ranked.json`, a cached App Store ranking artefact already committed to this repository on 9 Sep 2026 — one day after this review corpus was extracted. See §1.6.
- **This corpus is 58% launch-week.** Read §1.7 before treating any global percentage as a picture of the app today.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

## 1. This is the most monetisation-damaged corpus in the set, and the damage is a single decision

Atoms is not a broken app. It is a well-built app whose packaging destroyed its written reception.

**39.13% of the entire corpus (457 reviews, mean 2.38) touches money.** One theme alone — an explicit objection to the price or its value-for-money — carries **392 reviews (33.56%, HIGH-PRIORITY), mean 2.18, 65.3% of them 1–2★, only 7.4% of them 5★.**

The concentration in the negative bands is extreme:

| Band | n | % of band that objects to price |
|---|---|---|
| 1★ | 208 | **153 (73.6%)** |
| 2★ | 142 | **103 (72.5%)** |
| 3★ | 135 | **76 (56.3%)** |
| 4★ | 102 | 31 (30.4%) |
| 5★ | 581 | 29 (5.0%) |

**Nearly three out of four one-star reviews of the official Atomic Habits app are about the price, not the product.** Only 15 of the 208 one-star reviews (7.2%) are about the app failing to load, and 12 (5.8%) about logging being broken.

The mirror image is just as sharp. **109 of the 392 price objectors (27.8% segment rate) explicitly praise the app in the same review** — its design, its simplicity, or its effect on their life. **43 of the 208 one-star reviews (20.7%) contain an explicit praise theme.** This is not a corpus of people who dislike the product.

> `10995055502`(GB,1★): *"This app is definitely the best looking habit tracking app I've downloaded… However, due to the ridiculous subscription fee it was a very quick uninstall for me."*
> `11090232087`(GB,5★): *"I loved it and it worked really well for me. I would have definitely considered purchasing it, but the cost is just too high for what I can afford. A shame as it really was making a difference."*

## 2. The free tier is one habit — and that single number is the mechanism

**76 reviews (6.51%, HIGH-PRIORITY)** complain that the free tier permits exactly **one** habit. Mean **2.22**, **61.8% are 1–2★**, only 7.9% are 5★.

The reviewers' argument is always the same, and it is a *product-logic* argument, not a price argument: a habit tracker that tracks one habit is not a habit tracker.

> `10984314443`(NL,2★): *"you can only track one habit without upgrading to Pro… This is useless for me, I have more than 3 habits to track before I have breakfast."*
> `11340151425`(US,1★): *"Only allowing 1 habit on the free plan is simply too limited, essentially meaning the app is useless without a $10 mo. commitment."*
> `12199586867`(US,1★): *"Tracking only 1 habit for free isn't worth the storage space for a glorified Reminders app."*
> `11400713510`(US,1★): *"the free version would better be renamed to 'Atom,' since you're only supported for one change at a time."*

The cap has been **1 free habit continuously from 24 Feb 2024 (`10975917055`) to 14 May 2026 (`14064134860`)** — 27 months, 76 dated complaints, unchanged.

Because the free tier is unusable, **the free tier cannot do trial work.** Every other habit app in this folder converts through a usable free tier; Atoms converts through a 28-day all-features trial that ends in a wall. §0.4 shows what that produces.

## 3. The *paid* tier is capped too — and that is the finding nobody expects

**75 reviews (6.42%, HIGH-PRIORITY)** complain about the cap on the **paid** tier. Mean **2.57**, 57.3% are 1–2★.

The cap was **3 habits at launch** and was raised to **6** in the second week of March 2024. Both numbers are visible in the dated record:

| Cap | First dated reviewer report | Last dated reviewer report | Span | Mentions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pro = **3** habits | `10975518449`(US,1★, 24 Feb 2024) | `11883256863`(US,1★, 28 Oct 2024) | — | 48 |
| Pro = **6** habits | `11033626383`(US,1★, 11 Mar 2024) | `14287877372`(US,2★, 10 Jul 2026) | **2y 4m** | 26 |

`11033626383`(US,1★) is the hinge review and it is the most-voted cap complaint in the corpus (8 helpful votes): *"Update: They went from 3 to 6 habits for pro at $100+/yr?! That's your fix? Absolutely not. Deleted."*

Paying customers are still hitting the ceiling in 2026:
> `14287877372`(US,2★, Jul 2026): *"A paid user pays $5/month or $40/year just to create only 6 habits simultaneously? Other similar apps use hard limit to incentivize in-app purchase, not locking them down to a limited version."*
> `13573785597`(US,3★, **confirmed payer**): *"I bought premium so I could track more than one habit… the maximum 6 habits they give on the app isn't enough to cover everything—forget about stacking."*
> `13222746779`(US,5★): *"I fully understand what the limit is trying to help, but it's far, far more unhelpful than helpful. Don't control us."*

**Combined, the two caps carry 135 reviews (11.56%), mean 2.44, 57.8% 1–2★.** This is the single largest addressable product decision in the corpus.

**The counter-case is real and should not be ignored.** **20 reviews (1.71%, MEANINGFUL) defend the constraint**, mean **4.60**, **80% are 5★** — the constraint is a genuine differentiator for the people it fits:
> `10950990017`(US,5★): *"you can only add a second habit after you've done your first 1 at least 3 times. It forces your to slow down and actually develop at a comfortable pace."*
> `12115363939`(US,4★): *"People complain about only 6 habits but they're missing the point of building a habit."*
> `13329955083`(US,5★): *"tried to add more and the app said 'don't add too many', which is usually what I do and end up overwhelmed."*
> `10980656747`(ZA,5★): *"the gamification that rewards you with ability to record more habits by recording habits."*

The constraint is loved at 3–6 habits and hated at 1. **The design principle survives raising the free tier; it does not survive a free tier of one.**

## 4. The 28-day trial is experienced as a trap, and it is producing the app's angriest reviews

**41 reviews (3.51%, VERY STRONG)** describe the trial-to-paywall transition as a bait-and-switch. Mean **2.44**, 58.5% are 1–2★. The mechanism reviewers describe is precise and repeated: the app *encourages you to build a streak*, then removes the ability to log it.

> `12482534390`(US,3★): *"Originally the fact that the app is paid was undisclosed… once you get sucked in, you don't want to switch so you're 'forced' to pay. And this is the reason I'm not gonna pay as it feels unfair."*
> `13613868361`(US,1★, 7 helpful votes): *"It bothers me that this app was setup in a way to get you in the habit of making good habits, then after you do something ten times you can't have more than a single habit without a subscription… I feel like this is manipulative from James Clear."*
> `12600470632`(US,3★): *"the trial model is a bit sly in that it encourages people to start habits and continue them for three weeks and then hits you with a steep bill. It's counter to the mission of making people more productive."*
> `14302077000`(US,2★): *"Being suddenly locked out and charged is NOT the principles the book taught… I look at the book Atomic habits differently now."*
> `13882025149`(CA,1★): *"Was using the free version, now all of a sudden I can only track one habit… just put some ads in to the free version to generate income. Deleting."*

Worse: **losing the subscription does not return you to a usable free tier.** `13573892543`(US,2★, **confirmed former payer**) is the clearest case in the corpus:
> *"I deleted 3 of my 4 established habits but I still can't edit my 1 remaining habit, view detailed history, or even mark it completed. It's my habit for walking my dog, who is recently deceased, and I don't want to delete these records… I guess the app is bricked for me now."*

**This is a churn mechanic that also destroys the goodwill of the people most likely to come back.** `11150554851`(CA,2★): *"You're supposed to be able to continue tracking one habit. The copywriting even says 'we wouldn't leave you hanging!' But then they left me hanging."*

**This theme is the only major negative that is still growing:** 2.36% (P1) → 5.07% (P2) → 4.21% (P3) → **7.23% (P4, 2026)**.

## 5. Confirmed payers are the angriest group in the corpus

**17 reviewers (1.46%, MEANINGFUL) state in their own words that they paid for Atoms.** They are a small cohort, and that itself is informative — but their ratings are not ambiguous.

| | Confirmed payers (n=17) | Rest of corpus (n=1,151) |
|---|---|---|
| Mean rating | **2.41** | 3.62 |
| 1–2★ | **58.8%** | 29.5% |
| 5★ | 17.6% | 50.2% |

**Payers rate 1.21 stars below everyone else and are twice as likely to leave 1–2★.** Every buyer in this corpus is a person who *already* got past the price objection — and a majority of them still ended up writing a negative review.

What they complain about (segment rates, paid denominator = 17):

| Complaint | In paid cohort | Global count of that theme |
|---|---|---|
| Price / value still not justified | 10 (58.8%) | 392 |

| Data loss | 3 (17.6%) | 9 |

The full paid cohort with dates and outcomes is in §5.1. The pattern that matters: **five of the seven billing complaints in the whole corpus come from the seventeen people who paid** — a 71.4% segment concentration.

> `11015185142`(US,1★): *"I have now subscribed three separate times ($360 in total) and have saved the screenshot of it thanking me for my payment and then every day it says I do not have a paid account… I cannot use the application. I am annoyed and can't seem to reach anyone for resolution."*
> `12988241997`(US,1★): *"A year later, I was auto-charged for an annual renewal with no reminder and no warning. I reached out to support the same day of the charge, but was told it was my fault for not cancelling and that no refund would be issued."*
> `11990095414`(US,1★): *"It looks like you are signing up for a free subscription of 28 days but they actually charge you right away. And they won't issue you a refund either."*

**And the price-objection rate barely moved: 30.97% (P1) → 50.69% (P2) → 24.74% (P3) → 30.12% (P4).** Only **6 reviews (0.51%) acknowledge the price drop at all**, and **five of those six are still 1–2★**:
> `11438714327`(US,2★): *"I really appreciate that there was a price drop from the insanely high original price. But $70 a year still seems over priced."*
> `11346837271`(US,1★): *"price dropped to $70 according to an email I got on June 4… This just seems like a big money grab."*
> `11980741663`(CA,5★) is the only positive one: *"I originally left a lower review because of the very high pro pricing at the time, it's nice to see it has been lowered to a somewhat more affordable price."*

**Reading: the objection is not to the number. It is to the shape.** 37 reviews (3.17%, VERY STRONG, mean 2.16) ask specifically for a one-time or lifetime purchase; 53 (4.54%, VERY STRONG) ask for a cheaper tier, a student rate, or a discount. Cutting the annual price by 67% did not fix a problem that reviewers state as *"I will not rent a checklist."*

> `13613868361`(US,1★): *"I would gladly pay a one-time $20 for just the habit tracking, but that's not an option."*
> `12356777390`(DK,4★): *"WHY isn't there a lifetime subscription option??? Pay 125$ once and help people create a better life without leeching on them???"*
> `11685719155`(GB,1★): *"Procreate is a one time purchase of £12… Built once, pay once. £10 per month is insane."*

## 8. What people actually love — and it is not the tracker

Three praise clusters carry the 5★ band, and all three are about *content and craft*, not about tracking:

1. **Simplicity / ease of use** — 183 (15.67%), mean **4.73**, 82.0% 5★, **zero 1–2★**
2. **Design, UI, animation, visual craft** — 179 (15.33%), mean 3.49 — *and this is the important one, see below*
3. **The James Clear brand and the book** — 435 reviews reference it (37.24%); when the rating is 4–5★ it reads as a halo, 268 reviews (22.95%), mean **4.90**, 90.3% 5★

Then the differentiated content:
- **The Mindset tab / daily lessons / articles** — 59 (5.05%), mean 4.07, 66.1% 5★
- **The press-and-hold atom, haptics, the fill animation** — 45 (3.85%), mean 4.02
- **Identity framing ("cast a vote for the person you want to become")** — 35 (3.00%), mean 4.43, 71.4% 5★
- **Life change / "best app"** — 72 (6.16%), mean 4.49, 77.8% 5★

**Design praise is the most rating-agnostic theme in the corpus.** Of the 179 design-praise reviews, **56 are 1–2★** (31.3%) — people complimenting the craft on the way out the door. Mean of the 85 reviews that praise the design *and* object to the price: **2.36**.

> `10993476523`(US,2★): *"It's a beautifully designed app with some lovely haptic elements. However it's light on substance, especially for the price."*
> `10984334544`(GB,2★): *"The app is pretty and aethestically pleasing to use… But ultimately it's just another app abusing over-the-top subscription fees."*

**The haptic completion loop is the product's genuine, defensible craft asset** and reviewers describe the mechanism unprompted:
> `10979085353`(US,5★): *"Surprisingly satisfying to hold and fill the circle 🟡"*
> `10989132915`(US,5★): *"the app has a satisfying hold while the circle expands ending with a vibrate reward… it's a bit Pavlovian in a good way!"*
> `10992749538`(US,5★): *"The long press with a vibration is literally an energizing, motivating reward for my efforts… It's only my second day, but I'm beginning to crave that feeling."*

**Warning:** praise for this loop is decaying fast — 4.87% (P1) → 4.15% (P2) → 1.05% (P3) → 1.20% (P4). A 2024 redesign replaced the floating circles with a list; three reviewers name the loss directly (`11343906321`, `11814577640`, `11390526677`, `11368755180`).

**16 reviews (1.37%, MEANINGFUL) attack James Clear personally or say the app has damaged the book for them.** Mean **1.38** — the lowest-rated theme in the corpus. **93.8% are 1–2★. Not one is above 3★.**

> `10993434698`(GB,1★): *"Over the top pricing will harm legacy."*

## 10. Reliability is getting worse, not better — and it is now the second story

**103 reviews (8.82%, HIGH-PRIORITY) report a technical failure.** Mean **2.45**, 59.2% are 1–2★. The rate has more than doubled across the life of the app:

| Period | Reliability-complaint rate |
|---|---|
| P1 launch (Feb–Mar 2024) | 6.19% |
| P2 rest of 2024 | 9.68% |
| P3 2025 | **14.74%** |
| P4 2026 | **14.46%** |

The launch bugs were real but were fixed within days — `10976173296`(US,5★, 26 Feb): *"There's no bug! Those were old reviews before the app was launched to the public."* The **2025–26 failures are different, and they are core-loop failures**:

- **App freezes / will not get past habit creation — 31 reviews (2.65%), mean 1.90, 74.2% 1–2★, zero 5★.** Fifteen of the 31 are dated 2025 or later and describe the *same* screen: `12492982373`(US,1★) `12515484177`(US,1★) `12539170479`(US,1★) `12542075765`(US,1★) `12555385251`(DE,1★) `13453138963`(US,2★) `13505886224`(AR,2★) `13512385502`(GB,1★) `13669770209`(PT,2★) `13724948462`(PL,1★). This is a live, unresolved onboarding failure fifteen months old.
- **Logging silently fails — 12 reviews (1.03%), mean 2.00, 83.3% 1–2★, zero 5★.** Includes a confirmed payer (`13837724169`, Mar 2026: *"Randomly stopped updating when I tracked my habits. I pay for this app"*).
- **Data / history loss — 9 reviews (0.77%), mean 2.78.** `11858415210`(GB,5★, payer): *"the app has randomly decided to wipe out over a week's worth of log history for all 6 habits I'm tracking… considering the price of its yearly subscription, this bug/issue is simply unacceptable."* Also `13732701636`(CA,1★, Feb 2026): *"2 days in and all my habits disappeared."*
- **Repeated forced logout — 10 reviews (0.86%), mean 2.10, zero 5★**, clustered Oct 2024–Jun 2025, aggravated by a two-factor change (`11928001093`).
- **The app requires a live internet connection to tick a box — 9 reviews (0.77%), mean 2.56, zero 5★.** `10977084069`(US,1★): *"Like why in the world does 'ticking a box' require connectivity!"*; `11339178421`(US,3★): *"I work in remote areas around the world and don't have internet for several days at a time… that means I can't use the app."*

## 11. The two cheapest missing features in the corpus

**No dark mode — 21 reviews (1.80%, MEANINGFUL), mean 3.76, only 9.5% are 1–2★.** This is a *high-goodwill* request: these are people who like the app and want one setting. It is also the fastest-growing feature request: 0.29% (P1) → 2.76% (P2) → 5.26% (P3) → 3.61% (P4). Two reviewers frame it as accessibility, not preference (`11936034388`, US,1★: *"some of us can't read a blinding white screen… with such shoddy accessibility I would never pay for it"*; `14138142114`, NL,3★: *"My eyes hurt"*).

**No back-logging — 33 reviews (2.83%, MEANINGFUL), mean 3.39, only 21.2% are 1–2★.** You can log today and yesterday, and nothing else. Reviewers describe losing streaks they actually earned:
> `11049027904`(US,4★): *"I accidentally lost my streak because I forgot to log my activity 2 days ago… my streak is permanently reset."*
> `11245427777`(IE,5★): *"I've completed 5 days and I don't like looking at my record that says I've done 2!"*
> `12909615292`(FR,1★): *"I have an existing habit… you cannot create a habit in the past. Contacted the support and they didn't want to do anything about it."*
> `11572795669`(AU,3★): *"Especially for someone with ADHD who struggles with admin."*

Both are single-digit-effort changes sitting in the 3★/4★ band. **Neither has shipped in 31 months.**

## 1.4 Processing method

1. **Full read.** All 1,168 records were sorted chronologically, written to 12 batches of ~100, and read individually in the original language before any classification was written. Non-English reviews (approx. 60, 5.1%) were read in-language; no machine translation was used.
2. **Taxonomy built from the read**, not from a pre-existing list. 57 themes.
3. **Classification is hybrid.** Broad praise themes (simplicity, design, brand, mindset content, haptics, identity) use multilingual regex over `title + body` and were spot-checked. **Every high-stakes theme** — price objection, both habit caps, the bait-and-switch cluster, moral/cash-grab language, brand betrayal, the paid cohort, billing problems, all reliability themes, data loss, support, privacy, back-logging, and every theme with n<25 — was **hand-audited from a printed candidate list, review by review**, with explicit add/remove sets recorded in the classifier (§9.3).
4. **The price theme uses proximity logic, not keyword matching.** A review is counted only when a price/subscription/currency token occurs within 110 characters of a negative token, or matches an explicit phrase list. The full matched set and the full 1–3★ non-matched set were both printed and read; **8 false positives were removed by hand** (price *defences* such as `10975681427`, `10983622751`; a "lost 50 pounds" weight reference, `12616174078`) and **32 misses were added by hand** (e.g. `10983135069` *"So it's like the book….every month"*; `11054245890` *"The price is astronomical"*).
5. **The paid cohort was audited line by line.** A wide first-person purchase sweep returned 146 candidates; **19 were removed as book, journal or competitor-app purchases** (e.g. `10980935471` *"I paid for your book"*, `11489179909` *"I bought Streaks instead"*) and 3 ambiguous cases were moved to a separate `paid_ambiguous` set excluded from every paid statistic. Purchase *intent* ("I'd gladly pay", "I was ready to subscribe") is excluded throughout.
6. **HTML entities** were unescaped and whitespace normalised before matching.

## 1.5 Limitations and judgement calls

- **Launch-window dominance is the single biggest limitation.** 678 of 1,168 reviews (58.05%) fall in the first six weeks. Every global percentage is disproportionately a snapshot of Feb–Mar 2024. **Where a finding depends on the app as it is today, the P4 (2026) column in §7 is the number to use, not the global one.** Post-launch-only figures are given wherever they differ materially.
- **Selection bias.** Written reviews are 5.6% of all raters, and §0.7 quantifies a −1.21-star gap against tap-only ratings in every storefront checked. This corpus over-represents the motivated and the annoyed.
- **A launch-day review-solicitation campaign is visible in the corpus and shapes it.** `10977084069`(US,1★) alleges the beta list was asked to post: *"they specifically asked the testers to come out here and 'overcome outdated reviews' — in other words, astroturf."* 23 reviews (1.97%) self-identify as beta testers; they are strongly bimodal (47.8% 5★, 47.8% 1–2★). No claim in this report rests on the launch-week 5★ mass alone.
- **No version field.** All release attributions are inferred from reviewers' own dated statements, never from a changelog (none was reachable in this session). The 3→6 Pro-cap change and the June 2024 price drop are dated from reviewer reports, which is why both are given as reviewer-reported ranges.
- **External store data could not be refreshed.** `itunes.apple.com` was blocked by this session's egress policy (HTTP 403 at the proxy on every storefront attempted). §0.7 therefore uses this repository's own cached ranking artefact, which is one day newer than the review extraction. Its rating counts are tap-inclusive App Store figures, not downloads.
- **Small samples outside the top four storefronts.** 72 of 76 storefronts have fewer than 50 reviews. All are counted globally; none carries a standalone conclusion without a `[limited evidence]` marker.
- **Language coverage.** Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Russian, Turkish, Thai and Chinese reviews were read in-language. The two Chinese-script and one Thai-script reviews are too few to support any storefront conclusion.
- **Hand-curated small themes are floors, not point estimates.** A review that expresses a theme obliquely can be missed. Regex-driven praise themes carry an estimated ±3–5% relative error; because they are large and one-directional, no label changes.

## 2.2 Monetisation model

**Structure, as reviewers experience it:** free download → **28-day full-Pro trial, no card required** → hard drop to a 1-habit free tier → subscription-only Pro (monthly or annual). No one-time purchase has ever existed in this corpus's window.

Two things are genuinely well designed here and reviewers say so:
- **The trial does not auto-charge.** `10986971491`(US,5★): *"I respect that the app doesn't force you to subscribe to get the free trial and make you immediately cancel the subscription in your settings in order to not get charged if you forget about it. Moral behavior is noticed and appreciated!"*; also `11061933855`(US,5★) `10985371106`(CA,5★).
- **There is almost no upsell nagging.** Only 8 reviews (0.68%) complain about pop-ups or notification pressure — an unusually low rate for a subscription app.

Both of those assets are destroyed by the shape of the drop-off. **A no-pressure trial that ends in an unusable free tier reads to reviewers as a *worse* trick than a nagging one**, because they only discover the wall after three weeks of investment (§0.4).

**Prices reported by reviewers** — see the full timeline in §0.6. Highest reported: **A$200/yr** (`11002040769`), **£215/yr if billed monthly** (`10985301089`), **C$150/yr** (`11113968468`), **CHF 120/yr** (`10990115203`), **¥18,000/yr** (`11846275530`). Lowest reported: **$40/yr** (`13853134874`, Mar 2026) and **$5/mo** (`14287877372`, Jul 2026).

**The free/paid split as a monetisation design:** the paid feature that reviewers most resent losing is not habit slots — it is **the Mindset content**, because reviewers correctly identify it as material they already bought.
> `11010361389`(US,2★): *"You must have pro to get access to the mindset content and daily lesson at a price tag of $120 a year… since I already read the book which only cost me $15 one time why would I pay anywhere close to that monthly to get the same information from an app?"*
> `11097642923`(FR,1★): *"suddenly after 3-4weeks of use with access to all the mindset tips, daily lessons etc. Suddenly no more access at all to any of it!"*
> `12819752757`(US,1★): *"now there's the Pro version that doesn't allow me to read any of my saved articles… Shameful that they're taking away features and driving away loyal supporters."*

**This is the core packaging error stated plainly by the corpus: Atoms paywalls the two things its audience already owns (the ideas, and the willingness to track), and gives away the one thing that costs nothing to give (a second habit slot).**

## 5.2 What made people pay

Only four purchase triggers appear in the corpus, and three of them are weak:

**a) Brand trust, bought sight-unseen.** `10983459334`(US,5★) subscribed on day 3 of using it. `11858415210`(GB,5★) *"felt invested enough to purchase their yearly subscription pretty early on, within weeks of the app's original launch date."* This is the only trigger that produced 5★ reviews.

# PART 7 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 7.1 Method

Four cohorts by review date, cut at the natural breaks in the volume curve: **P1** = launch through 31 Mar 2024 (n=678), **P2** = Apr–Dec 2024 (n=217), **P3** = 2025 (n=190), **P4** = Jan–Sep 2026 (n=83). Review records carry **no app-version field**, so every release attribution is inferred from reviewers' own dated statements. Where a change cannot be dated from the corpus, no attribution is made. P4 has only 83 records and September 2026 is partial (1 review); no conclusion rests on P4 alone unless the direction is consistent across P2→P3→P4.

## 7.2 Trend 1 — The launch bought a rating and then spent it in six weeks

| Period | n | Mean | 5★ | 1★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 launch | 678 | **3.88** | 59.7% | 14.7% |
| P2 rest of 2024 | 217 | **3.00** | 28.6% | **24.9%** |
| P3 2025 | 190 | 3.38 | 41.6% | 20.0% |
| P4 2026 | 83 | 3.42 | 42.2% | 19.3% |

**The mean fell 0.88 stars between the launch window and the nine months that followed, and has never returned to launch level.** The floor since April 2024 is a mean of 3.22 with 22.0% one-star reviews — that, not 3.60 and not 4.81, is the app's written steady state.

The mechanism is visible within the launch window itself: 24 Feb mean 4.49 → 27 Feb mean 3.84, as the price became widely known. `10976398063`(US,5★, 25 Feb) is the launch-day mood; `10979802836`(CA,2★, 25 Feb, 13 votes) is what replaced it 24 hours later.

# PART 8 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation cites the findings it comes from. This corpus supports an unusually clean conclusion: **the product is good and the packaging is the problem.** Nothing below asks for a rebuild.

## 8.1 Immediate — act without further research

**1. Raise the free tier from 1 habit to 3.**
→ §0.2, §0.3, §3.3, §4.4. 76 reviews, mean 2.22, 61.8% 1–2★, 27 months unchanged. The three-habit number is not a guess — it is what reviewers repeatedly propose (`10975505569` `10988610880` `11749997696` `13838905269` `10986199056`). **The constraint's defenders (20 reviews, mean 4.60) are defending a limit of 3–6, not 1** — raising the free tier to 3 keeps the philosophy and removes the objection. This is the single highest-leverage change available and it is a configuration value.

**2. Remove the 6-habit cap from the paid tier, or raise it to 12–15.**
→ §0.3, §5.2. 75 reviews, mean 2.57, 29 months unchanged. **A cap on the tier someone has already paid for has no monetisation function** — it cannot drive an upgrade because there is no higher tier. It only produces reviews like `14287877372`, `13573785597` and `13222746779`. Two of the three payers who bought specifically to escape a cap rated 3★ after hitting the next one.

**3. Fix the freeze-on-first-habit-creation bug.**
→ §0.10, §7.3. 31 reviews, mean 1.90, 74.2% 1–2★, **zero 5★** — the only theme other than cash-grab language with a zero-5★ rate. Ten dated instances between Apr 2025 and Feb 2026 describe the identical screen. **This bug kills users at the moment of first value, before they have anything to lose, which is why it produces no partial credit.**

**4. Give the free tier back to cancelled subscribers, and stop deleting their history.**
→ §0.4, §5.4. `13573892543` is the whole case: a former payer locked out of editing, viewing or completing the one habit the free tier is supposed to allow. **A cancelled subscriber who keeps a read-only history is a candidate for resubscription; one whose data is hostage is a 2★ review and a permanent loss.**

**5. Answer the seven support-failure reviewers and the five billing cases, and publish a support address in the app.**
→ §5.4, §0.5. 7 support reviews (mean 2.14) and 7 billing reviews (mean **1.43** — the lowest of any theme). Two reviewers could not find any contact route and wrote the review *instead* (`10977940087` `10992497041`). The one reviewer who was reached edited their rating upward (`11011057187`). **Refunds refused on auto-renewals the customer did not use (`12988241997` `11990095414` `12165173158`) are producing the corpus's angriest writing and are cheap to reverse.**

## 8.2 Monetisation — repackage, do not reprice again

**6. Ship a one-time / lifetime SKU.**
→ §0.6, §5.3, §3.4. 37 reviews (3.17%, mean 2.16) ask for it explicitly; Streaks is named 8 times and the reason is always the one-time price. **The price has already fallen ~67% with no measurable effect on the objection rate (30.97% → 30.12%), which is the strongest available evidence that the problem is the *shape*, not the number.** Reviewer-stated reservation prices cluster at $10–$30 one-time (§5.3) — that band is testable immediately.

**7. Stop paywalling the Mindset content, or unbundle it.**
→ §2.2. Paywalling the ideas from a book the user has already bought is the packaging decision reviewers find hardest to forgive (`11010361389` `11097642923` `12819752757`), and mindset-content praise has fallen 6.78% → 1.20% since it went behind the wall. **The content is a differentiator that currently generates more resentment than revenue.** Consider: content free, habit slots paid — the exact inverse of today.

**8. Recognise book buyers.**
→ §0.9. 18 reviews (1.54%, mean 2.61). Reviewers propose the mechanism themselves: a code (`11097642923`), a barcode scan (`12513582477`), a discount or a free month (`10983993222`). **The corpus contains no reviewer who bought the book and felt the app respected that; it contains eighteen who felt the opposite.**

**9. Introduce regional pricing.**
→ §5.3, §6.2. 11 reviews, and `11001435276`(PE) does the PPP arithmetic for you. Rest-of-world reviewers ask for this 6.7× more often than high-spend-market reviewers. Adding a student tier is the same fix from a different direction (`13456206729` `10983993222` `10984142146` `11105451365`).

**10. Redesign the trial's ending, not its length.**
→ §0.4, §7.4. The bait-and-switch complaint **tripled** from launch to 2026 and is the fastest-growing negative. The trial itself is well-liked (it does not auto-charge, §2.2). **What breaks is the cliff.** Options the corpus supports: taper (keep 3 habits free forever), warn earlier and in-app rather than by email (`13616010098` `14046766451` never saw it coming), or preserve the streak read-only after expiry.

## 8.3 Product — converts 3★/4★ into 5★

**11. Ship back-logging.** 33 reviews, mean 3.39, only 21.2% at 1–2★ — a request from people who mostly still like the app, in the two bands where ratings are cheapest to move (9.8% of all 4★, 7.4% of all 3★). Reviewers also want to *import* an existing streak (`12014435210` `12259757598` `11128803895`).

**12. Ship dark mode.** 21 reviews, mean 3.76, fastest-growing feature request, framed as accessibility by two reviewers. One setting.

**13. Make logging work offline.** 9 reviews, mean 2.56, zero 5★. A habit tracker that cannot tick a box on a plane, on a hike, or on patchy service is failing at its only job (`10977084069` `11339178421` `13090001429`).

**14. Loosen the habit model.** 39 reviews (3.34%), and this is the **third-fastest-growing negative (2.51% → 7.23%)**. Specifically: allow "X times a week, any day" (`11569901162` `14201996700` `11018442332`), allow habits with no fixed time (`11007001740` `14237890130`), allow multiple logs per day (`11015467978` `11087707385` `11383246654` `13517748845`), support habit *stacking* — which the book is famous for and the app does not implement (`11490817427` `11402101270` `11764648914`), and let people edit the identity phrasing (`12228073166` `14237890130`).

**15. Restore the satisfying completion loop.** Haptic-loop praise fell from 4.87% to 1.20% after the list redesign, and four reviewers name the change directly (`11343906321` `11814577640` `11390526677` `11368755180`). **This was the app's most-praised piece of craft; it should not be a casualty of density.** Offer the circle view as an option.

## 8.4 Positioning — free rating, no engineering

**16. Localise, starting with Spanish.** 36 reviews (3.08%); 14 of them Spanish; 7.76% of all tail-storefront reviews. The book exists in ~60 languages. **This is the widest brand-reach-to-product-reach gap in the corpus and the cheapest new market available.**

**17. Say what the free tier is, in the listing, before download.** Eight reviewers state they had no idea the app was paid: `11562525291` `12250461429` `12482534390` `12591641304` `12959086974` `13616010098` `13807288552` `14046766451`. **A clear "1 free habit, Pro for more" line on the listing converts a 1★ ambush into a 3★ informed decision — and would convert far better if paired with recommendation #1.**

## 8.5 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

- **What is the actual conversion rate, and did the two price cuts move it?** The corpus contains 17 declared buyers and no conversion data. Nothing here should be read as a conversion estimate.
- **Do the 1★ price reviewers ever become buyers at a lower price?** Only one reviewer in 1,168 is observed changing their mind after a price cut (`11980741663`).
- **How much of the 4.81 tap-rating is trial-period sentiment?** §5.5 suggests it is material, but the corpus cannot size it. An in-app rating prompt fired before vs. after day 28 would answer it directly.
- **Is the freeze bug device-, OS- or storefront-specific?** No device or version field exists. Ten reproductions exist with dates and storefronts — that is enough to start, not enough to diagnose.
- **Would a 3-habit free tier cannibalise Pro?** The corpus shows the current 1-habit tier converts almost nobody and costs 76 negative reviews. It cannot tell you what 3 would do.
- **Is the review-solicitation campaign still running?** The launch spike and `10977084069`'s allegation suggest a deliberate push in Feb 2024. Whether the 2025–26 5★ flow is organic is unknowable from this data.

# PART 9 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 9.1 Counting rules

- A review belongs to **all** themes it expresses. Counts are non-exclusive and do not sum to 1,168.
- **Global percentages always use n=1,168.** Rating-band percentages use that band's n (5★=581, 4★=102, 3★=135, 2★=142, 1★=208). Country percentages use that country's n. Period percentages use that period's n. Segment rates are labelled and carry both denominators.
- "Mean" is the arithmetic mean star rating of the reviews carrying that theme.
- A theme's **direction is inferred from context, not from the star rating.** A 5★ review objecting to the price is counted in `price_objection`; a 1★ review complimenting the design is counted in `praise_design_ui`.
- `paid_explicit` requires a first-person statement of purchase **of Atoms**. Book, journal and competitor-app purchases are excluded; intent is excluded; 3 ambiguous cases are held separately and excluded from all paid statistics.
- `price_objection` requires a price/subscription/currency token within 110 characters of a negative token, **or** an explicit phrase match, **and** was hand-audited in both directions.
- Signal labels are applied to the **global** share (n=1,168) and, separately, to each eligible country's own share.
