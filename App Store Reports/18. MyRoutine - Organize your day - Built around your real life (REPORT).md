# MyRoutine (App Store ID 1518956326) — Full Review Analysis

**Target folder:** `App Store Reviews/18. MyRoutine - Organize your day - Built around your real life`
**Product:** MyRoutine / 마이루틴 — "Organize your day · Built around your real life"
**Developer:** Minding. co., Ltd. · **Bundle ID:** `com.minding.myroutine`
**Category:** Productivity — routine / habit tracking with to-dos, diary, timer and a light social layer
**Corpus:** **2,048 written reviews**, **29 storefronts**, **6 Jul 2020 → 6 Sep 2026** (75 calendar months)
**Written-review mean rating:** **3.824** · **Store listing (KR, accessed 10 Sep 2026): 4.8★ from ~25,000 ratings; US: 4.8★ from ~3.3K ratings; version 34.28**
**Decision this informs:** how to price and package a habit app that people genuinely love, when five years of monetisation changes have converted its most loyal users into its angriest reviewers.

### Four things to know before using any number here

**1. This is overwhelmingly a Korean corpus.** 1,599 of 2,048 reviews (78.08%) are from the Korean storefront. Japan is 223 (10.89%), the US 84 (4.10%). Only these three storefronts clear the 50-review bar for standalone analysis. Every "global" figure is therefore, in practice, a Korea-weighted figure, and is labelled as such where it matters.

**2. Storefront ≠ language.** 13 of the 84 US reviews are written in Korean, as are reviews in the CA, AU, NZ, IE and FR storefronts, and one JP review is in Korean and one in Chinese. The corpus contains Korean, Japanese, English, Traditional Chinese, Cantonese, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Thai, French, German, Dutch, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Russian and Polish. All of it was read; none was discarded for language.

**3. The public rating is ~1 star higher than what people write.** The KR listing shows **4.8★ from ~25,000 ratings** (accessed 10 Sep 2026) against a **written-review mean of 3.824** — a gap of **0.98 stars**, and the KR-only written mean (3.949) still leaves a 0.85-star gap. Tap-through star ratings and written reviews are measuring different populations. **Nothing in this report should be read as a statement about the average user; it is a statement about the population motivated enough to type.**

**4. Counts come from a keyword classifier that was manually audited; they are floors, not measurements.** Method, precision audit and known error rates are in §1.4 and §9.3. Where a finding rests on close reading rather than the classifier, it says so and gives IDs.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

## 0.1 The product is loved. The business model is what people write about.

Two theme families dominate, and they point in opposite directions.

| Family | n | % of 2,048 | Mean ★ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Praise: design / cuteness / intuitiveness** | 164 | 8.01% | 4.46 |
| **Praise: habit formed, life changed** | 131 | 6.40% | 4.54 |
| **Free-tier restriction blocks core use** | 139 | 6.79% | **2.32** |
| **Trial / auto-billing / refund dispute** | 144 | 7.03% | **2.90** |
| **Data loss / records reset** | 123 | 6.01% | 3.27 |
| **Crash / won't launch** | 111 | 5.42% | 3.15 |

All six are **high-priority signals** (>5%). The app has a genuinely differentiated product and a monetisation and reliability problem of equal size. Reviews praising the product and reviews condemning the paywall are frequently *the same reviews*.

## 0.2 The single most consequential mechanic in the corpus: the free tier blocks the check-off button

The paywall does not gate advanced features. It gates **marking a habit done** — the one action the entire product exists to perform.

**139 reviews (6.79%, high-priority, mean 2.32★)** report free-tier restrictions blocking core use. Within them, the recurring, literal complaint is that tapping "achieve/complete" produces a purchase sheet instead of a check mark:

- `13977781381` (kr, 5★, Apr 2026): *"무료버전은 완료했다는 체크 자체가 안되네요… 그럼 루틴 어플로서의 기능이 없는 건데"* — "the free version can't even check things off… then it isn't a routine app at all." Rated **5★ deliberately**: *"많은 분들이 보시라고 5점 드립니다"* ("I'm giving 5 stars so more people see this").
- `13666442337` (kr, 1★, Jan 2026): *"무료는 습관 달성을 할 수 없습니다. 습관을 써놓을 수는 있어요. 근데 달성이 안됩니다. 그냥 써놓고 알람만 오는겁니다."* — "You can write habits down. You just can't complete them. You write them and get alarms."
- `14517754076` (kr, 1★, 6 Sep 2026 — the final Korean review in the corpus): habits 1–6 check fine, the 7th throws a purchase sheet. *"구매할 의향 90%는 있었고… 걍 정뚝떨어져서 삭제함"* — "I was 90% ready to buy… I just lost all feeling for it and deleted it."
- `12576858445` (jp, 2★), `13534993454` (jp, 1★, *"悪徳なやり方"* — "an unscrupulous method"), `14484116611` (jp, 1★), `12495446710` (us, 3★: *"I don't think you should say that you're a routine app and not allow people to create a routine"*), `13894901307` (au, 1★: *"Predatory dark pattern"*), `14393820099` (hk, 1★), `14261275910`, `14468573022`, `13868908368`, `13894400612`, `14065497753`, `14047366673`, `14068889624`, `12036038200`.

**One Hong Kong reviewer supplies the mechanic nobody else spells out.** `14393820099` (hk, 1★, Aug 2026): the free tier advertises 10 habits, but **ticking a habit consumes the short-memo quota, which is capped at 14 per week — i.e. two check-offs a day.** *"原本以為set習慣限10個夠晒用啦… 點知原來tick完成係計落短備忘錄，一個禮拜限14次，啫係一日最多tick兩次"*. If accurate, this single quota explains the entire five-year pattern of users reporting the app "stops working on day 3 / day 5 / day 6 / day 10": the advertised limit is the number of habits, but the binding limit is the number of completions.

**This is the highest-leverage finding in the corpus.** A habit tracker whose free tier meters *completions* teaches new users, in their first week, that the product does not work — precisely during the window when a habit-formation product must prove it does.

The store listing (accessed 10 Sep 2026) shows an unusually wide ladder of concurrently-live price points:

| Pro monthly | ₩3,500 / ₩3,900 | $4.99 / $5.99 |

Five simultaneous annual prices in the US and four in Korea. **144 reviews (7.03%, high-priority, mean 2.90★)** are trial/billing/refund disputes, and a distinct, repeating pattern inside them is *"I paid, and then the app immediately offered me a cheaper price"*:

- `14146289685` (kr, 1★): charged both ₩33,000 and ₩25,000, wants both back.
- `13829350794` (kr, 5★): charged ₩33,000 and ₩25,000; only the ₩25,000 is refundable.
- `11881028381` (kr, 1★): "40% off" advertised at ₩28,000, charged ₩33,000; re-subscribed at ₩28,000 and was charged again — ₩61,000 total.
- `13420003023` (kr, 3★): chose ₩33,000, charged ₩45,000. `13734105920` (kr, 1★): banner said ₩28,000, charged ₩33,000.
- `12417115787`, `13773500213`, `14160907779`, `14215712231`, `14291964350`, `12109687708` (resolved by support).
- Taiwan reproduces it exactly: `13994502099` (*"優惠價與實際刷卡消費金額差了兩倍"* — "the promo price and the actual card charge differed by 2×"), `14344435230`, `14286926161`, `14488115233` (jp, ¥2,900 checkout → ¥4,150 charged).

**Users do not read this as a pricing experiment. They read it as fraud.** The words used are 詐欺 / 詐騙 / scam / fraudulent / Abzocke / 사기 / 피싱앱 수준. In Taiwan — the lowest-rated storefront in the corpus at **2.60 mean** — billing is essentially the whole story.

## 0.4 Money is taken from people who cannot use the product, and given to people who then can't access it

Two mirror-image failures, both high-cost:

**(a) Charged before they could evaluate.** The trial converts, or appears to convert, immediately: `10402774146` (*"사기치는것도 아니고"*), `10504712330` (*"기망 아닌가요"* — "isn't this deception?"), `10656594581`, `10665782696` (asked for monthly, charged annual), `10808234292` (jp), `13735625152` (jp: *"私の場合は無料期間はありませんでした"* — "in my case there was no free period"), `13997904350` (jp), `14196174434` (tw), `12806633553` (id — app frozen since minute 10, still charged a full year), `11785990316` (br), `13751955979` (de).

**(b) Paid and then locked out.** **25 reviews (1.22%, meaningful, mean 2.44★)** are entitlement failures — the purchase succeeded and the app still demands payment: `14201976473`, `14183433420`, `14171622208`, `14442984779`, `14486310873`, `14133850956`, `11032526889` (₩33,000 annual, still capped at 8 routines), `10682135962`, `9962008254`, `11902546307` + `12068983875` + `11854385750` (lifetime buyers denied Pro), `12777592140` (jp, ¥8,890 lifetime paid via PayPay, plan never applied), `14247429732` (jp, subscription active in iOS settings, premium unusable), `14031409160` (tw — held a **lifetime** plan, accidentally bought an annual, cancelled the annual, and **the lifetime entitlement was deleted too**).

**The worst single case in the corpus is `14238803827` / `14248568509`** (jp, 1★, Jun–Jul 2026, posted twice). A lifetime buyer (¥6,890) lost power mid-session; on restart the app reset to onboarding, **deleted every routine and all five routine modes**, then demanded a Pro subscription. Support was e-mailed with purchase screenshots and did not reply for over a week, including to a follow-up asking merely whether the case was open. The review ends: *"大好きだったこのアプリを大嫌いなアプリにしないでください"* — "Please don't turn the app I loved into the app I hate." It is the clearest statement in the corpus of how this product loses its best customers.

## 0.6 September 2024: the app removed the exact thing that made people pay for it

**32 reviews (1.56%, meaningful) between 6 Sep 2024 and 29 Jan 2025 protest one redesign**, and their mean rating is **4.25★** — this is the *loyal, paying* base, not churned free users.

Before: routines and one-off to-dos appeared **interleaved in a single, reorderable, time-ordered list**. After: two separate tabs reachable by swipe.

- `11700463203` (kr, 5★) gives the definitive statement: interleaving *expressed* when a to-do had to happen ("if it sits between the morning and evening routines, I know I must finish it before evening"), and it expressed priority for free. Separated, *"메모장에 할일쓰는 거랑 크게 다르지 않은 것 같아요"* — "it's barely different from writing tasks in the Notes app," and it forfeits MyRoutine's one advantage without matching dedicated to-do apps on their own ground.
- `11727453236` (kr, 5★): *"루틴과 투두를 함께 볼 수 있단 점이 큰 메리트로 느껴져 바로 1년 구독 했는데.. 며칠 쓰지도 않았는데 업데이트로 이 둘이 분리됐네요"* — subscribed for a year *because of* the merged view; it was removed days later.
- `11714930552` (kr, 5★): paid, and the split shipped **the next day**.
- `11729996278` (kr, 3★): 22-day daily streak, 1-year subscription, now *"잘 안들어가게됩니다"* — has stopped opening the app.
- `11702770360` (kr, 5★, paying >1 year), `11702631845`, `11705286327`, `11714427576`, `11723701426`, `11736479818`, `11796061345`, `11840024288`, `11858372385`, `11861613648`, `11917633623`, `12122589601` — and in English/Japanese: `11703079091` (us, 1★, ADHD: *"The best features are gone now and I'm going back to using the notes app 😭"*), `11701377230` (jp).
- Still being asked for **19 months later**: `13922137845` (Apr 2026, paying), `14128340502` (May 2026, paid then regretted).

Two collateral removals in the same release:
- **Weekly view** — used to verify "3× per week" habits and to compare trend on numeric habits: `11696910432`, `11700553046`, `11710196595`, `11712906881`, `11756484782`, `11834968287`, `11932729042`, and in Singapore `11707812003`.

**Nobody asked for the split, and the corpus contains only one clear defender (`11703838844`) plus a handful who came to prefer it (`12118393284`, `12287914815`, `12118115943`).**

## 0.7 The onboarding is a conversion leak with an identifiable shape

**34 reviews (1.66%, meaningful) — but with a mean of 2.03★, the lowest of any theme in this report.** These reviewers never reached the product.

The complaints are specific and consistent:
1. **The survey is long and feels like a funnel, not personalisation.** `14031934887` (us, 2★): *"makes you fill out a survey then wants you to pay for a subscription BEFORE YOU EVEN SEE THE APP"*. `13848157788` (ca, 1★): *"I might've paid for this, if I could have seen literally anything past the questionnaire."* `13218586893` (ca), `13729043534` (ca), `11481732875` (tw), `14407318888` (tw), `14437815273` (gb, **a Pro subscriber forced through the questionnaire again**), `12445646540` (au), `13390879707` (jp), `12762108716` (jp).
2. **It plans *for* you.** `12098514129` (kr, 1★): *"내가 주도해서 플랜을 짜는게 아니라 지네들이 짜주는거임"* — you don't lead the plan, they do; titled *"당신을 매우 평범한 기계로 만들어주려는 앱"* ("an app that wants to turn you into a very ordinary machine"). `13515738048`, `12495446710` (us: can't save your own routine), `10785117382` (us: *"They won't let me right my own routine"*).
3. **It excludes anyone without a 9-to-5 weekday life.** `12445646540` (au, 1★) is the sharpest positioning critique in the entire corpus: *"It kept asking me irrelevant questions… I do shift work and that's not an option. 5. **No skip button, and it re-runs on existing accounts.** `14232098480`, `12802740109`, `13953352894` (restore-purchase loop returns you to onboarding), `12265439660` (hk: *"Don't treat me like an idiot"*), `14437815273` (gb).

## 0.8 What people actually love — and it is not the tracker

The praise clusters are specific and repeatable, and they are what the roadmap should protect:

1. **The traffic light (신호등) with a user-set completion threshold.** You get a green day at ~60–80% completion, not 100%. **92 reviews (4.49%, very strong, mean 4.24★)** discuss the light/streak/badge system. The mechanism praise is unusually articulate: `9744682977` (jp, ADHD/ASD): *"達成度がパーセンテージで出るアプリだと100をめざして苦しくなってしまいますが、これは青信号になってれば良いやといい意味で切り替えられます"*. `11978551289`: *"루틴에 너무 몰입하면 강박이 되기도 해서… 60% 기준으로 체크하고 초록불을 켜주니까 성취감은 100%처럼 느껴요."* `12492881974` (au): *"I never feel guilted or pressured like some other habit tracking apps."* `13268786498` (jp), `9514694714`, `6529483781`.
2. **Rest / postpone / skip instead of pass-fail.** `10367219461`: *"무조건 yes or no가 아닌, 하루 미루기와 쉬어가기 같은 선택지가 있는 것도 너무 좋아요."* `6859591028`: rest framing means *"루틴을 달성하지 못했을때의 박탈감도 적어요."*
3. **The routine timer** (shipped ~Nov 2024) — **46 reviews (2.25%, meaningful, mean 4.37★)**, and it converts: `12353891566` sets *every* routine to 1 minute purely to defeat activation energy — *"그랬더니 제가 시작을 하더라구요???"*. `11993264527` (us) values the 30-second voice warning. `14045116089` (jp), `13503736524`, `12822094053`, `12815650430`.
4. **A light social layer that is explicitly not a social network.** **66 reviews (3.22%, very strong, mean 4.45★)**. `7835571277`: *"다른 루티너들 보면서 동기부여를 하는데, 또 SNS 같지는 않아서 좋아요."* `10598930656` (a teacher) chose it over rivals *because* the social features are lighter. `10492723138` describes following a friend as *"교환일기"* (a shared exchange diary).
5. **ADHD / executive-function fit.** **36 reviews (1.76%, meaningful, mean 4.53★)** self-identify with ADHD, ASD, depression, burnout or 무기력. This is now in the KR store title itself ("ADHD 하루 계획표 앱"). `11067291310` (us), `11993264527` (us), `12428102869` (jp), `11888055232` (jp), `12121119977` (ca), `13842532454` (kr — see §0.9).
6. **Routine + to-do in one time-ordered list** — the thing removed in Sept 2024 (§0.6).

## 0.9 The motivation system needs to be dial-able in both directions

This is the subtlest and most actionable product finding, and the corpus states it from both ends.

**Too soft — the "shield" (방패) invalidates the streak.** Introduced ~Aug 2025, it preserves a streak through a missed day. Users experience that as a lie:
- `13260473006`: *"연속 일수에 표시되는 숫자가 가짜로 만들어진 것 같아서 오히려 동기가 떨어지는 것 같습니다… 오늘쯤은 안 해도 숫자가 사라지지 않으니까 괜찮겠지 하면서 안일해지는 것 같아요."*
- `13104946209`: *"안한날에 자동으로 방패 돼서 못한날에 초록색되는게 무슨의미가 있나싶어요… 주객전도되는 느낌."*
- `13765930951`, `13056561637` (asks for an on/off toggle), `13769975479` (jp, annual payer: the green-light cut-off rules changed silently), `14415129984` (tw).

**Too hard — the streak is a quitting trigger.** `13842532454` (kr, ADHD) states the design principle outright: *"streak(연속 달성) 기능도 누군가에게는 동기부여가 되겠지만, 누군가에게는 streak이 깨지면 루틴을 때려치는 기능일 겁니다. 아마 ADHD 진단을 받은 사람들은 후자일 가능성이 높을거라고 생각해요. 이러한 UI도 숨길 수 있으면 좋겠습니다."* Same request: `12424534015` (wants the light off entirely — *"하루가 마무리 될 쯤에야 루틴 완수가 가능한데, 그 전까지 계속 붉은 상태로 지내야 해서 마음이 불편해요"*), `12618795582`, `12719246363`, `11116355780`.

# PART 1 — SCOPE AND METHOD

## 1.1 Files used

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `reviews.jsonl` | Primary corpus — 2,048 records, one JSON object per line |
| `by_country/*.jsonl` | 29 per-storefront files — used only for reconciliation |
| `manifest.json` | Extraction metadata (app id, developer, per-country totals, rating distribution) |
| `_state.json` | Extractor cursor state; confirms every storefront was crawled to completion (`complete: true`) |
| Apple App Store listing, KR and US (`apps.apple.com/{kr,us}/app/id1518956326`) | External check on price ladder, version, public rating, language list — **accessed 10 Sep 2026** |

## 1.2 Schema

Every record has 13 fields, all populated on all 2,048 rows:

`review_id`, `app_id`, `app_name`, `country`, `country_name`, `rating` (1–5), `title`, `body`, `author`, `date` (ISO-8601 UTC), `vote_count`, `vote_sum`, `is_edited`.

Fields used for analysis: `review_id`, `country`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `date`, `is_edited`.
**There is no app-version field**, so no finding in this report is attributed to a specific build number. Release events are inferred from dated review clusters and are labelled as inferences.

## 1.3 Coverage and reconciliation

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Lines in `reviews.jsonl` | **2,048** |
| Unique `review_id` | **2,048** — zero duplicates, so **no deduplication rule was applied or needed** |
| Sum of `by_country/*.jsonl` ids | **2,048** |
| Per-country ID sets, main file vs `by_country` | **Identical for all 29 storefronts — zero mismatches** |
| `manifest.json` `total_reviews` | **2,048** ✓ |
| `manifest.json` `reviews_per_country` | matches computed counts for all 29 ✓ |
| `manifest.json` `rating_distribution` (5:1069, 4:322, 3:216, 2:109, 1:332) | matches computed ✓ |
| `manifest.json` `mean_rating` 3.824 | matches computed 3.8237 ✓ |
| Records with empty `body` | **0** |
| Date range | 2020-07-06 → 2026-09-06 |

**Every one of the 2,048 records was read in full**, in 21 sequential batches of 100, in the original language, with per-batch notes retained. No sampling, no keyword pre-filter, no language exclusion, no length exclusion. Reading notes are the source of §0, the event timeline, and every quoted excerpt; the classifier is the source of the global/country/era rates.

## 1.4 Processing method

1. **Parse & reconcile** — programmatic load, ID/count/country/rating reconciliation against `manifest.json` and `by_country/`.
2. **Full read** — all 2,048 records read in date-then-storefront order (KR → JP → US → TW → GB → AU → CA → rest), 100 per batch. Per-batch structured notes captured feature inventory, event windows, verbatim quotes and candidate IDs.
3. **Taxonomy from reading** — 34 themes defined from what reviewers actually wrote, not from a pre-set list.
4. **Multilingual regex classifier** — each theme is a set of Korean, Japanese, English and Chinese patterns matched case-insensitively against `title + body`. Themes are non-exclusive.
5. **Precision audit** — random samples drawn from each theme's matches and judged by hand:
   - `paywall_v3` (free-tier restriction): 45 sampled, **36 true positives → precision ≈ 80%**.
   - `trial_billing_dispute`: 12 sampled, 10 true → ≈83%.
   - `data_loss`: 12 sampled, 10 true → ≈83%.
   - `crash`: 12 sampled, 11 true → ≈92%.
   - `entitlement_failure`: 12 sampled, 10 true → ≈83%.
   - `paid_confirmed`: 12 sampled, 11 true → ≈92%.
   - Two themes were **rejected and rebuilt** after audit: `shift_work` (a bare "모드" pattern was matching 다크모드/가로모드/트래킹 모드) and `onboarding_friction` (60% precision on v1 → 1.66% v2 at higher precision). One theme, `notifications`, was split into a precise "notification problem" subset because the original bucket mixed praise and complaint.
6. **Recall check** — for the headline paywall theme, all 136 unmatched reviews containing 무료/無料/free/免費 were sampled; several were genuine paywall complaints. **The paywall count of 139 is therefore a floor.**
7. **Aggregate** — global, per-storefront (own denominator), per-era, per-star-band, and per-payer-cohort tabulations.

## 1.5 Limitations and judgement calls

- **Selection bias is severe and asymmetric.** The listing shows 4.8★ from ~25,000 KR ratings; the written mean is 3.824. Written reviews over-represent people with a grievance and people in a moment of enthusiasm; they under-represent steady, satisfied users. All rates are rates *within the reviewing population*.
- **Korea dominance.** 78.08% of the corpus is one storefront. Global rates ≈ Korean rates.
- **Counts are floors with ~20% over-count risk.** Classifier precision on the headline theme is ~80% and recall is incomplete. Where the direction of the error matters, it is stated.
- **No version field.** Every "an update did X" statement is an inference from dated review clusters, not from release metadata. The three best-evidenced (Oct 2022 ads, Nov 2023–Jan 2024 paywall tightening, Sept 2024 split) each rest on 10+ independent same-window reviews and are labelled high-confidence; the rest are labelled as inferences.
- **Developer replies are not in the corpus.** 90 reviews (4.39%) are flagged `is_edited`, and several visibly edit *in response to* a developer reply (`8519301616`, `12023009972`, `13039376354`, `11103012143`, `14374239484`, `12109687708`, `14479635630`). Support quality can therefore only be assessed from the user side.
- **Possible seeded / marketing-style reviews.** A cluster of native-English 4–5★ reviews with near-identical "serial app-hopper who finally stuck with one" phrasing appears in a tight window: `12757322029`, `12767129702`, `12778751472`, `12831425768` (gb, 10–29 Jun 2025) and `12756530951`, `12790218793` (au, 10 & 18 Jun 2025). One Japanese reviewer independently alleges fake reviews (`13997904350`: *"レビューにサクラがかなりいます"*), and one German reviewer alleges fake lifetime promo codes (`12543396557`). **Treat these six as limited-evidence and excluded from qualitative conclusions.** They remain in all counts.
- **Aggregate ratings are not feature preferences.** A 5★ review containing a bug report is counted in both the praise and the bug theme; star level is never used as a proxy for liking a feature.
- **No causal claims.** Rating movements around inferred release events are correlations. The corpus cannot establish conversion, retention or revenue effects.

# PART 6 — WHY PEOPLE STAY: THE RETENTION MECHANISM

Separately worth naming, because it is the asset the monetisation is spending down.

**131 reviews (6.40%, high-priority, mean 4.54★)** describe a behaviour change. The mechanism they describe is consistent and unusual:

1. **Partial credit removes the failure state.** You don't need 100%; you need your threshold. (§0.8, §0.9)

Tenure evidence: 3–4 year users (`12780156991`, `13356295904`, `14401843895` jp, `14345482795`), 500+ day streaks (`13399863062`), 1,000 routine completions (`11340256601` jp), 199 days (`14108167464`), 700 days (`13769975479` jp). ### Korea (n=1,599, mean 3.95) — the home market and the whole business

Korea is the only storefront where the **social layer** is a very strong signal (3.69%) and where the **merged routine+to-do view** matters at scale. It is also where the paywall history is lived in full: the 2022 ads, the 2023 to-do removal, the 2024 cap cut, the friend-invite revocation, the 2024 split, the 2026 billing failures. Korean reviews are the longest and most structured in the corpus — several read as unpaid product consultancy (`10444375042`, `10851299280`, `11700463203`, `14184795326`, `13042903108`).
KR-specific mechanics: **KakaoTalk login** breaks repeatedly on PC/Mac (`8221813827`, `8472416777`, `8572620032`, `10816022292`), KakaoTalk is the only support channel and is closed at weekends (`10574355802`), and reviewers ask for KakaoPay and Kakao gifting (`11697109244`, `13244453495`).

### Japan (n=223, mean 3.37 — 0.58 below Korea)

Japan's deficit has three distinct causes, all fixable:

1. **Localisation (3.14%, very strong; 0.31% in KR).** Japanese-language Instagram ads led to an app that opened in **Korean**, with a Korean KakaoTalk login: `8991795108`, `9042880275` (uninstalled), `9000746331` (*"日本語が変"*), `9041592638` (half-Korean UI). 2. **Entitlement failures are catastrophic here — 5 reviews, every one 1★ (mean 1.00).** `14238803827`, `14248568509`, `14247429732`, `14179422656`, `12777592140`.
3. **The support channel is broken.** The in-app feedback form's e-mail field rejects all valid addresses and its text is invisible in dark mode (`13757721182`, `13576075338`, `13712167027`).

Japan's strengths are also distinct: **timer** (6.28%, high-priority vs 1.31% KR) and **ADHD/neurodivergent fit** (3.14%). The single best-argued review in the corpus on why coarse progress beats percentages is Japanese (`9744682977`). Japanese reviewers, uniquely, ask for **auto-advancing timers** (`14328062792`, `14045116089`, `13435011015`, `12428102869`) and **Apple Watch timer parity** (`13431240197`, `13435011015`).

# PART 9 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation traces to numbered findings above. Ordered by expected impact per unit of effort.

## 9.1 Immediate — act without further research

**I1. Stop metering completions. (→ §0.2, §4.1, §5.3)**
Cap habits if you must; never cap check-offs. A habit tracker that refuses to record a completion on day 3–6 is demonstrating that it does not work, to a user who is currently deciding whether it works. This single mechanic sits under the largest negative theme (139 reviews, 6.79%, mean 2.32★), 19.6% of all 1★ reviews, and the corpus's most explicit lost sale (`14517754076`: *"구매할 의향 90%는 있었고… 정뚝떨어져서 삭제함"*).

**I2. Show one price per plan per storefront. (→ §0.3, §7.2)**
Five concurrent annual prices in the US and four in Korea are producing charges that don't match the banner, double purchases, and public accusations of fraud in six languages. Never surface a discount to someone who has just paid; if a cheaper offer exists, apply it. Make the refund path reachable in-app.

**I3. Fix entitlement propagation, and instrument it. (→ §0.4, §7.1)**
25 reviews (1.22%, mean 2.44★) — every Japanese instance is 1★. A lifetime purchase that stops working is not a bug, it is the end of the customer relationship (`14238803827`). Add a purchase-state self-check on launch and an in-app "restore" that actually restores without dumping the user into onboarding (`13953352894`).

**I4. Never re-run onboarding on an existing or paying account, and add a skip button. (→ §0.7)**
Onboarding is the lowest-rated theme in the report (mean 2.03★). A Pro subscriber forced back through the questionnaire (`14437815273`) and a lifetime buyer whose data was replaced by two survey-chosen routines (`14238803827`) are the same defect.

**I5. Fix the Japanese feedback form and the Taiwanese price/refund flow. (→ §5.4, §7.2)**
Japan cannot report bugs (`13757721182`, `13576075338`, `13712167027`). Taiwan is at 2.60★ almost entirely because of billing. Both are days of work in top-tier spend markets.

**I6. Label paid features before use, and never destroy user work at the paywall. (→ §2.2, §5.3)**
`12433476315` and `12536725596` each spent an hour building routines, paid to save them, and lost the work anyway. `12350930181` and `13894400612` were paywalled on features documented as free.

## 9.2 Monetisation — repackage, stop confiscating

**M1. Move to an "unlimited free core, paid depth" model. (→ §0.5, §5.4)**
Five years of reviews make the same argument in three languages: monetise **new** capability, not **existing** capability. The corpus already names what people will pay for: **export**, **statistics with trend**, **routine modes**, **timer with time tracking**, **Apple Watch parity**, **themes**, **web/Mac** (§5.5). Every one is additive.

**M2. Ship a genuine, permanent free tier and say so on the listing. (→ §5.3)**
The reviews that convert best are from people who used the app free for a week or a month first (`8363704388`, `8961449067`, `10995640730`). The reviews that generate refund demands are from people charged before they could evaluate. The current arrangement optimises for the second.

**M3. Add a student/child tier or a family option. (→ §5.3)**
Self-identified students, teens and children appear repeatedly and sympathetically, including a teacher who used it with a whole class until the to-do list went paid (`10598930656`). The store rates the app "all ages" while the onboarding age bracket starts at "18 and under" (`12857404582`).

**M4. Stop marketing to people who have already paid. (→ §5.4)**
`13492813619` states the rule. Remove the promo bar, the Dynamic Island countdown, and the annual-upsell nag for anyone with an active plan.

**M5. Keep the lifetime tier and protect it absolutely. (→ §5.1)**
34 lifetime buyers, mean 4.12★ — the most committed segment in the corpus. Two of the corpus's most damaging reviews are lifetime buyers who lost their entitlement (`14238803827`, `14031409160`).

## 9.3 Product — what converts 3★ and 4★ into 5★

**P1. Restore an optional merged routine + to-do view. (→ §0.6)**
32 reviews from the paying base, mean 4.25★, and it was the named purchase trigger for at least four subscribers. Ship it as a toggle, not a replacement — a minority genuinely prefers the split (`12118393284`, `12287914815`).

**P2. Make reordering direct, and allow editing any date. (→ §3.1, §8.6)**
130 reviews (6.35%), five years, mean 4.07★ — this is loyal-user friction, and it is the most-repeated single request in the corpus. Long-press to drag; remove the "you cannot edit future dates" restriction introduced ~Jun 2023.

**P3. Build statistics that answer "am I getting better?". (→ §3.3, §5.5)**
57 reviews. Per-habit achievement %, trend over weeks/months, annual view, and a calendar of green/yellow/red. `10444375042` and `10851299280` specify the requirement precisely.

**P4. Ship export. (→ §3.3, §5.5)**
18 reviews, mean 4.44★, asked continuously since the app's fifth month (`6164039764`), and named as a purchase condition twice. Export is also the honest answer to five years of data-loss reports: it lets users protect themselves.

**P5. Aggregate the diary. (→ §3.3)**
22 reviews, **mean 4.68★ — the highest-rated request theme in the corpus.** People write daily reflections for years and cannot read them back except one date at a time.

**P6. Make the motivation system configurable in both directions. (→ §0.9)**
Per-user toggles for: streak display, the shield, the traffic light itself, cheer-message frequency. Default the shield to **off**.

**P7. Finish routine modes. (→ §8.7, §7.1)**
Right feature, wrong execution: switching a mode rewrites past days' lights (`13685716991`, `13812236928`), modes apply forward-only rather than per-date (`13795524849`), edits inside a mode don't persist (`14108692347`), and it is buried in Settings (`13556531783`).

**P8. Apple Watch and widget parity with the timer. (→ §7.1, §8.4)**
Japan and the US both rate the timer as a high-priority strength and both ask for it on the watch and in the widget (`13431240197`, `13435011015`, `12736883056`, `14127953337`).

**P9. Let users hide what they don't use. (→ §0.7, §3.3)**
Complexity is a high-priority signal in all three eligible storefronts (KR 6.75%, JP 10.76%, US 8.33%) and rising as features accumulate. `13842532454` (ADHD) asks to hide social, recommendations, to-do, diary and streaks. `11064706172` asks them, explicitly, *not* to add more.

## 9.4 Positioning — free rating, no engineering

**Q1. The app is named MyRoutine and the onboarding builds *its* routine.** `12445646540` (au) makes the point better than any brief could. Let people write their own habit first and answer questions later, or never.

**Q2. Serve irregular schedules in onboarding, not just in Pro.** Shift workers, night workers, parents and students are a repeating, articulate, *paying* segment (`12245337065`, `12784378291`, `12132150109`, `12580123414`, `13795524849`, `12478377114`). The questions currently assume a weekday 9-to-5.

**Q3. The ADHD positioning is real — earn it.** Highest-rated theme cluster, now in the store title. Deliver on it: configurable streaks (P6), hideable UI (P9), no punitive paywall on completion (I1).

## 9.5 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

1. **Is the completions quota (14/week) real, current and global?** One Hong Kong review (`14393820099`) supplies the mechanism; several dozen reviews supply the symptom. Verify in code and in the store metadata.
2. **Why do some US and Canadian users describe the app as "completely free"** in the same months Korean and Japanese users are blocked (`11432555244`, `12230682122`, `13866825904`)? Regional config, cohort assignment, or quota not yet reached?
3. **What is the actual conversion effect of each paywall tightening?** The corpus shows the sentiment cost precisely and the revenue benefit not at all.
4. **What proportion of the 4.8★ / 25,000 tap-through ratings comes from users who have hit the completion wall?** The 0.98-star gap between tapped and written ratings is the most important unexplained number here.
5. **Is the GB/AU 5★ cluster of June 2025 organic?** (§1.5)
6. **Do routine modes actually retain shift workers,** or only win them back briefly? `14454264522` returned; `13795524849` returned and immediately hit bugs.

# PART 10 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 10.1 Counting rules

- **Denominator** is 2,048 for all global figures; the storefront's own n for country figures; the era's own n for era figures; 161 for payer-segment rates; 332/109/216/322/1,069 for star bands.
- **Themes are non-exclusive.** A review reporting a crash, a refund request and praise for the design is counted in all three. Percentages therefore sum to well over 100%.
- **No deduplication was applied** — all 2,048 review_ids are unique. Two Japanese reviews (`14238803827`, `14248568509`) have near-identical text from the same reviewer; both are retained and counted, and the duplication is disclosed wherever they are cited.
- **A review is counted in a theme if any of that theme's patterns matches `title + body`, case-insensitively.**
- **"Confirmed payer"** requires an explicit first-person purchase statement. Interest in paying, questions about price, and refund requests without a stated purchase do **not** qualify.
- **Segment rates** (e.g. "9.94% of payers") are always labelled as segment rates and always shown alongside the global count.
- Star-band means are computed over the matched reviews only.

## 10.2 Theme definitions (selected)

| Theme | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Free-tier restriction blocks core use** | The reviewer states that a free-tier limit prevents checking off, creating, saving or otherwise using the product's core function, or that the free tier is functionally unusable. Excludes pure price complaints and pure billing disputes. |
| **Trial / billing dispute** | A refund request, an unexpected or duplicate charge, a trial that converted early or without notice, a cancellation that failed, or a price-charged ≠ price-shown report. |
| **Entitlement failure** | The reviewer states they completed a purchase and the app still restricts them or demands payment. |
| **Promo-nag** | Complaints about upgrade popups, the persistent bottom promo bar, or the Dynamic Island discount countdown — distinct from third-party-style ads. |
| **Data loss** | Records, check-ins, memos, diary entries, routines or streaks disappearing, resetting, or failing to save. |
| **Order/edit restriction** | Reordering not working or not persisting, and the inability to edit routines on past or future dates. |
| **Onboarding friction** | The pre-use survey, tutorial, forced habit selection, pledge/signature step, or forced sign-up preventing or discouraging use. Excludes onboarding *crashes*, which are counted under Crash. |
| **Confirmed payer** | See §10.1. |
| **Partial-completion praise** | Explicit approval of not needing 100% completion, of the rest/skip options, or of the absence of guilt/pressure. Deliberately narrow — count is a conservative floor. |
