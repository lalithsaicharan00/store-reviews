# Awesome Habits: Habit Tracker (App Store ID 1514915737) — Full Review Analysis

**App:** Awesome Habits: Habit Tracker — *Streaks, days since & goals*
**Developer of record:** Michal Tuma · **Bundle:** `com.dreamer.Habits`
**Corpus:** 815 written reviews · 67 storefronts · 21 July 2020 → 4 September 2026
**Corpus mean rating:** 4.591 · **Extracted:** 8 September 2026 (`itunes MZStore userReviewsRow`)
**Analysis date:** 11 September 2026
**Business model:** Free download, **freemium with a hard 3-habit cap on the free tier**, unlocked by subscription *or* a one-time "Lifetime Premium" purchase. Store listing (accessed 11 September 2026) shows: Monthly Premium **$4.99**, Yearly Premium **$12.99** (with trial), **Lifetime Premium $22.99**. **No advertising anywhere in the corpus** — six reviewers explicitly praise its absence.

---

## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Every claim traces to an exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several themes. 704 of 815 records (86.4%) carry at least one theme; the remaining 111 are short generic praise (median 69 characters; 95 of 111 are 5★) and are counted in every denominator.
- Signal thresholds: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.

### ⚠️ Eight warnings before any number here is used

1. **This is an unusually positive corpus and that changes how it must be read.** 743 of 815 reviews (91.2%) are 4★ or 5★; only 44 (5.4%) are 1★ or 2★. There is no dominant product failure. The useful signal is not "what is broken" — it is **which small frictions are converting into the few bad ratings, and what the 5★ reviewers say the product is beating.** Reading this corpus as a bug list will find almost nothing.
2. **Only four storefronts clear the 50-review bar** — US (201), DE (91), GB (64), CA (51). Together they are 407 records (49.9%). Every other country observation in Part 7 is explicitly labelled limited evidence; 49 of 67 storefronts have fewer than 10 reviews.
3. **Japan, China and South Korea are effectively absent** (4, 4 and 5 reviews). Any statement about high-spend Asian markets in this corpus is unsupported, and none is made.
4. **Classification was hand-audited, not blind-automated.** All 815 records were read in full. Regex candidate sets were then generated and every candidate list for a decision-critical theme was manually inspected; the paid-purchase, paywall, price, subscription, defect, localization and unmet-need themes are **hand-curated ID lists**, not raw regex output. Broad praise themes (design, simplicity, comparison, widgets, sync, Health, Watch, Shortcuts, support, stats, motivation) are regex-derived "mention" counts and are reported with a star-direction breakdown rather than as pure positives. Boundary rules are in Part 10.1.
5. **Paid-user evidence is real but small: 60 reviewers (7.36%) give direct first-person evidence of having paid.** Reviews are a self-selected channel. **No conversion rate can be inferred from this corpus and none is claimed.**
6. **At least five records have a star rating that contradicts their own text** (`11802469749` US 1★ "Excellent :: Excellent"; `10078111201` CZ 1★ "The best on the market… Perfect app"; `12154375821` US 5★ "This app just deleted all my habits and history"; `13276009260` CA 5★ "Developer is lying there is no widget feature… this is a scam"; `14428620119` AU 3★ "genuinely no complaints"). They are retained in every denominator, but rating-only analysis of this corpus carries measurable noise.
7. **The store listing's stated latest version (7.4.1, 17 August 2025, accessed 11 September 2026) is inconsistent with the corpus**, which contains reviewers thanking the developer for an Italian localization in June 2026 (`14135424478`) and Traditional Chinese in June 2026 (`14139118342`). Treat the listing version date as unreliable; treat the localization events as review-evidenced.
8. **Prices changed materially over the corpus window** and reviewers quote different numbers at different times (€14.50/yr in 2021 → €28 lifetime in 2023 → €25 lifetime in 2026). Price-objection rates across eras are therefore not comparing a constant price. This is disclosed everywhere it matters.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. The single most valuable asset in this corpus is that Awesome Habits wins head-to-head comparison shopping, and it wins it overwhelmingly.**
**160 reviews (19.63%, high-priority, mean 4.94)** explicitly state the reviewer tried, tested or compared multiple habit trackers and chose this one. The distribution is the finding: **151 of those 160 are 5★, 8 are 4★, 1 is 3★, and not a single one is 1★ or 2★.** That is 24.1% of all 5★ reviews (151/627). Evidence: `7305149420`, `7323930695`, `8435023786`, `8482606810`, `8967436415`, `9761451102`, `10127928558`, `10874988299`, `11173751309`, `11286668790`, `11493203641`, `11511859366`, `12162498485`, `12459206032`, `13322024143`, `13833089535`, `14298082271`, `14489261806`. Representative: *"I've tried maybe 2 dozen habit-tracking apps and this is probably the best one"* (`8435023786`), *"I tested all habit apps over the last 15 years, this is by far the BEST!"* (`12162498485`), *"Ich habe wirklich ausnahmslos alle Habit Tracker im Appstore probiert, aber keine kam nur ansatzweise an Awesome Habits heran"* / "I have tried literally every habit tracker in the App Store and none came even close" (`11500845940`). **Interpretation: this app converts on the comparison page, not on the category page. Its growth problem is discovery, not persuasion — and two reviewers say so outright: *"It deserves a way higher ranking and visibility in the search results"* (`9400089129`), *"I'm surprised that this app is not yet as famous as others. Ratings are great, must be only a matter of marketing"* (`11493203641`), *"this app doesn't get the attention it deserves"* (`13228802467`), *"I hope that it gains traction and ranks higher in the search results, since I didn't find it the first time"* (`8013660114`).**

**2. The 3-habit free cap is the largest single negative in the corpus and it is almost the entire explanation for the 2★ band.**
**28 reviews (3.44%, very strong, mean 2.32)** complain about the free tier's 3-habit limit or the fact that the app is paid at all. Those 28 reviews account for **10 of 15 two-star reviews (66.7%)** and **9 of 29 one-star reviews (31.0%)**. Evidence: `6824720982`, `6825465567`, `8077884105`, `8406116058`, `9160925249`, `9556644123`, `9710705599`, `10931710398`, `11109118040`, `11580359210`, `11857530634`, `11985104724`, `12131335894`, `12156551236`, `12202681717`, `12385678485`, `12470582996`, `12518959349`, `12659289186`, `12778509682`, `12819643720`, `12985048890`, `13563498977`, `13627048536`, `13781637874`, `14014520516`, `14169218014`, `12028492119`. Representative: *"A habit app that doesn't let you have more than 3 habits is not a habit app"* (`12659289186`, FI, 1★), *"you can't get a proper demo out of this without having at least 8 or 10 habits. Please consider raising the free habit limit!"* (`12778509682`, US, 4★), *"make it at least 10ish goals free and then people will pay for more"* (`8077884105`, CA, 2★). **Interpretation: the cap is not being read as a pricing decision, it is being read as the product not working. Several of these reviewers never got far enough to see the thing everyone else is raving about.**

**3. But the cap also converts — and the evidence for that is explicit, which makes this a tuning problem, not a removal decision.**
**Five reviewers (0.61%, emerging, mean 4.80) describe the 3-habit tier working exactly as a trial is supposed to**, and four of the five went on to pay: *"I used the free version for a few weeks (only three habits) and decided I might as well do the lifetime upgrade"* (`13393275122`, US, 5★), *"The free version gives you 3 habits to track, which is plenty for most people… it's well worth it for $1.99 a month"* (`12877429608`, US, 5★), *"on peut tester l'app avec 3 objectifs : amplement suffisant pour se faire une idée"* / "you can test the app with 3 goals: entirely enough to form an opinion" (`14309794575`, FR, 5★), *"I pretty quickly paid for premium to try it out for a month as only 3 tracked items is a low number"* (`11215681102`, US, 4★), `11817074234` (SG, 5★). **Interpretation: the cap is simultaneously the strongest conversion lever and the biggest rating liability. The design question is not "3 or unlimited" — it is whether the cap can be moved to a dimension that does not make the product look broken (see Part 9.2, Experiment E1).**

**4. Monetization friction — not defects — is what produces bad ratings here.**
Union of paywall-cap, price and subscription-model objections: **46 reviews (5.64%, high-priority, mean 2.80)**. Those 46 are **21 of the 44 one- and two-star reviews (47.7%)**. By contrast the union of every reliability defect theme (sync failure, data loss, bugs/performance, widget defects, Health-sync gaps) is **50 reviews (6.13%, high-priority, mean 3.46)** and produces only **13 of the 44 one- and two-star reviews (29.5%)**. **Interpretation: this product's rating is set at the paywall, not in the app.**

**5. The one-time "Lifetime" option is a named, repeated purchase driver — it is the most-cited commercial feature in the corpus.**
**81 reviews (9.94%, high-priority, mean 4.70)** mention the lifetime / one-time purchase option; 67 of the 81 are 5★. Evidence: `7305149420`, `7489814726`, `8290170982`, `8414522828`, `8694232589`, `8883282086`, `10802417983`, `11449917454`, `11773757505`, `12081345787`, `12239848520`, `13189731645`, `13418978546`, `14298082271`, `14342654245`, `14511086289`. Representative: *"Thank you very much for providing a single purchase option. It is much appreciated in the sea of subscriptions we are currently in"* (`8883282086`), *"I hate apps that don't offer lifetime premium as a purchase option, but thankfully this one does"* (`11449917454`), *"the option to pay once makes this a no-brainer to me"* (`7305149420`), *"It's simple, well-designed, and they offer the non-subscription option (lifetime purchase). What's not to love?!"* (`13418978546`). **Interpretation: for a meaningful slice of this audience the absence of a subscription requirement is itself the feature. Two reviewers report being unable to find the lifetime option and were blocked from paying because of it (`12128314444`, `11360590820`) — that is a direct, recoverable revenue loss.**

**6. Nobody in this corpus is angry about the app's reliability, but a small, high-severity data-loss cluster exists and it is the only thing here that can destroy a paying customer.**
**6 reviews (0.74%, emerging, mean 3.33)** report losing history or habit data: `12535283569` (BG, 1★, *"After 215 days of tracking… everything (EVERYTHING!!!) has been deleted, all history lost! No chance to restore"*), `12701004929` (CH, 1★, *"ALL DATA (months of history) were deleted from BOTH old and new device. This app is a joke"* — on device migration), `12154375821` (US, *"This app just deleted all my habits and history after 6 months of using"*), `12157688910` (AU, *"Sometimes there are bugs which cause data loss"*), `11141273586` (FR, habit descriptions self-deleting), `11834060415` (US, recovered — *"Developer was very nice and helped me restore my data"*). **Interpretation: two of the six are iCloud/device-migration events. That is the highest-severity, lowest-frequency risk in the corpus and the one where a single recurrence costs a multi-year user.**

**7. Sync is the app's most-praised capability and its most-reported defect, simultaneously.**
**90 reviews (11.04%, high-priority, mean 4.49)** praise cross-device sync / iCloud / Mac / iPad coverage — *"This is the best habit tracker for someone who wants to track across multiple devices"* (`6344341522`), *"Es synchronisiert sich über alle Geräte… iPad, iPhone, Apple Watch und Mac"* (`14298082271`). Against that, **13 reviews (1.60%, meaningful, mean 3.08)** report sync failing: `8452677698`, `8580802546`, `8751455990`, `9104605843`, `11176831683`, `11872139244`, `12157688910`, `12219810473`, `12701004929`, `13279660290`, `13439662440`, `13465174002`, `14334740214`. **Four of the 13 are from reviewers who gave direct evidence of paying** (`8452677698`, `8751455990`, `9104605843`, `14223824557` via Health). **Interpretation: sync is the promise the app is bought on. When it breaks, it breaks the purchase rationale, which is why sync failures skew to paying reviewers and to 1★.**

**8. Frequency flexibility is the clearest unmet feature need, it is stable across six years, and it is explicitly costing stars.**
**13 reviews (1.60%, meaningful, mean 3.85)** ask for "X times per week without fixing which days" or "every N days"; a further **9 (1.10%, meaningful, mean 4.00)** ask to see *which days* a weekly/monthly habit was completed. Evidence (frequency): `6982393836`, `8656250182`, `9906262176`, `11105723095`, `11174670037`, `11184020601`, `11405961424`, `11494813596`, `12198311158`, `12529452345`, `12979275111`, `13971210487`, `14042363994`. Evidence (day detail): `9028038591`, `10107026159`, `10392822751`, `11107932681`, `11872027872`, `12153222646`, `12529452345`, `13585104492`, `14303067862`. Representative: *"you can't set a habit for an amount of times per week… my work schedule means I sometimes need to adjust the days I train"* (`11105723095`, GB, 2★), *"I would like the option of doing something 3 times a week, not necessarily on the same day every week"* (`14042363994`, NL, 4★), *"There is no way to see on which day a weekly or monthly habit was recorded"* (`10392822751`, DE, 5★). **Interpretation: this is one request, asked twice. The app models "which days" and "how many times" as alternatives; users want both at once. It is the only feature gap in this corpus that appears in every year from 2021 to 2026.**

**9. Localization is a live, converting lever — and the corpus contains the proof of both directions.**
**16 reviews (1.96%, meaningful, mean 3.56)** ask for a missing language (Russian ×5, Spanish ×4, Portuguese ×2, German, French, Czech, Italian, general). **Zero come from the US or GB.** Evidence: `6329018597`, `10440246972`, `10635939819`, `11205195054`, `11398546682`, `11733827091`, `11786388052`, `11804482599`, `11872139244`, `13299630252`, `13309363635`, `13502703203`, `13509177027`, `13580190550`, `13958217751`, `14099685276`. One explicitly says the missing language is what is blocking the purchase: *"le falta el idioma español, es por eso que aún no acabo de lanzarme a comprarla, y no le pongo las 5 estrellas"* / "it lacks Spanish, that's why I haven't gone ahead and bought it, and why I'm not giving 5 stars" (`11398546682`, ES, 4★). And when localizations shipped, reviewers came back to reward it: *"Ho aperto l'app oggi e mi ritrovo un aggiornamento dove introducono finalmente la lingua italiana. Sono corso subito a mettere 5 stelle"* / "I opened the app today and found an update finally introducing Italian. I ran straight here to give 5 stars" (`14135424478`, IT, 5★, June 2026), plus `14139118342` (TW, Traditional Chinese, June 2026). **Russian is the single largest unserved language and is not on the store listing's 11-language list.** **Interpretation: localization here has a measured, immediate rating response. Russian is the highest-yield remaining target on review evidence.**

**10. Canada is the weakest of the four analysable markets and it is weak for one reason: price.**
CA mean **4.353** vs US 4.627, DE 4.582, GB 4.672. CA carries the highest rate of every monetization objection: price objection **4/51 = 7.84%** (global 2.58%), subscription objection **3/51 = 5.88%** (global 1.23%), free-cap complaint **3/51 = 5.88%** (global 3.44%). CA also has the highest direct-payer rate (**7/51 = 13.7%**), so this is not disengagement — it is an actively split market. Evidence: `8460171885` (1★, *"not worth 30 bucks annually"*), `12131335894` (1★, *"I could see maybe paying a one time fee of under $5"*), `12109867402` (4★, *"The app is excellent… Yet, I think it's over-priced"*), `11790255448` (4★, *"I will switch to another one just to avoid the subscription"*), `9710705599` (1★), `8077884105` (2★). **Interpretation: limited evidence (n=51) but internally consistent across four separate objection types.**

**11. Developer responsiveness is a named, repeated purchase reason — and it shows up inside paying reviewers' text.**
**66 reviews (8.10%, high-priority, mean 4.73)** mention support or the developer's responsiveness; **7 of the 60 direct payers** name it. Several describe it as the thing that closed the sale: *"During my initial subscription month… I emailed the developer a feature related question. He responded within hours. Twice. Outstanding support. As such, I am buying the app outright"* (`11055117867`, AU, 5★), *"After exploring the app more and speaking with the customer service guys… I am adding a star for this. I will be purchasing the app outright"* (`9877978258`, US, 5★), *"J'en ai fait la demande aux développeurs et ils viennent de l'ajouter 🤩"* / "I asked the developers for it and they've just added it" (`14282886646`, FR, 5★). **Interpretation: one-to-one support is functioning as a conversion channel, not just a cost centre. It is also fragile — it is one person, and the corpus shows the support-mention rate collapsing to 1.7% in 2025 before recovering to 9.4% in 2026.**

**12. Two support/payment paths are broken enough to be worth fixing on their own.**
(a) **The in-app support link opens Mail and dead-ends users who don't use Mail** — 2 reviews (0.25%, weak, but both cost stars): *"When I tried contacting them for help, it is asking me to download the mail app, which I do not use… no contact info"* (`11393377160`, US, 4★, on trial, *"likely will cancel"*), *"support email should be text in the app - it opens Mail app which I don't use, and I get stuck with it forcing me to add a mail account"* (`12113172399`, US, 4★). (b) **There is no in-app cancellation path and at least one subscriber could not find one** (`7205630038`, GB, 4★, *"I would like to unsubscribe and delete the app but there are no means to do that via the app"*), and one refund request went unresolved in public (`11450707006`, UA, 1★, *"Terrible service. They didn't give me a refund"* — the only refund report in the corpus, 0.12%, weak).

**13. The cheapest high-value moves, in evidence order:** raise or re-shape the free-tier cap (28 reviews, 47.7% of all 1–2★ come from monetization friction) → make the Lifetime option always discoverable (2 blocked buyers, `12128314444`, `11360590820`) → ship "X times per week, any days" plus per-day detail on weekly habits (13 + 9 reviews, six-year persistence) → harden iCloud device-migration and add visible backup/restore (6 data-loss reports, 2 of them migration) → ship Russian (5 requests, largest unserved language) → replace the `mailto:` support link with in-app text + web form (2 reviews, both from trial/paying users) → add optional text labels to widgets (8 reviews) → yearly/long-horizon goals (6 reviews).

## 1.5 Processing method

1. Parsed all 815 JSON records programmatically; verified ID uniqueness, field presence and country-file reconciliation (Part 1.3).
2. **Read all 815 reviews in full**, in date order, including every non-English review. No sampling. No length filter. No language filter.
3. Built regex candidate sets per theme across English, German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, Polish, Czech, Hungarian, Italian, Turkish, Russian/Ukrainian, Japanese, Korean and Chinese terms.
4. **Manually inspected every candidate list** for the 44 decision-critical themes and converted them to fixed, hand-curated ID lists (Part 10.3). Corrections made during audit included: removing "purchased a new iPhone" from purchase evidence (`12701004929`), removing praise of *other* apps' pricing from the price-objection theme (`9400089129`, `9557762161`, `6683101163`, `11133275048`), removing "not too expensive" from the price-objection theme (`12927109452`, `12239848520`), and adding six direct payers the regex missed because they wrote "buying the premium" / "signed up to the yearly plan" / "am now a Premium subscriber" (`8751455990`, `9047728455`, `8949712027`, `8952156169`, `12661574865`, `11055117867`).
5. Ran a second, deliberately over-broad regex sweep against the curated purchase list to find stragglers; it surfaced three records, all correctly excluded. This is the validation check for the highest-stakes theme in the report.
6. Aggregated by rating, country, year and half-year; applied the signal thresholds to every result.
7. Broad praise themes were left as regex "mention" counts and are reported with a per-star breakdown so that negative mentions inside a positive-sounding theme remain visible (e.g. `watch_praise` includes three reviews complaining Watch sync is broken).

## 1.6 Limitations and known biases

- **Written reviews ≠ users.** The store listing shows 925 ratings against 815 written reviews in this corpus. Silent users are invisible; satisfied payers are systematically under-represented relative to frustrated non-payers, who have a stronger motive to write.
- **The corpus is 49.9% US+DE+GB+CA.** Global percentages are weighted toward those four markets.
- **Star ratings are noisy here** (Warning 6). Five records contradict their own text; at n=815 that is 0.61% of the corpus but it is concentrated in the small 1★ band, where it is 2 of 29 records (6.9%).
- **Prices changed during the window** (Warning 8). Era-over-era price-objection comparisons are not like-for-like.
- **Residual classification error** is highest in the broad `simplicity_praise` (338), `design_praise` (284) and `motivation_praise` (61) themes, which use generous patterns and are reported as context, not as decision drivers. The hand-curated themes carry materially lower error risk; the purchase theme was independently re-swept.
- **Translations** in this report are the analyst's and are labelled; original text is preserved.
- **No causal claim** is made anywhere from a correlation between a theme and a rating.

## 5.6 Cancellation, refunds and churn among payers

**5 reviews (0.61%, emerging, mean 2.60):**

## 7.2 Trend 1 — The 2025 dip is real, is concentrated in H1, and is a monetization event · **worsening then recovering, medium-high confidence**

**Interpretation: something changed about how the paywall was encountered in the first half of 2025. The corpus cannot say what — a price change, a paywall redesign, an acquisition-channel shift, or an App Store featuring that brought in users with different expectations. It recovered. Flagged as the highest-value research question in Part 9.3.**

## 7.3 Trend 2 — Comparison victory is persistent and is the corpus's most stable positive · **persistent, high confidence**

9.8% (2020) → 16.0% → 20.0% → **32.1% (2023)** → 19.1% → 16.2% → 24.0% (2026). The 2023 peak coincides with the lowest review volume year of the mature era (84), so it is partly a small-denominator effect; the floor across six years is 16%. At no point does the theme fall below one in six reviews.

**Interpretation: this is not a launch-honeymoon artefact. Six years in, roughly one in five reviewers is still arriving from a comparison and saying this app won it.**

## 7.4 Trend 3 — Behaviour-change stories surged in 2026 · **emerging, medium confidence**

7.8% (2020, launch enthusiasm) → 3.7% → 3.2% → 2.4% → 2.6% → 2.3% → **10.4% (2026)**. Ten of the 32 behaviour-change reviews in the corpus are from 2026 alone, in a partial year: `13601624683`, `13833089535`, `13984268382`, `13993034824`, `14010059946`, `14056047525`, `14081110708`, `14489261806`, `14501021178`, `12766245644`.

**Interpretation, offered cautiously: this is the profile of a cohort maturing. Several 2026 reviewers state multi-year tenure explicitly (`13125981351` "3 years", `12893154534` "three years", `14511086289` "over a year", `14334740214` "since 2022"). A user base old enough to report outcomes is a different marketing asset than a user base reporting first impressions. Sample is 96 partial-year reviews; treat the magnitude as provisional.**

## 7.5 Trend 4 — Support-praise collapsed in 2025 and recovered in 2026 · **watch-list, medium confidence**

11.8% (2020) → 16.0% → 9.5% → 7.1% → 8.5% → **1.7% (2025, 3 of 173)** → 9.4% (2026). The 2025 figure is a 5× drop from the surrounding years and coincides exactly with the 2025 rating trough.

**Interpretation: two readings are consistent with the data and the corpus cannot separate them. (a) Support capacity was strained in 2025 — plausible for a one-developer product whose review volume had just tripled from 84 (2023) to 235 (2024). (b) The 2025 cohort was dominated by paywall-bounce reviewers who never contacted support at all. Reading (b) is partly supported by the cap-complaint spike in the same year. Either way, the recovery in 2026 is real and the developer-responsiveness asset appears intact.**

## 7.6 Trend 5 — Apple Watch discussion has fallen by two-thirds · **declining, medium confidence**

2.0% (2020) → 13.6% (2021) → 13.7% (2022) → 10.7% (2023) → **2.1% (2024)** → 5.2% (2025) → 4.2% (2026).

**Interpretation: the 2021–2022 peak is the period immediately after the Watch app shipped, when it was the thing reviewers came to talk about. The fall is most likely normalization — a shipped feature stops being news — not regression, because the three Watch *complaints* in the corpus are spread across 2022, 2025 and 2025 rather than clustered. Named as declining discussion, not declining quality.**

## 7.7 Trend 6 — The free-tier cap is the only negative that is worse now than at launch · **worsening, high confidence**

0.0% (2020) → 3.7% → 2.1% → 2.4% → 2.6% → **6.4% (2025)** → 4.2% (2026). Six-year direction is unambiguously up, and 2026's 4.2% is still above every year from 2021 to 2024.

**Interpretation: the cap has stayed at 3 while the category's norms, the app's feature surface and its price have all moved. It is the one friction in this corpus that is compounding.**

## 7.8 Trend 7 — Localization demand is rising and is being answered · **persistent, improving, medium confidence**

2020 2.0% → 2021–2022 0.0% → 2023 2.4% → 2024 2.6% → 2025 2.3% → 2026 3.1%. The demand has not fallen despite Czech (2025-10), Italian (2026-06) and Traditional Chinese (2026-06) shipping in the window — because the demand is for *different* languages, principally Russian.

## 7.9 What did not change

- **Simplicity and design praise** hold at 35–50% across every year. The core proposition has not drifted.
- **The frequency-flexibility gap** appears in 2021 (`6982393836`), 2022 (`8656250182`), 2023 (`9906262176`), 2024 (`11105723095`, `11174670037`, `11184020601`, `11405961424`, `11494813596`), 2025 (`12198311158`, `12529452345`, `12979275111`) and 2026 (`13971210487`, `14042363994`). # PART 8 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation cites the findings it rests on. Immediate fixes are separated from experiments and from research questions.

## 8.1 Immediate fixes — evidence is unambiguous, no further research needed

**F1 · Make the Lifetime SKU permanently discoverable and verify the purchase entry point renders in every storefront.**
*Findings:* Part 5.5 — `12128314444` (GB, 5★, could not find the lifetime plan they wanted to buy), `11360590820` (UA, 5★, saw it during trial, then it vanished), `10195683230` (US, "bummed I missed out on the lifetime deal"), `10901569398` (CN, 4★, **no purchase entry point visible at all**). Part 3.1 Theme 6 — the lifetime option is the single most-cited commercial feature in the corpus (81 reviews, 9.94%, mean 4.70).
*Why immediate:* these are customers with intent and no path. It is the only defect class in the report where the fix is pure revenue recovery with no product trade-off.

**F2 · Replace the `mailto:` support link with in-app contact text plus a web form.**
*Findings:* Part 5.6 / Executive Summary #12 — `11393377160` (US, 4★, **on trial, "likely will cancel"**, blocked from reporting a bug because the link demanded the Mail app), `12113172399` (US, 4★, *"I get stuck with it forcing me to add a mail account"*). Part 5.4 also shows support being the *only* resolution path for data loss (`11834060415` — *"Developer was very nice and helped me restore my data"*), which makes the channel's reliability a data-integrity dependency, not just a service one.
*Why immediate:* support is a documented conversion channel (Part 5.2, trigger 3). A support channel that fails for non-Mail users silently removes that channel for an unknown share of them.

**F3 · Harden iCloud device migration and surface an explicit, user-visible backup/restore + "last backed up" state.**
*Findings:* Part 3.3.3 — 6 data-loss reports (0.74%, emerging, mean 3.33), **two of them device-migration events** (`12701004929` CH 1★ *"deleted from BOTH old and new device"*, `11834060415` US, recovered only via direct developer intervention); plus `12535283569` (BG, 1★, 215 days lost, *"No chance to restore"*), `12154375821` (US, 6 months lost), `12157688910` (AU, *"bugs which cause data loss"*), `11141273586` (FR, descriptions self-deleting).
*Why immediate:* lowest frequency, highest severity. Every one of these is a multi-month or multi-year user lost permanently. `11834060415` demonstrates the data was recoverable — the user just had no way to do it themselves.

**F4 · Fix premium-entitlement state and make "Restore Purchases" reliable.**
*Findings:* Part 5.4 — `8452677698` (DE, 4★, 2022, premium bought on iPhone never syncs to iPad), `13439662440` (CA, 4★, 2025, *"sometimes the app treats me as if I am on the free version, not allowing me to view stats… neither does tapping 'restore purchases'"*). Two reports three years apart.
*Why immediate:* showing a paywall to someone who already paid is the single most damaging bug state available to this product, and it maps directly onto the corpus's largest negative theme.

**F5 · Add an explicit in-app language selector and stop overriding the user's chosen language.**
*Findings:* Part 6.9 item 3 — `13509177027` (FR, **1★**, *"Why this language change to German??? I can't set it back to French?"*), `13309363635` (CZ, 5★, wants to keep English after the Czech localization shipped). Two storefronts, one defect, one of them a 1★ from a self-described former fan.
*Why immediate:* this is a regression introduced *by* shipping localizations. It converts a feature investment into a rating loss.

**F6 · Make skip / miss / not-yet visually distinct in history.**
*Findings:* Part 3.3.4 — `9373324084` (GB, 3★, **a payer who never used the app** because *"what does it mean when you forget to record a habit as completed, is that skipped or recorded as a miss. I don't understand how the stats work"*), `14303067862` (UA, 4★, 2026, *"you don't understand whether you forgot to tick that day or whether it was a skip… it's somehow not obvious"*), plus `11107932681` (JP, 4★, asking for an explicit "未達"/"not achieved" state distinct from skip), `11872027872` (DE), `13971210487` (US), `11725650343` (AU, 4★, *"I would love a feature where if you didn't do the habit you could mark no if you missed it"*).
*Why immediate:* five years apart, same ambiguity, and in one documented case it cost the entire value of a purchase.

**F7 · Ship optional text labels on widgets.**
*Findings:* Part 3.4 rank 4 — 8 reviews (0.98%, emerging, mean 4.25): `13826645362` (FR, 4★, *"When you have +10 habits, one habit widget with only icon and no clue to remembering the habit is a pain"*), `11886448055` (US, 5★, *"to track different subject like math, physics, literature I only have to rely on icons"*), `10415080649` (AU, 4★, *"widgets do not display any text… so I use another tracker alongside this app"*), `7193643397` (IN, 1★, 2021 — the earliest version of the same complaint), `13465174002` (PL), `6339215858`, `6544718854`, `6543708670` (icon searchability, 2020).
*Why immediate:* `10415080649` is running a competitor alongside this app purely because of it. The widget is the app's most-praised surface (111 reviews); this is its one named limitation.

## 8.2 Experiments — clear hypothesis, uncertain magnitude

**E1 · Move the free-tier gate off "number of habits."**
*Hypothesis:* The 3-habit cap generates 66.7% of 2★ and 31.0% of 1★ reviews (Part 4.4, 4.5) because it makes the product look non-functional rather than merely limited — *"A habit app that doesn't let you have more than 3 habits is not a habit app"* (`12659289186`). But the cap also demonstrably converts (Part 3.1 Theme 41; `13393275122`, `12877429608`, `11215681102`, `14309794575`). Gating on **history depth, statistics, widgets, sync or customisation** instead of on habit count would preserve the "try it properly, then pay" motion while removing the "this app is broken" reading.
*Test:* A/B the gate dimension. Primary metric: paid conversion. **Secondary metric that this report argues is equally important: 1–2★ rate.** The reviewers themselves name the number that would defuse it — **5 to 10 habits** (`8077884105` "10ish", `11580359210` "5/7", `12778509682` "at least 8 or 10", `13781637874` "4/5").
*Risk:* four reviewers describe the current cap working as a trial. A change could reduce conversion among decisive buyers. This is why it is an experiment and not an immediate fix.

**E2 · "X times per week, any days" as a first-class frequency type — with per-day history.**
*Hypothesis:* This is one request asked two ways (13 + 9 reviews, 2.70% combined, six consecutive years, Part 3.4 ranks 1–2). The app currently forces a choice between *which days* and *how many times*; users want a weekly quota with a visible day record. Closing it converts 4★ to 5★ (8 of the 13 frequency reviews are 4★) and removes the corpus's oldest unmet need.
*Test:* ship behind a flag; measure 4★→5★ movement and whether `11105723095`-class 2★ reviews stop appearing.
*Risk:* violates the "add nothing" instruction (Part 3.2.2) if it adds a new setup step. Mitigation: make it a third option inside the existing frequency picker, not a new concept.

**E3 · Russian localization.**
*Hypothesis:* Russian is the largest single unserved language (5 requests across AM, BY, KG, TR, ID — Part 6.9 item 1) and is absent from the current 11-language listing. The corpus contains direct evidence that shipping a language produces an immediate 5★ response (`14135424478` Italian, `14139118342` Traditional Chinese, both June 2026) and that a missing language blocks a purchase (`11398546682`).
*Test:* ship it; measure review volume, mean rating and payer self-reporting in Russian-language storefronts over two quarters.
*Risk:* low product risk; the storefronts involved are all sub-50 today, so the upside is unproven at this app's current scale. **Explicitly limited evidence.**

**E4 · Regional pricing for the lifetime SKU.**
*Hypothesis:* Price objections concentrate in Canada (7.8%, 3× global — Part 6.5) and are explicitly framed as purchasing-power problems outside high-spend markets (`13528830783` TR, *"the one time purchase fee is a bit too much, at least in my country"*; `12985048890` BR quoting R$8/month approvingly against the lifetime price; `8278322299` US asking for a student rate). Reviewers name reserve prices: €10 (`9473367265`), <$5 (`12131335894`), £3.99 (`11755092191`).
*Test:* purchasing-power-adjusted lifetime tiers in the 63 sub-50 storefronts, where the cap complaint rate is already 2.1× the high-volume markets (Part 6.7).
*Risk:* the named reserve prices are far below current pricing and may not represent the market. Treat the specific numbers as anecdotes, not as a pricing input.

**E5 · Lead with the comparison win in store metadata.**
*Hypothesis:* 19.63% of reviewers (mean 4.94, zero of them below 3★) describe arriving from a comparison and staying. Four reviewers independently say the app is under-discovered (`9400089129`, `11493203641`, `13228802467`, `8013660114`). The store listing does not currently lead with the property the corpus says wins.
*Test:* screenshot/description tests emphasising (a) the one-time purchase option, (b) the non-binary percentage ring, (c) full Apple-ecosystem coverage including Mac and Watch, (d) no ads / no account. Each of these is a top-10 corpus theme.

**E6 · Publish a short "how this app models habits" onboarding card.**
*Hypothesis:* The onboarding theme is small (8, 0.98%) but the lowest-rated non-monetization theme (mean 2.88) and the failures are semantic, not navigational (Part 3.3.4). A one-screen explanation of build vs break, skip vs miss, count vs days, and what lists are for addresses `9373324084`, `12067428931`, `14303067862`, `11867342767` and `10973931494` simultaneously.

## 8.3 Research questions the corpus raises but cannot answer

1. **What changed in the first half of 2025?** The corpus shows the worst half-year rating (4.406), the highest-ever cap-complaint rate (6.4% for the year) and a 5× collapse in support mentions, all at once. It cannot distinguish between a price change, a paywall redesign, an acquisition-channel shift, and support capacity strain. **This is the single highest-value question in the report** because it is the only period where the app's satisfaction profile broke.
2. **Are Spanish and Portuguese actually reaching ES/CL/BR/PT storefronts?** They appear on the current listing; four reviews dated April 2024 – January 2026 say they are missing (`11205195054`, `11398546682`, `11804482599`, `13580190550`, `13299630252`).
3. **Is the lifetime SKU being hidden deliberately (promotional windows) or by a bug?** Three reviewers describe it disappearing (`12128314444`, `11360590820`, `10195683230`) and one sees no purchase UI at all (`10901569398`). The answer changes whether F1 is a bug fix or a pricing decision.
4. **Is Watch sync currently reliable?** Complaints are sparse but span 2022 (`9104605843`), 2025 (`12157688910`, `13279660290`) and touch a payer each time. Three reports over four years is below any signal threshold on its own; instrumentation would settle it.
5. **What is the actual free→paid conversion rate, and how does it differ between the high-volume four and the 63-storefront tail?** Self-reported payer rates differ 2× (9.83% vs 4.90%), but self-reporting is not conversion.
6. **Does the review prompt run too often?** Exactly one reviewer complains (`14406073990`, CH, 1★, Aug 2026) — below every threshold. It is recorded only because 111 of 815 reviews (13.6%) are contentless praise with a median length of 69 characters, which is the signature of prompted reviews, and because prompting materially affects the mean rating this analysis is built on.

## 8.4 What not to change

The corpus contains an unusually explicit, repeated, six-year instruction that should constrain the entire roadmap above:

- **Do not add a to-do list, a journal, a pet, a plant, challenges, a social feed, or an AI coach.** `8414522828` (*"Please don't try to be an all in one app!"*), `8238514048`, `8384484490`, `9243307416`, `10691911378`, `11482024114`, `12239848520`, `13465174002`, `14489261806` (*"no gamification overload"*), `14455804453` (*"doesn't overload you with features you'll never use"*), `12395956072`, `11574965608` (*"all of the rest similar apps I tried have a ton of distracting and bloatware features"*).
- **Do not introduce ads.** Six reviewers name the absence of advertising as a reason they chose this app (`6953324112`, `9557762161`, `12395956072`, `13278332346`, `14455804453`, `8238514048`) and one explicitly prefers ads to the current subscription (`6825465567`) — a minority of one against six.
- **Do not require an account.** `11221350396` (*"No account or login is needed"*), `9492349496`, `13278332346`, `14455804453`, `11055117867`, `14309794575` (*"Pas de compte à créer, pas de données collectées : un développeur respectueux"*), `11892242464`. And note `9104605843`, who cancelled a subscription rather than enable iCloud.
- **Do not drop the one-time purchase option.** 81 reviews mention it, 67 at 5★; three reviewers say it is why they chose this app over a competitor (`12128314444`, `11790255448`, `6969311100` in reverse).
- **Do not make the percentage ring binary or streak-first.** `13969274929` sets out at length why the non-binary ring is the reason they bought; `11332444396`, `11481149340`, `12673892745` corroborate. One reviewer asks for streaks to be more prominent (`12673892745`) — treat as a minority request against an explicit differentiator.

# PART 9 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 9.1 Theme definitions and boundary rules

**General rules.**
- Themes are **non-exclusive**; one review may carry many.
- Denominator for every global percentage is **815**. Denominator for a country percentage is that country's own total.
- A review is assigned to a theme on the basis of **what the reviewer states**, not on the star rating.
- HTML entities were unescaped before matching. Case-insensitive throughout.
- Curated themes (C) were built by generating a regex candidate set and then **manually inspecting every candidate**. Regex themes (R) are raw pattern matches and are reported as "mentions" with a star breakdown.

| Theme | Type | Included | Excluded |
|---|---|---|---|
| `paid_direct` | C | First-person statement of having bought, subscribed, paid, possessing premium, or requesting a refund | Intent ("I will buy"), praise of the option ("great that there's a lifetime plan"), purchase of a *different* app or a phone, describing the paid tier in third person |
| `trial` | C | Reviewer states they are on or just finished a trial | "Free version" usage without the word trial |
| `cap_complaint` | C | Complains about the 3-habit limit, asks for a larger free tier, or objects that the app requires payment to be useful | Neutral/positive description of the cap (→ `cap_neutral_positive`); pure price objections with no reference to the gate (→ `price_objection`) |
| `price_objection` | C | States this app's price is too high, or names a lower price they would pay | Criticism of *other* apps' prices; "not too expensive"; "worth it despite being pricey" is included but noted as mild |
| `sub_objection` | C | Objects to the recurring-payment model specifically | General price complaints |
| `sync_failure` | C | States sync between devices failed or is unreliable | Praise of sync; requests for a *new* sync target |
| `data_loss` | C | States habit data or history was lost | Deliberate reset requests; "restore purchases" failures (→ entitlement) |
| `bugs_perf` | C | Reports a crash, freeze, lag, battery drain, or a control that does not respond | Statements that the app has *no* bugs; feature absences |
| `widget_defect` | C | Widget missing, stale, slow, unreadable, or not matching the store preview | Requests for *additional* widget types (→ separate) |
