### ⚠️ Read these five things before anything else

**1. The paying cohort is the only cohort whose satisfaction is collapsing, and it has collapsed by 1.4 stars.**
1,381 reviews (1.97%) contain explicit purchase evidence. Their mean rating fell **4.40 (2022) → 4.01 (2023) → 3.82 (2024) → 3.19 (2025) → 2.96 (2026)**. Over the same window the non-paid mean barely moved (4.785 overall). In 2026, **36.7% of reviews with purchase evidence are one-star** (92/251). This is the single most important number in the corpus. *(§6.1)*

**2. "You promised to remind me before charging" is the highest-intensity complaint in the corpus.**
Two themes — `M-trial-no-reminder` (160 reviews, mean **1.98**) and `M-trial-charged` (149, mean **1.79**) — describe the same failure: the app states it will warn users before the trial converts, and reviewers say it does not, or that they were charged immediately on starting the "free" trial. Combined with cancellation friction and refund refusal, the **billing-dispute family is 404 reviews (0.58%, EMERGING) at a mean of 1.97** — the worst-rated family in the entire corpus. **17.45% of all paid-evidence reviewers (241/1,381) are in it.** *(§4.2, §6.3)*

**3. Data loss is a structural product defect, not an incident, and it is getting worse.**
551 reviews (0.79%, EMERGING globally; **9.09% of all one-star reviews**) report a corrupted pet, a wiped account, or an unrecoverable login. The data-integrity family (`D-data-loss` + `D-no-backup` + `D-login-account` + `D-sync-devices`) runs **0.42% (2022H1) → 1.76% (2026H1)**. The mechanism reviewers describe is consistent across five years: progress is stored on-device, backup is manual and opt-in, and the standard remedy offered is a new bird plus 5,000 rainbow stones. In an app whose entire retention mechanic is emotional attachment to one pet, this converts the product's greatest strength into its greatest liability. *(§4.3, §8.4)*

**4. Two dated engineering failures are visible in the corpus at day resolution, and one of them is the largest quality event in the app's history.**
On **4 May 2022** the app crashed on launch: 128 reviews that day (vs. a ~90/day baseline that month), mean 3.72, 34 one-star, **42 crash reports in a single day**. In **February 2026** the "Wonderland / Queen of Hearts" event shipped a growth-potion animation that made the pet oversized and crashed the app on load for a large cohort: **120 crash-tagged reviews in one month** (2.03% of all 2026 reviews vs. a 0.36–0.53% baseline in 2023–2024), peaking at 20 crash reports on 2 Feb 2026 alone. Reviewers named the exact trigger. *(§8.2)*

**5. In June 2026 the monthly event became a paid movie tie-in, and the reaction is the clearest values-breach signal in five years.**
`C-brand-collab` is 127 reviews at mean **3.09**; 107 of them (84%) are in 2026, 68 in June 2026 alone. The complaint is precise and repeated: subscribers say they are now paying to be advertised to, in a mental-health app, in a month they expected to be Pride. This theme did not meaningfully exist before 2025 (2 reviews in 2022, 5 in 2023, 3 in 2024). *(§8.6)*

**What is genuinely working, and is worth more than all of the above:** 20,883 reviews (29.82%) carry a core-benefit theme at a mean of **4.89**. 8,398 reviews (11.99%) report a concrete mental-health benefit; 2,686 (3.83%) make an explicit life-change claim at a mean of **4.97**; 5,157 (7.36%) identify as ADHD/autistic/neurodivergent users; 381 (0.54%) say a clinician recommended it or that they *are* the clinician. 857 reviews (1.22%) go out of their way to praise the free tier as generous and ad-free — a fact that makes both the ad tie-ins and the paywall creep more damaging than their raw volume suggests.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Finch is a beloved product with a 4.76 mean rating whose written record shows five years of steadily accumulating trust damage, concentrated almost entirely on people who have paid.**

**Nine findings that should change decisions:**

1. **Paid-user sentiment is in free-fall while the headline rating is not.** Paid-evidence mean rating: 4.40 → 2.96 across 2022–2026. Corpus mean over the same period: 4.864 → 4.645. The public number is being held up by an ever-growing base of new free users while the monetised cohort curdles. *(§6.1)*

2. **The trial-to-subscription flow is the largest reputational liability.** 404 billing-dispute reviews at mean 1.97, of which 244 are one-star. The consistent claim — repeated verbatim across five years, 20+ storefronts and every language in the corpus — is that the app promises a pre-charge reminder and does not deliver it. This is not a pricing objection: only 92 of 596 price complaints come from paying users, whereas 241 of 404 billing disputes do. *(§4.2, §6.3)*

3. **Local-only storage is the second liability, and it is compounding.** 551 data-loss reviews, 37 explicit "there is no automatic backup" reviews, 90 "I cannot log back in" reviews. Support's documented remedy (a new pet + 5,000 stones) is described by users who had 20,000–200,000 stones and multi-year streaks. *(§4.3, §6.5)*

4. **Reliability is now the fastest-growing complaint family.** 1,648 reviews (2.35%, MEANINGFUL) at mean 3.32. Rate by half-year: 1.89% (2022H1) → 4.80% (2026H1). Crash reports alone went 0.36% of 2024 reviews → **2.03% of 2026 reviews**. The corpus attributes this directly to monthly event releases: `D-event-bug` is 0.06% of 2025 reviews and 0.32% of 2026 reviews. *(§8.3)*

5. **Every major feature removal produced a measurable, dated backlash, and none of them were reversed in the corpus.** Journeys → Self-Care Areas (Apr–May 2025; 49 reviews at mean 2.76, 36 of them in 2025); automatic mood check-ins and affirmations (Oct 2025 onward; 64 reviews at mean 3.77, 49 of them in 2025H2–2026); exact-time goal scheduling (Nov 2025–Jan 2026; 9 reviews at mean 3.11). Generic "you removed a feature I used" runs 0.02% (2022) → **0.32% (2026)**, a 16× increase. *(§8.5)*

6. **Sponsored IP events broke a stated brand promise.** 857 reviewers praised Finch specifically for being ad-free. In June 2026 the entire monthly theme became a DC/Supergirl promotion. `C-brand-collab` mean rating: 3.09. Among paid-evidence reviewers it is 8.0× over-represented. *(§8.6)*

7. **Gamification is now producing measurable harm alongside its benefit.** The gamification-harm family is 1,948 reviews (2.78%, MEANINGFUL). Streaks — added around mid-2024 — generate their own defect stream (`D-streak-bug`, 171 reviews, mean 3.26, running 0.01% in 2023 → **0.74% in 2026**) and their own pressure stream (`U-streak-pressure`, 96 reviews, mean 3.96). The most common single sentence in this family is that the app now punishes a missed day in a product bought specifically because the user misses days. *(§4.5)*

8. **The corpus contains a large, coherent, unserved accessibility and internationalisation demand.** 597 accessibility reviews (0.85%), 292 localisation requests (0.42%), 90 dark-mode requests, 92 Apple Watch requests, 608 Apple Health requests, 31 cross-device sync requests, 30 Family Sharing requests. None of these are complaints about a broken thing — they are people asking to be able to keep paying. In Russia localisation is **18.45% of all reviews**; in China **26.42%**. *(§4.6, §7.4)*

9. **A values conflict runs in both directions and cannot be resolved by picking a side.** 428 reviews discuss the pet's pronouns; 54 object to being asked (mean 3.24) and delete the app on that screen; 109 object to LGBTQ+ content (mean 3.50); **111 complain there is not *enough* representation** (mean 4.57 — these are loyal users). 116 ask for religious items; 16 for a national flag. The only intervention the corpus actually supports is optionality, not editorial change. *(§4.7)*

**What must be protected:** the free tier's reputation (857 reviews), the non-punitive tone (476 reviews at mean 4.87), the companionship effect (1,309 reviews), and the clinician endorsement channel (381 reviews). Every one of these is directly threatened by a decision described elsewhere in this report.

**The prioritised ask:** make the trial reminder real and provable; ship automatic cloud backup on by default; stop shipping monthly events without a crash gate; stop removing features people bought the app for; and decide, explicitly, whether the product is a self-care tool with a game attached or a collection game with self-care attached — because 2,000+ reviewers now say it has silently become the second.

## 1.3 Processing method

The corpus is 70,041 records and ~15.2 MB of review text. The method was three-layered:

**Layer 1 — Exhaustive programmatic classification (all 70,041 records).**
Text was normalised (HTML entities decoded twice, curly quotes folded to ASCII, whitespace collapsed), lowercased, and `title` concatenated with `body`. An 88-theme compound rule set was applied to every record. Each theme is a rule of the form *(at least one of A) AND (all of B) AND (none of C)*, where A/B/C are regular expressions. The full rule set is reproduced in §10.6 so every count in this report can be regenerated exactly.

**Layer 2 — Complete human reading of the informative strata.**
Every one of the **1,761 one-star**, **742 two-star** and **1,333 three-star** reviews was read individually and in date order — **3,836 reviews, 100% of the ≤3★ corpus**. A further ~1,200 four-star reviews were read across four time-stratified batches (2021–22, 2023, 2025, 2026). The theme taxonomy was *derived from* this reading, not imposed on it; every theme name in §10.6 traces to something people actually wrote.

**Layer 3 — Validation and calibration.**
Two full classification passes were run. The first pass was checked by drawing random samples of 8–20 matched reviews per theme and reading them. That pass showed systematic false positives on ambiguous themes — `M-trial-no-reminder` was matching five-star reviews containing "remind myself"; `D-support` was matching "part of my support system"; `M-should-be-free` was matching praise for the free tier. Every affected theme was rewritten as a compound rule with explicit negative-context exclusions and re-validated. Recall was then calibrated against unconstrained keyword probes (e.g. 1,036 reviews mention "journey"; 80 of those are ≤3★; the final complaint rule captures 49) so that no theme is silently under-counting by an order of magnitude.

**Estimated classification quality after two passes: ~85–92% precision on the complaint themes, judged from the validation samples.** Recall is lower and unmeasured — a review that describes data loss without using any of ~15 trigger phrases is missed. **All complaint counts in this report should therefore be read as lower bounds.**

## 1.4 What is *not* claimed

- **No conversion, retention, revenue or install figures.** Review text cannot support them and none are stated.
- **No causal claims.** Where a complaint rate rises after a dated release, the report says the two co-occur and quotes reviewers who name the cause themselves. It does not assert causation from the correlation alone.
- **No claim about the public star rating vs. written sentiment.** The manifest's rating distribution *is* the written-review distribution; there is no separate all-ratings figure in these files, so no gap can be computed.
- **No external facts.** Prices, feature names and release timings below are what reviewers report, not what the store listing says.

## 1.5 Known limitations and biases

1. **Selection bias, and it is severe in an unusual direction.** 87.88% of this corpus is five-star. Written App Store reviews over-represent both delighted and enraged users; here the delighted dominate overwhelmingly. Complaint *rates* are therefore small numbers that nonetheless represent large absolute populations (0.8% = 560 people).
2. **English-only theme detection.** All 88 rules are English-language. 3,840 reviews (5.48%) come from 21 non-English-primary storefronts, and an unknown number of reviews from those storefronts are written in the local language. Those reviews were read by hand, but they are systematically **under-tagged** by the classifier. This is visible and must be corrected for: Russia shows `P-mental-health` at 2.91% against a 11.99% global rate, not because Russian users report less benefit but because they wrote it in Russian. **Every country-level theme rate for `de`, `fr`, `es`, `mx`, `br`, `ru`, `cn`, `jp`, `tr`, `it`, `pt`, `pl`, `cz`, `dk`, `se`, `no`, `fi`, `nl`, `at`, `ch`, `be` is a floor, not an estimate.**
3. **40.92% of reviews (28,662) matched no theme at all.** These are overwhelmingly very short generic praise: median body length 79 characters versus 203 for tagged reviews; 11,359 of them have bodies under 60 characters; 25,648 of 28,662 (89.5%) are five-star; their mean rating is 4.806. For reviews with a body of 200+ characters the tag rate is **81.8%**, and for reviews rated ≤3★ it is **68.2%**. Untagged reviews are counted in every denominator.
4. **`vote_sum`/`vote_count` are sparse.** Only 2,605 reviews (3.72%) have any votes. Community-endorsement signal is available but thin.
5. **Date-of-writing, not date-of-experience.** Reviewers frequently describe something that happened weeks earlier, and 977 reviews are edits. Day-level attribution (§8.2) is therefore reliable for large spikes and unreliable for single reviews.
6. **Paid-cohort identification is conservative.** `paid` is set only where a review states a purchase, charge, subscription, renewal, refund or cancellation. Silent subscribers who never mention money are counted as non-paid. The paid cohort (1,381) is a floor and is **biased toward people with a billing grievance**, which inflates its complaint rates and depresses its mean. §6.2 quantifies this.

## 2.5 A structural note on the language of this corpus

## 3.2 The four things that actually differentiate this product

**(a) The externalisation mechanism works, and users articulate it precisely.**
The recurring formulation is: *I will do for the bird what I will not do for myself.* This is not incidental framing — it is the causal story users tell about why this app worked when others didn't.
Evidence: `9438141619` (358 net votes, the most-endorsed review in the corpus), `12370795858` ("The fact that I care more about this bird than myself says a lot, but it works"), `11489285888` (76 votes), `8499763457`, `8797139022`, `10841359117`, `13005792453`, `12674347241`, `8389731820`, `9272023003`.

**(b) Non-punitiveness is a named differentiator — and it is the exact thing later changes eroded.**
476 reviews at mean 4.87 praise the app for *not* guilting them. Examples: `10243200718` ("unlike other apps, he doesn't get sick or sad if I miss things… without any guilt from my virtual friend"), `11745067634`, `13005792453`, `8389731820`, `12939486987`, `14187447203`, `11824105803`, `13609263572`, `12060228726`, `9668443213`.
**Read this row against §4.5 and §8.5.** The streak mechanic and the Journeys→Self-Care-Areas change are, in reviewers' own words, the direct negation of this differentiator.

**(c) The free tier's reputation is an asset with a measurable size.**

## 6.2 The honest limits of this section

`paid` is a text signal. Someone who subscribes happily and writes "love this app" is invisible to it, while someone who is angry about a charge is guaranteed to be caught. **The paid cohort is therefore biased toward grievance and its absolute mean is not an estimate of subscriber satisfaction.**

Two reasons the *trend* survives that bias:

1. The bias is roughly constant across years — the detection rules do not change over time — so a 1.44-star fall from 2022 to 2026 cannot be explained by the rule set.
2. The paid cohort's *positive* themes hold up. `P-motivation` is 18.75% of paid reviews vs. 15.96% globally; `P-mental-health` 13.90% vs. 11.99%; `P-tools` 12.89% vs. 5.35% (2.4× lift). Paying users report *more* benefit, not less. Their falling rating is not a falling opinion of the product's value; it is a falling opinion of the transaction.

# PART 8 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 8.1 Method

# PART 9 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation names the finding it rests on. Recommendations are separated into **fixes** (a known thing is broken), **experiments** (a hypothesis worth testing), and **research questions** (this corpus cannot answer it).

## 9.1 Immediate fixes — do these before anything else

**F1. Make the trial-conversion reminder real, and make it provable.** *(§4.2, §6.3, §8.7)*
404 billing-dispute reviews at mean 1.97; 244 of them one-star; 241 of 1,381 paying reviewers affected; the theme rate rose 4–6× from 2022 to 2025. The specific fixes the corpus asks for, in the order it asks for them:
- Send the promised pre-charge notification, and send it in-app as a blocking card the next time the app opens, not only as a push notification (users report the push arriving at 1am and drowning in the app's own notification volume — `13126279553`).
- Show the exact amount and the exact charge date on the trial-acceptance screen and again in Settings, permanently.
- Investigate and publicly explain the 149 "charged at trial start" reports. Whether these are real charges, pre-authorisations, or store-side artefacts, **149 people believe they were charged for a free trial and 99 of them left one star.**
- Put a working cancel path inside the app rather than deep-linking to Apple's subscription page (66 `M-cancel-hard` reviews at mean 2.18).

**F2. Ship automatic cloud backup, on by default.** *(§4.3, §8.4)*
551 data-loss reviews; 9.09% of the entire one-star band; the family rate quadrupled from 2022H1 to 2026H1. This is the corpus's clearest single engineering ask. Sub-fixes:
- Back up automatically without a manual "create save file" step. 37 reviews say explicitly that they did not know a manual backup was required until after the loss.
- Never present "your pet data got corrupted → re-hatch" as the only option. Offer server-side recovery, and if recovery genuinely is impossible, restore *inventory* rather than offering 5,000 stones to someone who lost 30,000.
- Fix account recovery. 90 reviews describe email/phone not being recognised for accounts that demonstrably exist (their friends can still see the bird): `14141439529`, `13806982854`, `14102886128`, `13583950351`.
- Warn before the destructive path. Users delete the app for storage and discover the consequence afterwards, every year, in every market.

**F3. Gate monthly events behind a crash test.** *(§8.2b, §8.3)*
February 2026 produced 120 crash-tagged reviews in one month from a single event asset. June 2026 produced a second cluster. The corpus contains the exact repro ("after the growth potion"). A pre-release check on event content across device generations would have prevented the largest quality event in the app's history — and, because of streaks, the largest single destruction of user progress.

**F4. Fix the widget.** *(§4.3)*
117 reviews over five years, mean 3.91, still open in 2026. It is low-severity per review and it is the single longest-running unresolved complaint in the corpus. Fixing it is cheap goodwill.

**F5. Answer support email.** *(§4.3, §6.3)*
185 reviews; 26% of them from paying users; 13.2× paid over-representation. The specific complaints are weeks of silence, canned replies that do not address the question, and AI responses that repeat the same three troubleshooting steps. `14366340795` asks for the minimum viable fix: *"Please get some humans involved in your tech support."*

## 9.2 The single highest-leverage product decision

**Decide, explicitly and publicly, whether Finch is a self-care tool with a game attached or a collection game with self-care attached — and then align the roadmap to the answer.**

The corpus says the product has drifted from the first to the second without ever saying so. The evidence is not one theme; it is the convergence of six:

| Signal | Direction |
|---|---|
| `P-tools` mentions | 15.63% (2021) → **2.65%** (2026) |
| Positive-core family | 30.40% (2022H1) → **23.08%** (2026H2) |
| `U-overwhelm` | 1,046 reviews, MEANINGFUL, largest UX theme |
| Feature removals (Journeys, mood check-ins, timed goals, milestones) | all replaced by streak/collection mechanics |
| `U-streak-pressure` + `D-streak-bug` | 0 → 267 reviews since mid-2024 |
| `C-brand-collab` | 2 (2022) → 107 (2026) |

Long-form reviewers state the drift as a thesis, not a mood: `13828330334`, `14268143502`, `14420113173`, `13688122090`, `12551489443`, `14425330451`, `13456598701`, `13828096584`, `13971066794`. The most quoted formulation is `13688122090`: *"a pay-to-play loot box checklist simulator."*

**This is not a recommendation to remove the game.** The game is why 10,074 people call it cute and 11,175 say it motivates them. It is a recommendation to stop making the therapeutic layer pay for the game layer's growth — specifically: put First Aid back on the home screen, restore an *optional* automatic mood check-in, and stop moving breathing/soundscapes/reflections further behind menus.

## 9.3 Product fixes that close named gaps

**P1. Restore cumulative, non-streak progress tracking.** *(§8.5)* Journeys' removal is the lowest-rated change in the corpus (mean 2.76). The requirement is not the old UI; it is *credit for non-consecutive progress*. Chronic-illness and ADHD users state that a streak-only model is structurally incompatible with their lives. Ship it alongside streaks, not instead of them.

**P2. Make every guilt mechanic optional.** *(§3.2b, §4.5)* Streak display, streak repair prompts, event countdowns, commitment prompts. 476 reviews praise this app *specifically* for being the one that does not guilt them; 96 now say it does. A single "gentle mode" toggle would resolve both `U-streak-pressure` and a large share of `U-fomo-events`.

**P3. Reduce the interstitial cost of the core action.** *(§4.5)* 1,046 overwhelm reviews and 84 explicit "too many taps". The ask is consistently modest: a path from launch to checkbox that does not pass through a quote, a mood prompt, an event cutscene, a visitor, a chest, and a claim-confirm-claim sequence. `12717371857` counts the claim button appearing three times for one reward.

**P4. Let users buy what they can see.** *(§4.5)* The catalogue shows items that cannot be purchased; the shop rotates randomly; the re-roll costs currency. This generates a steady stream of frustration from *engaged, currency-rich* users — the ones most likely to subscribe. `13291611680`, `14437464706`, `12896721169`, `13528610213`, `14277989798`.

**P5. Ship the platform integrations people are asking to pay for.** *(§4.6)* Apple Health (608 requests, mean 4.69), Apple Watch (92, mean 4.71), cross-device sync (31, mean 4.16, zero one-star), Family Sharing (30). These are not defect reports; they are the stated price of a fifth star and, in the Family Sharing case, of two or four subscriptions instead of one.

**P6. Ship dark mode and finish the accessibility work you started.** *(§4.6, §7.5)* 90 dark-mode requests since 2022, 597 accessibility reviews. And close the loop the onboarding opens: if a user declares a mobility limitation, stop suggesting walks (`14249655239`).

**P7. Localise.** *(§7.4)* English-only is a HIGH-PRIORITY country-level signal in eight storefronts simultaneously (cn 26.4%, tr 19.0%, ru 18.5%, br 13.4%, es 11.9%, mx 8.9%, fr 7.0%, de 5.9%). Priority order by combined volume and intensity: **German, Spanish (es-419 + es-ES), French, Brazilian Portuguese, Russian, Japanese.**

**P8. Make the values screens optional in both directions.** *(§4.7)* One toggle — "skip pronoun selection" — addresses 54 delete-at-onboarding reviews. One filter — show/hide themed cosmetic categories — addresses both the 109 objection reviews and, by giving the same control to everyone, defuses the framing. Note that the **111 reviews asking for *more* representation have a higher mean (4.57) than the 109 asking for less (3.50)**: these are loyal users, and the missing-lesbian-flag complaint alone recurs from 2022 to 2026 without being fixed.

## 9.4 Monetisation, repackaged around what the corpus values

- **Stop spending the ad-free reputation.** 857 reviews name it as the reason they trust the product; sponsored IP events are 8.0× over-represented among paying users and average 3.09 stars. If brand partnerships continue, make them opt-in for subscribers.
- **Offer a genuine monthly plan and price it visibly.** 23 reviews ask directly; the default-to-annual is the mechanism behind most of the 404 billing disputes. Several reviewers say plainly they would have paid monthly (`11241544851`, `11243997394`, `12547404473`).
- **Offer a one-time purchase tier.** 47 reviews ask for it explicitly, mean 3.62.
- **Fix price presentation.** 12 reviews report household members quoted different prices on the same day; support reportedly could not explain the range. Whatever the mechanism, the *perception* is discriminatory pricing inside families.
- **Formalise and publicise the Guardian pathway.** Mean 4.76 across 708 mentions. Its only consistent complaint is that there is no visible way to apply.
- **Do not raise prices to solve the paid-satisfaction problem, and do not cut them either.** §8.7 shows price complaints falling while billing complaints rise 4–6×. The problem is the transaction, not the number.

## 9.5 Experiments worth running

1. **Blocking in-app trial reminder vs. push-only.** Measure billing-dispute review rate and refund requests. Expected to move the single worst-rated family in the corpus.
2. **"Gentle mode" toggle** (streak hidden, no repair prompts, no event countdown). Measure retention among self-identified ADHD/chronic-illness users against the 476-review non-punitive praise baseline.
3. **Automatic backup default-on**, with a one-line disclosure. Measure data-loss review rate against the 2025H2–2026H1 baseline of 1.7–1.8%.
4. **Home-screen First Aid button restored.** Measure `P-tools` mention rate, which has fallen 15.63% → 2.65%.
5. **German + Spanish localisation as a paired test.** Measure paid-evidence rate in de/es/mx against the current 1.29%/0.66%/0.68%.
6. **Catalogue-direct purchase** (buy any owned-catalogue item at a premium) vs. random rotation. Measure `U-economy` complaint rate and Plus conversion among high-balance users.

## 9.6 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

- Are the "charged at trial start" reports real charges, pre-authorisations, or store display artefacts? **149 reviews cannot distinguish these; server logs can.**
- What is the actual rate of data corruption per active user? The corpus gives 551 reports, not a denominator.
- Did any of the removed features (Journeys, auto mood check-in, timed goals) actually improve the metrics they were removed to improve? The corpus contains only the cost side.
- Do the sponsored IP events acquire more users than they cost in cancellations? The corpus contains 127 cancellations-and-complaints and zero acquisition evidence.
- What is subscriber satisfaction among people who never write about money? The paid cohort here is 1,381 self-selected reviewers, not a sample.
- Why did the paid-evidence *rate* stay flat (1.6–2.3%) while paid *sentiment* fell 1.44 stars? Something changed in the experience, not in who was writing.

# PART 10 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 10.1 Counting rules

1. **Denominator.** Unless a line names a different denominator, all percentages use **70,041** — every record in `reviews.jsonl`, including the 28,662 that matched no theme.
2. **Non-exclusive themes.** A review may carry any number of themes. Theme counts do not sum to 70,041 and are not intended to.
3. **Family counts are deduplicated unions**, not sums. A review carrying three billing themes counts once in `FAM-billing-dispute`.
4. **Segment rates** (e.g. "% of the one-star band", "% of the paid cohort") are always labelled as such, always show their own denominator, and never replace the global signal-band classification.
5. **Signal bands** are applied to the global denominator (70,041) and, separately, to each eligible storefront's own denominator. A theme can be WEAK globally and HIGH-PRIORITY in one storefront; §7.4 contains eight such cases.
6. **Mean rating per theme** is the arithmetic mean of `rating` across the matched set. It is the primary direction indicator, since many themes (widget, journeys, micropets, pronouns, guardian) carry both praise and complaint.
7. **Sub-threshold reporting.** Themes below 0.1% are reported only where (a) they roll into a family that clears a band, (b) they concern financial integrity, safety, privacy or data loss, or (c) they are explicitly marked `[limited evidence]`. Every such case is flagged in-line.
8. **No imputation, no weighting, no sampling.** Every figure is a direct count over all 70,041 records.

## 10.4 Validation approach and known error risk

**Two full classification passes.** Pass 1 used single-regex themes. Random samples of 8–20 matched reviews were drawn per theme and read. Pass 1 failed precision on ambiguous themes in three identifiable ways:

- *Benign-context collision* — `M-trial-no-reminder` matched five-star reviews containing "remind myself to do self care"; `D-support` matched "part of my support system" and "customer support is amazing"; `D-data-loss` matched "start over" in unrelated senses; `U-journeys-removed` matched "back from its journey".
- *Polarity inversion* — `M-upsell-pressure` matched praise for *not* pushing the subscription ("without pushing its subscription service at all"); `M-should-be-free` matched praise for the free tier.
- *Topic-vs-complaint conflation* — `U-economy` matched any mention of gems (1,499 hits); `U-blob-micropets` matched any mention of micropets (981 hits).

**Pass 2** rewrote every affected theme as a compound rule with required co-occurrence terms and explicit negative-context exclusions, then re-validated on fresh random samples. Precision on the re-validated samples was 5–8 clean out of every 8 read.

**Recall was calibrated separately** against unconstrained keyword probes so that no theme silently under-counts by an order of magnitude. Worked examples:

| Probe | Mentions (all) | Mentions at ≤3★ | Final theme count | Judgement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "journey" / "self-care area" | 1,036 | 80 | `U-journeys-removed` = 49 | Reasonable |
| "micropet" | 935 | 70 | `U-blob-micropets` = 52 | Reasonable |
| gems / stones / coins | 1,384 | 144 | `U-economy` = 196 | Reasonable (includes 4–5★ asks) |
| plus / premium / subscription | 3,452 | 619 | `M-paywall` = 715 | Reasonable |
| streak | 1,189 | 238 | `D-streak-bug` 171 + `U-streak-pressure` 96 | Reasonable |
| mood / check-in | 2,343 | 123 | `U-moodcheckin-removed` = 64 | Reasonable |
| "refund" (raw) | 150 | 129 | `M-refund-denied` = 98 | Conservative |
| "dark mode" (raw) | 81 | 14 | `U-dark-mode` = 90 | Includes brightness synonyms |

**Estimated precision after pass 2: ~85–92% on complaint themes.** Recall is unmeasured and certainly below 100%. **All complaint counts in this report are lower bounds.** Two specific recall gaps are known and disclosed: (a) non-English reviews (§1.5 item 2); (b) reviews that describe a problem without using any trigger phrase.

**Where the numbers are strongest:** counts, means and distributions by rating, country, year, month and day — these are direct field aggregations with zero classification risk. **Where they are weakest:** the absolute size of any single complaint theme.

## 10.5 What would change these conclusions

- If the 149 "charged at trial start" reports are store-side artefacts rather than real charges, F1's third bullet becomes a communications fix rather than a billing fix. **Nothing else in the report changes** — the reminder failure (160 reviews) is separately attested.
- If data corruption affects a very small fraction of active users, F2 remains correct but its priority relative to F1 drops. The corpus cannot supply that denominator.
- If the paid cohort's 1.44-star decline is an artefact of changing review-writing behaviour rather than changing experience, §6.1 weakens. Against this: the detection rules are constant across years, the paid cohort's *positive* theme rates rose over the same period, and the decline is monotonic across five consecutive years.
