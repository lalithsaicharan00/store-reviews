### ⚠️ Eight warnings before any number here is used

1. **This is a Brazilian app with a Brazilian review corpus.** 375 of 498 reviews (75.30%) come from the `br` storefront and 74.70% of the corpus is written in Portuguese. **Brazil is the only storefront that clears the 50-review bar.** Every other storefront — including the US (27) — is reported as **[limited evidence]** and carries no standalone conclusion. Read Part 7 before generalising anything here to a global audience.
2. **The written corpus is not the rating base.** 498 written reviews against **5,366 public ratings** = **9.28% coverage**. Public average **4.76**; written mean **3.677**. That is a **1.08-star gap** — a normal written-review negativity skew, not an anomaly, but it means this report describes *what people bother to write about*, which is disproportionately what broke.
3. **One theme owns the last three years of the corpus.** `REM_FAIL` — "the app does not send notifications" — appears in 66 reviews (13.25%), and **58 of the 127 reviews written since Nov 2023 (45.7%) name it**. For a habit tracker, this is not a feature gap; it is the product failing at its one job. See §3.3 N1 and §8.4 I5.
4. **Monetization changed three times in six years, and each change is visible in the ratings.** Free-only (mean 4.49) → optional premium (4.19) → ads + a 5-habit free cap (2.98) → the notification era (2.98). §8.2 gives the era table; §2.2 gives the price history reviewers actually report.
5. **Reported prices are inconsistent enough to be a defect in their own right.** Four reviewers across 2022–2023 report the monthly price as **R$299–R$300** or **R$600** where others report **R$29.90** (`8787259760`, `8841492479`, `8982876143`, `9305007119`). One of them uninstalled over it. See §2.2 and §6.3.
6. **49 reviewers (9.84%) state they paid, and their mean rating is 2.061** — against a corpus mean of 3.677. **27 of 49 payers (55.1%) left one star.** People who pay for this app are, on this evidence, its least satisfied users. Part 6 is the most consequential section of this report.
7. **6 reviews (1.20%) contradict their own star rating**, all six of them 5★ reviews whose text is negative — including `7006243585` (*"Absurdo de caro o premium"*) and `9145791905` (*"the app has been crashing whenever i open it"*). They are not silently dropped; see §5.6.
8. **No review burst distorts this corpus.** The busiest calendar day is 4 reviews across 431 distinct days; no day exceeds 0.80% of the corpus. There are **zero exact text duplicates, zero repeated authors**, and only one edited review. Trends in Part 8 are genuine drift, not one event.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

**Executive summary in one line:** Rabit earned a genuinely loved position in Brazil — a cute, simple, plant-growing habit tracker whose free tier was praised as the most generous in its category — and then spent four years dismantling that position: a premium tier that removed what people already had, ads that fire on habit completion, a free cap cut to 4–5 habits, and finally a **notification system that has been broken or entirely absent for three years**, which is the single largest theme in the corpus and the reason a habit app stops being a habit app.

## 0.1 Notifications are broken, and that is the whole story since late 2023

`REM_FAIL` = **66 reviews (13.25%, High-priority, mean 2.91★)**. Concentrated almost entirely after Nov 2023: **58 of the 127 reviews written in that window (45.7%)**.

The reports are mechanically precise and independently consistent, which is what makes them credible. **14 reviewers (2.81%) do not report "notifications are unreliable" — they report that Rabit does not appear in the iOS Settings → Notifications list at all**, so permission cannot be granted from either side:

| `10879516524` | 2024-01-30 | `us` | 4 | *"there is no option to turn on notifications for the app"* |

That symptom — absent from the OS notification registry, across iPhone and iPad, across seven storefronts, across 31 months — is not a per-user permission mistake. > `13253396607` (`ca`, 2★): *"That is the point of an app like this, having reminders."*

**It is also directly costing revenue.** Three reviewers state they were about to buy and stopped because of it — `11213091836` (*"Eu ia comprar o Vitalício… Sem notificação o app não faz muito sentido, uma pena eu ia assinar o vitalicio"*), `11410699904` (*"estava pensando em comprar o acesso vitalício, mas…"*), `10819138875` (*"Estou testando para poder assinar, porém duas funções primordiais para mim não funcionam"*) — and three more paid first and then found out (`11038596675`, `11391895419`, `11783159029`).

## 0.3 The free tier was the differentiator, and it was removed

For two and a half years, the single most-repeated praise in this corpus was *"and it's free"* — and specifically, *"free without the three-habit cap everyone else has."* `PAY_FREE_OK` = **48 (9.64%, mean 4.88★)**, and it was explicitly positioned against competitors:

> `7187761828` (`mx`, 5★): *"te deja añadir más de 3 hábitos en su versión gratuita"*
> `9056065887` (`us`, 5★): *"no ridiculous paywalls. everything you need is free. you can log more than three habits for the free version"*
> `7701107699` (`br`, 5★): *"A maioria dos apps que propõem uma organização por meio de rotinas tem compras dentro do app que limitam muito o consumidor!"*
> `8718500987` (`br`, 5★): *"Fiquei perplexa ao baixar e vê que as funções premium não são as funções principais"*
> `7922511254`, `8319400804`, `9154786620`, `11564126929` say the same thing.

Then the cap arrived. `PAY_FREE_LIMIT` = **34 (6.83%, mean 2.00★)**, of which **26 fall in Jan–Nov 2023 alone (26.3% of that window)**. Reviewers report the cap at 3, 4, 5 and 6 habits in different months — `9668193107` (3), `9702733718` (4), `10188502479` (4), `9550931363` (5), `10032904573` (5), `10047293918` (6), `10166609858` (6) — which suggests the limit was moved repeatedly rather than set once.

The reaction is the mirror image of the earlier praise. `PAY_REGRESS` = 21 (4.22%, mean 1.48★); `COMP_WORSE` = 11 (2.21%, mean 2.09★):

> `9550931363` (`br`, 1★): *"um aplicativo que era incrível e muito bem avaliado conseguiu se tornar um aplicativo de hábitos como qualquer outro"* — an app that was incredible and very well rated managed to become a habit app like any other.
> `9933931455` (`au`, 2★): *"now it's like every single habit tracker app out there"*
> `9552323568` (`br`, 2★): *"Antes o Rabit não tinha anúncios, e dava para colocar vários hábitos… Eu adorava esse app, fiquei muito triste com a mudança."*
> `9574499267` (`br`, 2★): *"eu recomendei pra umas 30 pessoas, ate eles se importarem mais em ganhar dinheiro que ajudar os outros"*

**The strategic point:** Rabit's free tier was not a cost — it was the acquisition engine and the review engine. `PAY_FREE_OK` carries a mean of 4.88★ and was named in 48 reviews. It ran at 15.3% of era E2 and fell to 2.2% in E3.

## 0.4 Ads were introduced on completion, which is the worst possible trigger

`ADS_BAD` = 15 (3.01%, mean 2.33★), 13 of them in E3. The complaint is not the volume in the abstract — it is **where** the ad fires:

> `9554820095` (`mx`, 3★): *"Cada que completo un hábito me aparece un anuncio!!!"* — every time I complete a habit an ad appears.
> `9468996936` (`mx`, 1★): *"No te permite terminar una sola tarea porque ya te sale un anuncio… vengo a desinstalarla después de ver unos 40 anuncios"*
> `9425433726` (`us`, 2★): *"the ads appear every 30ish seconds. They don't even let you finish what you were doing"*
> `9582620346` (`br`, 2★): *"a cada hábito que eu crio é um anúncio"*

An app whose entire behavioural model is *complete the habit → see your plant grow → feel rewarded* inserted an interstitial exactly at the reward moment. `9554337978` names the intended mechanism out loud: *"se o objetivo é fazer pagar o premium ou desistir de usar o app"* — if the goal is to make you pay for premium, or give up on the app.

The plant/garden metaphor is named as the motivating mechanic by 21 reviewers (4.22%, mean 4.62★):

And people report outcomes. `[U:positive_outcome]` = **68 (13.65%, mean 4.88★)**; `OUT_LIFE` alone is 47 (9.44%) at mean 4.89★ — weight loss (`6056995445`), quitting destructive habits and learning a language (`7118806721`), medication adherence (`10386047751`), managing depression (`12993620841`), exam routines (`8521754652`, `9545808359`), and ADHD (`8564602907`).

## 0.6 The widget has been requested for five years and never shipped

`REQ_WIDGET` = **43 (8.63%, High-priority, mean 4.30★)** — the single largest feature request, and unusual for carrying a *high* mean rating: these are satisfied users telling you the one thing missing. First asked on 2021-01-15 (`6872045225`, days after the iOS 14 widget launch); still being asked on 2025-08-25 (`13059862926`). It runs at 7.9% / 10.9% / 11.0% of eras E2 / E3 / E4 — it has never faded.

**Four reviewers make a purchase conditional on it**: `11570813316` (*"I would gladly pay for the premium version, but there are no widgets"*), `12002090537` (*"I'll definitely buy the lifetime plus version if you add widgets"*), `12794885676` (*"If this app had them I would 100% buy the lifetime"*), `10316293721` (*"I am going to hold off on subscribing until at least the first feature is met"*).

## 0.7 Scheduling by fixed weekdays is the most-requested product change

`CORE_FREQ` = 9 (1.81%, mean 4.11★) — *all nine from Brazil*, spread across five years, all describing the same missing capability: **"X times per week, any day"**.

> `8437662124` (`br`, 4★): *"Preciso estudar música 3x por semana (0/3). E ao ir concluindo durante a semana, o hábito mostrasse o progresso ou (1/3), (2/3) e (3/3)."*
> `12403935823` (`br`, 4★): *"minha rotina não permite sempre me exercitar nos mesmo dias"*
> `8590687133` (`br`, 4★): *"Sem isso a assinatura não vale a pena."* — without this the subscription isn't worth it.

Related and equally structural: `6621159448` and `9444567013` cannot record partial completion of a multiple-times-per-day habit; `12590814225` and `8590687133` want to skip a legitimately impossible day without killing the plant — `12590814225` asks, in earnest, *"are we supposed to lie?"*

## 0.8 Stability has been a recurring, not a constant, problem

`[U:stability]` = **73 (14.66%, High-priority, mean 2.04★)**. It arrives in waves tied to releases, not as a steady background rate — see §8.4 for all five incident windows. The two sharpest:

- **A first-run block, 16–31 Jul 2021**: 12 of 26 reviews in that fortnight (46.2%, mean 1.42★) report being unable to pass the onboarding intro. Multiple reviewers describe the identical stopping point — the "good habits are like growing a tree" screen (`7609032665`, `7624625109`) or "the second dot" (`7583592939`, `7625465634`). It was fixed: `7630352804`, 29 Jul, *"CORRIGIRAM O BUG… OBRIGADAAAA!"*
- **A crash wave, Apr–Nov 2022**: 13 reviews (19.4% of the window, mean 1.77★) report the app closing 2–3 seconds after launch. **Eight of the thirteen are from paying subscribers.** `9243504386` (1 Nov 2022) notes it had already been reported and assumed fixed: *"Havia visto comentários anteriores sobre isso, mas achei que já havia sido resolvido."*

The same first-run class of bug returned in 2023–2024 in a new form: the onboarding questionnaire's advance button renders **below the visible screen** (`11052620853`, `11520264880`, `11036949364`) — a layout bug, not a logic bug, and trivially fixable.

## 0.9 The app now looks unmaintained, and reviewers say so

`DEV_STALE` = 5 (1.00%), all in 2025–2026. The tell is in-app copy that never changed:

> `12262271121` (`us`, 4★, Feb 2025): *"It hasn't been updated in a while though, and it keeps saying to start your '2024' year strong… when it's 2025."*
> `12297664838` (`br`, 4★, Feb 2025): *"a mensagem inicial ainda é de 2024"*
> `14502281920` (`br`, 4★, Sep 2026): *"Poderiam verificar o anúncio de compra que está em 2024?"* — **still 2024, eighteen months later.**
> `14201849929` (`br`, 2★, Jun 2026): *"Tinha tudo pra ser do topo de app de hábitos mas não atualiza"*

Review volume tells the same story: 120 reviews in 2022 → 106 in 2023 → 67 in 2024 → 30 in 2025 → 12 in the first eight months of 2026.

## 0.10 The seven decisions this corpus supports

1. **Ship a notification fix as the next release, before anything else.** 66 reviews, 45.7% of everything written since Nov 2023, at least three abandoned purchases. Start by verifying the app requests notification authorization at all — 14 reviewers say it is absent from iOS Settings.
2. **Audit the entitlement path.** All 9 `PAY_ENTITLE` reports come from payers; restore-purchase and cross-device sign-in are broken in a way that makes people pay twice or lose access entirely (§6.4).
3. **Answer support email.** Five 1★ reviews, all payers, all describing silence. This is the cheapest rating repair available.
4. **Move the ad out of the completion moment.** §0.4.
5. **Restore a defensible free tier.** The free tier *was* the differentiator; capping it at 4–5 habits converted the app's best asset into its most common complaint (§0.3).
6. **Ship the widget.** Five years of requests at a 4.30★ mean, with four explicit conditional purchases attached (§0.6).
7. **Ship "X times per week."** Nine requests, all Brazilian, one of which names it as the reason not to subscribe (§0.7).

## 1.4 Deduplication rule

**No records were deduplicated.** There are **zero exact `(title, body)` duplicate pairs** and **zero authors appearing more than once** in this corpus, so the question does not arise. The denominator for every global percentage is therefore 498.

## 1.5 Processing method

1. `65-dump.py` — unescape, sort by `(date, review_id)`, assign working index 1–498, emit `65-all-reviews.txt`, `65-chunks/`, `65-index.tsv`.
2. Read all ten chunks in order. Record one line per review in `65-cls/NNN.txt` (`index CODE,CODE`). The codebook grew while reading; codes introduced late were applied backwards by re-reading the affected reviews (two codes were renamed this way — `ADHD`→`NEURO`, `REQ_ICON`→`REQ_HABITICON` — and `CORE_FREQ` was split out of `CORE_FLEX_NEG` after review #199 made the distinction clear).
3. `65-themes.py` — the 131-code, 13-family taxonomy with a one-line definition and a direction for every code.
4. `65-build-classification.py` — resolves index → `review_id`, asserts against the source, refuses on any undefined code, duplicate index, or unclassified record. Writes `65-review-classification.py`. That file is generated; the hand-made artefact is `65-cls/`.
5. `65-validate.py` → `65-validate-out.txt`. All eight integrity checks pass (§1.3 table).
6. `65-lang.py` — per-review language. Regex seed plus an explicit hand-set override table for every record the regexes could not settle (short, emoji-only, stylised-unicode, or gibberish reviews). Each override was set by reading the review.
7. `65-aggregate.py`, `65-extra.py`, `65-tables.py` — counts, unions, rating cuts, storefronts, eras, incident windows, per-theme ID lists, the Part 10 index.
8. `65-build-report.py` — fills `GEN`, `F` and `ID` placeholders from generated data and refuses to write on any unresolved placeholder. Every number in this report is machine-filled from the classification; none was typed by hand.
9. `65-verify-report.py` → `65-verify-out.txt` — post-write checks on the finished file (§A.6).

## 1.6 Limitations — read these before citing any figure

1. **Single-storefront dominance.** 75.30% of the corpus is Brazil. Global percentages in Part 3 are, in practice, Brazilian percentages with a 123-review tail. Where a theme's Brazil and rest-of-world rates diverge materially, §7.4 says so.
2. **Only one storefront clears 50 reviews.** No standalone claim is made about the US (27), Mexico (14), Spain (8), Canada (8), or any of the other 23.
3. **Written reviews are not users.** 498 written reviews against 5,366 public ratings (9.28% coverage). Nothing here supports a claim about the silent majority, and no conversion or churn *rate* is claimed anywhere in this report.
4. **`PAY_PAID` is evidence of payment, not a sample of payers.** It captures people who said they paid — a group heavily skewed toward those with a problem worth writing about. §6.1 states this again where it matters.
5. **No version field exists in the data.** "After the update" claims are the reviewer's own attribution; the corpus cannot confirm which build they were on. Era boundaries in §8.1 are derived from when complaints *appear*, not from a release manifest.
6. **Themes are non-exclusive and hand-assigned.** A single reader applied the codebook; there is no second-rater agreement statistic. §A.4 lists the judgement calls where a different reader would plausibly differ.

## 2.4 One structural observation about the model

Rabit monetises the **number** of habits. Its behavioural engine — plants, streaks, reminders — is what people love, and it rewards *consistency*, not *volume*. So the paywall taxes the axis users do not value while leaving the axis they do value free-but-broken. That mismatch shows up directly: `10008521499` asks for exactly the inverse model — *"Seria legal manter uma versão free com outras funcionalidades pagas mas não bloquear o uso do aplicativo"* — keep a free version with other paid features, but don't block use of the app.

The competitive position was not lost to a competitor's feature. It was given away.

- **14 reviewers (2.81%) report the app is absent from iOS Settings → Notifications** — the permission cannot be granted at all. This points to a missing or failing authorization request. §0.1 lists them.
- The remaining ~52 report notifications configured in-app that simply never fire. That is a scheduling/delivery failure, and it may be the downstream symptom of the same root cause.

Cross-cutting evidence that it is one defect, not user error: it is reported from iPhone 13 (`10819138875`), iPhone 14 / iOS 16 (`10142865647`) and iPad (`10966742339`); in Portuguese, English, Spanish, German and Turkish; by free users and by payers; and by people who explicitly report reinstalling (`10775104885`, `13253396607`) and checking both settings screens (`10879516524`, `12988341658`).

**Rating effect:** `REM_FAIL` reviews average 2.91★. But note the direction of the damage — 15 of the 66 are 4★ and 9 are 5★. These are people who *like the app* and are telling you the one thing that stops it working. Representative: `14001951104` (4★) *"Depois de baixar vários apps esse com certeza é o melhor… falta apenas isso então o app seria perfeito."*

## 3.7 What is *not* a finding here

- **Social / friends / accountability partners.** Zero mentions, in either direction.

# PART 4 — WHAT ACTIVE USERS SAY ABOUT THE PRODUCT

Part 3 ranks themes. This part reads them as a product description: what the people who actually use Rabit day to day report about it.

## 4.1 The garden is the retention mechanic, and people describe it as such

Reviewers do not describe Rabit as a checklist. They describe a relationship with a plant. `7352688259` warns others *"só tenha cuidado na hora de não fazer as a tarefas pq aí sua plantinha morre"*; `7419859413` says *"ver minhas plantinhas crescendo me incentiva a continuar"*; `7066205785` says *"quando vc faz algo sua plantinha cresce, isso dá muita vontade de sempre estar cumprindo com sua rotina"*. `8373713257`, a US reviewer of a year's standing, calls it *"motivating, beautiful, clever and engaging"* and asks for more plants.

Two consequences follow, and both are actionable:

1. **The garden is under-built relative to its importance.** Three reviewers ask for more plant varieties (`8241031108`, `8373713257`, `8417919585`); one asks for the garden to become a place rather than a list (`13038216598`: custom placement, tap-a-plant-for-detail, *"more like FarmTown"*); one asks the widget to show the garden and warn when a plant is close to death (`9823208283`).
2. **Plant death is the single riskiest design decision in the product.** It is the source of both `GAM_PLANT`'s positives and `GAM_PLANT_NEG`'s negatives, and the difference between the two is entirely about *fairness*. Users accept the penalty when they deserved it and reject it when they did not — which is why `BUG_STREAK` (false failures) and the absence of `CORE_SKIP` (legitimate exemptions) are disproportionately damaging here compared with a plain streak app.

## 4.2 Scheduling is where the product model meets real life and loses

Rabit schedules habits by **fixed weekdays**. Nine Brazilian reviewers over five years ask for frequency-based scheduling instead (§0.7), and the reasons they give are all the same shape: *my week is not identical every week*. `12403935823`: *"minha rotina não permite sempre me exercitar nos mesmo dias."* `6319309581`, as early as Aug 2020, asked for a weekly-any-day option (*"testa uma nova receita toda semana"*).

Adjacent gaps in the same area:
- Multiple times per day exists as an option but cannot be partially completed (`6621159448`: *"tenho que tomar vitamina 2x ao dia, porém não consigo marcar caso eu tenho tomado apenas 1x. É tudo ou nada"*; `9444567013`: *"the 'times per day' option… you can't check off a habit multiple times during the day? … i'm not really sure what it is supposed to be used for"*).
- The day-reset time is fixed at midnight (`10316293721`: *"My day does not start or end at 12am… I track my water usage past midnight and it doesn't reset after I sleep??"*).
- Week start cannot be Monday (`7633137657`, `7700545328`, `8222996948` — all three report only Friday and Saturday being offered).

Taken together, `CORE_FREQ` + `CORE_SKIP` + `CORE_MULTI` + `REQ_RESETTIME` + `BUG_WEEKSTART` = **15 reviews (3.01%, very strong)** all describing the same underlying model limitation.

## 4.3 The timer is an unexploited differentiator

Few habit trackers ship a per-habit timer. Rabit does, and reviewers notice it — `8020728448` lists it among the reasons to install; `8088356750` (*"tem até timer e é de graça!!!!!"*); `7565776979` uses it for study revision; `6109090746` for studying. But it has been broken the same way since month two of the corpus: **it stops counting when the app is backgrounded or the screen locks** (`6088717287` in Jun 2020, `6093103985` four days later, and still `10819138875` in Jan 2024 — *"contagem de tempo (que após uns minutos o app para de contar)"*). `6088717287` spells out the consequence: *"eu estudei por volta de 1 hora e marcou apenas alguns segundos."*

`7851322556` proposes the cheap workaround the team never shipped: manual time entry, *"Hoje isso só acontece com o timer em tempo real."*

## 4.4 Lists, routines and grouping are half-built

The to-do list is a real, separately-monetised surface (`7483566770` calls it *"having different lists for tasks is great"*). But: it is paywalled in a way reviewers call unfair (`9154786620`: *"só o todo-list que não é [gratuito], achei meio injusto"*), it disappeared for one payer (`9506447076`), it has no reminders at all (`11268878695`: *"die sinnlose Aufgabenliste ohne Erinnerungsfunktion… Total sinnfrei und schade"* — the pointless task list with no reminder function), and grouping habits into routines caps at two even for subscribers (`10370996938`) or duplicates habits and empties tabs (`10802638244`).

## 4.5 One surface, no ecosystem

`[U:platform_gaps]` = 55 (11.04%). Rabit is an iPhone app and nothing else. No widget (43 requests), no Watch (5), no working iPad layout (`6971635908` in Feb 2021 asked for one; `10914233763` in Feb 2024 reports it crashing on iPad iOS 12.5; `10966742339` reports reminders not appearing on an iPad screen), no desktop (`8656794708`, `10316293721`), and sync that people ask for as though it does not exist (`6172014611` in 2020, `11357344130` and `11363423435` in 2024).

The Android build exists but does not bridge: `7743115044` moved Android→Android fine and then could not find the sign-in button on iPhone; `9330471845` states the conclusion flatly — *"a opção de restaurar só acontece de celular androide para androide"*.

## 4.6 Specialist and sensitive uses recorded for completeness

Small, but they change what a defect costs:

- **Medication adherence** — `10386047751`: *"agora eu não estou mais esquecendo de tomar meus remédios do dia a dia"*. A reminder failure for this user is not an inconvenience.
- **Depression management** — `12993620841` (`fr`, 4★): *"lappli est génial et m'aide à surmonter ma dépression seulement je ne reçois aucunes notifications donc impossible de se rappeler"*.
- **ADHD** — `8564602907`: *"tenho tdah e sou mto esquecida/distraída. Esse App é tudo que eu sempre quis poder fazer no celular."*
- **Autism** — `10135390586` asks for an alternate icon: *"Sou autista e ele me incomoda muito."*
- **Study and exam routines** — `6109090746`, `7565776979`, `7989575537`, `8521754652`, `9545808359`.
- **Morning routine / "milagre da manhã"** — `6111763048`.

Driven by design and by the free tier, not by features. `GEN_PRAISE` (56) and `DES_CUTE` (54) lead; `PAY_FREE_OK` (44) and `OUT_LIFE` (44) follow. Only 15 five-star reviews name working reminders.

- **49 reviews in the corpus (9.84%) carry only `GEN_PRAISE`/`GEN_NEG`** — content-free. Almost all are 5★. They are genuine but tell you nothing about the product.
- **Nine 5★ reviews carry `REM_FAIL`** and six carry `META_CONTRA` (§5.6). Roughly 6% of the 5★ band is not a straightforward endorsement.

What the substantive 5★ reviews reward: the plant loop working as designed (`6056995445`, `9892861064`, `9611635620`), a free tier that beat paid competitors (`8718500987`, `9056065887`), and concrete outcomes (`7118806721`, `9459940060`, `7735144785`).

## 5.2 Four stars (n = 76, 15.26%)

**The four-star band is a feature-request queue.** `REQ_WIDGET` is its top theme at 23.68% of the band — higher than any theme in any other band except 1★'s `PAY_PAID`. `REM_FAIL` is second at 19.74%, `CORE_FREQ` and `PAY_BARRIER` at 7.89% each.

This is the most commercially useful band in the corpus: people who like the product enough to rate it 4★ and who name, precisely, the one thing withholding the fifth star. Three of the seven reviewers who make a purchase explicitly conditional on a missing feature sit in this band — `12002090537`, `12794885676` and `8590687133` — with the rest at 2★, 3★ and 5★ (§0.6, §6.3).

## 5.3 Three stars (n = 45, 9.04%)

Dominated by `REM_FAIL` (28.89% of the band) and by payers (`PAY_PAID` 17.78%). This is where "I paid and something is wrong, but I still think the app is decent" lives — `10819138875`, `10954472559`, `11391895419`, `11461818487`, `13494106663`. `BUG_UPDATE`, `DATA_LOSS` and `BUG_STREAK` each appear in 3 of 45.

## 5.4 Two stars (n = 43, 8.63%)

**`REM_FAIL` at 44.19% — the single highest theme concentration in any band in this report.** Nineteen of 43 two-star reviews are about notifications. Next: `PAY_FREE_LIMIT` (18.60%), `PAY_REGRESS` (13.95%), `ADS_BAD` and `COMP_WORSE` (11.63% each).

Two stars is where the monetization change and the notification failure meet: people who liked the app, watched it get worse, and are on the way out.

## 5.5 One star (n = 91, 18.27%)

**The one-star band is a payer band.** `PAY_PAID` is its top theme at **29.67%** — more than a quarter of all 1★ reviews come from people who state they paid. Then `BUG_CRASH` and `BUG_ONBOARD` (18.68% each), `PAY_FREE_LIMIT` (18.68%), `PAY_REGRESS` (14.29%).

## 6.7 What would change a paying user's mind

Derived from what payers say they wanted, not from inference:

1. **Deliver notifications.** 10 of 49 payers, plus at least three abandoned purchases. It is the most common post-purchase disappointment in the corpus after the app simply not running.
2. **Make the entitlement survive a reinstall, a new phone and a platform switch.** 9 `PAY_ENTITLE` + 3 `ACCT_RESTORE` + 5 `ACCT_SIGNIN`, spanning 2021–2026.
3. **Reply to support email.** Five 1★ reviews say nobody did. Each of the five describes a problem that a single reply could have converted into a 3–5★ update.
4. **Verify the displayed price in BRL.** §6.3.
5. **Offer the one-time purchase prominently.** 3 explicit requests; the lifetime tier already exists (`10936188003`, `11213091836`) but reviewers in 2023 were still asking for it as though it did not.
6. **Ship the widget and frequency scheduling.** Five reviewers made a purchase explicitly conditional on one of them (`10316293721`, `11570813316`, `12002090537`, `12794885676` for the widget; `8590687133` for frequency scheduling).

## 8.6 What the trend data does *not* support

- **Attributing any change to a specific build.** There is no version field. Era boundaries are complaint-onset dates, and a complaint can lag a release by weeks.
- **Reading the 2025–2026 decline in complaint volume as improvement.** Volume fell from 120/year to 12; the mean did not recover (3.03, then 2.50). Fewer reviews with the same mean is attrition, not repair.
- **Claiming ads were removed.** `ADS_BAD` falls in E4 but `12802262432` (Jun 2025) and `14502281920` (Sep 2026) still report heavy ad and upsell pressure.
- **Any month-level trend.** The monthly table (§A.7) shows medians of 5–7 reviews; individual months are too small to carry a claim.

## 9.4 Product and retention work

1. **Build out the garden.** It is the retention mechanic and it is thin: 3 ask for more plants, `13038216598` asks for placement and tap-to-detail, `9823208283` asks the widget to warn when a plant is near death. This is the cheapest available investment in the thing people already love.
2. **Re-examine the "ADHD Help" positioning.** Only 2 reviewers in 498 mention ADHD or autism. Either the positioning is not reaching that audience or it is not what the audience experiences — worth checking before spending further on it.
3. **Treat the 4★ band as a roadmap.** It is the only band whose top theme is a feature request rather than a defect (§5.2).
4. **Re-engage the E1/E2 cohort.** `9575158231` had the app since 2020 and uninstalled in 2023; `9574499267` recommended it to 30 people before leaving. These are the highest-value users the app has lost and they left over policy, not capability.

## 9.5 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

1. **Is the R$299 price display real?** Four reports, one storefront, no way to verify from review text. Check the App Store Connect price tier for BRL and the paywall's own string formatting. **Highest-value single check in this list.**
2. **What is the actual notification failure mode?** The corpus says "absent from iOS Settings" — that is a strong hint, not a diagnosis. Needs a device test on current iOS.
3. **How large is the silent majority's satisfaction?** 4.76 public vs 3.677 written. Nothing here resolves it; an in-app survey would.
4. **Did Spanish ever ship?** Requested Dec 2020; Spanish-language reviewers from 2022 write about in-app specifics, which suggests yes. Not determinable from the corpus.
5. **Why is `CORE_FREQ` exclusively Brazilian?** Genuine local expectation, or an artefact of a 75%-Brazilian corpus? Test with non-Brazilian users before treating it as a localised need.
6. **What happened to the free habit cap between Jan and Oct 2023?** Reported as 3, 4, 5 and 6 in different months. The release history would settle whether this was experimentation or a bug.
7. **Is the app still under development?** Volume, `DEV_STALE`, and an in-app string frozen at 2024 all point one way, but the corpus cannot distinguish "abandoned" from "quietly maintained".

## 9.6 What to do first

If only one thing ships: **F1, the notification fix.** It is 13.25% of the corpus, 45.7% of the last three years, the top theme of the 2★ and 3★ bands, present in 10 of 49 payer reviews, and named as the blocker on at least three abandoned purchases. It is also the one defect that makes the product's own described mechanism — cue, routine, reward (`9942909088`) — impossible to run.

If three things ship: **F1, F2 (entitlement), F3 (support email).** Together they address every one of the top six payer complaints, cost no product decisions, and are the fastest available route from a 2.061★ payer mean back toward the corpus mean.

If a quarter is available: add **S1 (widget)**, **S2 (frequency scheduling)** and **M1 (restore a defensible free tier)** — the three things the corpus shows people have been asking for longest and, in five documented cases, offering money for.

## A. Counting rules

1. **Denominator.** Every global percentage uses **498** — the full corpus. No review is excluded for being short, old, low-information, non-English, or content-free.
2. **Non-exclusive themes.** A review may carry several codes; 1095 assignments over 498 reviews, mean 2.20. Codes within one review never repeat (asserted by `65-validate.py`).
3. **Composite unions** count each review once even when it carries several of the merged codes. Marked `[U:name]` in the text; defined in §B and listed with counts in §3.2. **A union total is always ≤ the sum of its parts and is the number to quote.**
4. **Segment rates** (e.g. "18.4% of payers") always state the segment denominator and the global count beside them, and never substitute for the threshold classification in §3.1.
5. **Country percentages** use that country's own total as the denominator. Only Brazil (n = 375) clears 50 and carries standalone claims.
6. **Era percentages** use that era's own total.
7. **Mean ★** for a theme is the unweighted mean rating of the reviews carrying it.
8. **Bands** are applied exactly as specified: <0.1 Ignore · 0.1–0.5 Weak · 0.5–1 Emerging · 1–3 Meaningful · 3–5 Very strong · >5 High-priority.
9. **No deduplication** was applied; there is nothing to deduplicate (§1.4).
10. **External numbers** (public ratings, ranks, market tiers) come only from `Tools/habit_apps_ranked.json` and `markets.py` in this repository and are always labelled as such.

## C. Judgement calls and known error risk

A single reader applied the codebook to all 498 reviews; there is no inter-rater statistic. The calls where a different reader would plausibly differ:

1. **`GEN_PRAISE` vs a specific code.** "Muito bom" with no further content is `GEN_PRAISE`; "muito bom, fácil de usar" is `DES_SIMPLE`. The boundary is whether an attribute is named. 56 reviews sit in `GEN_PRAISE`; a stricter reader would move a handful out.
2. **`REM_FAIL` vs `REM_NOPERM`.** `REM_NOPERM` requires the reviewer to state that the app is absent from the OS notification settings. Reviews saying only "I enabled notifications and nothing arrives" are `REM_FAIL` alone. 14 met the stricter bar; a looser reading would give more.
3. **`PAY_PAID`.** Requires an explicit statement of paying, subscribing, renewing, being charged, or seeking a refund. "I'm premium" counts; "the premium is expensive" does not. This is deliberately conservative — the true payer count in the corpus is likely higher.
4. **`PAY_FREE_LIMIT` vs `PAY_HARDWALL`.** `PAY_HARDWALL` requires the reviewer to say the app cannot be used *at all* without paying (`11319648289`, `12938907395`, `13721715557`, `14022775133`). A cap on habits is `PAY_FREE_LIMIT`. Four reviews sit close to this line.
5. **`PAY_REGRESS`.** Requires an explicit before/after ("antes era…", "it was good until…"). A complaint about the current cap with no comparison is `PAY_FREE_LIMIT` only.
6. **`CORE_FREQ` was split out of `CORE_FLEX_NEG` mid-read**, after `8437662124` made the distinction concrete. Reviews #27, #37 and #50 were re-read and recoded; `CORE_FLEX_NEG` retains only the two genuinely unspecific requests.
7. **`ADHD` was renamed `NEURO`** to cover the autistic reviewer (`10135390586`) as well. Only reviews that *name* ADHD or autism carry it; "I have difficulty following routines" (`8884457541`) does not.
8. **`META_CONTRA`** requires the text to be substantially negative at 5★. Reviews that are net-positive with a complaint attached (`10663002825`, `11639387542`) are *not* marked, which is why the count is 6 rather than 20-odd.
9. **`BUG_CRASH` vs `BUG_OPEN` vs `BUG_ONBOARD`.** Crash = opens then closes; Open = never renders / stuck on launch screen; Onboard = gets into the intro flow and is blocked there. Reviews describing "não abre" ambiguously were coded `BUG_OPEN`; a few could belong elsewhere.
10. **Language.** `65-lang.py` uses a regex seed plus a hand-set override for every record the regexes could not settle. Short and emoji-only reviews were assigned by reading. The Portuguese/Spanish boundary is the likeliest place for a residual error, and it affects only §7.7.

**Estimated error rate:** the codebook was applied in one pass while reading, then re-checked against the per-theme ID lists in §B for the twelve highest-impact codes (`REM_FAIL`, `REM_NOPERM`, `PAY_PAID`, `PAY_ENTITLE`, `PAY_FREE_LIMIT`, `PAY_REGRESS`, `BUG_CRASH`, `BUG_ONBOARD`, `ADS_BAD`, `DES_CUTE`, `REQ_WIDGET`, `COMP_BEST`). Residual error is most likely to be in the ±1–2 range for the mid-volume codes and would not move any band classification.
