## How to read this

Every number resolves to named review IDs. Part 10 lists all 71 reviews with the themes assigned

### ⚠️ Eight warnings before any number here is used

1. **The corpus is 71 reviews.** One review is 1.41% of it. This is a small-sample report.
2. **The spec's signal-threshold table therefore cannot discriminate here.** Its lowest band
   ("Weak signal", 0.1–0.5%) is unreachable: the smallest non-zero share possible is 1.41%, which
   already reads "Meaningful signal", and 4 reviews (5.63%) already reads "High-priority signal".
   Bands are reported because the spec requires them, **but they are the wrong instrument at this
   N.** Every headline figure below also carries a Wilson 95% confidence interval. §1.6 covers this.
3. **No storefront reaches the 50-review threshold.** The largest is Germany at 17. **There are no
   standalone country claims in this report** — Part 7 is limited-evidence throughout.
4. **14 of 71 reviews (19.72%) are content-free 5★ ratings** ("Love it", "Nice app", "Great app").
   All 14 are 5★. They inflate the mean and dilute every share. §1.7 gives the ex-low-info figures
   alongside the headline ones; the gap is material (corpus mean 4.31★ → 4.14★ without them).
5. **Only 4 reviewers state they paid**, plus 1 who implies it. **No conversion, renewal, refund
   or churn rate is derivable from this corpus**, and none is claimed. One reviewer names a refund
   or cancellation: zero.
6. **Text reviewers are not the rating base.** These 71 text reviews sit on a public base of
   **339 ratings** across the same 28 storefronts, ratings-weighted mean **4.58★** against this
   corpus's **4.31★**. 268 people rated without writing.
7. **Apple publishes no release before 2025-09-29 (v2.6.4).** The first 8.5 months of the corpus
   — 36 of 71 reviews — cannot be tied to any dated release. Every pre-October-2025 trend below is
   date-based only, never release-attributed.
8. **Translations are mine.** Original-language text is quoted first. 17 of 71 reviews (23.9%) are
   not in English.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

## 0.1 The concept works. Nobody argues with it.

This app's differentiator is stated in its own Store title — *habit tracking without streaks* — and
it is the single best-supported finding in the corpus.

specifically**, by name, as the reason the app works for them. Their mean rating is **4.89★**.

## 0.3 The free tier is one habit — and the Store listing never says so

Three reviewers, on three different storefronts, across ten months, independently state the same
number without prompting:

| ID | Store | ★ | Date | Quote |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `13349206242` | ca | 1★ | 2025-11-03 | "can only add **1 habit** then after that this subscription keeps popping up 😑" |
| `14080954809` | es | 4★ | 2026-05-19 | "la versión gratuita solo te deja **1 hábito**" — *the free version only lets you have 1 habit* |
| `14355567891` | de | 2★ | 2026-07-27 | "**Nur ein Streak ist „free"**… Ich hatte erwartet, dass mehrere Gewohnheiten frei sind" — *Only one streak is "free"… I had expected several habits to be free* |

Their mean rating is **2.33★**. A fourth reviewer objects to the cap without naming the number
(`14209508686`, in, 3★: "I think we should get unlimited habit without paying"), taking the
cap-objection total to **4 of 71 (5.63%, CI 2.2–13.6%), mean 2.50★**.

I checked this against the listing. **The App Store description states no habit-count limit
anywhere** (S1, accessed 13 Sep 2026). It says the app "helps you build your new habit" — singular
— but never that the free tier stops at one. The word "free" appears in the description only in
the app's own name.

Two reviewers say in as many words that the boundary surprised them
(`13307114320`, pl, 1★: "What's the point of making a free app if it's unfunctional unless you buy
a premium plan"; `14355567891`, de, 2★, above). **`MON_UNEXPECTED` = 2 (2.82%), mean 1.50★.**

**A one-habit free tier is a defensible business decision. Not disclosing it is a separate,
cheaper-to-fix problem, and it is the one the corpus actually punishes.**

## 0.4 The paywall's shape is externally corroborated — and it is confusing

The listing sells **10 in-app purchase SKUs, $2.99 to $29.99, under three different product
names, and not one of them displays a billing period** (S1):

| Product name | Prices listed |
|---|---|
| Momentumly Plus | $2.99, $3.99, $5.99, $14.99, $29.99 |
| Premium Habit Tracking | $9.99, $12.99, $14.99 |
| Premium | $8.99, $13.99 |

Meanwhile the description's only monetization paragraph is headed **"Premium Subscription"** and
describes auto-renewal terms. It names **no price, no tier, no trial, and no one-time purchase.**

Three consequences appear directly in the review text:

- **Nobody can tell whether a one-time purchase exists.** `12552768633` (br, 2★, 2025-04-17) says
  "I've **subscribed for the lifetime version**". Twenty-six days later `12652787691` (ro, 2★,
  2025-05-13) says "**there is no possibility to get a lifetime version** of subscription" — a
  two-star review whose entire content is that sentence. Eleven months after that, `13941527190`
  (mx, 5★, 2026-04-10) praises "**The one time purchase** rather than a subscription model". The
  corpus cannot resolve this and neither can the listing, because the SKU list shows no periods.
- **There is no trial, and someone asked for one.** `12530006457` (bg, 4★): "there should be
  somewhat of a free trial because **you can't really explore the app**."
- **Three separate product names reach users as three separate things.** Reviewers variously call
  it "premium", "Premium Abo", "the pro subscription", "premium plan" and "Momentumly Plus".

## 0.5 The one-star episode: a 70-day window, then it stopped

The sharpest time-shaped fact in this corpus:

> **All 6 one-star reviews fall between 2025-08-26 and 2025-11-03.** The 32 reviews before that
> window contain **zero** one-star reviews. The 28 reviews after it contain **zero** one-star
> reviews.

| | Reviews | Mean ★ | 1★ | 1–3★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before 2025-08-26 | 32 | 4.56 | 0 | 4 (12.5%) |
| **Inside the window** | **11** | **2.82** | **6 (54.5%, CI 28.0–78.7%)** | **6 (54.5%)** |
| After 2025-11-03 | 28 | 4.61 | 0 | 3 (10.7%) |

The window opens with the only reliability regression in the corpus (`13064284382`, dk, 1★,
2025-08-26: "now we have reached a point with **the new update**, where the app can't even open
without crashing"), and then runs through five consecutive monetization one-stars. It closes seven
days before **v3.0 shipped on 2025-11-10** (S3).

**Read carefully.** At n=11 inside and n=60 outside, this is a real and very visible pattern, but
the corpus cannot say *why* it ended. What it supports is the factual claim — *the app had a bad
autumn 2025 and has not had a one-star review in the ten months since* — and the negative claim
that **no evidence here attributes the recovery to any specific release.** The 2025-08-26 "new
update" that broke launch predates Apple's published release history entirely (S3 begins
2025-09-29), so even that regression cannot be tied to a version number.

## 0.8 What people cannot work out

Small but pointed. **4 reviews (5.63%) show a comprehension failure**, mean 3.00★:

- **The momentum number itself.** `12579762746` (kr, 3★): "모멘텀은 어떠한 근거로 생기는 건지, 왜
  표시 되는게 좋은지 등을 잘 모르겠다" — *I can't really tell on what basis momentum arises, or why
  it's good that it's displayed.* And `12168189000` (de, 5★): "Ich hatte anfangs ein wenig
  Probleme, die Punkte Logik nachzuvollziehen, aber als ich es jetzt begriffen habe, bin ich
  begeistert" — *At first I had some trouble following the points logic, but now that I've grasped
  it, I'm thrilled.* **Same obstacle, two outcomes.** One user pushed through and became a
  five-star advocate; the other did not and rated three.
- **The absence of a tick-mark.** `13340807598` (in, **1★**): "Daily habits, tick mark not
  available." A one-star review caused by a user not finding the completion gesture in a habit app.
- **Tracking in general.** `14359876847` (us, 3★): "the tracking part itself is a bit difficult
  for me personally."

This is the only place where the no-streak concept has a cost: **removing the streak also removed
the obvious scoreboard, and a minority cannot find the replacement.**

## 0.9 The two requests that repeat

Almost every feature request in this corpus is a single voice. Two are not:

- **A multi-habit widget** — `13429536002` (us, 5★): "only able to select 1 habit to track at a

## 0.11 The eight decisions this corpus supports

1. **State the free limit in the App Store description.** It is one habit; three reviewers found
   out the hard way; two said the surprise is why they rated low. (§0.3)
2. **Collapse the SKU catalogue and show periods.** 10 SKUs under 3 names with no periods shown
   is the structural cause behind the lifetime contradiction in §0.4.
3. **Settle the lifetime question publicly.** Three reviews across 12 months disagree about
   whether a one-time purchase exists; one of them is a 2★ whose entire content is that question.
4. **Ship "x times per week" scheduling.** The only feature request made twice by otherwise-happy
   4★ users, 11 months apart, second one expressing surprise it is missing. (§0.9)
5. **Ship a multi-habit widget, and reconsider putting widgets behind the paywall.** Second
   repeated request; the paid-widget complaint is a 1★ whose whole text is that sentence. (§0.9)
6. **Explain the momentum number in-product.** Two reviewers could not derive it; one recovered
   and became an advocate, one did not. One 1★ could not find the completion gesture at all. (§0.8)
7. **Do not treat the 5★ share as satisfaction.** 14 of 50 five-star reviews are content-free, and
   3 more are complaints. (§0.6, §1.7)
8. **Do not spend on reliability ahead of monetization clarity.** Zero defect reports in 37
   reviews and 12 months; 7 money complaints in the same span. (§0.7, §0.6)

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Dump** (`77-dump.py`) — parse, HTML-unescape twice to fixpoint, sort by `(date, review_id)`,
   assign working index 1–71, emit `77-all-reviews.txt` (full text) and `77-index.tsv`.
2. **Reconcile** (`77-reconcile.py`) — the 24 checks in §1.3.
3. **Read** — all 71 reviews read individually in date order from `77-all-reviews.txt`, in the
   original language. Non-English reviews were read as written; translations in this report are
   mine and are always shown after the original.
4. **Classify by hand** (`77-cls/001-071.txt`) — one line per review: working index, codes, and a
   note quoting the text that drove each assignment. **This is a human-judgement artifact, not an
   automated classifier.** No keyword rule, no clustering and no model-scored labelling was used
   to assign any code.
5. **Build the map** (`77-build-classification.py`) — resolves index → `review_id` after asserting
   the index file field-by-field against `reviews.jsonl`, then emits
   `77-review-classification.py`. It is the only writer of that file; the classification is edited
   only in `77-cls/`.
6. **Validate** (`77-validate.py`) — 30 integrity checks, §1.9.
7. **Aggregate** (`77-common.py`, `77-aggregate.py`, `77-extra.py`, `77-extra2.py`) — counts,
   unions, Wilson intervals, ratings, storefronts, eras, quarters, languages, market groups,
   absence tests, burst checks, corpus-vs-public comparison.
8. **Generate tables** (`77-tables.py` → `77-gen/`) — 15 tables plus a 114-entry `_facts.json`.
9. **Write and fill** (`77-report-src-*.md` → `77-build-report.py`) — the prose is hand-written
   with `GEN:TABLE:` placeholders; the builder refuses to emit the report if any placeholder is
   unresolved.
10. **Verify after writing** (`77-verify-report.py`) — §1.9 and §9.F.

**Taxonomy:** 78 codes in 12 families, built bottom-up while reading. **Every one of the 78 is
used at least once** — no aspirational codes. 19 composite unions count each review once for
cross-cutting questions.

## 1.6 Limitations — read these before citing any figure

**1.6.1 — n = 71.** One review is 1.41%. Two is 2.82%. Sampling noise dominates every comparison
in this report.

**1.6.2 — the spec's signal bands are near-uninformative at this N.** The table is applied as
required, but note what it does here: the *smallest possible non-zero* finding lands in
"Meaningful signal", and four reviews lands in "High-priority signal". So every Wilson 95% interval
is also given. Where a band and an interval disagree, **trust the interval.**

**1.6.3 — no storefront is eligible for standalone analysis.** The 50-review threshold is met by
none; Germany (17), India (10) and the US (10) are the only ones in double digits. Part 7 makes no
standalone country claim.

**1.6.4 — 19.72% of the corpus is content-free.** 14 reviews (all 5★) carry no product
information. They are retained in every denominator, as the spec requires, but §1.7 reports the
key shares both ways.

**1.6.5 — selection bias, quantified.** 71 text reviews against 339 public ratings on the same 28

### P1 — The no-streak concept — `U:concept_positive` = 19 (26.76%), mean 4.89★, CI 17.9–38.1%

**High-priority signal.** The strongest and cleanest finding in the corpus. Three codes compose it:

say anything substantive names the concept.**

### P2 — Design and craft — `PRAISE_DESIGN` = 17 (23.94%), mean 4.65★, CI 15.5–35.0%

**High-priority signal.** "Cute" is the recurring word across four languages: "extremely cute"

### P3 — Simplicity — `PRAISE_SIMPLICITY` = 14 (19.72%), mean 4.57★, CI 12.1–30.4%

**High-priority signal.** Simple, clean, intuitive, easy to start. `12806969276`, `12809333699`,
`12529237684`, `12371788548`, `12522499633`, `12292550440`, `13019604470`, `12756758711`,

corpus: minimalism is simultaneously the third-largest praise and the joint-largest non-monetary
complaint.

### P4 — Motivation — `PRAISE_MOTIVATING` = 11 (15.49%), mean 5.00★

**High-priority signal.** Every one is 5★. The distinguishing detail is *how* they describe it —

(`12768970190`), "lieve en opbouwende UX" — *sweet and constructive UX* (`13634096636`), "Very

### P5 — Real outcomes — `U:outcome_any` = 11 (15.49%), mean 5.00★, CI 8.9–25.7%

**High-priority signal.** Every one is 5★. Three sub-claims:

  `13019604470`, `13809152355`.
- **A named behaviour changed** — 3 reviews: `12799674597` (stretching), `14237526621` (reading

- **Consistency improved** — 6 reviews: `12775276195`, `12893716000`, `12905347299`,

For a habit product this is the outcome metric that matters, and **it is the one theme where every
single review is a five-star review.**

### P6 — Beat the alternatives — `COMP_TRIED_MANY` = 4 (5.63%), `COMP_BEST` = 3 (4.23%), both mean 5.00★

**High-priority / very strong.** Four reviewers state they tried many trackers before this one

### P7 — Developer responsiveness — `PRAISE_DEV` = 3 (4.23%), mean 5.00★

**Very strong signal**, and worth noting because **all three fall inside the first 90 days of the

**So the corpus contains 14 money complaints and 5 money compliments.** The complaint is not that
the product is overpriced — see N4 below.

# PART 5 — PAID-USER AND UPGRADE ANALYSIS

## 5.1 The finding that governs this entire part

> **Four reviewers out of 71 (5.63%, CI 2.2–13.6%) state that they paid. One more implies it.
> Zero mention a refund. Zero mention a cancellation. Zero mention a renewal.**

Everything below is built on that. **No conversion rate, renewal rate, refund rate or churn rate
is derivable from this corpus, and none is given anywhere in this report.** Where a mechanism is
inferred rather than stated, it is labelled inference.

## 5.2 Every piece of direct purchase evidence

- `12339835181` paid, used it for 20 days, and left — over missing feedback loops (no achievement
  feeling, no monthly missed-day view), not over money.

## 5.3 What causes people to consider paying

**Direct evidence: one review.** `13941527190` (mx, 5★) is the only reviewer who states an
intention to buy, and they name what sold them: the concept ("more useful for me than more
traditional habit trackers") and **the purchase model** ("The one time purchase rather than a
subscription model is also a lovely thing"). They also name the blocker: "the moment I can afford
it" — an affordability barrier, not a value objection.

**Inferred triggers — labelled as inference**, from the three reviewers who volunteer that the
price is right (`12905347299`, `13019604470`, `13410412793`, all 5★). What they name is
*comparative* value against other habit trackers they have already paid for or tried:
"really affordable compared to others" (`12905347299`), "the price is just right"
(`13019604470`). Two of these three also carry `COMP_TRIED_MANY`. **The inference — and it is an
inference from n=3 — is that this app converts against a reference price the user has already
formed elsewhere, not against zero.**

**No reviewer names a specific feature as the thing they paid for.** Not one. The nearest is
`14331668442` buying forest tiles, which is cosmetic content rather than a capability.

## 5.5 The one habit cap, as a conversion instrument

A one-habit free tier is an aggressive but coherent strategy: it makes the product's core loop
un-experienceable without paying, because a habit tracker with one habit is a demo. The corpus
shows the cost side of that trade precisely:

- The four reviewers who hit it rate it **2.50★** on average.
- One of them (`12530006457`, on trial availability) states the consequence directly: **"you can't
  really explore the app."**
- `13349206242` describes the resulting experience: add one habit, then be interrupted repeatedly.

**What the corpus cannot say** — and this is the decisive gap — **is whether the cap works.** There
is no conversion data here. Four unhappy reviewers are visible; any number of quiet converters is
not. **This report does not recommend removing the cap.** It recommends disclosing it (§9.1),
because disclosure costs nothing and the surprise is separately evidenced as a rating driver.

## 5.6 Refund, cancellation and renewal — an explicit blank

Searched across all 71 reviews in all six languages for: *refund, erstattung, reembolso, money
back, 환불, cancel, kündig, cancelar, 해지*. **Zero hits.**

There is **one** churn event in the entire corpus (`12339835181`), and the reviewer describes
uninstalling, not refunding. **This is a genuine blank in the evidence, not a clean bill of
health.** App Store reviews systematically under-represent post-purchase experience because the
review prompt fires early in the lifecycle; the absence here should be read as *unobserved*, not
*absent*.

## 7.2 T1 — The one-star episode of autumn 2025 — *resolved, cause unknown*

Covered in §0.5. The facts, restated for the trend record:

- **6 one-star reviews, all between 2025-08-26 and 2025-11-03.**
- **0 one-star reviews in the 32 reviews before that window.**
- **0 one-star reviews in the 28 reviews after it — a span of 10 months and 3 days.**
- Inside the window: mean 2.82★, 54.5% rated 1–3★. Outside: mean 4.58★, 11.7% rated 1–3★.
- E2 (the Badges block, 2025-09-29 → 2025-11-09) is the worst 42 days in the corpus on every
  measure: n=7, **mean 2.14★**, 57.1% money-negative (CI 25.0–84.2%), 71.4% rated 1–3★.

**Direction: resolved.** **Confidence: the pattern is real; the cause is not established.** The
window opens with a launch-crash regression attributed by the reviewer to "the new update"
(`13064284382`) that predates Apple's published history entirely, and closes 7 days before v3.0.
At n=7 in E2, **the 2.14★ mean is one or two reviews away from unremarkable** — the CI on its
money-negative share spans 25% to 84%. Treat the *window* as established and the *explanation* as
open.

## 7.3 T2 — Monetization complaints changed register, not volume — *worsening in a new way*

Covered in §0.6. Pre-v3.0: 7 money-negative reviews, mean **1.57★**, 1 of 7 rated 4–5★. From v3.0:
7 money-negative reviews, mean **3.86★**, 4 of 7 rated 4–5★.

**Direction: persistent in volume, materially changed in character.** The 2026 complaint is not a
departure; it is a retained user registering an objection — which is *better* for retention and
*worse* for signal quality, because it means **the recent ratings no longer carry the monetization
problem.** 2026 Q3 is the clearest case: 5 of 9 reviews money-negative at a 4.44★ mean.

**Confidence: moderate.** n=7 in each half. The volume claim (flat) is solid; the register claim
rests on a 1.57★ → 3.86★ shift across 14 reviews, which is large but underpowered.

## 7.4 T3 — Defect reports fell to zero and stayed there — *improving*

5 defect reports, all between 2025-02-22 and 2025-08-26. **The 37 reviews written since 2025-08-26
contain none** — no crash, no slowness, no broken integration, across 12 months and 3 major
release blocks.

**Direction: improving. Confidence: good by the standards of this corpus.** 37 consecutive
defect-free reviews is the longest clean run of any theme here. The caveat is that users who
uninstall over a crash often do not return to review, so this measures *reported* reliability only.

## 7.5 T4 — The free-tier cap became visible in the second half — *emerging*

All 4 cap objections date from 2025-11-03 onward: `13349206242` (2025-11-03), `14080954809`
(2026-05-19), `14209508686` (2026-06-21), `14355567891` (2026-07-27). **Zero in the first 42
reviews; 4 in the last 29.**

**Direction: emerging. Confidence: weak, and the ambiguity is important.** Three readings fit the
same data equally well and the corpus cannot separate them: (a) the cap tightened at some point;
(b) the cap was always one habit and grew more visible as the user base grew; (c) chance, at n=4.
**No claim that the cap changed is made anywhere in this report.** What *is* established is that
the three reviewers who state a number all state the same number, and the first of them is
2025-11-03.

## 7.6 T5 — The developer-responsiveness signal stopped — *worsening, weak*

All three `PRAISE_DEV` reviews fall between 2025-01-26 and 2025-04-09, in the first 15% of the
corpus by time. **No reviewer has mentioned developer responsiveness in the 61 reviews since.**

**Direction: worsening. Confidence: weak — n=3.** Recorded because the *shape* is characteristic:
early-adopter communities praise responsiveness, later cohorts do not notice it. It may mean
nothing. It is cheap to check against the developer's actual response behaviour, which this corpus
cannot see (the schema has no developer-reply field).

## 7.7 What persisted unchanged across the whole 603 days

- **The concept never drew a negative reaction.** `U:concept_positive` appears in **every one of
  the 7 quarters** and is never below 4★, first review to last.
- **Praise density stays high but is not flat.** 84.51% of reviews carry praise overall; by
  quarter it ranges 50% (2025 Q4, the one-star window) to 100% (2025 Q1, 2026 Q2). It is above
  83% in six of the seven quarters.
- **Simplicity-as-emptiness never resolved.** `UX_TOO_MINIMAL` runs 2025-02-22 → 2025-09-29 and
  the underlying complaint reappears as `UX_TRACKING_HARD` in 2026-07.
- **Nobody ever mentioned privacy, data loss, support, accessibility, battery, ads, a refund or a
  cancellation.** In 603 days, across 28 storefronts and six languages, **zero.**

## 7.8 What this corpus cannot say about time

- **Nothing about the first 8.5 months in release terms.** 36 reviews predate Apple's published
  history (S3). The app shipped 2024-12-18 (S2) and the corpus begins 23 days later, so the

- **Nothing about whether Badges or the Habit Forest worked.** Both shipped inside the corpus
  window. **Badges are mentioned by no reviewer at all.** The Forest is mentioned by exactly one
  (`14331668442`, 5★, 2026-07-21, positively, and as something he bought) — eight months after

- **Nothing about seasonality.** Two of 21 months are empty and the largest month is 9 reviews.
- **Nothing about the relationship between release cadence and sentiment.** S3 shows 18 releases
  in the 10 months from 2025-11-10, an unusually high cadence; the corpus shows no reviewer
  reacting to it either way after 2025-04-09.

# PART 8 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation names the findings it rests on. Nothing here is proposed from a single review
unless that review is externally corroborated, and where the evidence is thin it says so.

**The one-line summary of this corpus:** *the product thesis is validated and the reliability is
fine; everything that costs this app ratings happens at the free/paid boundary, and most of it is
a disclosure and structure problem rather than a price problem.*

## 8.1 Do first — disclosure. Cost: hours.

**8.1.1 State the free limit in the App Store description.**
Findings: §0.3 (`U:cap_any` = 4, mean 2.50★), §N3 (`MON_UNEXPECTED` = 2, mean 1.50★), S1 (the
description states no limit). One sentence — "The free version includes one habit; Premium unlocks
unlimited habits" — addresses the two lowest-rated complaints in the corpus. **This is the highest
ratio of impact to effort available here.** It will not make anyone happier about the cap; it
removes the *surprise*, which is what the 1★ and 2★ text actually objects to.

**8.1.2 Show billing periods on every SKU, and reduce the SKU count.**
Findings: §0.4, S1 (10 SKUs, 3 product names, $2.99–$29.99, **no periods shown**), §N5. Three
reviewers hold three different beliefs about whether a one-time purchase exists — one of them a 2★
whose entire review is that question. A user who cannot tell what is being sold does not buy.

**8.1.3 Settle the lifetime question in the description.**
Findings: `12552768633` (bought lifetime 2025-04-17), `12652787691` (told none exists 2025-05-13),
`13941527190` (praises "the one time purchase" 2026-04-10). If a lifetime SKU exists, say so — it
is the only monetization *attribute* anyone in this corpus has ever praised by name.

**8.1.4 Name the features in the description.**
Findings: §2.2. Widgets, Apple Health, the calendar, badges and the Habit Forest are all absent
from the description and all present in reviews. Two of them (widgets, Health) generated
complaints from users who did not know what tier they were in.

## 8.2 Do next — the two repeated requests. Cost: sprints.

**8.2.1 "x times per week" scheduling.**
Findings: §0.9. `12630919125` (es, 4★, 2025-05-07) and `13946139457` (de, 4★, 2026-04-11), eleven
months apart, both otherwise-positive, the second saying it "surprises me that this doesn't
exist". Both describe the same real use case — *go to the gym three times a week, any three days*.
**This is the only feature in the corpus requested twice by users who otherwise rate 4★.** At n=2
it is a weak count; what elevates it is that it is a *scheduling model* gap, not a nice-to-have,
and it interacts directly with the product's own thesis: an app built on "missing a day is fine"
still forces users to name the days.

**8.2.2 A multi-habit widget — and reconsider whether widgets belong behind the paywall.**
Findings: §0.9, `U:widget_gap` = 5 (7.04%), mean 3.80★. A per-habit widget exists and is praised
(`12300467429`). Two reviewers want all habits in one widget (`13429536002`, `13839542276`), and
`13429536002` names the cost precisely: "I have to open the app to check which habits I have yet
to complete." Separately, `13277885578` (pt) rated **1★** with the entire review being "You have
to pay for widgets".

The paywall question is a judgement call the corpus cannot settle — it is one review — but note
the interaction: **a widget is the cheapest daily-retention surface a habit tracker has, and this
one is gated and limited to a single habit at the same time as the free tier is limited to a
single habit.**

## 8.3 Do next — close the comprehension gap. Cost: days.

Findings: §0.8, `U:comprehension` = 4 (5.63%), mean 3.00★; `UX_NO_TICK` (1★).

**8.3.1 Explain the momentum number where it is displayed.** Two reviewers could not derive it
(`12579762746` kr 3★, `12168189000` de 5★). The second is the important one: they struggled, *then
got it*, and became an enthusiastic advocate. **The gap between those two reviews is the whole
opportunity** — a one-tap explanation of what momentum is and why the number moved converts a 3★
into a 5★ path.

**8.3.2 Make the completion gesture unmissable.** `13340807598` (in) rated **1★** because "Daily
habits, tick mark not available." Whether the tick genuinely is missing or merely hard to find,
the outcome was a one-star review in a habit app over the core action.

**8.3.3 Give users a monthly hit-and-miss view.** Findings: `U:calendar_gap` = 3 (4.23%).
`12339835181` — "difficult for you to know which days you have missed and which days you have done
from a monthly perspective"; `12300467429` — wants "my habit in a calendar graphic";
`12579762746` — "달력 부분을 조작하는게 잘 안된다" (*the calendar part doesn't operate well*). A
calendar exists; it is not doing this job. This also addresses `UX_NO_ACHIEVEMENT_FEEL` — a filled
month is the achievement feedback the streak used to provide.

## 8.4 Positioning and monetization strategy

**8.4.1 Lead with the concept everywhere, because it is the only thing with no detractors.**
Findings: §P1 (19 reviews, 26.76%, mean 4.89★, **zero below 4★**), §P5 (11 outcome reviews, all
5★), §P6 (`COMP_BEST` = 3). The description already does this well; the ASO title already does it.
The gap is that the *paywall* does not: a user who converts is converting on the concept, and the
concept is experienceable only with more than one habit.

**8.4.2 Reconsider the one-habit cap on strategy grounds, not on review grounds.**
Findings: §5.5. The corpus shows four unhappy reviewers and cannot show converters. **This report
does not recommend raising the cap** — the data to justify that is conversion data the corpus does
not contain. It recommends that the decision be made knowing that at one habit, the product's own
differentiator (forgiving multi-habit momentum) is not experienceable free, and that the reviewers
who did convert (`13019604470`, `12905347299`, `13410412793`) converted on *comparative value*
against trackers they had already tried — i.e. on an informed judgement they could only make after
using it.

**8.4.3 Offer a trial, or an unambiguous free window.**
Findings: `12530006457` (bg, 4★): "you can't really explore the app." Zero reviewers in 71 mention
using a trial, and the description mentions none. This is a one-voice finding — but it is the
mechanism that would let §8.4.2 be tested without changing the cap.

**8.4.4 Fix the paywall prompt frequency.**
Findings: `13349206242` (ca, **1★**, title "annoying"): "can only add 1 habit then after that this
subscription keeps popping up 😑". One review; recorded because it is specific, cheap to check
internally, and the kind of thing that is invisible in aggregate metrics until it shows up as
churn.

## 8.5 Do not do

- **Do not fund a reliability push ahead of the above.** Findings: §0.7, §7.4 — zero defect
  reports in 37 reviews and 12 months. The one open reliability question is Apple Health
  (`12552768633`, `13063341493`), and it is small.
- **Do not treat the 5★ share as a satisfaction metric.** Findings: §4.2 — 14 of 50 are
  content-free and 3 are complaints; **17 of 50 five-star reviews (34%) do not evidence
  satisfaction with anything.**
- **Do not read the 2026 rating recovery as the monetization problem resolving.** Findings: §7.3 —
  complaint volume is flat and the complaints have simply moved into 4–5★ reviews.
- **Do not build from the request backlog as a list.** Findings: §3.5 — 14 of 16 requests are
  single-voice, and one reviewer accounts for five of them.
- **Do not draw a country conclusion from this corpus.** Findings: §6.1, §6.8.

# PART 9 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 9.A Counting rules

1. **Denominator.** Every global percentage uses **n = 71**, the complete corpus. No review is
   excluded from any denominator for any reason — not length, not language, not age, not
   nonsense content.
2. **Non-exclusive counting.** A review carrying k codes is counted in all k themes. 241 code
   assignments across 71 reviews, mean 3.39 per review, range 1–9.
3. **Unions count a review once.** `U:` unions are set unions over member codes; a review matching
   three members counts once.
4. **Means.** "mean ★" beside a theme is the mean star rating of reviews carrying that code.
5. **Signal bands** are the spec's table applied to the global share. **At n = 71 they are coarse
   — see §1.6.2.** Wilson 95% score intervals accompany every headline figure.
6. **Segment rates** (§5.7) always state their own denominator and are labelled as segment rates.
   They never replace a band.
7. **Direction** (`+` / `-` / `~`) is a property of the code, fixed at definition time, not
   inferred from the star rating of the reviews that carry it.
8. **Dates** are the `date` field, UTC, truncated to day. Era assignment uses the day only.
9. **Language** is the language the review is *written in*, assigned by reading, and is
   independent of the storefront.

## 9.C Judgement calls, borderline decisions, and known error risk

**This classification is hand-made. These are the calls a second reader could reasonably make
differently.**

1. **`13604788620` (de, 5★, "Problem Premium / Leider werden mir die Optionen nicht angezeigt").**
   Ambiguous between *"I paid and my premium options don't appear"* and *"the paywall screen won't
   load"*. Coded `PAID_ENTITLEMENT_FAIL` + `META_CONTRADICTION`, and **deliberately not**
   `PAID_STATED` — so it does not inflate the paid-user count. If the first reading is right, the
   stated-payer count is 5, not 4.
2. **`13200826254` (pk, 1★, "just wastage of funds").** "Funds" implies spend. Coded
   `PAID_IMPLIED`, never `PAID_STATED`, and excluded from every paid-user figure. Validation check
   20 enforces that the two never co-occur.
3. **`14343374736` (de, 5★, "pricey… basic function still worth using it").** Carries both a
   money objection and a money positive. Coded `MON_PRICE_LEVEL` + `MONP_FREE_SUFFICIENT` and
   **not** `META_CONTRADICTION`, because the reviewer resolves the tension themselves. Validation
   check 26 states this rule explicitly.
4. **`14209508686` (in, 3★, "we should get unlimited habit without paying").** Objects to the cap
   without stating its size. Coded `MON_CAP_OBJECTION` but **not** `MON_CAP_STATED_1`, which is
   reserved for the three reviews that name the number. This is why §0.3 reports "three state the
   number, a fourth objects without one".
5. **`13064284382` (dk, 1★, "Good concept, bad execution").** The concept praise is generic, so it
   is `PRAISE_GENERIC`, not `CONCEPT_NO_STREAK`. A second reader could argue for the latter, which
   would raise `U:concept_positive` from 19 to 20 and introduce its only sub-4★ instance —
   materially changing the §P1 claim. **Flagged because it is the single most consequential
   borderline call in this report.**
6. **`14405189937` (us, 5★, "spam gamepasess, love from the dylano").** No recoverable product
   meaning. Coded `META_NONSENSE` + `META_LOWINFO` and retained in all denominators rather than
   dropped.
7. **`13193129681` (eg, 5★, "Superrrr"/"Guuuut").** German text on the Egypt storefront. Language
   coded `de`, storefront `eg`. See §6.7.
8. **`12339835181` carries 8 codes and `12579762746` carries 9** — the two richest reviews. Both
   were re-read after the first pass. The Korean review is the one whose reading is hardest for a
   non-Korean-reading auditor to check, and it contributes uniquely to `REQ_AI`, `REQ_SOCIAL`,
   `REQ_MORE_STATS`, `UX_FOCUS_TAB_POINTLESS` and `DEF_CALENDAR_CTRL` — **five codes that rest on
   one review in one language.**
9. **`META_LOWINFO` threshold.** Applied to reviews whose body carries no product attribute.
   All 14 have bodies under 60 characters (check 27), but the code was assigned by meaning, not
   by length: `13220885234` ("It makes setting new habits not overwhelming at all", 51 chars) is
   short but **not** low-info.
10. **Requests vs. broken features.** `REQ_HEALTHKIT` (`13063341493`, asking for *more* Health
    coverage) is separated from `DEF_HEALTH_BROKEN` (`12552768633`, the connection failing). Same
    subsystem, different problem, different fix.

**Known error risk.** With 71 reviews and 241 assignments, a 5% assignment error rate would move
roughly 12 assignments. At the code level that could shift most single-review codes by one, which
is why **no recommendation in Part 8 rests on a single uncorroborated review.** The three
recommendations in §8.8 rest on 4, 3 and 2 reviews respectively, and the first two are
additionally corroborated by the App Store listing itself.
