**Corpus:** 70 written reviews, 14 storefronts, 15 Mar 2014 → 19 Oct 2021

## How to read this

Every quantified claim carries **count / percentage / denominator / time window / signal label / review IDs**. Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several labels. The global denominator is always **70** unless a segment denominator is named explicitly.

Signal thresholds applied:

| Share of reviews | Label |

| 0.1 – <0.5% | Weak |

### ⚠️ Four warnings before any number here is used

**1. The corpus is small (70). One review is 1.43%.** Every threshold label above is reached by a handful of records. A theme labelled "high-priority" here means 4–14 people, not hundreds. Percentages are given because the method requires them; the **counts are the honest unit**. Nothing in this report should be treated with the confidence a 400- or 4,000-review corpus would earn.

**2. No country qualifies for individual analysis.** The threshold is 50 reviews per country. The largest storefront here is the US at **20**. Per the analysis direction for this app, **no per-country section is produced** — all 70 reviews are pooled into global findings only. Where a language or market observation is material it is flagged inline as **limited evidence**.

**3. This corpus is history, not current state.** 60 of 70 reviews (85.71%) were written in 2014–2015. The last review is Oct 2021 and the last shipped version is **1.7, released 29 Dec 2020** (store listing, accessed 9 Sep 2026) — the app has gone roughly **five years and eight months without an update**. Findings describe a product lifecycle that has already ended.

**4. The store's current rating count cannot be reconciled with this corpus.** The US listing shows **3 ratings** (4.3★) while this corpus alone holds **20 US written reviews**. Written reviews are normally a small fraction of ratings, so the inequality runs the wrong way. The most likely explanation is a ratings reset at some version boundary, but the corpus cannot establish that. **Do not use the 4.3★ / 3-rating figure as evidence of anything**; it is recorded here only for completeness.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

## 1. The core mechanic worked. The business around it did not.

The product itself is well-liked. **14 reviewers (20.00%) — high-priority signal** state the app actually changed their behaviour or let them track progress, with a mean rating of **4.93** (13 of 14 are 5★). **10 (14.29%) — high-priority** praise its simplicity or ease of use, mean **5.00** — every single one is a 5★. **5 (7.14%) — high-priority** say explicitly they tried multiple competing habit apps and picked this one, mean **5.00**.

Nothing in this corpus suggests the concept failed. What killed the app is a short, specific list of execution failures — nag prompts, crashes, data loss, a broken purchase button, and eventual abandonment — each individually fixable, each documented below.

The clearest statement of the value proposition is `1076855737` (au, 5★): *"a great app if you're looking for a 'Seinfeld streak' tracker that's simple and easy to use… Marking off another 'green day' and keeping the streak going is really satisfying."*

## 2. The single most transferable finding: nagging cost this app a full star, and the corpus proves the fix worked

**6 reviewers (8.57%) — high-priority signal** complain about in-app prompts pestering them to upgrade and to leave a rating. Mean rating **3.00**. This is the **only theme in the corpus where users state, in plain text, the exact star penalty they applied**:

- `1113922936` (kr, 4★): *"every time I run the free version, windows keep popping up telling me to write a review or buy. **I docked one star for this**"*
- `1115315804` (kr, 4★): *"I don't usually leave reviews, but it kept telling me to, so here's one"*
- `1126869931` (kr, 4★): title *"Here's a review~"*, body in full: *"Happy now??"*
- `1130477064` (us, 2★): *"The app works great, but the constant nags to upgrade to the full version and provide a rating sour the experience. Hope the devs realize that's a sure-fire way to get bad reviews from annoyed users."*
- `1133797353` (us, 2★): *"Decent app, but killed with constant nagging for a rating and upgrade. Deleted."*
- `1099032136` (gb, 2★): *"Habit tracking needs to be quick & easy if I'm going to do it throughout the day; making me wait for the message then dismiss it is counterproductive. I'm very unlikely to pay for the upgrade just to get rid of the pestering; I'm more likely just to try a different app."*

Counting reviews that mention the prompts in **any** context — including positively — the theme reaches **9 reviewers (12.86%) — high-priority**, mean 3.67.

**The prompts were removed, and the corpus records it.** Every complaint falls between **18 Nov 2014 and 21 Jan 2015**. Then:

- `1193853972` (us, **5★**, 7 May 2015), entire body: *"They removed the pop ups. Nice!!"*
- `1208386132` (ru, **5★**, 7 Jun 2015): *"Good, intuitive, **not annoying at all**… Their competitor Way of Life, by the way, already managed to become tiresome by begging for an upgrade within the first minute."*

No further nag complaint appears in the remaining 6.4 years of the corpus. This is the cleanest before/after evidence available anywhere in this dataset: **an aggressive rating-and-upsell prompt generated 6 rating-suppressing reviews in 9 weeks; removing it produced unsolicited 5★ praise and a competitive advantage a rival was still giving away.**

Two second-order effects worth naming:

- **The nagging polluted the ratings data.** `1115315804` and `1126869931` are 4★ reviews written *only because the app demanded one*; `1122529516` (kr, 3★) consists of the word "Review" as both title and body. At least 3 records (4.29%) exist because of the prompt, not because of the product.
- **It directly caused a lost sale.** `1099032136` is an explicit refusal to pay *because of* the upsell mechanism — the upsell destroyed the conversion it was built to create.

## 3. Data loss is the only theme with a perfect 1★ record

**3 reviewers (4.29%) — very strong signal** report losing tracked history to an app update. Mean rating **1.00** — all three are 1★, the only theme in the corpus with no non-1★ member.

- `1195475837` (au, 1★, 10 May 2015): *"What use is an app like this if your data is so easily lost? After months of tracking it's all gone in the latest update. Stay away from this app if you value your data."*
- `1194862743` (ru, 1★, 9 May 2015): *"After the update all my marks disappeared"*
- `6684084592` (gb, 1★, 25 Nov 2020): *"I don't want to backdate all my progress by memory (**again**)"*

The first two are **one day apart**, which points to a single bad release in early May 2015 rather than a diffuse reliability problem. The 2020 review's "(again)" indicates it recurred at least once more, 5.5 years later.

For a streak tracker this is existential, not cosmetic: the accumulated chain **is** the product. `1195475837` is also the only review in the corpus that tells other buyers to stay away.

## 4. Crashes bracket the entire lifespan and are what the app died of

**6 reviewers (8.57%) — high-priority signal** report the app crashing, freezing, failing to advance the date, or responding too slowly. Mean rating **1.50** (4 of 6 are 1★) — the lowest-rated theme in the corpus.

| Date | ID | Ctry | ★ | Failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2014-03-18 | `961881835` | kr | 3 | *"It keeps crashing down from the beginning"* — on the then-latest iOS |
| 2014-10-09 | `1077393973` | jp | 2 | Date never rolls over; **device restart required every single day** to log |
| 2015-04-02 | `1176859130` | ru | 1 | *"It just glitches" / "Doesn't work at all"* |
| 2015-08-12 | `1241930865` | kr | 1 | *"it has too slow response"* |
| 2020-11-23 | `6678082433` | de | 1 | *"closes (crashes?) every time when touching the calendar to mark habit as done. It's completely unusable"* |
| 2020-11-25 | `6684084592` | gb | 1 | *"Opens but then crashes with new IOS 14. Same thing happened with the new IOS 13 and it took them a while to fix it"* |

The two 2020 reports are **two days apart** and describe the same OS-upgrade breakage. `6678082433` names the failure precisely: the crash fires on **the single most-used action in a habit tracker** — checking a habit off. `6684084592` adds the structural cause and a deadline the developer did not meet: *"Considering updates are months and years apart, if this is not fixed by next week I will just delete the app."*

Version 1.7 shipped 29 Dec 2020 — five weeks after those reports — and is still the current build. Whether it fixed the iOS 14 crash cannot be determined from the corpus: only two reviews follow it (`7082831771` 4★, `7929736347` 4★ *"Great interface, works well"*), which is suggestive of a fix but far too thin to call one.

**The pattern across seven years: this app broke at each major iOS transition and was repaired slowly or not at all.** The reliability problem is not a coding problem; it is a maintenance-capacity problem.

## 5. Purchase intent is three times more common than evidence of purchase, and the buy button was broken

This is the most commercially important asymmetry in the corpus.

**6 reviewers (8.57%) — high-priority signal** state intent to buy, mean rating **4.50** (all are 4★ or 5★):

| ID | Ctry | ★ | Statement |
|---|---|---|---|
| `1056568978` | ru | 4 | *"Please add option change type size… After I will buy, and will recommended it for all my friends"* |
| `1128016301` | kr | 4 | *"With free apps the whole point is to try it before you decide"* |
| `1130585398` | us | 5 | *"Trying it out a little longer before I purchase the full version"* |
| `1137963077` | kr | 4 | *"I think I'll go Pro"* |
| `1184550500` | au | 5 | *"Will continue to evaluate for a week or so but if I'm still just as happy I'll be upgrading to the full version"* |
| `1222427563` | gb | 5 | *"Well worth trying out and paying for the upgrade"* |

**Only 2 reviewers (2.86%) — meaningful signal — give direct evidence of having paid**, and 1 more (`1222427563`, 1.43%) implies it:

- `1026996445` (us, **1★**): *"Currently to[o] basic for the **$3 expended**. Might be worth it if weekly and monthly trending and habit forming we[re] added."* — a buyer whose money bought less analysis than they came for.
- `1036447473` (ru, **5★**): *"when my account had problems due to the move to a new monetisation model, they immediately sent me a promo code for the new app (which is paid)"* — records both a **monetisation model change in mid-2014** and the only support interaction in the corpus, which was excellent.

**And 2 reviewers (2.86%) — meaningful — could not complete the purchase even though they wanted to:**

- `6183382407` (mx, 4★, Jul 2020): *"I like the app but I wan[t] to add more habits[.] When I click upgrade button, it only apear[s] a[n] Orange screen and then disappears"*
- `5929885128` (br, 4★, May 2020): *"It's what I needed. But I couldn't find the extended version"*

Both are **4★ reviews from willing buyers holding money out**. Note the date collision: the upgrade path was reported broken in **May and July 2020**, five to seven months before the last-ever release. A freemium app whose free tier caps at 3 habits (`1174519073`) and whose upgrade button fails is monetising nothing.

Set against that, **2 reviewers (2.86%) refused to pay**, both 2★, and both name the nag prompts as the reason (`1099032136`, `1133797353`).

**What this does not show:** with 2 confirmed payers out of 70, **no conversion rate, ARPU, or paid-satisfaction rate can be computed from this corpus**, and none is claimed. What it does show is that demand to pay was present, articulated, and repeatedly obstructed — first by the upsell's own tone, later by a broken checkout.

## 6. Localisation was asked for, never delivered, and the store listing still shows English only

**25 of 70 reviews (35.71%)** are written in a language other than English: Cyrillic 10, Korean 8, Spanish/Portuguese 3, Chinese 2, Japanese 2. Russian-language storefronts (ru, by, ua) contribute **15 reviews (21.43%)** — more than any single market except the US.

**2 reviewers (2.86%) — meaningful signal** explicitly ask for Russian, and one of them says the missing language is why they cannot rate the app:

- `1157833711` (ru, 3★): *"How do I change the language to Russian? I can't rate it, because I've spent 20 minutes and still can't switch the language. **I really need an app like this, but in Russian!** Help please!!!"*
- `1265490751` (ru, 2★): *"Not great" / "There's no Russian?"*

The store listing (accessed 9 Sep 2026) lists **English** as the only supported language. Both requests went unanswered for the remaining decade of the app's life. Treat as **limited evidence** given the counts — but note the direction: the second-largest language group in the corpus was asking for a translation, in a product whose entire UI is a handful of labels and would be cheap to localise.

# PART 1 — SCOPE AND METHOD

## 1.1 Files used

| File | Role |
|---|---|

## 1.2 Schema

Every record carries all 13 fields, with no nulls:

`review_id`, `app_id`, `app_name`, `country`, `country_name`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `author`, `date` (ISO 8601 UTC), `vote_count`, `vote_sum`, `is_edited`.

**Fields used in analysis:** `review_id` (the traceability key), `country`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `date`.
**Fields not used:** `author` (not analysed, to avoid profiling individuals), `app_id`, `app_name`, `country_name` (constant or redundant).
**Fields that carry no signal in this corpus:** `vote_sum`/`vote_count` are non-zero on exactly 3 records, all with value 1 (`959856482`, `960474140`, `961110962`) — too sparse to weight anything by. `is_edited` is `false` on all 70 records, so no review here was revised after posting.

`title` and `body` arrive HTML-escaped (`&#39;` for apostrophes); all text was unescaped before reading.

## 1.4 Processing method

1. Parsed all 70 records programmatically; verified count, uniqueness and duplicates.
2. Reconciled the merged corpus against the 14 per-country files, ID set by ID set.
3. **Read all 70 reviews individually in full, in chronological order** — no keyword search, no sampling, no rating filter. At 70 records with a 60-character median body, full manual reading is feasible and was the primary method; scripts were used only for counting, cross-tabulation and reconciliation, never for classification.
4. Translated the 25 non-English reviews; translations are given beside every quotation and in the appendix.
5. Assigned non-exclusive theme labels by hand, recorded as explicit ID lists, then counted by script so that no count is transcribed by hand.
6. Verified every ID in every theme list resolves to a real record, and that no theme list contains a repeated ID (both assertions pass).
7. Retrieved the live store listing for external context (see §1.6).

**Classification error risk:** classification is manual, by a single reader, so it is consistent but not independently validated. The main judgement calls are disclosed in §1.5.

## 1.5 Limitations and judgement calls

- **Sample size is the dominant limitation.** 70 reviews across 7.6 years and 14 storefronts. One review = 1.43%. Themes of 1–3 reviews are recorded for completeness, and labelled, but should be read as anecdotes with IDs attached, not as measured rates.
- **No country analysis is possible or attempted.** Largest storefront is US at 20, well under the 50 threshold; per direction for this app, only global findings are produced.
- **Selection bias, unquantifiable.** Written reviews are a self-selected minority of users. Worse, §2 establishes that this app actively solicited ratings in-app during 2014–15, so an unknown share of the early positive records exist because of a prompt rather than an opinion. At least 3 records (4.29%) say so outright.
- **The corpus is heavily front-loaded**: 85.71% of reviews come from the app's first 22 months. Any comparison of "early" against "recent" pits 60 reviews against 10.
- **Low information density.** 18 reviews (25.71%) are 30 characters or fewer of substantive content — e.g. `1202765053` (us, 1★) in full: *"Bad" / "Just bad"*. These count toward the denominator and toward rating totals, but contribute no theme evidence. 12 of the 34 five-star reviews (35.29% of 5★) fall in this group.
- **Judgement call — `1367475729`** (kr, 3★, *"Storage exceeded — please remove it from my purchase list"*) was **not** counted as paid evidence. The Korean App Store "purchase list" includes free downloads, so it does not establish payment. Counting it would raise `paid_explicit` from 2 to 3 (2.86% → 4.29%).
- **Judgement call — `1222427563`** (*"well worth trying out and paying for the upgrade"*) is coded `paid_implied`, kept separate from the 2 confirmed payers, and excluded from all paid counts.
- **Judgement call — `1122529516`** (kr, 3★, body *"Review"*) is coded low-information rather than a nag complaint. Its content strongly suggests prompt-driven authorship, but the review does not say so. Including it would raise the nag-complaint count from 6 to 7 (8.57% → 10.00%).
- **No version field exists** in the schema, so themes cannot be attributed to releases. Version references come only from review text (`1197160923` names 1.4.2; `6684084592` names iOS 13 and 14).
- **Nothing here supports a causal claim.** Ratings correlate with themes; they do not prove that a theme caused a rating, except in the handful of cases where the reviewer states the mechanism themselves (e.g. `1113922936`, "I docked one star for this").

## 5.2 What made people want to pay

2. **The upsell mechanism repelled the buyer (2 reviewers, 2.86%).** `1099032136`: *"I'm very unlikely to pay for the upgrade just to get rid of the pestering; I'm more likely just to try a different app."* `1133797353`: *"Deleted."*

## 5.4 Post-purchase experience

Only 2 confirmed payers exist, and they land at opposite poles:

## 7.2 Trend 1 — Review volume collapsed, and there is a 3.8-year hole in the corpus

**60 of 70 reviews (85.71%) were written in the app's first 22 months.** After 8 Jul 2016 (`1407746285`) the corpus records **nothing for 1,403 days** until 11 May 2020 (`5929885128`).

Treat the gap as an observation, not a measured decline in usage.

4.30 (2014) → 3.78 (2015) → 3.25 (2016) → 2.50 (2020). The 2021 uptick (4.00) rests on **2 reviews** and means nothing.

The 2014→2015 fall of 0.52 stars is the only movement with a usable sample on both sides (23 vs 37). # PART 9 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 9.1 Counting rules

- **Denominator:** 70 for every global percentage, without exception. Segment rates (e.g. "of 4★ reviews") are labelled as such and always shown alongside the global count.
- **Non-exclusive coding:** one review may carry several themes. `6684084592` carries three (crash, data loss, slow updates); `1174519073` carries four (free cap, dated/confusing UI, no tutorial, feature request). Theme columns therefore sum to more than 70.
- **Language:** classified by script/character range; the "Spanish/Portuguese" bucket is assigned by vocabulary. Reviews with non-Latin-script storefronts but English bodies (e.g. `1195162150` kr, `1318342739` kr) are counted as English.
- **Low-information:** a review with ≤30 characters of substantive content and no identifiable theme. Counted in all denominators and rating totals; contributes no theme evidence.
