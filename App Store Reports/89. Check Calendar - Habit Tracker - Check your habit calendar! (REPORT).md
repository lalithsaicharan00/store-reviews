# Check Calendar - Habit Tracker (App Store ID 1563837295) — Full Review Analysis

**Corpus:** all **253** text reviews in `App Store Reviews/89. Check Calendar - Habit Tracker - Check your habit calendar!/reviews.jsonl`
**Window:** 2021-06-18 → 2026-09-05 (1,905 days) · **Storefronts:** 40 · **Languages:** 12 · **Corpus mean rating: 3.332★**
**Developer:** Naoki Otsu (individual developer; bundle `com.checkcalendar`) · **Released:** 2021-04-27 · **Current version:** 14.47.0 (2026-07-24)
**Method:** every review read individually in its original language; 95 theme codes across 10 families (92 hand-assigned, 3 mechanical) and 24 composite unions; 1,073 code assignments; 43 integrity checks; 20 absence tests; external evidence captured 13 September 2026.

---

## How to read this

Every quantified claim carries a **count, a percentage, the denominator, the market scope, the time window, a signal label and exact review IDs**. Review IDs are `review_id` values from `reviews.jsonl`; the bracketed `#n` is the working index (date order) used by Part 10, so any number resolves to specific reviews and any review resolves to the findings it supports. §9.J lists every review ID behind every code.

Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — a review that praises the one-tap check and also complains about the trial is counted in both. Composite unions (`U:` prefix) count each review **once**. Unless a sentence names a narrower scope, the denominator is **253 reviews, all 40 storefronts, 2021-06-18 → 2026-09-05**. Claims written as `` `CODE` = n (x%) `` always use that denominator; segment rates are written "n of N".

### ⚠️ Nine warnings before any number here is used

1. **Text reviewers are a negatively selected minority.** The same 40 storefronts carry **7,065 public ratings** averaging **4.676★**; this corpus is **3.58%** of that base and averages **3.332★**, a gap of **+1.344★**. In the US, 1★ is 49 of 1,492 (3.28%) public ratings but 17 of 61 (27.87%) text reviews; in Japan, 68 of 1,646 (4.13%) against 18 of 60 (30.00%). **This corpus describes what makes people write, not how the user base rates the app.**
2. **Only two storefronts reach the 50-review threshold:** the United States (61) and Japan (60). The other 38 storefronts hold 132 reviews between them; nothing said about them is more than limited evidence (§6.6).
3. **The trial wall is not externally dated.** The store's version history shows only the latest 25 releases (all since 2026-05-11), each with identical boilerplate notes. The first review describing the trial is dated **2024-01-06**; that is a lower bound on when it shipped, not the ship date.
4. **Reviews carry no version field.** Every link between a change and what reviewers say is **timing, not proven cause**.
5. **Purchase evidence is explicit statements only.** 31 reviewers say they paid. Another 15 describe the price favourably without saying they bought; they are not counted as buyers.
6. **Prices quoted by reviewers are what they saw at the time, in their currency.** The listing today sells one item, "Premium (Pay Once)": **$4.99** in the US, **¥780** in Japan. Archived US listings show $3.99 in May 2024 and July 2025.
7. **Half the corpus is not in English.** 126 of 253 reviews (49.80%) are in eleven other languages, 59 of them Japanese. All were read in the original; translations here are the analyst's own and are labelled.
8. **79 reviews (31.23%) have under 60 characters of body text.** §9.D re-runs the headline shares without them.
9. **Ten reviews carry a star rating that contradicts their text** (e.g. a 5★ review titled "(1 STAR RATING) DO NOT INSTALL THIS"). They are kept at the star the reviewer gave and listed in §4.7.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

## 0.1 The paywall is the single largest subject in the corpus

**`U:paywall_any` = 107 (42.29%, CI 36.4–48.5%, mean 1.82★, High-priority signal)** — reviews saying the app must be paid for, stops working after a free period, locks their records, reversed free features, limits free calendars, or is not worth charging for. It is larger than any praise theme except `U:praise_any`.

| Code | n | % of 253 | What it records |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `PAY_MUST_PAY` | 102 | 40.32% | "you have to pay" in any form |
| `PAY_TRIAL_WALL` | 73 | 28.85% | the app stops working after days or weeks |
| `PAY_NOT_WORTH` | 28 | 11.07% | too simple to charge for / price too high |
| `PAY_UNDISCLOSED` | 27 | 10.67% | the trial or the price was not stated up front |
| `PAY_SCAM_LANGUAGE` | 18 | 7.11% | "scam", "misleading", "詐欺", "estafa" |
| `PAY_DATA_LOCKED` | 8 | 3.16% | own records unreadable after the trial |

**It explains the low ratings almost entirely: 64 of 71 one-star reviews (90.14%) and 80 of 91 reviews at one or two stars (87.91%) carry a paywall code.** The 146 reviews without one average 4.438★.

The complaint is specific. People start logging, the app works, and after a period it stops:

- `10882459680` (#54, 2024-01-30, us, 3★) — "It was very cleanly, organized and easy to use. However, it says nothing about a trial."
- `11513669707` (#80, 2024-07-20, ca, 1★) — "when it ends, you're basically unable to do ANYTHING without paying. I can't even copy the notes I had written down"
- `12179154565` (#110, 2025-01-13, us, 1★) — title "Holds your data hostage for money"; "once the trial is done it completely locks your data youve logged so far"
- `13715567301` (#202, 2026-02-05, jp, 1★) — "特にトライアル期間については記載がないにも関わらず、7日後には使えなくなりました。詐欺みたいなアプリです。" *[translated: there is no mention of a trial period, yet it became unusable after 7 days. It's like a fraud app.]*

**The store listing still does not mention a trial.** The description captured on 13 September 2026, and the archived US descriptions of May 2024 and July 2025, list features and "No login required" but say nothing about a trial, Premium, a price, or any feature being paid (§9.E).

## 0.2 It is a disclosure and lock-out problem more than a price problem

The reviews separate *being charged* from *how* the charge arrives.

- **`U:trust_breach` = 42 (16.60%, CI 12.5–21.7%)** object to non-disclosure, deception, a free option that cannot be selected, or free use being withdrawn.
- **`U:value_objection` = 33 (13.04%, CI 9.4–17.8%)** object to the price itself or name a free substitute.
- **30 of the 107 paywall reviews (28.04%) also praise the app** (11.86% of 253). These users liked the product and were stopped by the wall.
- **`U:would_pay_signal` = 25 (9.88%, CI 6.8–14.2%)** say the price is fair, praise the one-time model, or say they would pay under a condition.

Reviewers write the fix themselves:

- `10882459680` (#54, us) — "If the app was upfront and said in the future, I'd have to pay I would have because I really like the app, but the dishonesty has made me delete the app"
- `11511386286` (#79, jp, 5★) — "最初に案内があればすぐに課金して使っていました。" *[translated: if I had been told at the start, I would have paid right away.]* The same reviewer: "買い切り600円は安いとはおもうのですが‥" *[translated: I do think 600 yen one-time is cheap…]*
- `13086753669` (#161, us) — "It's not expensive, but it's pretty shady and dishonest to spring this on your users."
- `12775365144` (#146, jp) — "告知した上でなら、納得して買ってくれる人は多いと思います。" *[translated: if it were announced, I think many people would buy it willingly.]*

## 0.3 The developer acknowledged the problem in writing, and the reports continued

On the Japanese listing the developer replied to trial complaints twice (§9.I) [external]:

- **2024-12-18:** apologised that the pricing "caused misunderstanding" and said the UI would be improved "in an update".
- **2025-02-10:** apologised that the pay-after-free-period mechanism is "hard to understand" and said they were considering showing which features are available before paying.

**After 2025-02-10, 15 of the 139 reviews still report non-disclosure (`PAY_UNDISCLOSED`) and 37 of the 139 still describe the trial wall.** The latest is `14385347572` (#250, 2026-08-04, us) — title "Secretly paid app". `DEV_PROMISE_UNKEPT` = 3 (1.19%) name the gap directly: `13293180955` (#171, 2025-10-21, jp) "他のレビューで指摘されて改善しますとほざきながら一切しないダメ運営" *[translated: tells other reviews it will improve, then does nothing]*; `13864528351` (#209, 2026-03-19, jp); `12775365144` (#146, 2025-06-15, jp).

The same developer's other habit app, Habit Check Calendar (analysed as app 74 in this repository), shows the same pattern: one-time Premium, trial not mentioned in the listing, and trial grievances in 23.84% of its reviews. **In this corpus the trial wall is reported about a year earlier** (first review 2024-01-06 here; 2025-01-04 in app 74's report).

## 0.4 Paid users mostly praise the product, and half of them hit a problem

**`PAID_EXPLICIT` = 31 (12.25%, CI 8.8–16.9%)** say they bought Premium. Their mean is 3.48★.

- **15 of 31 (48.4%) report a post-purchase problem** (`U:paid_problem`, segment rate): the purchase not recognised after a device change or reinstall (`DEF_RESTORE_PURCHASE`, 9 — all paid users), records lost (`DEF_DATA_LOSS`, 5 of the 6 in the corpus), ads shown after paying (2), a refund request (1) and a disputed card charge (1).
- **The failure point is the device change.** `10789194783` (#37, br, 1★) *[translated: I have the premium version but had to change devices… it's asking me to pay again]*; `11804387968` (#96, us, 1★) "i took the premium version of the app for 1 reason: so they store my data and i don't loose it, i changed my phone and lost evrything".
- **This has not been reported in 2026.** The last restore failure is dated 2025-04-29 and the last data loss 2025-09-04; `U:paid_problem` is 0 of the 69 reviews in 2026 (§7.4). The current listing advertises backup and restore "even across different devices". Whether that fixed it is not established.

What buyers value is the same as everyone else: simplicity and the one-time price. `10498260582` (#31, jp) "買い切りの課金なので有料版を購入しました。" *[translated: it's a one-time purchase, so I bought the paid version]*; `14195716095` (#232, jp) title "課金する意義がある" *[translated: worth paying for]*.

## 0.5 What people value is a calendar they can tick in one tap

- **`U:simplicity` = 81 (32.02%, CI 26.6–38.0%, mean 4.42★)** — `PRAISE_SIMPLE` 54, `PRAISE_EASE` 49, `PRAISE_ONE_TAP` 7.
- **`PRAISE_NEED_FIT` = 31 (12.25%)** — "exactly what I needed".
- **`U:calendar_core` = 25 (9.88%, mean 4.72★)** — one-tap checking, one calendar per habit, the month at a glance, editing past days.
- **`U:use_case_any` = 32 (12.65%)** — fitness 18, health and medication 14, other 4. The developer's own site says the app began as a way to remember medication.

`11171003334` (#61, jp, 5★) sets out the whole proposition: "よくあるアプリは…記録までに最低でも2タップ必要なことが多く" *[translated: typical apps often need at least 2 taps to record]*; "このアプリはカレンダーの日付部分をタップするだけで記録が完了します" *[in this app, tapping the date on the calendar completes the record]*; "ストレスフリーでできるようになりました" *[it became stress-free]*. `13635728301` (#193, gb) "I wanted a basic calendar with yes or no to track gym attendance this does exactly what I wanted".

**The simplicity that earns the praise is also the paywall's biggest obstacle.** 28 reviewers (`PAY_NOT_WORTH`) say an app this basic should not cost money — `11478965968` (#78, us) "Not paying any amount of money to put check marks on a calendar"; `13981173021` (#219, jp) "誰がカレンダーに課金すんねん" *[translated: who pays for a calendar?]*.

## 0.6 Product problems outside the paywall are few, and most concern the record itself

- **`U:defect_non_payment` = 14 (5.53%)**, 9 of them in Japan. **`U:record_integrity` = 20 (7.91%, mean 2.60★)** — checks not counted in totals (2), checks vanishing (1), notes failing to save (2), date not rolling over (1), records lost on a device change (6), records locked after the trial (8).
- **`REPORT_ALL_MARKS_COUNTED` = 4 (1.58%)** — reports count a ❌ day as done. `12787415461` (#147, us, 5★) "I went to the gym 5 times this week… But I will also put 2 times the x… when I see the report, it shows 7 counts."
- **Requests are small and mostly already shipped.** `U:request_any` = 39 (15.42%). Widgets (`REQ_WIDGET` = 5 (1.98%)) and notifications (`REQ_NOTIFICATIONS` = 5 (1.98%)) are both in today's listing; no request for either is dated after 2025-08-23.

## 0.7 What this corpus shows is *absent*

Tested against all 253 reviews with multilingual patterns; every hit was read individually (§9.F). **No crash or freeze report. No Face ID, accessibility, battery, streak, Apple Health or Family Sharing mention. Not one review praises a widget**, although widgets are in the listing and five reviewers asked for them. **No notification failure** — all six notification hits are requests or the paywall notice.

## 1.4 Processing method

1. `89-dump.py` — parse all 253 records, HTML-unescape, sort by `(date, review_id)`, emit `89-all-reviews.txt` (every review in full) and `89-index.tsv`.
2. **Every review read individually, in full, in its original language, in date order.** No sampling, no keyword filtering, no clustering. 126 non-English reviews (Japanese 59, Spanish 23, Portuguese 19, Russian 8, Chinese 7, Vietnamese, Italian, French and Korean 2 each, German and Catalan 1 each) were read in the original.
3. `89-themes.py` — a 95-code, 10-family taxonomy built **from** the corpus after reading it, with a definition and direction for every code, plus 24 unions. **Every code is used.**
4. `89-cls/001-085.txt`, `086-170.txt`, `171-253.txt` — the hand classification: one line per review with the working index, the `review_id`, its codes, its language and a note quoting the exact words that drove each code (with translations). **This is the expensive artefact.**
5. `89-build-classification.py` — asserts every line's `review_id` against the index rebuilt from `reviews.jsonl`, refuses unknown codes, duplicate lines, unclassified reviews or hand-assigned mechanical codes, then emits `89-review-classification.py` (CLASSIFICATION, THEME_TO_IDS, LANG, META, NOTES). `META_LOW_INFO`, `META_EDITED` and `META_HAS_VOTES` are assigned **mechanically** from the source record.
6. `89-validate.py` — **43 checks, all passing**: 10 structural (zero unknown IDs, zero intra-theme duplicates, zero unassigned records, every code used), 3 mechanical, 3 language, 19 that tie codes to the literal review text, 8 consistency rules. **The first run caught two real classification errors and one wrong code direction** (§9.C).
7. `89-aggregate.py` — every count, union, star band, storefront, eligible-country table, market group, era, paid segment, price point, sensitivity run, burst check and absence test; writes `89-gen/`.
8. `89-build-report.py` assembles this report; `89-verify-report.py` re-derives its claims afterwards (§9.M).

## 1.5 Signal thresholds

Applied to the global corpus (denominator 253) and separately to the US (61) and Japan (60) with their own denominators.

| Share | Label |
|---|---|
| <0.1% | Ignore by default |
| 0.1–<0.5% | Weak signal |
| 0.5–<1% | Emerging signal |
| 1–<3% | Meaningful signal |
| 3–5% | Very strong signal |
| >5% | High-priority signal |

**A caution on this scale at n = 253.** One review is 0.40% (Weak), two are 0.79% (Emerging), three are 1.19% (Meaningful) and thirteen are 5.14% (High-priority). In the US and Japan a single review is already 1.6–1.7%. Read the count and the confidence interval, not only the label, for anything under ten.

## 1.6 Limitations, stated plainly

1. **Negative selection** (warning 1). The corpus over-represents people with a grievance.
2. **Two eligible countries.** Everything else is limited evidence.
3. **No version field and no dated release notes.** The trial-wall date is inferred from reviews.
4. **Purchases are self-reported.** No conversion or refund rate can be derived.
5. **The app was not tested.** Whether the ad-supported option works, whether records are readable after the trial, and whether restore now works are all reported by reviewers, sometimes inconsistently (§2.3).
6. **Translations are the analyst's own.** Short idioms ("ゴミ", "Quá tê", "Altos chantas") are rendered by meaning.
7. **Non-exclusive counts.** Codes overlap by design; unions give once-per-review counts.

### What happens, in reviewers' words

**The wall arrives after the reviewer has invested in the app.** `PAY_TRIAL_WALL` = 73 (28.85%, CI 23.6–34.7%). The typical sequence is: install, log for days, then lose the ability to tick a box.

- `12583373249` (#125, us, 1★) — "Works great the first weeK. After the free trial ends, even the basic function of checking off 1 day is behind a paywall"
- `14249597998` (#237, us, 3★) — "it says you have to pay 6 dollars after I already did a lot of stuff with the app"
- `10815980713` (#44, jp, 3★) — "2週間ほどで急に使えなくなり、課金を求められました。はじめから、体験版ですよ、とのお知らせがほしかったです。" *[translated: after about two weeks it suddenly became unusable and asked for payment. I wanted to be told from the start that it was a trial.]*
- `11361427540` (#70, ch, 2★) — "Die App wäre wunderbar, aber leider muss man nach einer Woche bezahlen" *[translated: the app would be wonderful, but unfortunately you have to pay after a week]*

**Non-disclosure is the aggravating factor.** `PAY_UNDISCLOSED` = 27 (10.67%, CI 7.4–15.1%); 26 of the 27 also describe the trial wall. `12163557097` (#108, us) "Upon installation it says nothing about a “free trial period.”"; `14347811446` (#245, lt) "NOT A SINLGE WORD was said about the need to pay"; `11545639546` (#81, br) "não avisam que é uma semana grátis" *[translated: they don't tell you it's one week free]*.

**Fraud language follows.** `PAY_SCAM_LANGUAGE` = 18 (7.11%, mean 1.61★): "Money grubbing scammers" (`11756829140`), "Paywall scam" (`13953491590`), "textbook bait and switch tactic" (`13086753669`), "una estafa" (`11973301485`), "詐欺みたいなアプリ" (`13715567301`). US reviewers use it most (7 of 61, §6.3).

**Locked records hurt most.** `PAY_DATA_LOCKED` = 8 (3.16%). `11667422038` (#86, us) "Now I can't get back what I wrote down"; `12183886855` (#111, br) "Agora não consigo nem ver as informações que inseri esses 7 dias, estão bloqueadas." *[translated: now I can't even see the information I entered in those 7 days; it's blocked]*; `13864528351` (#209, jp) "今まで入力してきたメモも課金しないと見れなくなっていて悲しいです" *[translated: it's sad that I can't even see the notes I've entered without paying]*. This matters more for a medication tracker than for a to-do list.

**Price objections are about simplicity.** `PAY_NOT_WORTH` = 28 (11.07%, mean 1.71★). The argument is almost always that ticking a calendar should not cost money, not that $4.99 is a lot: `13627694926` (#192, us) "Why would using a calendar cost money? You can do this for free on your iPhone."; `13315450144` (#174, mx) "es demasiado sencilla para pagar 90 pesos" *[translated: it's too simple to pay 90 pesos]*. Only one reviewer argues a lower number: `12716103218` (#140, ru) "Пару сотен было бы самое то" *[translated: a couple of hundred (roubles) would be about right]*.

**Free substitutes are named.** `COMP_FREE_ALTERNATIVE` = 9 (3.56%, mean 1.11★): a physical calendar (`11282003485`), the phone's default calendar (`13021233018`), Google Calendar (`13657817683`), a paper diary (`13989810360`), other free apps (`11798002642`, `13939475879`), and building one with an AI assistant — `14362887124` (#247, in, 2026-07-29) "Uninstalled this and created my own in 10 mins with Claude."

**Stated loss.** `U:lost_user_signal` = 27 (10.67%, mean 1.81★): `CHURN_STATED` = 13 (5.14%) deleted, uninstalled or will switch; `WARN_OTHERS` = 14 (5.53%) wrote to warn others.

**Before the trial wall.** In 2022–2023 the paywall complaints are different: two free calendars only (`PAY_CALENDAR_LIMIT` = 3), dark mode moved behind payment and "previous free features into paid ones" (`8685787447`, `8746219852`), and ads (§3.3). `PAY_FEATURE_REVERSAL` = 7 (2.77%) also covers users locked out after long free use — `12995883665` (#151, ph) "Used this app around last year and it worked great, the premium features makes sense like more tabs, and more customization. But recently… now you need to pay for the WHOLE features".

# PART 8 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation is tied to findings by section number and labelled **Immediate fix**, **Experiment** or **Research question**. Ordering reflects how directly the evidence supports each item and how much of the corpus it touches.

## 8.1 Immediate fix — disclose the trial before install and before the first check (§0.1, §0.2, §0.3, §3.2, §7.5)

**Evidence:** `PAY_TRIAL_WALL` = 73 (28.85%); `PAY_UNDISCLOSED` = 27 (10.67%); `U:trust_breach` = 42 (16.60%); 64 of 71 one-star reviews carry a paywall code; the listing description (current and archived 2024 and 2025) never mentions a trial; the developer promised in December 2024 and February 2025 to make it clearer, and 15 non-disclosure reviews followed the second promise.

**Recommendation:**
- Put the trial length and the one-time price in the **first lines of the store description** and on the **first screen** after install.
- Show a **visible countdown** ("5 days left of your free trial, then Premium $4.99 once") from day one, and a reminder the day before it ends.
- Say **"one-time, not a subscription"** wherever the price appears (`PAY_SUBSCRIPTION_BELIEF` = 5 (1.98%); §3.3).

**Why first:** it addresses the largest negative theme with text changes, and reviewers (`10882459680`, `11511386286`, `12775365144`) say it would have made them buyers.

## 8.2 Immediate fix — never lock people out of their own records (§3.2, §3.5, §7.4)

**Evidence:** `PAY_DATA_LOCKED` = 8 (3.16%); "Holds your data hostage for money" (`12179154565`); medication and health use `USE_HEALTH` = 14 (5.53%); records are a stated reason to buy (`11046112570`, `11804387968`); one reviewer said locked records were fixed in January 2025 and another reported them locked in March 2026.

**Recommendation:** after the trial, keep history **readable** and **CSV export available** (the export already exists) even if new checks require Premium. Verify the current build against the 2026-03-19 report (`13864528351`).

## 8.3 Immediate fix — confirm restore and device migration for buyers (§0.4, §5.5, §7.4)

**Evidence:** 15 of 31 buyers (48.4%) report a post-purchase problem; `DEF_RESTORE_PURCHASE` = 9 (3.56%), all buyers; `DEF_DATA_LOSS` = 6 (2.37%); none reported in 2026 (5 buyers).

**Recommendation:** treat the 2026 silence as unconfirmed. Test new-device restore of both the purchase and the records, on iPhone → iPhone and iPhone → iPad (`11126142010`), and make *Restore purchase* and backup status visible in settings. Give support a reachable path (`SUPPORT_UNRESPONSIVE` = 2).

## 8.4 Experiment — a usable free tier instead of a hard wall (§2.3, §3.3, §5.6)

**Evidence:** `PAY_NOT_WORTH` = 28 (11.07%) object to charging for a basic calendar; `COMP_FREE_ALTERNATIVE` = 9 (3.56%); two reviewers ask for ads instead of payment and two report an ad-supported option that could not be used; three report using the free version; before 2024 the app was freemium (two calendars free) and paywall complaints were 11.1% of reviews, against 42.0–56.7% in each year since.

**Experiment:** A/B an ongoing free tier (e.g. one or two calendars, or ads) against the current wall, measuring purchases, uninstalls and the review stream for `PAY_NOT_WORTH`, `PAY_TRIAL_WALL` and `WARN_OTHERS` text. **The corpus cannot say which converts better**; it says which one reviewers resent.

## 8.5 Experiment — sell Premium on what buyers name, not on basic ticking (§5.4, §3.4)

**Evidence:** buyers name the one-time price (`PAY_ONCE_PRAISE` = 10 (3.95%)), keeping records safe, and specific uses. Paid-extra willingness predates the wall (`7684285778`: reminders, icons, colours — "有料オプションでも良いので" *[even as a paid option]*). Widgets were a stated purchase condition (`10792547110`).

**Experiment:** test positioning Premium around backup/sync across devices, unlimited calendars, per-emoji reports and widgets, with basic ticking free.

## 8.6 Immediate fix — make counts match what users marked (§3.7, §3.8, §6.4)

**Evidence:** `REPORT_ALL_MARKS_COUNTED` = 4 (1.58%) — a ❌ day counts as done (one 1★, two buyers); `DEF_COUNT_WRONG` = 2 and `DEF_CHECK_LOST` = 1, all Japanese, 2025-11 → 2026-07.

**Recommendation:** count per emoji/mark in reports; reproduce the two "total doesn't match" reports and the "checks vanish after reopening" report. For a record-keeping app these defects undermine the reason to pay.

## 8.7 Research question — how is the review prompt used, and what does it do to the rating? (§0.7, §6.8, §7.6, §4.7)

**Evidence:** corpus 3.332★ vs public 4.676★ over 7,065 ratings; seven five-star reviews with negative text; very short reviews rising to 44.9% in 2026; one complaint of daily review requests (`14513815810`).

**Research question:** how often does the prompt fire, and at what point in the trial? If it fires before the trial ends, the public rating mostly reflects the pre-wall experience. Store rules on prompt frequency should be checked.

## 8.8 Research question — reconcile the privacy statements (§2.4)

**Evidence:** "never sent to third parties" vs "Data Used to Track You: Diagnostics" vs a policy that sends edited data "to the analysis service" [external]; one reviewer objects to cloud backup (§2.4).

**Research question:** which statement is accurate for the shipped app? Nothing in the corpus shows harm; the risk is to a positioning a reviewer already questions.

# PART 9 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 9.A Counting rules

1. **Denominator** is 253 (every review in `reviews.jsonl`) unless the sentence names a narrower one: a star band, an era, the US (61), Japan (60), the rest of the corpus, a market group, or the 31 explicit buyers. Segment rates are written "n of N".
2. **Codes are non-exclusive** (mean 4.24 per review including mechanical codes). **Unions count each review once.**
3. **Mean ★** for a code is the mean rating of the reviews carrying it.
4. **Signal band** is applied to the share of the stated denominator (§1.5); Wilson 95% intervals throughout.
5. **Mechanical codes** — `META_LOW_INFO` (HTML-unescaped, stripped body under 60 characters), `META_EDITED` (`is_edited` true), `META_HAS_VOTES` (`vote_count` > 0) — are assigned by the build from the source record, never by hand.
6. **`PAID_EXPLICIT`** requires the reviewer to say they paid, bought, have the paid/pro version, or were charged. Praise of the price alone does not count.
7. **`PAY_TRIAL_WALL`** requires a time element — days, a week, "trial", "after a while", "until I got a notification to pay". A bare "you have to pay" is `PAY_MUST_PAY` only. Every `PAY_TRIAL_WALL` also carries `PAY_MUST_PAY` (check C5).
8. **`PRAISE_GENERIC`** is assigned when a review approves but names no product quality; price, developer and use-case mentions do not count as qualities. It never co-occurs with specific praise (check C1).
9. **Eras** are E1 2021-04-27 → 2024-01-05, E2 2024-01-06 → 2024-12-31, E3 2025, E4 2026-01-01 → 2026-09-05.
10. **Language** is hand-assigned and checked in both directions against script detection for Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Vietnamese and Cyrillic (check L1).
11. **No deduplication** was performed; none was needed (§1.3).
