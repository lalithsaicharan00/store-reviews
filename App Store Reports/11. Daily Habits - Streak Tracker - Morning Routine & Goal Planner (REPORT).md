### ⚠️ The threshold table does not function at this sample size — read counts, not percentages

**n = 33. One review = 3.03%.** A single review therefore lands automatically in the **"Very strong"** band, and two reviews clear **"High-priority."** The bands are reported for consistency with the other reports in this set, but at n = 33 they are arithmetically meaningless as rate estimates.

**Throughout this report the raw count is the primary unit and the percentage is decoration.** A theme at "9.09%" is three people. Treat every finding here as a **hypothesis with named witnesses**, not as a measured rate.

**No storefront reaches the 50-review bar for standalone country analysis.** The largest, Russia, has 9. Every country statement in Part 7 is explicitly marked **[limited evidence]** and none is promoted to a standalone conclusion.

**One structural warning specific to this corpus.** The corpus is not evenly distributed in time: **29 of 33 reviews (87.9%) predate 15 April 2026 and only 4 (12.1%) come from August 2026.** The most consequential findings in this report rest on those last four reviews. They are reported as high-severity *because of what they describe and because they are unanimous*, not because four reviews are statistically sufficient. Every such claim is flagged.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

**Executive summary in one line:** a six-year-old, genuinely loved, deliberately minimal habit tracker whose single largest stated value driver was *"it's free and it doesn't nag you"* retrofitted a paywall over previously-free features somewhere between **December 2025 and April 2026** — and **every one of the three reviewers who then paid lost the access they bought**, taking the corpus from **29 consecutive reviews at 4–5★ (mean 4.90, zero reviews below 4★ in five years) to 1★ / 2★ / 1★ in nine days of August 2026**.

## 1. Five years without a single bad review, then three in nine days

This is the cleanest before/after signal in this analysis set.

| Window | n | Mean ★ | 1–2★ | 5★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **14 Jan 2021 → 13 Apr 2026** | **29** | **4.897** | **0 (0.0%)** | 26 (89.7%) |
| **17 Aug 2026 → 25 Aug 2026** | **4** | **2.250** | **3 (75.0%)** | 1 (25.0%) |

In **five years and three months** the app collected **zero reviews below 4 stars**. There are no 3★ reviews in the entire corpus — the rating distribution is 27 / 3 / 0 / 1 / 2. Then, in **nine days**, it collected two 1★ and one 2★.

All three are about the same thing: **money paid, access lost.**

- `14438257886` (IE, **1★**, 17 Aug 2026) — *"You may notice I posted a review in April to say this app was now charging for features that were previously free. I paid the annual fee as I had 3 years worth of history I didn't want to lose. It's now trying to charge again even though I have a subscription for another 7 months. Appears to be a scam now. Avoid this app"*
- `14446220984` (BR, **2★**, 19 Aug 2026) — *"Estou com problema no acesso vip, paguei a assinatura, liberou por uma semana, depois travou tudo, tentei reinstalar mas não liberou. Mandei mensagem no suporte e não tive resposta."* — *"I have a problem with VIP access. I paid the subscription, it unlocked for a week, then everything locked up. I tried reinstalling but it didn't unlock. I messaged support and got no reply."*
- `14472099021` (PK, **1★**, 25 Aug 2026) — *"I bought lifetime subscription. It worked properly for few months then suddenly my membership cancelled and there is no way in app to talk with developer or team."*

**This is not three separate complaints. It is one defect described three times, in three countries, on three different SKUs (annual / "VIP" / lifetime), within nine days.**

## 2. 100% of direct paid-user evidence in this corpus is an entitlement failure

**3 of 33 reviews (9.09%) give direct evidence of a purchase.** All three (**segment rate: 3 of 3, 100%; global count 3 of 33**) report **losing access they had already paid for**. Their mean rating is **1.33** against a corpus mean of 4.576.

| ID | CC | ★ | SKU named | What was paid | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `14438257886` | IE | 1 | "annual fee" | paid, 7 months of term remaining | app is *"trying to charge again"* |
| `14446220984` | BR | 2 | "VIP" subscription | paid | unlocked 1 week, then re-locked; **reinstall did not restore**; support silent |
| `14472099021` | PK | 1 | "lifetime subscription" | paid | worked "a few months", then *"membership cancelled"* |

Three distinct failure modes point at one system: **`14446220984` says restore-purchases does not work; `14472099021` says a non-consumable lifetime entitlement expired; `14438257886` says an active subscription is being re-charged.** All three are consistent with a **receipt-validation / entitlement-persistence defect**, not with three unrelated user errors.

**Severity note:** the prompt's threshold table would treat 3 reviews as a signal to investigate. This is being promoted to the top of the report under the standing exception for **safety, legal, security, or severe data-loss concerns** — customers being charged for, and then denied, purchased access is a billing-integrity issue, and two reviewers use the words **"scam"** and **"fraud"** in their titles (`14438257886`: *"Avoid this app - appears to be a scam now"*; `14472099021`: *"Purchase fraud"*).

## 3. The paywall was retrofitted over features that were previously free, and the corpus dates it

This is the mechanism behind Finding 1, and the review text pins the window tightly.

**7 of 33 reviews (21.21%, mean 4.86) name "free" as an explicit reason they like the app** — `8800831234`, `10285428672`, `10985521600`, `11468101739`, `12221849177`, `12249396648`, `13476851347`. Two of them go further and state that *everything* was free, with dates:

- `12249396648` (RU, 5★, **30 Jan 2025**): *"Огромный плюс приложения в том, что все функции бесплатны."* — **"A huge plus of the app is that all functions are free."**
- `13476851347` (RU, 5★, **5 Dec 2025**): *"Еще и функции доступны бесплатно, Спасибо разработчикам!"* — **"And the functions are available for free — thank you to the developers!"**

Then:

- `14438257886` (IE, 1★, 17 Aug 2026): *"I posted a review in April to say this app was now charging for features that were previously free."*

**Free-tier feature parity is attested as intact on 5 Dec 2025 and reported as broken by April 2026.** That is a **~4-month window (Dec 2025 – Apr 2026)** for the paywall retrofit, established entirely from review text. The app shipped v1.0.165 on 12 Jul 2026 and the paid twin app was last updated 15 Apr 2026, both consistent with monetization work in that period.

**Why this matters more than a normal pricing change:** "free" was not incidental to this product's appeal, it was the **third-most-cited praise theme in the corpus** and it was frequently praised *in the same breath as* "no pressure" and "no imposed junk" — `12221849177` (RU, 5★, Jan 2025): *"нравится, что оно бесплатно (тут уж извините, что не купила), без всякой навязанной фигни"* — **"I like that it's free (sorry that I didn't buy) — without any imposed junk."** That reviewer explicitly declined to purchase while giving 5 stars. The free tier was the positioning.

## 4. The purchase driver named in the corpus is data hostage, not feature value

`14438257886` states the reason for buying in one clause: **"I paid the annual fee as I had 3 years worth of history I didn't want to lose."**

This is the **only stated purchase motivation anywhere in the corpus**, and it is not a value exchange — it is a person paying to avoid losing accumulated data. Two facts make it a structural problem rather than one person's bad week:

1. **There is no backup or export.** The current US store description contains **zero occurrences** of "iCloud", "sync", "backup", "cloud" or "export" (checked 9 Sep 2026). The oldest unmet request in the entire corpus is exactly this: `9327859322` (CO, 5★, **25 Nov 2022**) — *"lo único que añadiría es la posibilidad de guardar el historial en la nube o con iCloud"* — **"the only thing I'd add is the ability to save history in the cloud or with iCloud."** Four years later it is still absent.
2. **Long-tenure users are therefore maximally exposed.** A user with three years of local-only history has no way to leave, and no way to keep their record if the entitlement lapses. That is precisely the user who paid — and precisely the user whose entitlement then failed.

**A monetization design where the strongest purchase driver is fear of data loss, layered on a product with no export path, converts loyal long-tenure users into 1★ reviewers when billing misfires.** That is what the August 2026 cluster shows.

## 5. The paid ladder is incoherent and undisclosed

Assembled from the US store page and the Lookup API on 9 Sep 2026, alongside what reviewers say they bought:

| Price | SKU name as listed | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **$1.99** | "Habits PRO Functions" | store page |
| **$6.99** | "Habits PRO Functions" | store page |
| **$8.99** | "Habits PRO Functions" | store page |
| **$14.99** | "Daily Habits All Features Pack" | store page |
| **$9.99** | *separate app* "Daily Habits: Lifetime Premium" (ID 1550003470) | store listing |
| "annual fee" | — | `14438257886` |
| "VIP" | — | `14446220984` |
| "lifetime subscription" | — | `14472099021` |

Four problems, all fixable, all visible without a single line of code:

1. **Three different SKUs share the identical display name "Habits PRO Functions"** at $1.99 / $6.99 / $8.99. A user cannot tell from a receipt which one they own — which is exactly the situation `14472099021` and `14446220984` are trying to describe to support.
2. **The lifetime option is cheaper as a separate app ($9.99) than as an in-app purchase ($14.99)** — a $5 penalty for buying inside the product.
3. **Reviewers describe subscriptions ("annual fee", "assinatura", "subscription for another 7 months"); the store's IAP list names no periods at all.** Whatever the underlying configuration, the user-facing naming is not communicating recurrence.
4. **The store description does not disclose the paywall at any point.** The US description contains **zero occurrences of "premium", "subscription", "free", "PRO" (as a tier), "ads" or "unlock"** — it presents AI Habit Generator, Advanced Statistics, Home Screen Widget, Reminders & Weekly Schedule, Multiple Lists, Dark Theme and Rewards & Progress as an undifferentiated feature list, with no indication that any of them is gated. Compare `14438257886`'s complaint: features that *were* free are now charged, and nothing in the listing warned them.

**Inference, labelled as such:** the paid twin app's own pitch is *"Pay once and unlock the full Daily Habits experience forever — no ads, no banners, no subscriptions."* That wording implies **the free app now shows ads**. **No reviewer in this corpus mentions ads** — but the newest review that could have is from 25 Aug 2026 and the corpus is thin after April 2026. This is flagged as a monitoring item, not a finding.

## 6. What people actually love: simplicity, and the absence of pressure

The positive corpus is unusually consistent for a six-year span.

**19 of 33 reviews (57.58%, mean 4.95) praise simplicity, ease of use or lack of overwhelm** — `8756804446`, `8800831234`, `9327859322`, `9332848196`, `9758570716`, `9809639708`, `10285428672`, `10315480705`, `10796416820`, `10874846443`, `11375045777`, `11468101739`, `12113191512`, `12221849177`, `12269558510`, `12285262144`, `13469176367`, `13476851347`, `13685904641`. That is the single dominant theme of the corpus and it is stated in five languages.

Underneath it sits a sharper, more defensible positioning claim. **4 of 33 (12.12%, mean 5.00) praise the app specifically for *not* doing things** — `8756804446`, `11375045777`, `12221849177`, `13685904641`:

- `8756804446` (BR, 5★, Jun 2022): *"odeio app que exijam horário, e ele não me perturba em relação a isso"* — **"I hate apps that demand set times, and it doesn't bother me about that."** She continues: *"Pra quem como eu, que já possui uma rotina diária, não necessita de alarme"* — for someone who already has a routine, no alarm is needed.
- `11375045777` (US, 5★, Jun 2024): *"I was looking for a simple tracker, something to hold me accountable and not overwhelm me with settings and frills."*
- `12221849177` (RU, 5★, Jan 2025): *"без всякой навязанной фигни"* — **"without any imposed junk."**
- `13685904641` (BR, 5★, Jan 2026): *"Funcional, sem firulas e sem infantilização. Testei meia dúzia de apps similares antes de encontrar e ficar com esse."* — **"Functional, no frills and no infantilization. I tested half a dozen similar apps before finding this one and sticking with it."**

**"No infantilization" is the most commercially interesting phrase in the corpus.** It is a direct rejection of the gamified-pet / cute-companion design language that dominates this category (Finch, Habit Rabbit, Roubit, Blossom — apps #10, #28, #83, #58 in this set). This reviewer tested six competitors and chose this app for what it refuses to do.

**3 of 33 (9.09%, mean 5.00) explicitly switched from or compared against competitors** — `11904287504`, `13469176367`, `13685904641`. `13469176367` (RU, 5★, Dec 2025): *"удалил все остальные приложения, оставил ваше про дела и привычки"* — **"I deleted all the other apps and kept yours for tasks and habits."**

## 7. Free unlimited habits was the acquisition wedge — and it is the thing that was monetized

`11904287504` (SE, **5★**, 2 Nov 2024), entire body: **"First app that has more than 3 habits"**.

One review, but it is diagnostic. This user's *entire reason for rating* is that competitors cap the free tier at three habits and this app did not. In November 2024 that was the wedge. By August 2026 the app is charging up to $14.99 for "All Features".

Read against Finding 3, the arc is: **the app won its audience by being the un-paywalled option in a paywalled category, then adopted the category's paywall.** Nothing in this corpus says the app cannot be monetized — `12221849177` apologises for *not* buying — but it does say the free tier was the differentiator, and that the retrofit was executed without disclosure and with broken billing.

## 8. The one recurring usability pattern: everything moves one step at a time

**2 of 33 (6.06%, mean 4.50) describe the same interaction failure in two different screens** — `10985521600`, `11490387774`. They are 17 months and two countries apart and neither mentions the other's screen, which is what makes the pattern credible.

- **History navigation** — `10985521600` (GB, **4★**, Feb 2024): *"No month view, can only see the active week so hard to review progress. Literally have to skip back one day at a time."*
- **List reordering** — `11490387774` (RU, **5★**, Jul 2024): *"Хотелось бы, чтобы пункты можно было менять местами перетаскиванием! А то написал новый пункт, хочешь переместить в начало списка, а перемещение идёт по одному пункту вверх только."* — **"I'd like items to be reorderable by dragging. As it is, you write a new item, want to move it to the top of the list, and it only moves up one position at a time."**

Both describe a UI that offers only single-step increments where a jump is needed. `10016901878` (SA, 5★, Jun 2023) independently asks for the same reordering capability — *"wish that you add rearranging the tasks"* — making **reordering 2 of 33 (6.06%)** on its own.

## 9. The biggest feature gap is history depth — and the current listing claims it is now fixed

**3 of 33 (9.09%, mean 4.67) ask for deeper history, statistics or history storage** — `9327859322` (cloud/iCloud history), `9332848196` (weekly/monthly/yearly statistics), `10985521600` (month view, date filter, search, per-task completion statistics filterable by week/month/year).

`10985521600` is the most detailed critical review in the corpus and the only one that reads like a product brief. Its four asks were: month view, date-range filter, search, and filterable per-task completion stats.

**The current store listing claims two of the three are shipped.** "ADVANCED STATISTICS — Track your progress by weeks, months and individual tasks" and "DARK THEME" both appear in the 12 Jul 2026 description; the dark theme answers `9809639708` (CZ, **4★**, Apr 2023): *"the only disadvantage is that it is impossible to pick a dark theme… it is only yellow-white and that is not comfortable for eyes in the evening."*

**Two things follow.** First, this developer does ship what users ask for — the two clearest 4★ complaints in the corpus (dark theme, month/period statistics) appear to have been addressed. Second, **there is no post-ship review evidence confirming it**: the corpus has only six reviews after Dec 2025 and none of them discusses statistics or theming. The fix is claimed by the listing, not verified by users.

**Cloud backup / export remains unaddressed** and is now load-bearing, per Finding 4.

- The paywall complaints **began in April 2026, not August 2026**;
- The corpus systematically under-counts users who reviewed more than once;
- **The true count of paywall complaints is at least 4, not 3**, and this report's use of 3 is a floor.

## Monetization — repackage around what the corpus actually values

The corpus contains **zero price objections**. It contains one satisfied user apologising for not buying (`12221849177`). This is not a product that needs a cheaper price; it needs a paid tier that adds something rather than removing something.

- **Sell durability, not access.** Backup, export, multi-device, long-history analytics. `14438257886` was willing to pay for exactly this — the failure was that they were paying to *keep* what they had rather than to *gain* something.
- **Do not sell the quiet.** 4 reviewers (`8756804446`, `11375045777`, `12221849177`, `13685904641`) chose this app because it does not nag, gamify or infantilize. Ads and upsell pressure attack the differentiator directly.
- **Price for the actual base.** 86% of ratings sit outside high-spend storefronts, concentrated in RU/MX/CO/BR/CL/IN/AR/ES. A US-anchored $14.99 pack is not priced for this audience. *(This recommendation rests on external rating-volume data, not on review text — no reviewer complains about price.)*

## Positioning — a live tension the corpus flags but cannot resolve

The listing now leads with **AI**, and several storefronts have renamed the app around it. The corpus's strongest positive signal is **anti-coaching minimalism** — *"no infantilization"*, *"I hate apps that demand set times"*, *"don't overwhelm me with settings and frills"*, *"without any imposed junk"*.

**No reviewer has said anything about the AI feature, positive or negative.** This is not evidence it is wrong. It is evidence that the highest-risk change the app has made is completely unmeasured, and that its existing loyal audience selected for the opposite quality. Treat this as the top research priority, not as a finding.

## Research questions this corpus cannot answer

1. **Do MX and CO users share the RU/BR themes?** They are 143 of 537 ratings (26.6%) and 3 of 33 written reviews (9.1%). This is the largest blind spot in the analysis.
2. **What exactly moved behind the paywall in Q1 2026, and were unlimited habits part of it?**
3. **How many users were affected by the entitlement defect?** Three wrote reviews; the true count is unknowable from here, and the overwritten April review (§8.7) proves the corpus undercounts.
4. **Is the AI habit generator being used, and by whom?** Zero review signal.
5. **Does the free app now show ads?** Implied by the paid twin's copy, mentioned by no reviewer.
6. **Did the dark theme and period statistics actually ship and land well?** Claimed by the listing, unconfirmed by users.

## 10.3 Method and its known error risk

Because the corpus is 33 records, **no automated classification was used.** Every review was read individually and assigned one or more theme labels by hand. Counts, percentages, means, band labels, era splits and country splits were then computed programmatically from that hand-coded table, so the arithmetic is reproducible and the label assignment is auditable — Part 11 shows every review's labels.

**Known error risks, disclosed:**

- **Coder judgement is single-pass.** No second coder. Borderline assignments rest on one reading — for example, whether `10985521600`'s *"Again, decent for free"* counts as free-tier praise (coded as both `praise_free` and `value_graded_on_free`).
- **`6869680821` is genuinely ambiguous** ("Limited to 3 day usage?") and is deliberately excluded from every quantified theme except `onboarding_limit_confusion` / `expectation_mismatch`. Both readings — a 3-day trial and a 3-day history window — are recorded in §2; neither is asserted.
- **Low-information reviews.** 7 of 33 bodies are under 40 characters (`10315480705`, `10796416820`, `11468101739`, `12113191512`, `12119209447`, `12285262144`, `14462951464` and similar short entries). They are coded `low_info_praise`, retained in all denominators, and support no thematic claim beyond sentiment.
- **The August 2026 cluster is 4 reviews.** Every claim resting on it is flagged in text. It is promoted despite its size under the prompt's explicit exception for safety, legal, security and severe data-loss concerns.
- **Theme membership is non-exclusive.** `14438257886` carries eight labels; `10985521600` carries seven. **Percentages must never be summed.**
- **The paywall-retrofit date window (Dec 2025 – Apr 2026) is inferred from review text**, not from a changelog. It rests on `13476851347` (5 Dec 2025, "functions are available for free") and `14438257886` (Aug 2026, referring to an April complaint). No release note confirms it.

## 10.4 Known limitations and biases

1. **n = 33 is far too small for rate estimation.** Every percentage in this report has a confidence interval wide enough to change its band label. Read the counts.
2. **No country clears 50 reviews**, so Part 7 makes no standalone country claims. The largest storefront has 9.
3. **The corpus is time-lopsided.** 29 of 33 reviews predate 15 Apr 2026. The report's most consequential section rests on 4 reviews from a 9-day window.
4. **The corpus is provably incomplete for repeat reviewers.** `14438257886` references an April 2026 review that no longer exists (§8.7). The App Store keeps one review per user, so every user who reviewed twice is represented only by their latest. **All paywall-complaint counts in this report are floors.**
5. **Written reviews are ~6% of ratings** (33 written vs 537 ratings across the same 18 storefronts) and average **0.29 stars below** the volume-weighted displayed rating. The corpus over-represents both strong enthusiasm and grievance.
6. **The written corpus does not match the market.** MX, CO and IN together hold 173 ratings and contribute 3 written reviews. Conclusions about Latin America are effectively absent from this analysis.
7. **No version field**, so nothing is attributable to a specific release, and Part 8's trends may reflect audience mix as much as product change.
8. **Only 3 reviewers give direct purchase evidence**, and all three had a bad experience. This is a **qualitative finding that a defect exists**; it is emphatically **not** a claim that all or most purchases fail, and **no conversion rate is claimed anywhere in this report**.
9. **Language findings are not cultural findings.** That every Russian, Spanish, French and Italian review is 5★ while both 1★ reviews are English almost certainly reflects who writes long critical reviews plus the timing of the billing incident, not market attitudes.
10. **Star rating is never read as a feature preference**, and no correlation here is stated as causation. Where a causal path is asserted (paywall + entitlement failure → 1★), it rests on reviewers narrating that sequence themselves.
11. **Store-level figures are a single-day snapshot** (9 Sep 2026) and will drift.

## 10.5 Counting rules

- A review counts once per theme regardless of how many times it mentions it. Themes are **non-exclusive**.
- `praise_free` requires the reviewer to name free/gratis/grátis/бесплатно as a *positive*. `praise_free_all_features` is the narrower subset that asserts *all* features are free; those two reviews carry both labels.
- `paid_user` requires an explicit statement of a purchase or a charge — not an intent, an opinion about price, or a declined purchase. `12221849177` is therefore **excluded** from `PAID_EVIDENCE` and coded `purchase_declined`.
- `entitlement_lost` requires the reviewer to state that access they paid for stopped working or was re-charged.
- `SIMPLICITY_ANY` is the union of `praise_simplicity`, `praise_no_frills`, `praise_usability`, `praise_no_overwhelm`, `praise_fit_for_need` (n = 19).
- `ANY_CRITICISM` is the union of all `req_*`, all monetization-negative and all friction/reliability labels (n = 11).
- `MONETIZATION_ANY` is the union of all free-praise, paid-user, paywall and distrust labels (n = 10, 30.30%, mean 3.80).
- Segment rates (e.g. "3 of 3 paid reviewers") are always labelled as segment rates and always shown with the global count alongside.
- Percentages are of n = 33 unless a different denominator is stated on the same line.
