## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Any claim can be traced back to the exact source record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several themes.
- The signal thresholds used throughout: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.

### ⚠️ Five warnings before any number here is used

1. **This corpus is not a user-satisfaction sample. It is heavily a billing-dispute sample.** 25.76% of all reviews are 1★, and the single largest identifiable driver is being charged money the reviewer says they did not agree to. Do not read "mean 3.51" as product quality.
2. **Theme rates are not equally comparable across languages.** The classifier is regex-based and its coverage differs by language. Where a language's coverage is thin, that theme is *under*-counted, never over-counted. Cross-country comparisons in Part 6 lead with **rating distributions**, which are language-neutral, and treat theme rates as directional.
3. **"Paid-evidence" is not "paying customers."** A review mentions a transaction most often when the transaction went wrong. Part 5 therefore splits the evidence into **voluntary purchase** and **involuntary charge** and never merges them.
4. **The corpus is back-weighted to 2015–2020** (16,370 of 19,850 reviews, 82.5%). Only 627 reviews (3.16%) are from 2024–2026. Recent-period findings are real but rest on small samples; every one is labelled.
5. **No `version` field exists in this corpus.** Release-level attribution is inferred from review dates and from what reviewers explicitly say about "the last update." It is never asserted from metadata.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. This app was loved, and then the business model destroyed it. The date is knowable.**
For its first two years the app was one of the best-rated products in its category: 2015 mean **4.75**, 2016 mean **4.77**, with 5★ at **81–83%** and 1★ at ~**1%**. In **August 2017** the developer converted a $3.99 one-time purchase into a recurring subscription and, by many accounts, revoked the entitlement of people who had already paid. Ratings fell in a straight line and never recovered: 2017 **4.40** → 2018 **2.85** → 2026 **2.37**. This is the clearest monetisation-damage case in the corpus set.

**2. For every reviewer who says they chose to buy, there is one who says they were charged without consent.**
Voluntary purchase evidence: **836 reviews (4.21%)**, mean **3.06**. Involuntary-charge evidence: **856 reviews (4.31%)**, mean **1.27**, of which **89.0% are 1★**. These two populations are nearly the same size. That ratio, not the price, is the company's core problem.

**3. Price rose roughly 25× and the complaint followed it.**
Named prices move from **$3.99 one-time** (2015–16) → **$19.99–$24/yr** (2017–18) → **$30/yr** (2019–20) → **$40–60/yr** (2021–23) → **$80–100/yr and $3.99/week** (2024–26). "Price is too high" runs at **2.9%** of 2015 reviews and **26.7%** of 2025 reviews — a 9× rise, and by 2025 it is the single most-cited theme after generic sentiment.

**4. China and Korea are not weak markets. They are billing-complaint markets.**
China: 1,337 reviews, mean **1.84**, **73.3% 1★**, and **42.6%** carry auto-charge language. Korea: 571 reviews, mean **2.84**, **24.7%** ask for a refund and **24.7%** report they cannot cancel — roughly 10× the global rate. These two storefronts are 9.6% of the corpus and 27.8% + 11.4% of all paid-evidence reviews. A large share of that volume traces to **Douyin (TikTok China) advertising** that reviewers say did not disclose the price.

**5. There is a specific, diagnosable, still-unfixed reliability bug that bricks the app on launch.**
"App stuck on *Update in progress* / won't open after update" appears in three waves — **Nov 2019**, a large cluster in **Oct 2020**, and a recurrence **Sep 2025 → Aug 2026**. One 2026 review states the root cause plainly: *"Will not launch without internet — stuck on 'update in progress' until internet is available"* (`14453446467`). A local habit tracker that cannot open offline is a fixable, high-severity defect.

**6. The thing users actually pay for is small, and the company has never priced it that way.**
Satisfied voluntary buyers describe one benefit repeatedly: a simple daily checklist with reminders that produces a felt sense of accomplishment. **P-utility** appears in 44.3% of voluntary-buyer reviews and **P-worth-paying** in 25.5%. They do not describe content, coaching, or AI. Meanwhile the app added *Challenges* and *Explore* content tabs (2020–22) that reviewers largely asked to be able to hide.

**7. The cheapest unshipped wins are unambiguous.** In order of evidence weight: honest pre-download price disclosure; a working in-app cancel path; cross-device sync (2.50% globally, rising to 7.4% by 2026); Arabic and other localisation (Saudi Arabia 20.5% of that storefront's reviews); flexible scheduling (specific weekdays, every-N-days, N-times-per-week); and habit reordering that persists.

**No deduplication was applied.** 19,729 of 19,850 normalised texts are unique; the 121 repeats are short generic phrases ("great app", "좋아요") from different authors, dates and storefronts, and are legitimately distinct reviews. Every percentage in this report uses **19,850** as the global denominator unless a narrower denominator is stated.

## 1.4 Processing method

1. **Parse** all 19,850 records programmatically; reconcile against `by_country` and `manifest.json` (Part 1.3).
2. **Direct reading.** The analyst read reviews in ordered batches, in this order: the full 1★ stream from 2015 through 2019 (875 reviews read line by line), then the complete residual — every review the classifier could not place — plus stratified reads across 2★–5★. Direct reading drove taxonomy construction; it was not a substitute for full-corpus classification.
3. **Taxonomy construction.** 90 themes were defined from what was actually read, with patterns in English, Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), Korean, Japanese, Russian, Ukrainian, Portuguese, Spanish, German, French, Italian, Turkish, Dutch, Polish, Swedish, Norwegian, Danish, Czech, Thai, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Arabic and Hebrew.
4. **Full-corpus classification.** All 19,850 records were classified against all 90 themes. Assignment is non-exclusive.
5. **Residual closure.** After each classifier pass, every unclassified review was dumped and read. This loop ran seven times, cutting the residual from 37.24% → 16.94% → 14.54% → 10.91% → **10.64%**. The final 2,111 unclassified records (10.64%) were read in full; **no new theme survived** — they are short generic remarks, single emoji, off-topic text, and phrasings of themes already counted. They remain in the denominator.
6. **Validation.** Random samples were drawn from every theme above n=20 and inspected by hand. Two themes (`D-stuck-updating`, `U-weekday-schedule`) were found noisy at first pass, were rewritten with tighter patterns, and re-run. `D-stuck-updating` was then hand-audited record by record: **43 of 46 true positives (93.5% precision)**; the 3 false positives are named in Part 10.4.
7. **Segmentation.** Purchase language was split into *voluntary* and *involuntary* (Part 6.1), because merging them produces a misleading result.

## 1.5 Known limitations

- **Regex classification has recall gaps that vary by language.** Where a language's patterns are thin the theme is undercounted. Concretely: `M-free-limit` fires on 4.9% of US reviews but only 0.4% of Chinese ones, and that gap is a coverage artefact, not a market difference. Rating distributions are used for cross-country comparison because they are immune to this.
- **App Store reviews are self-selected** toward the delighted and the aggrieved. The silent majority is absent.
- **Recency is thin.** 2024 n=413, 2025 n=146, 2026 n=68. Findings from these years are labelled `[small sample]`.
- **No conversion, retention, revenue or download data exists in this corpus.** No such figure is claimed anywhere in this report. Review volume is used as a *review-volume* measure only, never as a proxy for installs.
- **Prices quoted are prices reviewers reported**, in their own currency and year. They were not verified against the App Store's live IAP list.
- **Developer replies are not in this corpus.** Reviewers' descriptions of replies (Part 4.6) are their characterisations, not verified transcripts.

**Fact:** the free allowance was tightened from 5 to 3 around 2018 and relaxed back to 5 around 2020. **This is corroborated by a reviewer who noticed it happening in real time** — `2849109648` (AU, 6 Jul 2018): *"I saw previous reviews about it only allowing 5 habits before needing to pay a subscription... # PART 5 — PAID-USER AND UPGRADE ANALYSIS

## 5.1 The evidence base, stated honestly

**1,736 reviews (8.75%) contain explicit transaction language.** Reporting that as one group gives mean rating 1.85 — but that number is an artefact, because people mention money mainly when money went wrong. The corpus must be split.

| Segment | Definition | n | % of corpus | Mean★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Voluntary purchase** | Reviewer says *they chose* to buy / subscribe / upgrade | **836** | 4.21% | **3.06** |
| **Involuntary charge** | Reviewer says they were charged without consent / want a refund | **856** | 4.31% | **1.27** |
| **Both** | Chose to buy, *then* had a billing dispute | 51 | 0.26% | 1.31 |

**This is the headline of Part 5: the two populations are the same size.** 836 people describe deciding to pay; 856 describe money being taken. Whatever the true underlying rates are, the *review-visible* ratio is 1:1.

**What cannot be claimed:** no conversion rate, no churn rate, no renewal rate, no revenue figure, and no estimate of how many actual subscribers exist. Review text supports none of these.

# PART 7 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 7.1 Method

Reviews are bucketed by calendar month from `date`. 136 months carry at least one review. Because the corpus has no version field, releases are inferred only where reviewers explicitly reference "the last update" and dates cluster. Periods used:

- **Era 1 — Paid app (2015-06 → 2017-07):** 5,104 reviews, mean **4.73**
- **Era 2 — Subscription conversion (2017-08 → 2018-12):** 2,812 reviews, mean **3.09**
- **Era 3 — Scale and paid acquisition (2019 → 2020):** 8,454 reviews, mean **3.18**
- **Era 4 — Decline (2021 → 2026):** 3,480 reviews, mean **2.85**

The four eras sum to 19,850 — every record is in exactly one.

| 1 | **Let the trial start without first authorising a subscription.** Trial ends → *then* ask. | `M-trial-autocharge` n=1,102, 5.55%, mean **1.20**, 999 one-star (Part 3.3, 5.3) | Removes the corpus's single largest 1★ cause |
| 2 | **Send a pre-charge reminder** 48h before the trial converts, by push and email. | Reviewers repeatedly say they simply forgot and were given no warning: `2761367882` `3245169646` `2922764410` `4547651109` `6437141830` | Directly addresses the stated failure mode |
| 3 | **Put a working "Manage / cancel subscription" link inside the app.** | `M-cancel-hard` n=448, 2.26%, mean **1.54**; Korea **24.7%** of that storefront (Part 6.6) | Would address ~1 in 4 Korean reviews on its own |
| 4 | **Never default the trial to the most expensive tier.** Let the user pick, and show the price before the Apple sheet. | `M-price-tiers` n=42; `3272238239` `2380437896` `2072447786` `3013464875` | Removes the "I never chose this plan" complaint |
| 5 | **State the price and the subscription requirement in the store listing's first line.** | `M-not-free` n=379, 1.91%, mean 1.73 — reviewers quote the words "Productive is a free tool" back verbatim (`2603968263` `3279151694` `3608386362`) | Converts a trust complaint into an expectation |
| 6 | **Stop the exit-intent discount.** Halving the price the moment someone declines tells every full-price buyer they overpaid. | `M-exit-discount` n=36; `6883350995` `4581049748` `3537334343` `4356723890` | Protects the paying base's goodwill |
| 7 | **Retire canned public replies.** Answer the specific complaint or do not reply. | `C-canned-reply` n=20, mean **1.40**, **zero 5★**; `D-support` n=57, mean 1.35 | Cheapest rating repair available |

**Regional priority.** Actions 1–3 should ship to **China and South Korea first**. Those two storefronts are 9.6% of the corpus but **62.4% of all involuntary-charge reviews** (534 of 856). In China specifically, password-free payment means there is no authentication step to catch a mistaken purchase (Part 6.5) — an explicit in-app confirmation step is required there, not optional.

## 8.2 Immediate — the launch-failure bug

**Fix the blocking on-launch update path.** `D-stuck-updating` n=46 across three waves (Nov 2019, Oct 2020, Sep 2025→Aug 2026); the Oct 2020 wave hit **5.9% of that month's reviews** and destroyed user data on reinstall.

The corpus supplies the diagnosis: the app will not launch without a network connection (`14453446467`), and it runs an in-app "update" that reviewers correctly note should not exist (`12844697075`). **A habit tracker must open offline and must never gate its local data behind a network call.** Make the migration non-blocking, cache locally, and never wipe on reinstall.

## 8.4 Monetisation — repackage around what the corpus actually values

**The evidence supports a specific repricing thesis:**

1. **Sell the thing people say they'd buy.** 239 reviews (1.20%) explicitly request a one-time purchase; the request appears in every year from 2017 to 2026 and inside 5★ reviews (`2054778874`, `1809268653`, `8833995869`). A perpetual "Pro" unlock alongside the subscription would convert a population that currently converts at zero.
2. **Move the first paywall behind engagement.** Part 5.2 shows the actual purchase trigger is *hitting the cap while already engaged*. Part 5.4 shows the paywall currently fires 3–15 times in the first three minutes. Delay the first interstitial until the user completes habits on **three separate days**.
3. **Re-examine the price ladder.** `M-price-high` runs at 26.7% of 2025 reviews `[small sample]`. Weekly billing at $3.99–€6 draws uniquely hostile reactions (Part 7.3) and is the format most associated with the word "immoral" in this corpus.
4. **Price by market.** `5525308783` (CN) makes the argument the data supports: the same USD price is "three meals" in one market and "fourteen meals" in another. China's mean of 1.84 is not a product verdict.
5. **Do not gate exact reminder times.** This is a habit app's core mechanic, it is the most-resented single gate in the corpus, and free competitors provide it (`2246849667` `2172919142` `2544359717` `6953060121`).

## 8.5 What a competitor entering this category gets free from this corpus

- The core product need is small and well-specified: **reminder → tick → visible streak**. 5,565 reviews describe exactly that and nothing more.
- **A one-time purchase option is a live differentiator**, not a nostalgic preference — it is requested continuously across eleven years and all major storefronts.
- **ADHD and neurodivergent users are a self-identifying, under-served segment** (`P-adhd-nd` n=463, 2.33%), as are mental-health contexts (n=100, mean 4.34).
- **Arabic is an open market.** One in five Saudi reviews asks for it.
- **The trust bar is on the floor.** Honest pre-download pricing and a working cancel button would be positioning, not table stakes, against this incumbent.

## 8.6 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

1. What is the actual trial→paid conversion rate, and how much of it is unintended? Review text cannot say.
2. How many subscribers currently sync across devices successfully? `D-sync` measures complaints, not incidence.
3. Would a perpetual unlock cannibalise subscription revenue or expand the base? Requires a price test.
4. Did the Oct 2020 launch failure cause measurable churn? Needs retention data.
5. Is the 2024 weekly tier net-positive on revenue despite the rating damage? Needs financials.
6. What share of Chinese Douyin-sourced installs understood they were purchasing? Needs funnel instrumentation.
7. Does the app still collect location, and why? The corpus records the complaint, not the current behaviour.

# PART 9 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 9.1 Counting rules

1. **Global denominator is 19,850** — every record in `reviews.jsonl`. No record was excluded, sampled out, or deduplicated.
2. **Themes are non-exclusive.** A review carrying five themes is counted once in each. Theme percentages therefore sum to more than 100%.
3. **A theme is credited on a keyword/phrase match anywhere in `title + " :: " + body`**, case-insensitive, after HTML-entity unescaping (`&#39;` `&#34;` `&amp;`).
4. **Themes are topic-level, not sentiment-level.** `U-icons` means icons were discussed, not that icons were criticised. Direction is carried separately by the theme's rating distribution, which is why every table reports mean★ and the 1★/5★ split.
5. **Segment rates are labelled as segment rates** and always shown next to the global count (Parts 5.2, 5.3, 5.5). They never substitute for the global threshold classification.
6. **Country percentages use that country's own review count as the denominator**, stated in every table.
7. **Signal labels** follow the threshold table exactly, applied to the global denominator (and, for country findings, to that country's denominator).
8. **Prices are as reported by reviewers**, in the currency and year they wrote. They are mention counts, not verified price points.

## 9.2 Validation approach and known error risk

**What was validated.** Random samples were drawn from every theme with n>20 and read by hand. Two themes failed the first pass and were rebuilt:

- `D-stuck-updating` — first pass n=27 with visible false positives ("keep the updates coming"). Rewritten with proximity-constrained patterns, then **hand-audited record by record**: 46 matches, **43 true positives (93.5% precision)**. The 3 false positives are `1258072249` (2015, praise: "keep the updates coming"), `6310921869` (TR, "Thanks for great updates"), `8210577885` (SE, unrelated). They are left in the count and disclosed rather than silently removed.
- `U-weekday-schedule` — first pass n=92 matched any "every … day" phrasing. Rewritten to require a complaint or capability clause within the same window; final n=79.

Two themes were rebuilt for over-firing before the main run: `P-recovery` (an earlier `na\b`/`aa\b`/`clean (for|and)` pattern matched Portuguese and Spanish function words; rebuilt, 1,099 → 16) and `P-adhd-nd` (an earlier `\badd\b` matched "value-add" and the verb "add"; rebuilt, 160 → 463 with correct targets).

**Known error risk, stated plainly:**

- **Recall varies by language and is the dominant error source.** `M-free-limit` fires on 4.9% of US reviews and 0.4% of Chinese ones; that gap is a pattern-coverage artefact. Effect: theme rates are **floors**, not ceilings, especially in CJK, Arabic, Thai and Vietnamese.
- **Precision on large themes was spot-checked, not exhaustively audited.** `G-praise-generic` (n=9,205) and `P-utility` (n=5,565) are broad by design and are used only for direction, never for fine-grained claims.
- **Sarcasm is not detected.** `1672427701` is a 4★ privacy review that is plainly sarcastic; the classifier counts it as a privacy mention, which is correct, but its rating is misleading.
- **Ratings are exact and were never inferred.** All rating-based findings (Parts 4, 6, 7) are immune to classifier error.

## 9.6 Reproduction

The full result set is regenerated by:

1. Reading all 19,850 lines of `reviews.jsonl`.

## 9.12 The one-paragraph version

Productive was a 4.75-star app with a $3.99 price tag. In August 2017 it became a subscription, existing purchasers reportedly lost what they had bought, and the rating fell 1.79 stars in nine months and never recovered. The price then rose roughly 25× to about $100/year, with weekly billing added in 2024. Across 19,850 reviews, monetisation is discussed in 32.83% of them at a mean rating of 2.34, and the single most damaging mechanic — being charged at the end of a trial without perceived consent — appears in 1,102 reviews at a mean rating of 1.20, with 999 of those reviewers giving one star. For every reviewer who says they chose to buy (836), there is one who says they were charged without agreeing (856), and 62.4% of the latter come from just two storefronts, China and South Korea. Meanwhile the product itself is well-loved and narrowly defined: 5,565 reviews describe the same simple loop — remind me, let me tick it off, show me the streak — and 837 five-star reviewers complain about the pricing while still giving five stars. Nothing on the eleven-year list of unfixed issues is expensive: working sync, flexible scheduling, habit reordering that persists, Arabic, an honest price on the store listing, a cancel button that works, and an app that opens without an internet connection.
