# Habit Tracker — Daily Goals (App Store ID 6480042457) — Full Review Analysis

**App:** Habit Tracker - Daily Goals — *Motivation & Accountability*
**Developer of record:** Uniqo Lab FZC · **Bundle:** `uniqo.habit`
**Corpus:** 366 written reviews · 33 storefronts · 20 June 2024 → 2 September 2026
**Corpus mean rating:** 4.552 · **Extracted:** 8 September 2026 (`itunes MZStore userReviewsRow`)
**Analysis date:** 11 September 2026
**Business model:** Free download. **No in-app purchases are listed on the store page** (accessed 11 September 2026) and **no reviewer anywhere in the corpus reports having paid this app any money.** The product is monetized off-ledger — from May 2026 onward three reviewers describe an in-app prompt asking them to enable **"web indexing"** (a bandwidth/IP-sharing opt-in) in exchange for an ad-free experience. That prompt, and the absence of any conventional paywall, is the central commercial fact of this corpus.

## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Every claim traces to an exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several themes. 327 of 366 records (89.3%) carry at least one theme; the remaining 39 are contentless praise (median 27 characters, **all 39 are 5★**) and are counted in every denominator.
- Signal thresholds: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.

### ⚠️ Eight warnings before any number here is used

1. **This is a small corpus (366) with a very high mean (4.552).** Percentage points move ~0.27% per review. Anything below ~4 reviews is a single-digit observation, not a rate. All "weak" rows should be read as anecdotes with IDs attached, not as measurements.
2. **The positive side of this corpus is nearly monothematic.** 172 of 366 reviews (46.99%) praise the app for costing nothing. No positive theme other than simplicity comes close. The app's entire public reputation rests on one attribute that generates no revenue.
3. **There is zero direct purchase evidence.** A regex sweep for `i paid / i bought / i purchased / i subscribed / my subscription / refund / restore purchase` returns **0 hits in 366 records**. Every monetization statement in Part 6 is therefore about *intent* and *reaction*, never about conversion. No conversion, ARPU, or refund rate can be or is computed here.
4. **The dominant defect is largely historical.** Calendar-bug reports ran at 17.7% of 2025 reviews and 2.6% of 2026 reviews. Reading Part 4 as a live incident would be wrong; reading it as a solved problem with a residual tail and a permanent rating scar is right.
5. **US dominance (54.9%).** Global rates approximate US rates. The second-largest storefront, India (57), behaves very differently and is broken out separately.
6. **Only 2 storefronts reach n = 50** (US 201, IN 57). No standalone claim is made for any other country, including the UK (26) and Canada (19).
7. **Written reviews only.** Star-only ratings are absent, so this corpus over-represents people with something specific to say. 23.0% of records are ≤50 characters and 90.5% of those are 5★ — a rating prompt is firing early (Part 3.4), which plausibly inflates the 5★ count.
8. **No app-version field exists.** Every time-based statement is inferred from review dates and reviewer testimony, never from release notes.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. "It is actually free" is not a feature of this product — it is the product.**
**172 of 366 reviews (46.99%, high-priority, mean 4.83)** cite zero cost as a reason for their rating: 121 (33.06%) say "free", 60 (16.39%) specifically praise the absence of ads, 44 (12.02%) specifically praise the absence of a subscription or paywall. Reviews that say this average **4.83 stars against 4.31 for every other review.** Representative: *"Yes it really is free!"* (`11668806995`, GB), *"there's finally an app that is free to download, no fuss, no adds, no subscription which is rare to find"* (`14001397712`, AU), *"apps with full service and no in app purchases are so rare but this app is just the opposite"* (`13490562375`, IN), *"Genuinely surprised to find an app nowadays without something behind a paywall"* (`14253731512`, US). Also `11444836560`, `11455984176`, `12266898528`, `12867479846`, `13044013224`, `13595921255`, `13661448098`, `14041180742`, `14302347258`, `14497877998`. **Interpretation: the single most valuable asset this app owns is a reputation for not charging. Any monetization change is not a pricing decision — it is an identity change, and the corpus shows what the reaction looks like (finding 3).**

**2. The users are comparison shoppers who arrived pre-annoyed at the category's pricing, and two-thirds of them name price as the deciding factor.**
**52 reviews (14.21%, high-priority, mean 4.83)** explicitly describe having tried, downloaded or rejected other habit trackers before this one; **34 of those 52 (65.4% segment rate; global count 34 of 366 = 9.29%)** also praise zero cost. The phrasing is consistently that of a search that ended: *"I've in the past 2 months downloaded 20 habit tracking apps and none was satisfactory"* (`12308098235`, UG), *"I have gone through a couple apps and they all made me pay for more than 3 habits"* (`12152186871`, US), *"they're all like 'premium this, subscription that'… here it's just free, like properly FREE"* (`13933385748`, GB), *"Took some time to find this on the App Store"* (`14001397712`, AU), *"took me forever to find one of this quality"* (`13980761590`, AU). Also `11406684047`, `11939963526`, `12169274305`, `12269619388`, `12509751174`, `12859184477`, `12920818324`, `12930469783`, `13172589582`, `13246295251`, `13513275431`, `13706946367`, `13832226279`, `14041180742`, `14181244719`, `14381720374`. **Interpretation: this app wins a bake-off it never enters. Its acquisition channel is competitor paywall fatigue, which means competitors' pricing decisions — not this app's marketing — drive its installs.**

**3. The "web indexing" monetization prompt is the highest-risk item in the corpus, and every reviewer who hit it gave 1★.**
**3 reviews (0.82%, emerging, mean 1.00)** describe being asked to enable a bandwidth/IP-sharing feature to get an ad-free experience. All three are 1★ and all three post-date 22 May 2026. Verbatim: *"This app now interrupts use unless you agree to run a malware proxy downloader"* (`14092044479`, GB, 1★, 22 May 2026); *"Didn't get past the opening screen of being asked to enable 'web indexing' to get the ad-less version. Basically your phone acts as the middleman for the apps partners to use your IP address to silently download and access 'free data' found on the web. No thanks"* (`14103247613`, US, 1★, 25 May 2026); *"You PAID for this free app… Your phone would act as an intermediary between the app and its partners, using your IP address to download and access 'free data' from the web. What a free app."* (`14391211979`, US, 1★, 5 Aug 2026). A fourth reviewer had already asked the question unprompted in December 2025: *"I also appreciate that there's no subscription fees or ads. Am I trading off data privacy for this? I'd love to know."* (`13506372310`, US, 5★). **Interpretation: 3 reviews is an emerging signal by count, but it is the only theme in the corpus with a 100% 1★ rate, it directly contradicts the reputation described in finding 1, and two of the three reviewers say they abandoned the app at the opening screen — before ever using it. This is promoted above its raw count under the "clear safety, legal, security" carve-out in the threshold rules.**

**4. The prompt has not yet reached most users — which is exactly why the window to change it is open now.**
Of the **62 reviews dated 1 May 2026 or later**, 3 mention the web-indexing prompt and **28 (45.2% of that window) still praise the app for having no ads or no subscription**, including `14312607560` (*"I love that there are no overbearing ads"*, 17 Jul 2026), `14381720374` (3 Aug 2026), `14432719449` (16 Aug 2026), `14437514738` (17 Aug 2026), `14493599797` (31 Aug 2026), `14497877998` (1 Sep 2026). Post-May-2026 mean rating is **4.516**, statistically indistinguishable from the corpus mean. **Interpretation (labelled): the rollout appears partial, geo-limited, or skippable for most users. The corpus cannot tell which. But it does show the app still enjoying its no-cost reputation four months after the prompt appeared, alongside three reviewers who experienced it as a bait-and-switch. Both things are currently true.**

**5. The dominant functional defect was a month-boundary date bug, and it is now largely fixed — but it cost roughly a full star for two years.**
**37 reviews (10.11%, high-priority, mean 2.95)** report the calendar showing the wrong date, refusing to advance, reverting to an earlier month, or misaligning weekday headers against dates. Reviews reporting it average **2.95 stars against 4.73 for the rest of the corpus.** The defect fingerprint is unambiguous: **29 of the 37 reports (78.4%) were filed on days 28–31 or 1–3 of a month, against a 27.6% baseline for all reviews.** Era rate: **2024 9.1% → 2025 17.7% → 2026 2.6%.** Evidence: `11676955165`, `11782200229`, `12127449842`, `12130015583`, `12252902482`, `12253630488`, `12254692368`, `12286885201`, `12299968183`, `12602757554`, `12603431541`, `12715196508`, `12835530003`, `12961148231`, `13084927585`, `13261739634`, `13337918653`, `13460543209`, `13577309961`. **Interpretation: a single rollover/day-of-week arithmetic fault, not a class of instability. It has been substantially resolved — but four reports survive into 2026 (`13579197540`, `13594048622`, `14195919004`, `14332675510`), the most recent from 22 July 2026.**

**6. Data loss is the part of the calendar bug that has not been forgiven.**
**9 reviews (2.46%, meaningful, mean 2.11)** report habits or history erased — the lowest-rated theme of any size in the corpus. *"It deleted my habits twice"* (`11673002290`, US, 1★); *"all my habits populated and data dissapeared"* (`12253431387`, CA, 1★); *"zerou meu histórico, perdi tudo"* / "it zeroed my history, I lost everything" (`12493412514`, BR, 1★); *"it updated yesterday and lost ALL my Habits"* (`12959121316`, US, 3★); *"Acabou o mês e mais hábitos não estão mais lá, vou ter que criar tudo de novo"* / "The month ended and the habits aren't there any more, I'll have to create everything again" (`13337918653`, BR, 1★). Also `12257572119`, `12604107314`, `12837138619`, `12961148231`. **Interpretation: there is no account, no cloud backup and no export in this app (Part 2.2). When the local store breaks, the user's entire history is unrecoverable, and three of these nine users describe re-entering everything by hand. The absence of a backup path converts a bug into a total loss.**

**7. Two features are requested repeatedly, are absent, and are both already table stakes in the category: a Home Screen widget and dark mode.**
**Widget: 16 reviews (4.37%, very strong, mean 4.19)** — `11725303303`, `12003653249`, `12043735920`, `12080359258`, `12133227404`, `12204928662`, `12269062164`, `12290197598`, `12410625311`, `13628525201`, `13674503459`, `13683386039`, `13764364876`, `13772306021`, `14257995934`, `14381720374`. **Dark mode: 15 reviews (4.10%, very strong, mean 4.67)** — `11723366610`, `11725303303`, `12290197598`, `12509751174`, `12548926052`, `12919657449`, `13730591333`, `13772306021`, `13877425702`, `13987910930`, `14001397712`, `14024707633`, `14254134741`, `14332675510`, `14500436553`. Both are asked for by otherwise-delighted users, and in three cases the absence is stated as the exact reason a star was withheld: *"Does the job, 1 star removed as widget is not available"* (`12204928662`, IN, 4★), *"Estaría ya inmejorable si la app tuviera modo oscuro… Lo que le falta para 5 estrellas"* / "It would be unbeatable if the app had dark mode… it's what's missing for 5 stars" (`14024707633`, ES, 4★), *"Please add a widget function for ios then 6 stars"* (`12080359258`, GB, 5★). **Interpretation: these are the two cheapest rating-points available in the corpus. Neither requires a business-model decision.**

**8. Flexible scheduling is the largest genuine capability gap, and it is the only unmet need that materially depresses ratings.**
**14 reviews (3.83%, very strong, mean 4.14)** ask for a frequency model beyond "pick specific weekdays": *x* times per week without naming days (`12085036764`, `12128407177`, `13614313384`), every other day or every other week (`12097192081`, `11700402770`), more than once per day (`12133227404`, `12504800201`, `12605709792`), monthly (`13480035328`, `13614313384`), and arbitrary custom intervals (`14297251498`). The clearest statements: *"I might want to practice golf 3 times a week but it doesn't matter on which days"* (`12128407177`, US); *"As someone who takes medication twice a day it would be incredibly helpful to have the ability to mark the same habit done twice a day"* (`12605709792`, GB); *"My off days Fri, Sat, Sun every other week. The app dont have every other week. And its frustrating"* (`11700402770`, US, **1★ — the only 1★ in the corpus driven purely by a missing feature**). Also `12141452926`, `12204928662`, `12699325873`, `13624270960`. **Interpretation: the weekday-checkbox model is the app's one real product constraint. Users with shift work, alternating schedules or twice-daily medication cannot represent their lives in it.**

**9. Removing the habit-count cap is a live differentiator and reviewers name competitors' caps explicitly.**
**12 reviews (3.28%, very strong, mean 4.92)** praise the absence of a habit limit — *"they all made me pay for more than 3 habits"* (`12152186871`), *"the only habit tracker i've tried… where you can actually track a decent amount of habits without having to upgrade"* (`11939963526`), *"Habit tracker lets you have more than 6 habits"* (`12781234520`), *"I could add up to 15 to 20 habits without getting charged"* (`14493599797`), *"the only app that doesn't limit my habits"* (`14495210194`). Also `12080359258`, `12257572119`, `12509751174`, `12528474609`, `12797457738`, `13595335326`, `13821769895`. Counter-evidence exists and must be flagged: one 3★ reviewer reports the opposite — *"it would only let me add one habit"* (`14163084101`, US, 9 Jun 2026). **Interpretation: the uncapped list is the specific mechanism by which this app wins comparison shoppers. If any future monetization touches habit count, it attacks the exact thing 12 reviewers came here for.**

**10. Support is effectively unreachable, and the one reviewer who found the support link did not trust where it led.**
**5 reviews (1.37%, meaningful, mean 2.00)** report contacting the developer with no reply, or a broken support path: *"I contacted the dev twice but got no response"* (`11676955165`); *"I emailed developers twice today with no response.. I am asking why?"* (`12837138619`); *"Submitted issue to developers- so far nothing"* (`12959121316`); *"o suporte é fraco, não da retorno"* / "support is weak, doesn't respond" (`12493412514`); *"I went to their customer support link, but it takes you to a super-sketch website, circumventing Apple's privacy settings"* (`13084927585`). A sixth reviewer pleads publicly: *"Alguém do suporte faça isso logo"* / "Someone from support please do this soon" (`12225388457`). **Interpretation: with no account and no backup, the review page is the only support channel users have. That is why bug reports here are written as public 1★ posts rather than tickets.**

**11. The rating prompt fires on the first interaction, and it is visibly manufacturing uninformed 5★ reviews.**
**3 reviews (0.82%, emerging, mean 4.00)** complain directly — *"Used the app for 2 minutes then a reminder popped up for me to rate the app. -1 star for not giving me enough time to use the app"* (`13598041567`, 4★); *"It asks for you to review it the moment you check one thing off"* (`13848623635`, 3★); *"Kept prompting rating and review so here I am, on day 1"* (`13059723695`, 5★). The structural evidence is larger than the complaint count: **39 reviews (10.66%) carry no theme at all, median 27 characters, and all 39 are 5★**; 84 reviews (23.0%) are ≤50 characters with a mean of 4.80; and multiple reviewers state they are reviewing on day one or after two minutes (`13172589582`: *"Only used for 2 minutes"*; `12512584748`: *"first day using it"*; `14459698931`: *"One day in"*). **Interpretation: the 4.552 mean is partly a prompt-timing artefact. It inflates the store rating and simultaneously starves the developer of usable feedback — the reviews that would explain churn are never written, because people are asked before they have an opinion.**

**12. India is the growth market and it behaves nothing like the US.**
**India: n = 57 (15.6% of corpus), mean 4.895, 89.5% 5★, and zero calendar-bug reports.** US: n = 201, mean 4.443, 73.6% 5★, 14.43% calendar-bug. Indian reviews are shorter (median 71 vs 123 characters) and skew toward gratitude for free access — *"You're doing a great service to students who cannot afford to spend on applications"* (`12133935681`), *"even paid apps cannot compete with this… if i had power i would nominate u for noble prize"* (`13143629702`), *"God will bless you one day"* (`13490562375`). India's distinctive request profile is **dark mode (5/57 = 8.77% vs 2.99% US)** and **flexible frequency (7.02% vs 2.99% US)**. **Interpretation (labelled): the US/India gap is best explained by review length and recency, not by a different product experience — Indian reviews are shorter and 93% of them post-date 2024. Treating India's 4.895 as evidence of higher satisfaction would over-read the data.**

**13. The cheapest high-value actions, in evidence order:** decide and disclose the web-indexing policy before it scales (3 reviews, all 1★, `14092044479` `14103247613` `14391211979`) → ship a Home Screen widget (16) → ship dark mode (15) → add flexible frequency: *x*/week, every-other-*n*, monthly, multi-per-day (14) → add local export/backup so a date bug can never again mean total loss (9 data-loss reports) → close out the residual month-rollover tail (4 reports in 2026) → delay the rating prompt past day 7 (3 direct + 39 contentless 5★) → fix notification lifecycle: stop reminders for completed and deleted habits (6 bugs + 3 requests) → add habit reordering/sorting (4) → open a working support address (5).

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Full read.** All 366 records were sorted chronologically, dumped into four files of ~92 records, and read end to end in the original language. No sampling, no keyword-only shortcut, no truncation.
2. **Candidate generation.** Multilingual regex patterns produced candidate sets per theme (English, Spanish, Portuguese, German, Italian, Russian, Chinese, plus accent-folded Latin variants).
3. **Manual audit of every candidate list.** Each candidate was reviewed with its surrounding text. Concrete corrections applied:
   - The `calendar/date` pattern matched 55 records; **18 were removed** on inspection as feature requests about *end dates* or *scheduling*, or as unrelated uses of "stuck"/"month" — e.g. `11492021762` (*"one thing I am stuck on is its FREE"*), `11624371093`, `11922911236`, `12656091993`, `13276697912`, `13480035328`, `13614313384`, `14341833236`, `14495210194` (*"stuck in freeze mode"*). **3 were added** that the pattern missed: `12132074900`, `12834935164`, `12961148231`.
   - `mental_health` was cut from 9 regex hits to 3 — six matched the word "health" in the ordinary sense of healthy habits (`11581026715`, `12016317906`, `14011499566`, `14466743179`, `11521506590`, `13675284721`).
   - `adhd_nd` was cut from 4 to 3 — `13506372310` matched on "hyper-organized", which is not a neurodivergence statement.
   - `stats_praise` was cut from 16 to 10 — six matched stats *requests* or *complaints* rather than praise (`11521506590`, `13460543209`, `13764364876`, `13897083789`, `13987910930`, `14297251498`).
   - `comparison_shopping` was refined twice: an early pattern caught "exactly what I was looking for", which is search intent rather than competitive comparison. The final rule requires an explicit reference to other apps/trackers or to having tried several. One record was removed (`11463686907`, "tried a bit") and two regex-missed records were added by hand (`12043735920`, which names the competitor "Check Me"; `12080359258`).
   - `behaviour_change` was cut from 40 to 38 and then re-added 3 hand-found records; removals were reviews where "organisation"/"motivation" appeared inside a feature request rather than a reported outcome (`12174401255`, `12587757164`, `12829927902`, `13506372310`, `13624270960`); additions were `11765500247`, `12097192081`, `12481241464`.
   - The three `privacy_web_indexing` reviews were explicitly **excluded** from `free_praise`, `no_ads_praise` and `no_sub_praise`, because they use those words to attack the model rather than praise it.
4. **Small or ambiguous themes were assembled by hand** from the full read rather than by regex: `data_loss`, `cant_edit_habit`, `cant_reorder_sort`, `notif_bug`, `support_unresponsive`, `review_prompt_nag`, `privacy_web_indexing`, `quantity_unit`, `end_date_options`, `willing_to_pay`, `fear_free_ends`, `habit_limit_praise`, and all single-record requests. These carry no classifier error rate but do carry assignment judgement; boundary rules are in Part 10.1 so a second reader can reproduce or dispute each call.
5. **Cross-tabulation** by rating, storefront, calendar year, half-year, and day-of-month (the last of these is what identified the calendar bug's month-boundary fingerprint).

**Known error risk.** The four large regex themes (`free_praise`, `simplicity_praise`, `ui_praise`, `recommend`) were audited but not re-read line by line after audit; they are deliberately inclusive and are used for magnitude, not precision. The hand-built themes were each verified against the full text of every member. On a re-inspection of 30 borderline records across `zero_cost_praise` and `simplicity_praise`, 2 were judged arguable in either direction — an implied error band of roughly ±2 records on those two themes, which does not change any signal label.

## 1.6 Limitations and known biases

- **Written reviews only.** The App Store shows far more ratings than written reviews. This corpus over-weights complaints and enthusiastic praise against the quiet middle.
- **Rating-prompt contamination.** See Executive Summary finding 11. The 4.552 mean should be treated as an upper bound on real satisfaction.
- **Small n.** 366 records. Every "weak" row (≤2 records) is an anecdote.
- **US dominance (54.9%) and only two eligible storefronts.**
- **Short corpus history.** 2 years 2 months. There is no "launch era vs mature era" comparison available, and no pre/post comparison for any pricing change other than the May 2026 web-indexing prompt (3 records).
- **Language coverage.** 24 records (6.6%) are written in a language other than English: Portuguese 8, Spanish 8, German 3, Russian 2, Chinese 2, Italian 1. All 24 were read in the original. Fine-grained themes may still under-count non-English records, because the classifier's English patterns are richer than its others; the English-language rate should be assumed the more reliable one.
- **No purchase data.** Zero reviewers report paying. Part 6 is about intent and reaction only.
- **Correlation, not causation.** Calendar-bug reviews average 2.95 against 4.73 for the rest. That is a difference in who writes what. It is consistent with the bug causing low ratings, but the corpus cannot prove causation alone.
- **The web-indexing finding rests on 3 records.** It is promoted on severity, not on frequency, and that promotion is a judgement call disclosed here.

# PART 4 — THE CALENDAR DEFECT IN DETAIL

## 4.1 Counting rules and what was excluded

A review counts as `calendar_bug` if the reviewer reports the app displaying a wrong date, refusing to advance to the current day, reverting to an earlier month, misaligning weekday headers against dates, or blocking check-off for today because of any of the above. Reviews were **excluded** if they only requested date-related *features* (end dates, monthly frequency, back-dating) or used "stuck"/"month" in an unrelated sense. 18 regex candidates were removed on that basis and 3 regex-missed records were added by hand; the full audit trail is in Part 1.5.

Final count: **37 records (10.11% of 366)**.

## 4.2 The month-boundary fingerprint

| Day of month the review was filed | Calendar-bug reports | All reviews |
|---|---|---|
| 30, 31, 1, 2, 3 (and 28–29) | **29 (78.4%)** | 101 (27.6%) |
| All other days | 8 (21.6%) | 265 (72.4%) |

Reviewers name the mechanism themselves, repeatedly and independently:
- *"It doesn't seem to know how to account for whether a month has 30 or 31 days and everything will be shifted for a period of time. I currently have the 31st listed twice for a month"* (`13460543209`, US, 3★)
- *"On the last day of every month, the app has no idea what day it is. For example, I opened the app this morning (April 30) and the app defaulted to Feb 28"* (`12603431541`, US, 4★)
- *"I've noticed at the end of every month the days are off by one day"* (`12299968183`, US, 4★)
- *"It goes Monday thru Sunday instead of Sunday thru Saturday but the dates go Sunday thru Saturday"* (`12715196508`, US, 4★)
- *"when the month changed for me it went backwards into April instead of forwards into July"* (`12835530003`, US, 2★)
- *"there's a small bug on the last day of each month where I'm unable to track my habits directly on the day"* (`14332675510`, US, 5★, 22 July 2026)

**Interpretation: this is one defect with two visible faces — a month-length rollover fault and a weekday-header offset — not a family of unrelated date bugs. The day-of-month concentration (78.4% vs a 27.6% baseline) is the strongest single piece of forensic evidence in this report.**

## 4.4 Where it turns into data loss

Nine reviewers report losing habits or history (Part 3.3 N2, mean 2.11). The sequence they describe is consistent: the date breaks → the user tries delete-and-reinstall as a workaround → the history is gone, because there is no backup.
- `12604107314` (US, 2★): *"I tried restarting the app and my phone, and even deleted/redownloaded it and reentered all my habits. No luck"*
- `12257572119` (US, 5★): *"I tried deleting it and adding habits again but still stuck on 1/30… **Update- waited til the next day and app was back to normal just sad I lost my data from uninstalling it**"*
- `11789546578` (US, 4★): *"I just wish I didn't have to delete and reinstalled it every 30 days for the calendar to work"*
- `12961148231` (US, 3★): *"When I manually moved it to July, it did not keep track of my habits and I would've had to re-enter them all again. I theorize that I would have to do this every day"*

**Interpretation (labelled, and this is the most actionable inference in the report): the app's own reviews taught users that reinstalling fixes the date. Reinstalling destroys their data. The bug fix alone does not close this loop — an export or backup path does, and the corpus contains three independent requests for one (`13276697912`, `13721332313`, `13987910930`).**

# PART 6 — PAID-USER AND UPGRADE ANALYSIS

## 6.1 Who is identifiable as a payer

**Nobody. 0 of 366 reviews (0.00%).** A sweep for `i paid`, `i bought`, `i purchased`, `i subscribed`, `my subscription`, `refund`, `restore purchase` and `bought the` returns zero hits. The store listing confirms no in-app purchases exist (Part 2.2).

This is not a gap in the data — it is the finding. **This app has no observable revenue relationship with any of its 366 reviewers.** Every statement below concerns intent and reaction only, and no conversion, ARPU, retention-of-payers or refund rate is computed or estimable from this corpus.

## 6.2 What users say they would pay for

**2 reviews (0.55%, emerging, mean 5.00)** express willingness to pay:
- `12003653249` (GB, 5★, 29 Nov 2024): *"Absolutely amazing app. Just please add widget support happy to pay for a lifetime subscription ❤️❤️💯"* — the offer is conditional on the single most-requested missing feature, and it names a **lifetime** structure rather than a recurring one.
- `13459746040` (US, 5★, 30 Nov 2025): *"Great app and love to be able to contribute to use it"* — framed as a contribution, in a review whose title is a bug report (*"No dates available after Nov29 2025"*).

**Interpretation: two data points cannot support a pricing decision. What they do support is a hypothesis worth testing — that the willingness that exists here is for a one-time or tip-style payment attached to a *visible new capability*, not for a subscription attached to existing functionality. The corpus contains a third supporting signal: the sub-theme with the highest mean rating in the entire report is "praises the absence of a subscription" at 4.98 across 44 reviews.**

## 6.3 What users say they will *not* tolerate

**4 reviews (1.09%, meaningful, mean 5.00)** pre-emptively warn against monetizing, unprompted, while giving 5★:
- `11813083900` (US): *"I will continue to use this app as long as it doesn't try to mark up the price from free."*
- `11639983783` (FR): *"I hope that it will last free of ads and subscriptions ☀️"*
- `14041958995` (US): *"I haven't been forced into a subscription to use it and **as long as this continues** I'm gonna use this app every day."*
- `12965689848` (AU): *"Only app to have no ads or subscriptions!! Love the work **never change**."*

**Interpretation: these four are the clearest statement of the commercial trap this product is in. Its most enthusiastic users have publicly conditioned their loyalty on the absence of monetization. That is a strong asset and a hard ceiling at the same time.**

## 6.4 The actual monetization — and the reaction to it

Three reviewers, all 1★, all from 22 May 2026 onward, describe the real mechanism (full quotes in Executive Summary finding 3): a launch-screen prompt asking the user to enable **"web indexing"** — routing third-party web requests through their device and IP address — in exchange for an ad-free experience.

| ID | Country | Date | Stage reached | Key phrase |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `14092044479` | GB | 22 May 2026 | Existing user, now blocked | *"now interrupts use unless you agree"* |
| `14103247613` | US | 25 May 2026 | **Never got past the opening screen** | *"Didn't get past the opening screen"* |
| `14391211979` | US | 5 Aug 2026 | **Never got past the opening screen** | *"You PAID for this free app"* |

Three things make this larger than its 0.82% share:

1. **It is the only theme in the corpus with a 100% 1★ rate.** Nothing else — not data loss (mean 2.11), not the calendar bug (2.95) — produces uniform bottom-rating.
2. **Two of three churned before first use.** These are not disappointed users; they are users who never became users. A review corpus cannot see the silent version of this cohort.
3. **It inverts the app's entire public value proposition.** `14391211979`'s title — *"You PAID for this free app"* — is precisely the opposite of the 172 reviews in P1. `14103247613`'s conclusion is *"No thanks."*

**The counter-evidence is real and must be stated with equal force.** Across the **62 reviews dated 1 May 2026 or later**, only 3 mention the prompt, while **28 (45.2% of that window) praise the absence of ads or subscriptions** — including six posted in August and September 2026 (`14381720374`, `14403840586`, `14432719449`, `14437514738`, `14493599797`, `14497877998`) and `14312607560` in July. Post-May-2026 mean rating is 4.516 against a corpus mean of 4.552.

**Interpretation (labelled; the corpus cannot resolve this): either the prompt is being rolled out to a subset of users, or it is skippable and most users skip it without comment, or it is geo-limited. What the corpus does establish is that the people who *did* hit it all rated 1★ and two abandoned immediately, and that as of September 2026 the app's public reputation is still the "actually free" reputation. Both conditions hold simultaneously, which is exactly the situation in which a disclosure decision is cheapest to make.**

## 6.5 Barriers to any future upgrade

Anything that would be sold here has to survive four things the corpus already establishes:

1. **The reputation itself.** 172 reviews (46.99%) are built on "no cost, no ads, no subscription". Introducing a paywall converts the app's single largest asset into its single largest liability, and the review page becomes the place that happens.
2. **The specific competitor grievance users arrived with.** 52 comparison shoppers named paywalls and habit caps as the reason they left the last app (`12152186871`: *"they all made me pay for more than 3 habits"*). A habit cap here would be the exact betrayal they are already primed to punish.
3. **No account and no sync.** There is no identity layer, so there is no restore path, no cross-device entitlement, and no way to make a purchase feel durable. One reviewer praises exactly this (`13625278409`: *"the fact I didn't even have to list my email sold me"*), which means adding an account to support billing has its own cost.
4. **A support channel that does not work.** Five reviewers report no reply; one reports a support link they judged unsafe (`13084927585`). **Charging money without a working support address converts every billing question into a public 1★ review.**

## 6.6 What a defensible monetization test would look like, on this evidence

Stated as hypotheses, not recommendations with confidence attached, because the corpus has n = 2 on willingness to pay:

- **Sell the widget, not the tracker.** The one explicit payment offer in the corpus is attached to the widget (`12003653249`), the widget is the most-requested feature (16), and it is the only absent feature with demonstrated substitution to a competitor (`12043735920`). A one-time unlock for widget + dark mode + flexible frequency touches nothing any existing reviewer currently has.
- **Never cap habit count.** 12 reviews praise the uncapped list by name; it is the mechanism that wins comparison shoppers.
- **Make the web-indexing choice explicit, reversible and clearly explained, and never a gate on first launch.** Both users who rejected it did so at the opening screen, before seeing the product. Moving the ask to after first successful use — and describing it in plain language — costs nothing and is the only change in this section supported by direct evidence rather than inference.

## 8.7 Trend 6 — The request mix shifted from "make it work" to "make it nicer" · **persistent theme, changing content, medium confidence**

2024H2 had the highest request rate (26.4%) and it was dominated by **structural gaps**: editing (4), frequency (5), widget (4). 2026 requests are dominated by **polish**: dark mode (8 of the 15 dark-mode requests are 2026) and widgets (7 of 16 are 2026). Flexible-frequency requests persist across all periods (2024H2: 5, 2025: 6, 2026: 3) and are the only request theme that never resolves.

## 8.8 Trend 7 — The rating prompt complaints are all recent · **emerging, low confidence**

All three (`13059723695` Aug 2025, `13598041567` Jan 2026, `13848623635` Mar 2026) fall in the last 13 months. The share of ≤50-character 5★ reviews is broadly flat across eras, so the corpus cannot confirm that prompting *increased*; it can only confirm that complaints about it are recent. n = 3, low confidence.

## 8.9 What did not change

- **Zero cost as the dominant value driver** — 42–61% in every half-year.
- **Simplicity as the second driver** — steady across all periods.
- **Flexible frequency as the top unresolved capability gap** — requested in every period from 2024H2 to 2026H2.
- **The absence of any purchase evidence** — zero in every period.
- **No widget and no dark mode** — requested continuously for 26 months, present in zero reviews.

| # | Experiment | Hypothesis | Why the corpus supports testing it | Why it is not a "fix" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **One-time "Pro" unlock for widget + dark mode + flexible frequency.** Never touch habit count, never touch the existing free surface. | New capability can be sold to this audience; existing capability cannot. | The only payment offer in the corpus is conditional on the widget and names a *lifetime* structure (`12003653249`); `13459746040` offers to "contribute"; the highest-mean sub-theme in the report is "no subscription" at 4.98 | n = 2 on willingness to pay. This is a hypothesis, not a finding. |

## 9.3 Research questions the corpus raises but cannot answer

1. **What fraction of users actually see the web-indexing prompt, and what do they do?** Three reviews cannot tell us whether this is 1% or 100% of sessions, whether declining degrades the app, or whether it is geo-limited. This is the highest-value instrumentation question in the report.
2. **Does the app's rating recover after the prompt scales?** Post-May-2026 mean (4.516) is currently indistinguishable from the corpus mean. If the prompt is at partial rollout, this number is not yet informative.
3. **Is the India/US satisfaction gap real, or entirely a review-length and recency artefact?** Part 7.3 argues the latter; only in-app telemetry can settle it.
4. **Would a one-time unlock convert?** Zero purchase evidence exists. E1 is untested in every respect.
5. **How many users hit the calendar bug and silently deleted the app rather than reviewing?** The 37 reports are the visible fraction of an unknown total, and the "reinstall destroys your data" loop (Part 4.4) means some of those users lost their history on the way out.
6. **Does the rating prompt's timing explain the 77.6% 5★ share?** The corpus shows 39 contentless 5★ reviews and several day-one reviews but cannot quantify the inflation.

## 9.4 What not to change

- **Do not cap habit count.** 12 reviews praise the uncapped list by name and 52 comparison shoppers arrived because a competitor capped them. This is the single most protected property in the corpus.
- **Do not add features to the main flow.** 119 reviews praise simplicity; 3 complain the app is too basic. The ratio is 40:1.
- **Do not require an account.** `13625278409` names it as the deciding factor; any backup should be optional and local-first.
- **Do not introduce a subscription.** "No subscription" is the highest-rated sub-theme in the report (44 reviews, mean 4.98), and 4 reviewers have publicly conditioned their loyalty on its absence (Part 6.3).

## 10.4 Counting rules
