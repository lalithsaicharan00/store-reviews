**Business model:** Free download, hard habit-count cap, subscription + lifetime IAP. ## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Every claim traces to an exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several themes.
- Signal thresholds: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.
- Where a segment rate is given (e.g. "% of substantive reviews"), the segment denominator and the global count are both shown. Segment rates never replace the global threshold classification.

### ⚠️ Seven warnings before any number here is used

1. **Half of this corpus is not organic feedback.** 83 of 166 reviews (50.0%) are exact or translated matches to a five-phrase bank ("I highly recommend it" / "so simple, perfect" / "the best one" / "I like it so much" / "very good"). **All 83 are 5★. Not one is 4★ or below.** Details and the full ID list are in Part 2.3. Every global percentage in this report is still computed on the full 166 denominator as required, but a **substantive-segment rate** is given alongside it, because the raw denominator is contaminated.
2. **A 166-review corpus moves 0.60 percentage points per review.** Any theme below n = 2 is a single observation with an ID attached, not a rate.
3. **Only one storefront reaches n = 50** — Russia (56). No standalone country claim is made for any other storefront, including the US (31) and GB (19).
4. **The US storefront contains zero English-language criticism.** 31 US reviews, **all 31 are 5★**, 22 are phrase-bank, and the single US review carrying any specific content is written in Russian (`14338216422`). Treat "US sentiment" here as unmeasured, not as positive.
5. **There is no `version` field.** Every time-based statement is inferred from review dates and reviewer testimony, never from release notes.
6. **Written reviews only.** Star-only ratings are absent. The US store page shows 4.9★ from 45 ratings while this corpus holds 31 US written reviews — so written reviews are the large majority of US ratings, which is itself unusual.
7. **Direct purchase evidence is thin but real.** 6 reviewers state outright that they paid, were charged, or tried to cancel (`13680381966`, `13670832453`, `13678734774`, `14264524351`, `13705764332`, `13882804582`). No conversion rate, ARPU or refund rate is computed or computable from review text.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. The free-tier habit cap is this product's defining problem, and it is the only theme that outranks everything else on the organic side.**
**15 of 166 reviews (9.04%, HIGH-priority signal, mean 2.00★)** name the free-habit limit as the reason for their rating. Against the 47 substantive reviews that denominator becomes **31.9% (15 of 47)** — **nearly one in three people with something specific to say came to complain about the cap.** It is the single largest driver of low ratings: it accounts for **8 of 19 one-star reviews** and **all 3 two-star reviews**. Representative: *"Se o app é de habitos, deixar cadastrar somente 2 é demais"* (`13441364320`, BR, 2★ — "if the app is about habits, allowing only 2 is too much"), *"yeah your premium plan not so expensive, but it should be at least 5 free habit entry"* (`13139338718`, KZ, 1★), *"It would be smarter to create attractive features that persuade the user to pay rather than limiting tracking to a mere two"* (`13286447070`, TR, 1★), *"Всего 2 бесплатные привычки…"* (`13015082250`, RU, 1★). Also `13057771438`, `13078324005`, `13283076453`, `13487320234`, `13678734774`, `13679689890`, `13707712022`, `13710940338`, `14007543484`, `14262833547`, `14438894096`. **Interpretation: a 2–3 habit cap is below the minimum viable unit of the job. A habit tracker with three habits is a to-do list; users say so explicitly. The cap does not convert them — it produces a 1★ review and an uninstall.**

**2. 84% of one-star reviews are about money, not about the software.**
**16 of 19 one-star reviews (84.2% segment rate; global 16 of 166 = 9.64%, HIGH)** are monetization or billing complaints: the cap (8), price-for-value (4), no trial (2), a failed charge (1), a denied refund (1), undisclosed IAP (1), mis-sold "lifetime" (1). Only **3 of 19** one-star reviews describe a software defect (`13576406218` can't edit a habit; `13511113509` + `13596102043` name-entry bug). **Interpretation: the app is not being rated down for quality. It is being rated down for its business model. That distinction determines the entire fix list — engineering work will not move this rating; packaging will.**

**3. The purchase experience itself is broken in at least four distinct, separately-evidenced ways.**
Not a general "paywall is annoying" theme — four concrete failures, each with a named ID: (a) **charged, premium never unlocked** — *"Списаны деньги за подписку, премиум функция не открылась"* (`13670832453`, RU, 1★); (b) **the monthly SKU cannot be bought** — *"не могу приобрести про версию на месяц, выдает оплату за год"* (`13678734774`, RU, 4★ — "can't buy the monthly pro version, it charges for a year"); (c) **"lifetime" sold as lifetime, delivered as annual** — *"Te dicen que es de por vida el plan y es solo para un año"* (`13705764332`, ES, 1★), which matches a store page carrying **both** a $59.99 Lifetime and a $44.99 Yearly SKU; (d) **no trial before a full annual charge** — *"I paid the anual subscription… It doesn't have a trial version and it wasn't what I was looking for"* (`13680381966`, AR, 1★) and `13679689890` (UA, 1★). Plus a refund dead end: `14264524351` (MX, 1★). **Interpretation: these are five of the six reviewers in the whole corpus who actually reached the payment step. Five of six had a bad outcome. This is the highest-severity finding in the report and it is invisible in the star average.**

**4. What people genuinely like is one attribute — minimalism — and it is stated with a specificity the templated reviews never reach.**
**23 of 166 (13.86%, HIGH, mean 5.00★)** praise simplicity/minimalism and **12 of 166 (7.23%, HIGH)** praise everyday convenience. Stripping the phrase bank, the organic version still holds: *"минималистичное без лишнего мусора"* (`14338216422`, US-storefront, Russian — "minimalist, no unnecessary junk"), *"Good app, minimalistic, effective"* (`13292317774`, KZ), *"Muy practica, minimalista y buena app!"* (`14008217420`, CL), *"Clean UI, awesome app"* (`13701569896`, UA), *"понятный и удобный интерфейс. Без заморочек"* (`12868175538`, RU). **7 reviews (4.22%, very strong)** report a concrete behaviour outcome — *"наконец-то стала придерживаться здоровому питанию и немного спорту"* (`12958650866`, RU), *"이 말이 딱 맞네요~ 위젯으로 띄어놓고 작은 습관 실천중"* (`14107277631`, KR — "that description is exactly right; I keep it as a widget and practise small habits"). **Interpretation: the product thesis works. The packaging is what fails. Do not respond to the complaints by adding features — 4 reviewers (2.41%) already call the app too thin *for its price*, which is a price objection, not a feature request.**

**5. The price complaint is a value complaint, and the corpus contains its own counter-evidence.**
**5 reviews (3.01%, very strong, mean 1.40★)** object to price — but two of the harshest sceptics say the price itself is fine: *"ce n'est pas cher"* (`13487320234`, FR, 2★ — still 2★, because of the cap) and *"your premium plan not so expensive, but…"* (`13139338718`, KZ, 1★), and one 5★ reviewer volunteers *"также понравилась цена!"* (`12933316146`, RU). The objection is always **price ÷ what you get free**: *"Una app muy cara para tan poco… Es un check list CARÍSIMO"* (`13755696898`, AR, 1★), *"The price is too high for this quality"* (`13625113466`, JP, 1★), *"para ser de pago es demasiado simple"* (`13705764332`, ES, 1★). **Interpretation: raising the free cap is a cheaper fix than cutting the price, and the corpus predicts it would neutralise most of the price objection too.**

**6. The Home Screen widget is paywalled and it is costing installs to competitors.**
Only **2 reviews (1.20%, meaningful)** name it, but one is an explicit, documented switch to a rival: *"скачивала только ради виджета. Оказалось, это платная фича… У конкурентов эта фича входит в бесплатную версию — ушла туда"* (`13081229555`, RU, 4★ — "I downloaded it only for the widget. Turned out it's a paid feature… competitors include it free — I went there"). The other (`13130702696`, BR, 5★) is a 5★ review whose only negative sentence is the same fact. A third reviewer (`13706323164`, UY, 5★) reports the widget shown in the store screenshots is **not the widget the app provides**. The one reviewer who *has* the widget is unreservedly positive (`14107277631`, KR, 5★). **Interpretation: low count, high diagnostic value — the widget is both an acquisition promise and a paywall, and the store screenshots are writing a cheque the free tier does not honour.**

**7. Scheduling flexibility is the only genuine feature gap with multi-market evidence.**
**4 reviews (2.41%, meaningful)** from 4 different storefronts ask for the same thing — habits that are not every-day: day-of-week selection (`13249103798` KZ, `13176696608` CL), weekly/monthly habits (`13032875149` GB), frequency configuration (`13705764332` ES). `13176696608` (CL, 4★) documents the downstream damage precisely: using Skip breaks the daily completion ring, and deleting a finished habit **destroys its history** because there is no end-date. **Interpretation: this is the one build-it item on the list, and it is small — a frequency field plus an end-date field.**

**8. Rating quality is deteriorating fast as the seeded reviews stop.**
Mean rating by half-year: **2025H2 4.65 (n=138) → 2026H1 3.17 (n=23) → 2026H2 3.00 (n=5)**. Phrase-bank reviews ran at 35/53 of July 2025 and **stop almost entirely after December 2025** (1 in Jan 2026, 0 after). On the non-phrase-bank subset the same decline is visible but milder: **4.14 → 3.09 → 3.00**. On substantive reviews only: **3.15 → 2.53 → 2.50**. **Interpretation: the public 4.4★ average is a decaying artefact. The organic trajectory the product is actually on is roughly 3.0★, and the cap is the reason.**

## 1.5 Processing method

1. Parsed all 166 JSONL records; reconciled counts against `manifest.json` and the 21 per-country files (Part 1.3).
2. **Read every review body and title in full**, in the original language (Russian 60+, Spanish 25+, English 50+, plus Portuguese, French, German, Dutch, Arabic, Turkish, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Ukrainian). Translations in this report are the analyst's and are marked as such.
3. Built a **template-detection pass**: normalised bodies (lowercase, strip punctuation/emoji, collapse whitespace), counted exact repeats, then grouped cross-language equivalents into 5 phrase-bank families. Matching is exact-normalised-string or containment within a body shorter than 80 characters — deliberately conservative, so the 50.0% figure is a floor.
4. **Hand-assigned themes.** With n = 166, no automated classifier was used; each review was assigned by reading. 29 leaf themes plus 4 union themes. Theme assignment is **non-exclusive**.
5. Flagged each review `substantive` / `non-substantive`. **Substantive** = names a specific feature, defect, request, or purchase/pricing fact. Contentless praise is non-substantive. 47 of 166 (28.3%) are substantive.
6. Computed global rates on n = 166, Russia rates on n = 56, segment rates on the substantive subset (n = 47) and on per-rating subsets, and applied the signal thresholds separately at each level.
7. Verification: re-read all 33 sub-5★ reviews and all 47 substantive reviews against their assigned themes; confirmed every ID in every theme list exists in `reviews.jsonl` by assertion.

**Known error risk:** theme boundaries between `free_habit_cap`, `paywall_too_aggressive` and `price_value_objection` overlap by design (11 reviews carry two of the three). Each is defined in Part 10.1 and the union figure (`any_monetization_friction`, n = 29) is the one to quote for scale.

## 1.6 Limitations and known biases

- **Contamination (primary limitation).** 50.0% of records are phrase-bank, all 5★. Any statistic computed on the full 166 is a blend of an organic corpus and a seeded one. This report always shows both.
- **Small n.** 0.60 pp per review globally, 1.79 pp per review in Russia.
- **Single eligible country.** Russia only. The US, the highest-spend market in the corpus, cannot be analysed standalone (n = 31) *and* is the most contaminated storefront (22/31 phrase-bank, 31/31 five-star).
- **No version field.** The 2→3 cap change in Part 8.2 is inferred from reviewer testimony and dates, not from release notes.
- **Storefront ≠ language ≠ nationality.** 11 reviews are written in a language that does not match their storefront (Part 2.3). Storefront is a billing-region label here, not a reliable market signal.
- **Selection bias.** Written-only reviews over-represent people with something to say, in both directions. The near-total absence of 2★ and 3★ reviews (7 of 166 combined) is itself consistent with a rating prompt that routes satisfied users to the store.
- **No developer-response field**, so support quality cannot be assessed. One reviewer references having read a developer reply (`13707712022`, RU: *"Уже читала ответ про ваших разработчиков"*), which confirms replies exist but not their content.

**Three conflicts between the store page and the corpus, each material:**

1. **The cap number is stale or inconsistent.** The page says 2. Reviews from April 2026 onward consistently say **3** (`14007543484`, `14262833547`, `14438894096`). Either the page is out of date or the cap varies by build/region.

## 2.3 ⚠️ Corpus integrity — the phrase bank

**83 of 166 reviews (50.00%) match one of five short phrases or its translation. All 83 are 5★ (100%, 0 exceptions). 1 of the 83 also carries substantive content.**

|---|---|---|

| VERY_GOOD | 19 | "Very good" · "Muy bueno" · "Super" · "Все супер" |
| LIKE_SO_MUCH | 11 | "I like it so much" · "Me gusta mucho" · "Ik vind het zo leuk" |
| BEST_ONE | 9 | "The best one" · "El mejor" · "De beste" |
| SIMPLE_PERFECT | 7 | "so simple, perfect" · "tan simple, perfecto" · "tellement simple, parfait" · "zo eenvoudig, perfekt" · "so einfach, perfekt" |

**Five independent corroborating signals:**

1. **Rating purity.** 83/83 are 5★. In the remaining 83 reviews the rating distribution is 1★19 · 2★3 · 3★4 · 4★7 · 5★50 (mean 3.80). A 100%-5★ subgroup of size 83 does not occur by chance.
2. **Cross-language identity.** `13403875241` (NL) and `13404072755` (**RU storefront**) are the same Dutch sentence, one day apart: *"Ik beveel het ten zeerste aan, super, ik vind het zo leuk, heel goed, zo eenvoudig, perfect, de beste"* — a concatenation of **all five** phrase families in one string. That is a phrase list, not a sentence.
3. **Storefront/language mismatches (11 reviews).** `13404072755` RU→Dutch · `13442553415` RU→Spanish ("el mejor") · `14338216422` US→Russian · `12928535001` US→Portuguese · `13406815450` FR→Spanish · plus 6 English-language reviews in UA/KZ/AR/TR storefronts.
4. **Temporal clustering by market.** GB+US phrase-bank reviews run 15 July–19 Sep 2025 (38 of them); ES+CL phrase-bank reviews run 18 Nov–22 Dec 2025 (16 of them). Two discrete campaigns in two language groups. Monthly phrase-bank counts: Jul 35 · Aug 18 · Sep 9 · Oct 0 · Nov 10 · Dec 10 · Jan 1 · **then zero for seven months**.
5. **GB stops dead.** The GB storefront has 19 reviews, 16 phrase-bank, and **none at all after 19 September 2025.**

**Full 83-ID list is in Part 10.3.**

**What this does and does not license.**
- It **does** mean the 4.398 mean, the 80.1% 5★ share and every US/GB/ES/NL figure are unusable as satisfaction measures.
- It **does not** mean the positive findings are false. 42 reviews (25.3%) give a *reason* for liking the app, and the specific, idiosyncratic ones (`14338216422`, `13292317774`, `14008217420`, `12868175538`, `14107277631`, `12958650866`, `13242409709`) read as genuine.
- It **does not** touch the negative findings at all: **0 of 83 phrase-bank reviews are below 5★**, so every complaint in this report comes from the organic 83.
- **Interpretation:** the seeded reviews were bought to establish a launch rating. They are now expiring, which is precisely why the half-year mean has fallen 4.65 → 3.00 (Part 8.1). *(Interpretation, not fact: the corpus establishes the pattern, not who produced it.)*

**Not supported and not claimed:** any conversion rate, ARPU, refund rate, trial-start rate, or the relative performance of the four SKUs. The corpus cannot see anyone who paid and was quietly happy.

## 8.3 Experiments — clear hypothesis, uncertain magnitude

| # | Experiment | Hypothesis | Read-out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** | Free cap at 5 vs 3 | Raising the cap increases both retention and paid conversion, because evaluation becomes possible. Predicted by every review in Part 3.3 and stated outright by `13286447070`. | Conversion rate, D7/D30 retention, 1★ rate |
| **E2** | Widget free vs paid | The widget is an acquisition driver being used as a paywall. `13081229555` documents a completed switch to a free-widget competitor; `14107277631` shows the widget *is* the engagement loop. | Install→activation, churn to competitors |
| **E3** | Collapse the SKU shelf | Four SKUs including two yearly prices and a $59.99 "Lifetime" generated `13705764332`. A simpler shelf should reduce refunds and mis-sale complaints. | Refund rate, 1★ rate on purchase themes |
| **E4** | Alarm-style reminders | `13267951780` (BR): users are setting a second alarm in the system Clock app because the notification is not strong enough. | Notification→completion rate |

## 8.4 Research questions this corpus raises but cannot answer

1. **What is the real reception in the US, GB, DE, FR, CA and AU?** 58 high-spend-market reviews yield 8 substantive records. The product is commercially blind in its highest-value markets (Part 6.3). *Method: in-app survey or a clean review-prompt cohort, not more store reviews.*
2. **Why are both billing failures Russian?** Payment processing, SKU availability, or simply the only market with continuous purchase attempts (Part 6.2)?
3. **Did the 2→3 cap change actually run, and when?** Reviewer testimony brackets it to Feb–Apr 2026; release notes would confirm (Part 7.2).
4. **Does anyone pay and stay happy?** The corpus contains zero satisfied-payer reviews. That is expected selection bias, but it means retention/renewal is entirely unmeasured here.
5. **What happens to the public rating over the next two quarters?** The organic trajectory is ~3.0★ (Part 7.3). This is forecastable internally and should be monitored, not discovered.

## 8.5 What not to change

- **Do not add features to the free tier's surface area.** Add *habit slots*. Simplicity is the single largest positive theme (23, 13.86%, HIGH) and the only attribute anyone volunteers unprompted.
- **Do not cut the price.** Three reviewers say the price is acceptable (`12933316146`, `13487320234`, `13139338718`), including two who rated 1★ and 2★ for the cap. The objection is value, not price.
- **Do not rely on the current star rating for any decision.** Parts 2.3 and 7.3.
