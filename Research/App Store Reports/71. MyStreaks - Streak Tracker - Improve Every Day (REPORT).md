### ⚠️ Seven warnings before any number here is used

1. **32 reviews is a very small corpus, and the threshold table saturates on it.** One review is **3.12%** — which already lands in the "Very strong signal" band, and two reviews land in "High-priority". **The band labels in this report are therefore reported but not trusted on their own.** Every finding also carries a raw count and a Wilson 95% confidence interval, and the text says plainly when a "High-priority signal" rests on two or three people. Treat counts as the primary evidence and the band as a formality the spec requires.
2. **No storefront reaches the 50-review threshold — not one.** The US has 28 (87.5% of the corpus); Italy, New Zealand, Brazil and South Africa have **one review each**. Part 6 therefore makes **no standalone country claims at all**, and every storefront line is labelled *[limited evidence]*. What is normally a country analysis is here a statement about why one cannot be made.
3. **This corpus is extremely positive, and the shape of that positivity is itself a finding.** 26 of 32 reviews (81.2%) are 5★, and there is **no 3★ and no 2★ review anywhere in the corpus** — a gap that a naturally-arriving review population does not usually have. 8 reviews (25.0%) carry any criticism at all, and those 8 do almost all of the diagnostic work here.
4. **The negative evidence is thin and concentrated.** There are 27 negative code assignments in total, and **13 negative codes rest on exactly one review each**. One review — `10372917549` — supplies 7 of those 27 assignments by itself. Section 0.4 treats that review as what it is: a single person's feedback list, not a measured rate.
5. **There is no version field on any review, and the corpus ends before most of the shipped product existed.** The last review is dated 2024-11-29. 9 of the 25 releases on the public version history — including the entire Challenges feature, Persona Notifications and the Journal view — shipped *after* the last review in this corpus. **Nothing here evaluates the app as it stands in 2026.**
6. **648 days separate the last written review from the extraction date.** No review in this corpus is from 2025 or 2026, while the developer shipped through December 2025. Section 0.5 treats this gap as a finding in its own right.
7. **Review-integrity flags apply to 7 reviews.** One author handle is literally `Streaking User`; one review is written in campaign register with a hashtag; two 5★ US reviews were posted **38 seconds apart**. 6 reviews (18.8%) name no product attribute at all. Section 1.7 discloses every case and §0.7 re-runs the headline findings without them.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

The eight things this corpus actually establishes, in the order they matter.

## 0.1 The free streak cap is the entire negative-rating story

Every review in this corpus rated 1★ is about one thing: being stopped from creating another streak without paying.

- `U-MONETIZATION-ANY` = **3 of 32 (9.4%)**, 95% CI 3.2–24.2%, mean rating **2.00★**. IDs: `10540595375`, `10932357315`, `12003973648`.
- The other 29 reviews average **4.90★**. The gap between those two means — 2.00 vs 4.90 — is the largest single split in the corpus.
- Both 1★ reviews (`10540595375`, `10932357315`) carry `MON-FREECAP`, and **there is no review rated 2★ or below that is about anything else**. Verified: `71-checks.py`, §0.1.

Three reviewers is three reviewers. But they are 3 of the 8 people who criticised anything, they are 100% of the bottom of the rating distribution, and the objection is identical each time and rises with time (§7). Two of the three name the alternative market out loud:

> "there are so many alternatives that don't make you pay when you've hit a max of 2 goals" — `10540595375` (1★, 2023-11-02)
> "I like this app but if I find a better one I'll definitely switch" — `12003973648` (4★, 2024-11-29)

**What is being sold is the thing the product is for.** A streak tracker whose free tier holds one or two streaks is not a limited version of the product; for the reviewer in `12003973648` it is a product that cannot do the job at all. Compare `9465102914` (2023-01-03), a 5★ reviewer running **3 streaks for over two years** — a shape of usage the later free tier does not permit.

## 0.2 The reported cap tightened, and the corpus is the only record of it

| Date | Review | ★ | Cap the reviewer reports | Exact words |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023-01-03 | `9465102914` | 5★ | 3 active streaks (no cap hit) | "I have 3 streaks that I have been doing for over 2 years now" |
| 2023-11-02 | `10540595375` | 1★ | **2** | "i was going to make my 3rd streak and i was met with an unavoidable monthly subscription" |
| 2024-02-13 | `10932357315` | 1★ | **2** | "a paywall for more than 2 streaks? And it's pricey" |
| 2024-11-29 | `12003973648` | 4★ | **1** | "the worst part is that you can't have more than one streak" |

Between February 2024 and November 2024 the reported free allowance moves from **2** to **1**. In the same window the external release notes show `v2.62 improved subscriptions` (2024-06-26) and `v2.64 Fix subscription bug` (2024-09-02), and earlier `v2.58` (2024-02-28) added *"Unlock unlimited streaks when 10 friends join through your invite link"* — a referral route past the cap rather than a larger cap.

**Stated carefully:** one reviewer reports each value, so the direction of change is *reported*, not *established*. `12003973648` could be describing a cap of 1, a trial that had lapsed, or a misremembered limit. What is solid is that the developer was actively changing subscription mechanics in exactly this window, and that every complaint about it post-dates November 2023 (§7.2).

## 0.3 This is a social product, and that is what people praise

MyStreaks is not experienced as a habit tracker with a feed bolted on. The community is the most-cited single surface in the corpus, tied for first place overall.

- `SOC-COMMUNITY` = **8 (25.0%)**, mean **4.88★** — tied first with `OUT-CONSISTENCY` (8).
- `U-SOCIAL-POS` (any positive social code) = **11 (34.4%)**, mean **4.91★**.
- Sharing with named people — spouse, family, students — appears in `SOC-SHARE` = 3: `8393594655`, `9422100038`, `9444357041`.

> "I love connecting with other Streakers on the community page. An app that encourages, uplifts, inspires" — `8444987832`
> "The community is a wealth of knowledge & will cheer on + likely inspire more streaks" — `9401363375`
> "it's fun to see other family member streaks and their progress (and we even have competitions sometimes too)" — `9422100038`

One reviewer goes further and treats the social layer as the entire proposition: *"The future of social media"* (`9618210059`). The strategic consequence is in §8: the paywall currently gates **streak count**, which is the private, single-player part of the product, while the part reviewers actually rave about — the feed — is not what they are being asked to pay for.

## 0.4 One review is a quarter of the entire defect backlog

`10372917549` (NZ, 4★, 2023-09-16) carries **10 codes**, 7 of them negative — **7 of the 27 negative assignments in the whole corpus**. It supplies, alone: reply-to-comments missing, community posting bugs, cadence not editable after creation, retired streaks not viewable, and editable counts inflating the Discover feed.

This is genuinely useful product input and it is **one person**. The honest framing: the corpus contains a *feedback list*, not a *defect rate*. Nothing in §0.4 should be prioritised by frequency — it should be prioritised by whether the mechanic described is actually broken, which is a question for the team's own build, not for this corpus.

The one structural complaint worth separating out is `INT-CHEAT`:

> "I feel like people cheat and just put a random number so they're on the discover feature… people still acknowledge those who have a large number of streaks" — `10372917549`

If streak counts are editable and Discover ranks on them, the social layer's core currency is forgeable. That is a design integrity question, not a bug, and it is the one item on this list that gets worse as the community grows.

## 0.5 The corpus went silent 648 days before extraction while the app kept shipping

The last written review is 2024-11-29. Extraction was 2026-09-08 — **648 days later**. No review in this corpus is from 2025 or 2026. Meanwhile the public version history shows continuous development through **2.75 (2025-12-02)**, including Challenges (Apr 2025), Persona Notifications (Nov 2024) and an improved Journal view (May 2025).

Two readings, and the corpus cannot separate them:

1. **Review flow genuinely stopped** — plausible for an app with 60 lifetime US ratings across six years.
2. **The collector's cursor did not reach 2025–26 reviews** — but `_state.json` marks all 9 storefronts `complete`, and the US cursor reads 28 of 28.

Either way the operative fact for a product decision is the same: **this report describes the app as it was up to late 2024.** Every feature shipped since is outside the evidence. Do not read §0.4's backlog as a current bug list.

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Dump** (`71-dump.py`) — all 32 records parsed, HTML entities unescaped, sorted by `(date, review_id)` into a stable working index 001–32, written to `71-all-reviews.txt` and `71-index.tsv`.
2. **Read** — every review read individually in full, in its original language, in date order. No sampling, no keyword pre-filtering, no truncation. At 32 reviews the entire corpus fits in one reading pass, so there is no batching artefact to disclose.
3. **Classify by hand** (`71-cls/001-032.txt`) — one line per review recording the codes assigned and a note quoting what drove each. This file is the judgement; everything downstream is arithmetic on it.
4. **Taxonomy** (`71-themes.py`) — 50 codes in 12 families, each with a direction and a written definition (Part 9 §B), plus 13 composite unions. The taxonomy was derived from the corpus, not imposed on it: every code is used, and **there are no unused codes** (validated).
5. **Resolve** (`71-build-classification.py`) — working indexes resolved to `review_id`, after asserting `71-index.tsv` field-by-field against `reviews.jsonl`. Emits `71-review-classification.py`, the single machine-readable map.
6. **Validate** (`71-validate.py`) — 11 integrity checks, all passing: map covers exactly the corpus; no unknown IDs; no unassigned records; no intra-review duplicate codes; every code defined; every review carries at least one non-meta product code; no unused codes; all union members defined; assignment total 133; eras cover every record.
7. **Aggregate** (`71-aggregate.py`, `71-extra.py`) — counts, unions, rating cross-tabs, eras, years, months, storefronts, the cap timeline, the paid cohort, bursts, language.
8. **Verify the narrative** (`71-checks.py`) — **67 targeted checks, all passing**, each tied to a specific sentence in this report, including all 17 "nobody mentions X" claims.
9. **Generate and build** (`71-tables.py` → `71-gen/`, `71-build-report.py`) — every table and every number in this report is generated from the map; the builder refuses to write if any placeholder is unresolved.
10. **Verify the written report** (`71-verify-report.py`) — post-write checks that every cited ID exists, every corpus ID is cited, and every table matches a recomputation.

**Classification confidence.** Every review was classified by hand by one analyst and there is no second rater, so inter-rater reliability is unmeasured. At 32 reviews the borderline calls are few and each is recorded in `71-cls/001-032.txt`; §9C lists the seven judgement calls that a different analyst could reasonably have made differently.

## 1.6 Limitations — read these before citing any figure

1. **32 reviews.** One review = 3.12%. The spec's threshold table was designed for large corpora and saturates here: nothing can be a "Weak" or "Emerging" signal, because the smallest possible non-zero share is already "Very strong". Bands are reported for compliance; **counts and CIs are the evidence**.
2. **Zero eligible countries.** No storefront reaches 50 reviews. Part 6 makes no standalone country claim.
3. **No paying reviewer.** Part 5 analyses a barrier, not a buyer (§0.7).
4. **No version field.** Every cause-and-effect claim is an inference from dates plus the external version history, and is labelled as such each time.
5. **The corpus ends 2024-11-29.** 9 of 25 listed releases are unevaluated (§0.5).
6. **Selection bias runs positive and is partly documented.** 81.2% 5★ with **no 3★ and no 2★ at all**, plus the burst and register flags in §1.7. The public US histogram (E2b) holds a 2★ and two more 1★ ratings that left no text — so the silent population is measurably harsher than the written one.
7. **One non-English review** (`9618663208`, pt-BR). Read in the original; the translation in this report is the analyst's and is marked. Machine translation was not used.
8. **South Africa, New Zealand, Italy and Brazil have one review each.** Two of them (ZA, NZ) happen to be the corpus's most detailed critiques. That is a coincidence of who wrote, not a property of those markets, and §6 says so.

# PART 5 — PAID-USER AND UPGRADE ANALYSIS

## 5.1 The central limitation, stated first

**There is no paying reviewer in this corpus.** Zero reviewers state they paid, subscribed, trialled, renewed or refunded (§0.7, verified in `71-checks.py`). This section therefore cannot analyse buyers, and does not attempt to. It analyses the **barrier**, which is the only part of the monetization funnel this corpus can see.

Explicitly not claimed anywhere in this report: conversion rate, trial-start rate, renewal rate, refund rate, ARPU, price elasticity, or the relative performance of any of the 10 SKUs in E2a.

## 5.2 What the corpus can see: the moment of refusal

3 reviews (9.4%, mean 2.00★), all US, all between 2023-11-02 and 2024-11-29.

| Review | ★ | Date | Where they were when they hit the wall | Outcome stated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `10540595375` | 1★ | 2023-11-02 | Creating their **third** streak — already invested, already using it | Looking at alternatives |
| `10932357315` | 1★ | 2024-02-13 | Before starting — *"I was really excited about this app"* | Abandoned |
| `12003973648` | 4★ | 2024-11-29 | Still using it on the free tier | Will switch if something better appears |

**The pattern across all three is the same and it is not "the price is too high".** It is: *the cap is set below the point where the product does its job*. Only `10932357315` calls the price itself "pricey"; the other two object to the **shape** of the offer, not its cost.

## 5.3 Purchase triggers — what the corpus does and does not show

No reviewer describes being persuaded to buy. What the corpus does contain is a set of behaviours that look like **latent willingness to pay**, all in the 5★ population, none converted in-text:

- Multi-year retention: *"over 2 years now"* (`9465102914`, `11900743150`), 823-day and 900-day streaks (`9422100038`, `9401363375`).
- Daily dependence: *"one of the few I open every day… I depend on it"* (`8444987832`).
- Ecosystem buy-in: 8 reviewers already bought the book or listen to the podcast.
- Advocacy: 7 reviewers actively recruit others.

**This is inferred interest, not purchase evidence, and the distinction is load-bearing.** `9465102914` running 3 streaks in Jan 2023 may have been grandfathered, may have paid, or may predate the cap entirely; the review does not say and this report does not guess.

| Barrier | Code | n | Reviews | What it means |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The cap sits below the job | `MON-FREECAP` | 3 | `10540595375`, `10932357315`, `12003973648` | One or two streaks is not a usable free tier for a multi-domain habit practice |

## 5.5 Refunds, cancellations and post-purchase problems

**Nothing. Zero reviews mention a refund, a cancellation, a billing failure or a post-purchase problem** (§0.6, tested). With no paying reviewers in the corpus this is expected and carries no information about the actual paid experience — it is an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence, and §8.6 lists it as a research gap.

The external release notes hint at churn-relevant friction the corpus cannot see: `v2.62` *"improved subscriptions"* (2024-06-26) and `v2.64` *"Fix subscription bug"* (2024-09-02) both suggest the purchase flow had problems in 2024. No review corroborates or contradicts this.

## 5.6 The referral unlock — a monetization instrument nobody mentions

`v2.58` (2024-02-28) shipped *"Unlock unlimited streaks when 10 friends join through your invite link"*. This is a direct, free route past the exact barrier in §5.4 — and **no reviewer mentions it, including `12003973648`, who wrote nine months later still complaining about the cap**.

Three readings, none decidable from the corpus: the mechanic is not discoverable at the paywall; ten referrals is too high a bar; or it was removed. Given that 7 reviewers are already recommending the app unprompted, a referral unlock that the most motivated advocates do not appear to know about is worth an audit regardless of which reading is true (§8.1).

The spec asks for a **high-spend** group and a **high-review-volume** group.

- **High-review-volume markets.** The group would contain exactly one member, the US, which is already §6.2. Reporting it as a "group" would imply a comparison that does not exist.

Whether the JP and CN storefronts were polled at all is not recorded in `_state.json` — only 9 storefronts appear there. **That is a collection gap, not a market finding**, and §8.6 lists it as the first thing to fix in the next collection.

## 7.2 The one demonstrable change: criticism appears, and it is monetization

|---|---|---|

**Zero monetization complaints exist before November 2023. All three exist after** (verified, `71-checks.py` §7). The mean rating drops a full point between the two windows, and the yearly series moves 5.00 → 5.00 → 4.54 → 4.25 monotonically downward from 2022.

**What this does and does not establish.** It establishes that complaints about the cap are recent and did not exist in the corpus's first two and a half years. It does **not** establish that the cap was introduced in late 2023 — an app can have a cap for years before anyone writes about it, and review volume is so low that the absence of early complaints is weak evidence. The strongest supporting detail is indirect: `9465102914` was running **3 streaks** in January 2023, which is above the cap that `10540595375` reports ten months later.

**Caution on the rating trend.** 2024's mean of 4.25 rests on 8 reviews, and 2021's 5.00 rests on 2 — both of which are §1.7-flagged. The downward direction is consistent across two independent cuts, but the magnitude is not measurable at this sample size.

## 7.4 The four trends this corpus supports

### T1 — Monetization complaints: **emerging, and the only rising negative** 
0 in E1 → 3 in E2 (33.3% of E2). All three post-date 2023-11-02, spread across 13 months. IDs: `10540595375`, `10932357315`, `12003973648`. *Confidence: moderate — three reviews, but a clean temporal separation and a consistent objection.*

### T2 — Criticism of any kind: **rising** 
17.4% of E1 → 44.4% of E2. The first critical review of any kind in the entire corpus is `9845025034` (2023-04-21) — **27 months and 17 reviews into the corpus**. Before that date, nobody wrote anything negative at all. *Confidence: moderate.*

### T3 — Community praise: **persistent, then quiet** 
`SOC-COMMUNITY` appears 8 times in E1 (34.8%) and **once** in E2 (`10372917549`). The last unambiguous piece of community praise is `10042208408` (2023-06-17). The four long-form 5★ reviews of 2024 (`11588751332`, `11896566865`, `11900743150`, `11919753703`) are about **personal outcomes, the book and the founders** — not one of them mentions other users. *Confidence: low — 9 reviews in E2. But the shift in what enthusiasts write about is visible and is worth watching, because §0.3 rests on the social layer being the differentiator.*

### T4 — The ecosystem channel: **persistent across the whole corpus** 
`ECO-BOOK` appears in Feb 2021 (`6946365307`, `6946540196`), Aug 2022 (`8969744750`) and Nov 2024 (`11900743150`) — the first and last thirds of the corpus. `ECO-FOUNDERS` appears 4 times, 3 of them in 2023–24. *Confidence: moderate — thin but evenly spread over 46 months.*

## 7.5 What persisted, and what the corpus cannot say

**Persistent across all four years:** outcome claims (`U-OUTCOME-ANY` present in every year), ease of use, and recommendation behaviour. Nothing that was praised in 2021 stopped being praised.

**What the corpus cannot say:**

- Whether anything improved. Not one reviewer describes a problem being fixed, and there is no version field to tie reviews to builds.
- Whether the 2024 rating decline continued. **The corpus stops 2024-11-29 and the app shipped for another 12 months** (§0.5).
- Whether Challenges, Persona Notifications or the Journal view — 9 releases' worth of product — landed well or badly. **No review in this corpus has seen them.**
- Whether the referral unlock (§5.6) works. Nobody mentions it.

## 8.3 Build — capabilities with a named asker

Each of the following has **exactly one asker**. That is stated plainly rather than dressed up, and each is cheap:

| # | Capability | Asker | Status after 25 releases |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Home Screen widget for one-tap check-off | `9845025034` | Open. Aligns with the corpus's loudest praise — the low-friction check-off (§3.4 P3) |
| 10 | Edit a streak's cadence after creation | `10372917549` | Open. Current workaround costs the user their streak record — the one asset §3.4 P4 says they most value |

**Item 10 deserves more weight than its single asker suggests.** The reviewer had to retire a 6-day streak and start over to change its schedule. Forcing a user to destroy the exact thing the product tells them never to break is a design contradiction, and it costs nothing in review volume to notice.

## 8.4 Positioning and monetization strategy

The strategic tension is stated once, plainly:

- **What reviewers praise most** is the community, the accountability and the outcomes (§0.3, §3.4 P1–P2) — 11 and 21 reviews.
- **What the company charges for** is the number of streaks — the private, single-player counter (§2.3).
- **What the category commoditises** is exactly that counter. Two reviewers say so directly: *"there are so many alternatives"*, *"if I find a better one I'll definitely switch"*.

**The recommendation follows from the misalignment, not from the counts:** charge for the part that is hard to copy (a real community, accountability partners, group streaks, the content ecosystem) and stop charging for the part every free competitor already gives away.

**The ecosystem channel is the asset to protect.** 8 reviewers (25.0%) mention the book, podcast or founders, and **every one of them is 5★** — the only union in the corpus with a perfect rating (§3.4 P6). Users arriving through that channel are pre-committed to the method. That is a durable acquisition advantage no habit-tracker competitor can replicate by shipping features.

## 8.5 Experiments the corpus raises but cannot settle

1. **Does a larger free cap increase or decrease revenue?** Test 5 and 10 against the current cap. The corpus supplies the hypothesis (§5.4) and nothing else.
2. **Does a feature-based paywall convert better than a count-based one?** §8.4 is a reasoned argument, not a measured result.
3. **Is the interface actually clunky?** 7 reviewers praise ease; 1 calls it clunky and 1 calls the menus awkward (§3.3 N4). These may describe different surfaces — check-off versus navigation. Usability-test the two paths separately.
4. **Does the community retain users, or just delight them?** Every community mention is 5★ and the theme goes quiet in E2 (§7.4 T3). The corpus cannot tell whether the feed drives retention or merely accompanies it.

## 8.6 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

1. **What do paying users think?** There are none in the corpus (§5.1). This is the single largest gap.
2. **What happened after November 2024?** 648 days and 9 releases are unevaluated (§0.5). **Re-collect before acting on §8.2 or §8.3.**
3. **Why is there no 3★ or 2★ review?** Prompting, or the product's shape (§4.1)?
4. **Why do GB, CA, AU and VN hold ratings but no text?**
5. **Were the JP and CN storefronts ever polled?** The app lists both languages; `_state.json` records only 9 storefronts and neither is among them (§6.5).
6. **Does the referral unlock still exist and is it discoverable?** (§5.6)

## 8.7 What to do first

1. **Re-collect the corpus** (§8.6.2). Everything in §8.2 and §8.3 is 2–3 years stale, and the current product is largely unreviewed here.
2. **Fix the paywall's shape, not its price** (§8.1.1–3). It is the whole of the 1★ population and the only negative that is growing.
3. **Ship the widget and the cadence edit** (§8.3.9–10). Both are cheap, both align with the corpus's strongest praise, and item 10 removes a design contradiction.
4. **Close the Discover-count loophole** (§8.2.6) before the community grows into the problem.

# PART 9 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## A. Counting rules

1. **Denominator.** Every global percentage uses **32**, the full corpus. No finding uses a reduced denominator without saying so in the same sentence.
2. **Non-exclusive themes.** A review may carry many codes; the mean is 4.16 (133 assignments over 32 reviews). Union counts count each review **once**, no matter how many member codes it carries.
3. **Segment rates.** Where a rate over a narrower pool is given (for example "100% of 1★"), the segment denominator and the global count are both shown, and the band label always comes from the **global** share.
4. **Signal bands.** Taken from the spec's table. **At 32 reviews the table saturates** — one review is 3.12%, already "Very strong". Bands are reported for compliance; counts and Wilson 95% CIs are the evidence. This caveat is repeated at §1.6.1 and beside the findings that depend on it.
5. **Wilson intervals.** 95% score intervals, continuity-uncorrected, computed in `71-common.py`. They are wide by design; that width is the point.
6. **Mean ★ per theme.** Arithmetic mean of the star ratings of the reviews carrying that code.
7. **Direction.** Fixed by the code's definition, not by the star rating of the review carrying it (§4.6).
8. **Meta codes** (`LOW-INFO`, `PROMO-TONE`, `AUTH-SUSPECT`) are disclosure instruments, not product findings. Every review carries at least one non-meta code (validated).
9. **No deduplication** was applied; none was required (§1.4).
10. **Translations** are the analyst's, marked where used. Machine translation was not used.

## C. Judgement calls and known error risk

One analyst classified all 32 reviews by hand; there is no second rater, so agreement is unmeasured. The seven calls most open to reasonable disagreement:

1. **`OUT-LIFECHANGE` vs `OUT-IMPROVE`.** The line drawn: an explicit transformation claim, or naming multiple life areas changed, earns `OUT-LIFECHANGE`; general helpfulness earns `OUT-IMPROVE`. `9486999099` ("Helps me do better in certain aspects of my life") was coded `OUT-IMPROVE`; a stricter reading of "aspects" could have made it `OUT-LIFECHANGE`.
2. **`ACC-SELF` vs `ACC-SOCIAL`.** Split deliberately: `11900743150` is accountable *to the app*, `8393594655` *to other people*. Collapsing them would have produced a misleadingly larger "accountability" theme.
3. **`9618210059` and `SOC-COMMUNITY`.** "The future of social media" praises the app's social *positioning* rather than the community itself. Coded `SOC-COMMUNITY`; a stricter reading would drop it, taking that theme from 8 to 7.
4. **`11362194869` ("Best app ever") and `ADV-UNQUALIFIED`.** A code was created rather than leave the review carrying only a meta code. It makes content-free praise countable instead of invisible — 1 review.
5. **`10807436448` and `CORE-TRACKVIS`.** "Great habit tracker" is faint evidence that tracking is the valued thing. Included; droppable.
6. **`MON-CAPASK` as its own code.** Separated from `MON-FREECAP` because a specific counter-offer ("5 or 10") is a different signal from an objection. 1 review.
7. **`9465102914`'s 3 streaks.** Treated as a **fact about that reviewer** (§0.2, §5.3) and deliberately **not** coded as evidence of the cap's value at that date, because the review does not say whether they paid.

**Known error risk.** The dominant risk in this report is not misclassification — at 32 reviews each was read in full and re-read while writing. It is **over-reading**: turning 1–3 reviews into a rate. Every section that could do so states the raw count in the same sentence as the percentage.
