### ⚠️ Seven warnings before any number here is used

1. **n = 45. One review = 2.22%.** A single review lands in the "Meaningful" band automatically, and three reviews clear "high-priority." The bands are reported for consistency with the other reports in this set, but they cannot be read the way they are read at n = 500. **Throughout this report the raw count is the primary unit and the percentage is decoration.** Treat every finding as a *hypothesis with named witnesses*.
2. **No storefront reaches the 50-review bar.** The largest are Turkey (8) and India (8). Every country statement in Part 6 is explicitly marked **[limited evidence]** and none is promoted to a standalone conclusion.
3. **18% of this corpus is one bug, over 39 hours.** Eight reviews (17.78%) landed between 04:33 UTC on 1 Jun 2026 and 19:16 UTC on 2 Jun 2026, all reporting that the app had stopped working (§7.3). They pull the corpus mean down by 0.32 stars (3.111 with them, 3.432 without). Every global rate in this report is affected by that single event; where it matters, both figures are given.
4. **The written corpus is not the rating base.** 45 written reviews against 536 public ratings = **8.40%**. The public average is **4.52**; the written mean is **3.111**. The 1.41-star gap is the normal written-review negativity skew, and it means this corpus describes *what makes people write*, not *what makes people rate*.
5. **No external store data could be fetched.** Prices, SKU names, the free-tier limit, the version history and the declared feature list are **reconstructed from review text** and carry the reviewers' own errors. Where reviewers contradict each other, the contradiction is reported, not resolved.
6. **17 of 45 reviews (37.78%) are not in English** (7 Turkish, 6 Spanish, 1 Italian, 1 Russian, 1 Arabic, 1 Vietnamese). All were read in the original; translations in this report are labelled.
7. **Four reviews contradict their own star rating** (8.89%) — including a **5★ review whose entire body is "App doesn't work."** They are not silently dropped; see §1.6 and §9.1.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

**Executive summary in one line:** a genuinely well-liked minimalist habit tracker — praise is the largest theme in the corpus and it is overwhelmingly about simplicity — is being held back by three separable things: **a June 2026 release that made the app untappable for at least 39 hours and produced 40% of all its one-star reviews, a 3-habit free tier that is the single most-named product boundary, and an entitlement check that fails paying customers** (offline, and after purchase). The most valuable feature in the corpus, the heat-map widget, is the only named purchase trigger — and it is also the feature paying users most want improved.

## 1. The June 2026 build broke the app, and it is the biggest single event in this corpus

**8 of 45 reviews (17.78%, high-priority, mean 1.62) report the app non-functional, and they arrive inside a 39-hour window:**

| 2026-06-01 14:16 | ar | 1 | `14131374072` | unstable, laggy, freezes, *"severe touch-responsiveness issues every time I try to select an option"* |
| 2026-06-01 15:52 | ar | 1 | `14131709751` | ES: clicking any option does nothing — *"you have to do a little swipe"* |
| 2026-06-02 11:13 | tr | 2 | `14134782126` | TR: **premium buyer** — the edit-previous-day buttons are dead |

Six of the eight name the same mechanism: **taps do not register** (`tap_unresponsive`, 6 reviews, 13.33%, mean 1.17). One reviewer found a workaround — *swipe instead of tap* (`14131709751`) — which points at a gesture-recognition or hit-testing regression rather than a crash.

**Why this is the most consequential finding in the report:**
- **6 of the 15 one-star reviews (40%) come from this 39-hour window.** Remove the burst and the 1★ share falls from 33.33% to 24.32% of the remaining 37 reviews.
- It hit **six countries in two days** (IN, AR×2, TR×2, ES, UA, SA) — not a device- or locale-specific edge case.
- **It caught paying customers mid-subscription**: `14134782126` (premium) and `14136173462` ($10, one week after purchase).
- **It blocked first-run onboarding** (`14136118921` could not create a first habit) — meaning the build also destroyed new-user acquisition for its duration, not just existing users' sessions.

**Evidence that it was fixed:** 16 reviews from 3 Jun 2026 onward average **3.875** stars and **none of them reports unresponsive taps**. August 2026 alone is 9 reviews at mean 4.00. The recovery is real, but the 1★ reviews it generated are permanent and still sit on the listing.

## 2. Praise is the biggest theme, and it is almost entirely one word: simple

**18 of 45 reviews (40.00%, mean 4.67) contain praise. 11 of 45 (24.44%, high-priority, mean 4.45) praise simplicity or minimalism specifically** — `12510217982`, `13332648782`, `13354961662`, `13366854737`, `13610103490`, `13759855538`, `13926027561`, `14191010644`, `14288307273`, `14421182094`, `14453647912`.

It is said the same way in six languages:
- `12510217982` (tr, 5★): TR *"Sade ve kullanışlı"* — "simple and useful."
- `13366854737` (tr, 5★): TR *"Sade ve kullanışlı bir alışkanlık takip uygulaması"* — "a simple and useful habit-tracking app."
- `14288307273` (py, 5★): ES *"Solo check y a seguir. Como debe ser"* — "just check and carry on. As it should be."

**Read the last one carefully.** Simplicity is this product's moat and it is not in dispute — even the churned users and the 1★ reviewers concede it. Nothing in this corpus asks for the app to become more complicated. The 4★ band asks for *depth without complexity* (§4.2), which is a very different request.

## 4. The 3-habit free tier is the most-named product boundary — and it is costing 5★ reviewers

**4 of 45 reviews (8.89%, high-priority, mean 2.75) name the 3-habit free limit explicitly**, and all four agree on the number:

- `12547737504` (in, **5★**): *"Just a small suggestion to add one time payment option or add at least one more free task as we already have 3 but having 4 as free will give edge to the app."*
- `12634608390` (tr, 3★): TR *"Nice, but you can only enter 3 habits; it wants a membership for more."*
- `13610103490` (in, 2★): *"the free version only allows tracking 3 habits, which feels very limiting, so I ended up uninstalling."*
- `14251077324` (ie, 1★): *"Only 3??????  You can only have 3 habits at a time this app is useless"*

The ratings run 5★, 3★, 2★, 1★ — **the cap is not a complaint from people who dislike the app; it is a wall that people walk into on their way out.** `13610103490` is the clearest case in the corpus: paragraph one is unqualified praise, paragraph two is an uninstall. A fifth review, `13680580523` (pl, 1★), is titled *"Limited option"* but its body only says *"Get one star for annoying me with pop-ups"* — counted under upsell pressure, not the cap, and flagged as a boundary call in §9.1.

**Note the asymmetry:** 3 free habits is below the size of the use cases reviewers describe. `14438650069` tracks walking, `14061534070` and `13956421952` water and steps, `14424678681` three distinct religious practices. A realistic starter set is 4–6 habits. The cap bites before the product has had a chance to prove itself.

## 6. The one named purchase trigger is the widget — and the widget is under-built

`13332648782` (in, 5★, *"Worth every penny"*) is the only review in the corpus that states what it bought and why: *"I bought the paid version of the app (single month), because I wanted to use the larger widget showing the entire heat map and i absolutely love it."*

The widget family is 4 reviews (8.89%, mean 4.50) and it splits cleanly:
- **Praise / purchase driver:** `13332648782` (bought for it), `14394122206` (in, 5★ — *"I use widgets for all my new habits. Without it I would underestimate the progress and tend to give up… I was able to successfully track and build new habits for the first time with this app"*).
- **Named gaps:** `14191010644` (no dark theme, too basic — from a **lifetime buyer**), `13616264173` (should surface the next unfinished habit).

**One review is the strongest outcome statement in the corpus and it credits the widget** (`14394122206`): the widget is not decoration, it is the retention mechanism. It is also the thing a lifetime purchaser is publicly disappointed by.

## 7. Data integrity: two reports, one total and one repeating

- `13330819055` (ua, 1★, Oct 2025): *"After several months of usage all my habits disappeared upon app launch"* — **total loss.**
- `14481733593` (kz, 2★, Aug 2026): *"Great app but unfortunately unreliable. Seems like each time when it updates the habits progress for the last week wipes. These happened two times already."* — **partial loss, on update, reproducible, twice.**

Two reviews is 4.44% — at n=45 that is two people, not a rate. But both describe destroyed history in a product whose entire value is an unbroken streak, they are 10 months apart, and the second names **app update** as the trigger. Per the signal-threshold rule on severe data loss, both are promoted regardless of share.

Two further reviews make the data model itself the problem rather than the storage: `13742691466` (us, 1★) cannot sync phone to iPad at all, and `14453647912` (in, 4★) points out that **any past day can be edited**, which makes streaks unfalsifiable.

`13212775795` (it, 1★) is the only review that attacks the core screen, and it is specific: IT — *"on the main screen you only see the habit titles with their icon, but the overall view is totally confusing: you see a sea of dots without being able to distinguish month, week and day."* The same dot/heat-map grid that `13332648782` paid money for and `14394122206` credits with their first successful habit is, to this reviewer, unreadable because **it carries no time axis.**

One review is one review. But it is a design-level claim that costs nothing to test, and it is consistent with `14421182094` (py, 4★) asking for the options to be **explained**: the UI is legible to people who already understand it.

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Parsed** all 45 records from `reviews.jsonl`, HTML-unescaped, sorted by date, and dumped to a single readable file (`Temp/62-all-reviews.txt`) with ID, storefront, rating, date, title and full body.
2. **Read every review individually, in full, in its original language.** No sampling, no keyword filtering, no machine classification, no truncation. At n=45 the entire corpus fits in one reading pass, which is why this report contains no automated-classifier error estimate — there is no classifier.
3. **Hand-built a theme → review-ID map** (`Temp/62-review-classification.py`): 61 themes, non-exclusive, every ID written literally.
4. **Validated the map programmatically** before writing a word of the report: zero unknown IDs, zero intra-theme duplicates, zero unassigned records, full reconciliation against `by_country/` and `manifest.json` (§1.3).
5. **Aggregated** counts, percentages, means, rating cross-tabs, storefront tables and time buckets from the validated map (`Temp/62-aggregate.py`) rather than by hand.
6. **Generated the Part 10 index table directly from the data + map** (`Temp/62-make-index.py`), so the audit trail cannot drift from the classification it audits.
7. **Attempted external verification** of prices, SKUs, version history and the declared feature list. All egress blocked (§9.5).

**Known error risk:** the judgement calls are mine and they are enumerated, not hidden — see §9.1 for the six boundary decisions that a second reader could reasonably make differently.

## 1.6 Limitations and known biases

1. **n = 45 is small.** Everything above "2 reviews" is a pattern; everything at 1–2 reviews is a named witness. No finding here should be treated as a measured rate.
2. **Written-review selection bias.** 45 written reviews vs 536 public ratings (8.40%). Written mean 3.111 vs public 4.52. People who write are disproportionately angry (or, at 5★, disproportionately brief).
3. **One event dominates.** The 1–2 Jun 2026 burst is 17.78% of the corpus and −0.32 stars on the mean. Global rates are given with and without it wherever it changes the reading.
4. **No external store data.** No price list, no SKU names, no version numbers, no release dates, no confirmation of what the listing promises. §2 is a reconstruction. Specifically unverifiable: the actual free-tier limit, the price ladder, whether an Apple Watch app exists, and whether HealthKit integration is advertised.
5. **Four rating/text contradictions** (8.89%) — `14136346335` (5★, *"App doesn't work"*), `13314856744` (5★, reports a critical entitlement bug), `14481733593` (2★, *"Great app but…"*), `14129863718` (1★, *"It was good until the last update"*). They are counted in both the rating band they carry and the theme their text supports; this is disclosed rather than corrected, because the star is what the store shows.
6. **No country qualifies for standalone analysis** (max 8 vs the 50 threshold). Part 6 is comparative and caveated throughout.
7. **Engagement fields carry no signal.** Only 4 of 45 reviews have any helpful vote (`12510217982`, `13332648782`, `13366854737`, `13926027561` — one vote each, all 5★, three of them Turkish). One review is flagged `is_edited` (`14191010644`). Neither field is used to weight anything.
8. **Burst disclosure, both directions.** The June cluster is a *defect* burst, not a solicited one: eight different authors, six storefronts, no shared phrasing. August 2026 is the second-highest-volume month (9 reviews, mean 4.00) spread over 22 days across 7 storefronts with ratings 5,5,5,4,5,2,4,4,2 — **that spread is not consistent with a solicited-review burst**, but a review prompt or a growth step cannot be excluded from review data alone. Flagged, not claimed.
9. **Two reviews are not really product feedback.** `14396613983` (sa, 5★) is a pre-purchase pricing *question* rated 5★, and `14136346335` (sa, 5★) is a defect report rated 5★. Both inflate the Saudi mean to 5.00 (§6.4).

- `14396613983` (sa, 5★) — asking which tier to buy: **pre-purchase intent**, not a purchase.
- `13742691466` (us, 1★) — *"reasonably priced"* but did not buy: **blocked intent**.
- `12547737504` (in, 5★) — asks for a one-time option: **preference for a SKU that may now exist** (§2.2).
- `14280213491` (ca, 5★) — titles the review *"Free good tracking app"*: **satisfied non-payer**.

## 5.2 What triggers a purchase

**Exactly one review in the corpus names its purchase trigger**, and it is unambiguous:

> `13332648782` (in, 5★): *"I bought the paid version of the app (single month), because I wanted to use the **larger widget showing the entire heat map** and i absolutely love it."*

That is a single review (2.22%) and it cannot carry a strategy on its own — except that it is corroborated from an unexpected direction: `14394122206` (in, 5★) independently credits **widgets** with the first successful habit formation of their life, and `14191010644` (uz, 4★) is a **lifetime buyer whose only complaint is that the widget is too basic**. Three of the six widget-related data points in this corpus connect the widget to money or retention.

**Working hypothesis for the roadmap (labelled as hypothesis):** the widget is this product's paid hook, and it is currently under-built relative to its commercial importance.

## 5.3 What paid users value after buying

Thin, and honestly so: only `13332648782` describes post-purchase satisfaction (*"Worth every penny"* — widget, UI/UX, simple and minimal, *"would definitely recommend"*). Everyone else who paid is describing a problem.

# PART 7 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 7.1 Method

Three periods, chosen to give roughly comparable counts while respecting the corpus's shape (§1.4) and placing the June 2026 event inside one bucket:

| Period | Window | n | Mean ★ | Money-neg | Reliability | Praise | Requests | Paid users |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Apr 2025 – Dec 2025 | 11 | **3.64** | 4 | 1 | 5 | 2 | 3 |
| **P2** | Jan 2026 – May 2026 | 10 | **2.50** | 3 | 0 | 3 | 5 | 0 |
| **P3** | Jun 2026 – Sep 2026 | 24 | **3.12** | 5 | **10** | 10 | 5 | 3 |

**Confidence warning applied to every trend below:** the largest period has 24 reviews and the smallest 10. Nothing here is a rate; these are direction-of-travel readings on named reviews. No trend is claimed where the counts cannot support it.

## 7.2 Trend 1 — Review volume more than doubled in the last four months · **growing, high confidence**

24 of 45 reviews (53.33%) fall in Jun–Sep 2026, against 21 in the preceding 14 months. June alone (10) exceeds all of 2025 H2. **Interpretation is split between two causes that this corpus cannot separate:** the June defect burst (8 of June's 10) and genuine growth in Jul–Sep (14 reviews, mean 3.79). The Aug–Sep figures (10 reviews, mean 4.10) are the healthiest stretch in the corpus.

## 7.3 Trend 2 — One release broke the app for at least 39 hours · **acute, resolved, high confidence**

The full table is in Part 0 §1. Timing: **first report 2026-06-01 04:33 UTC, last 2026-06-02 19:16 UTC.** Eight reviews, six storefronts, eight distinct authors, no shared phrasing — a defect burst, not a solicited or coordinated one.

Effect on the corpus:
- 17.78% of all reviews ever written for this app.
- **40% of all its one-star reviews.**
- Corpus mean **3.111 with it, 3.432 without** (−0.32).
- June 2026 mean: **2.20**, the second-worst month in the corpus.

Evidence it was fixed: **16 reviews from 3 Jun 2026 onward, mean 3.875, zero reports of unresponsive taps.** The regression is closed; the reviews it produced are not.

## 7.4 Trend 3 — The 3-habit cap is a constant, not a trend · **persistent, high confidence**

Cap complaints are spread evenly across the entire 17 months: `12547737504` (Apr 2025), `12634608390` (May 2025), `13610103490` (Jan 2026), `14251077324` (Jul 2026). Two in P1, one in P2, one in P3, across four different storefronts (in, tr, in, ie), and **the reported limit never changes** — every reviewer says 3. Nothing in the corpus suggests the free tier has moved in 17 months, and nothing suggests complaints about it are abating.

## 7.5 Trend 4 — Feature requests replaced monetization complaints as the main "constructive" theme · **emerging, medium confidence**

Requests: 2 in P1 → 5 in P2 → 5 in P3. As a share of the period: 18% → **50%** → 21%. P2 (Jan–May 2026) is the request-heavy period, and it is also the lowest-rated (2.50) — because in P2 the *only* things people wrote about were gaps: within-day counting (`14061534070`), Apple Watch and widget behaviour (`13616264173`), multiple reminders (`13956421952`), plus the cap-driven uninstall (`13610103490`) and the sync-blocked buyer (`13742691466`). **P2 is what this product's ceiling looks like when nothing is broken: people run into the edges of the feature set.**

## 7.6 Trend 5 — The widget rose from nowhere to the centre of the corpus · **emerging, medium confidence**

Widget mentions by period: P1 = 1 (`13332648782`, Oct 2025, the purchase trigger), P2 = 1 (`13616264173`, Jan 2026, next-unfinished request), P3 = 2 (`14191010644` Jun 2026 lifetime buyer's complaint, `14394122206` Aug 2026 the outcome claim). Four reviews is a thin trend line, but **all four connect the widget to either money or retention**, and the two most recent are the strongest on both counts.

## 7.7 Trend 6 — Data-integrity problems persist across the whole corpus · **persistent, medium confidence**

`13330819055` (Oct 2025, total loss) and `14481733593` (Aug 2026, repeating weekly wipe on update) sit 10 months apart at opposite ends of the corpus. Two reviews cannot establish a rate, but they do establish that **this class of failure was present before the June regression and was still present two months after it was fixed.** Whatever fixed June did not fix this.

## 7.8 Trend 7 — Paid-user evidence appears at both ends and not in the middle · **no trend claimed**

Paid reviewers: 3 in P1, **0 in P2**, 3 in P3. The gap in P2 is almost certainly sample noise at n=10, and it is reported here only to pre-empt a false reading of the P3 cluster as "more people are paying." **This corpus cannot measure that.**

## 7.9 What did not change across 17 months

- **Simplicity praise** — present in every period (P1: `12510217982`, `13332648782`, `13354961662`, `13366854737`; P2: `13610103490`, `13759855538`, `13926027561`; P3: `14191010644`, `14288307273`, `14421182094`, `14453647912`). The product's core appeal is stable.
- **The 3-habit cap** (§7.4).
- **The absence of ads, privacy, support and refund complaints** — zero in all three periods.
- **Low-information 5★ reviews** — 7 across the corpus, evenly spread; the store rating is partly built on reviews that say nothing.

# PART 8 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every item ties back to numbered findings. Ordered by evidence strength × cost of inaction, not by volume — at n=45 volume alone is a poor ranking signal.

## 8.1 Immediate — the evidence is unambiguous

| # | Action | Evidence | Why now |
|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | **Make the premium entitlement work offline.** Cache the receipt/entitlement locally; never re-paywall a verified purchaser because the network is unavailable | §5.4, `13314856744` (ec, 5★ *despite* the bug, calls it *"critical"*) | A habit app is used at times of poor connectivity by design. This converts paid customers into public fraud accusations. Single reviewer, but the mechanism is specific, reproducible and severe |
| I2 | **Audit the purchase-unlock path end to end**, including the case where a subscription is charged but the app still behaves as free | §5.4, `12863807910` (br, 1★, *"I paid for subscription monthly but still not working. | I3 | **Add a regression gate on tap/hit-testing before release**, plus a fast rollback path | §7.3 — 8 reviews, 6 storefronts, 39 hours, 40% of all 1★ reviews, including a blocked first-run (`14136118921`) | The regression is already fixed, but nothing in this corpus suggests a process change prevented the next one. ## 8.2 Monetization — repackage, do not reprice

**The price level is not the problem.** Three reviewers volunteer that it is good (`13759855538`, `13926027561`, `13742691466`); the only two flat objections are one-line reviews from reviewers unlikely to convert at any price (`13990739214`, `14259200458`). What the corpus attacks is **what the free tier contains** and **whether the purchase is legible**.

| # | Action | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Raise the free habit allowance from 3 to 5**, or make the 4th habit a time-limited trial rather than a wall | §0.4 / §3.4.2 — 4 reviews name the cap, rated 5★/3★/2★/1★; `12547737504` asks for *exactly one more*; `13610103490` uninstalled at the wall while praising the UI. | M2 | **Differentiate the two subscription tiers on the paywall itself** | §5.6, `14396613983` (sa) had to *publish a review* to ask what the difference between the two tiers was. Whatever that paywall says, one buyer could not read it |
| M3 | **Make the widget the explicit centre of the paid offer** | §5.2 — the only named purchase trigger (`13332648782`, bought the month *for* the heat-map widget); the credited retention mechanism (`14394122206`); and the one thing a **lifetime** buyer publicly complains about (`14191010644`) |

## 8.3 Product — what converts 4★ into 5★ (and defends the moat)

The 4★ band is six named people with one ask each (§4.3). Shipping against that list is the cheapest rating improvement available. **Constraint from §3.3.1: 11 reviewers praise simplicity and nobody asks for more on the main screen — so every item below must be opt-in, not default-on.**

| # | Action | Evidence | Priority rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Within-day counting**: log a habit N times a day, sum at day end; allow more than one reminder per day | §3.5 rank 1 — 4 reviews, 4 storefronts, 8 months: `13453953092` (dk), `13956421952` (tj), `14061534070` (in), `14438650069` (za) | The largest coherent feature gap in the corpus, and it makes two described use cases (water, steps) **serviceable for the first time** |

## 8.4 Research questions this corpus raises but cannot answer

1. **Does a lifetime SKU exist today, and is it discoverable?** `12547737504` (Apr 2025) asks for one; `14191010644` (Jun 2026) says they bought one. External verification was blocked (§9.5).

3. **Is the free limit still 3 habits?** Four reviewers across 17 months all say 3, but the most recent is Jul 2026.
4. **Does the listing promise Apple Health integration?** `14435767351` believes it did. If so, I5 is an expectation-mismatch fix; if not, it is a feature request.
5. **Does an Apple Watch app exist?** `13616264173` implies not as of Jan 2026; not verifiable here.
6. **What caused the Jun 2026 regression, and what shipped on 1 Jun 2026?** The corpus shows the symptom window precisely (04:33 UTC 1 Jun → 19:16 UTC 2 Jun) but no version numbers are available.
7. **Is the Aug 2026 volume increase growth or a review prompt?** 9 reviews at mean 4.00, spread across 22 days and 7 storefronts (§1.6 item 8).
8. **How much of the "Streaks" name collision costs in installs?** `13682484600` is one review, but the collision with `Streaks` by Crunchy Bagel (7,270 reviews, folder 23 of this repository) is structural and cannot be measured from review text.

## 8.5 What not to change

- **Do not add features to the main screen.** 11 reviewers (24.44%) praise simplicity; zero ask for a busier default view. Every item in §8.3 should be opt-in.
- **Do not raise prices, and do not cut them.** Three unprompted "the price is fair" reviews against two one-line objections. The lever is packaging (§8.2), not price.
- **Do not build ads.** Zero ad complaints today; a habit app whose appeal is calm minimalism has more to lose than to gain.
- **Do not chase the "accountability" positioning with social features.** No review asks for sharing, friends, leaderboards or accountability partners — and no review describes breaking a bad habit either (§3.3.3), despite the store subtitle.
- **Do not remove back-dating.** `14453647912` wants it *lockable*, not gone; `14134782126` (a premium buyer) was actively using the edit-previous-day buttons when they broke.

## 9.4 Counting rules, denominators and verification

- **Global denominator is always 45.** Every percentage in this report is *n* ÷ 45 unless the row or sentence names a different denominator.
- **Segment rates** (S1–S5 in §9.2) use their own denominator, are labelled "seg.", and are always published alongside the global count. They never substitute for the signal label.
- **Themes are non-exclusive.** Family totals therefore exceed the sum of their parts in places (§9.1 boundary call 2), and no table in this report should be summed to 45.
- **Means are unweighted** arithmetic means of the star ratings of the reviews in the set.
- **Signal labels** come from the §"How to read this" table applied to the global denominator. Two findings are **promoted above their band** under the severe-data-loss/safety clause: F23 (data loss) and F27 (offline entitlement re-paywall).
- **Dates are the `date` field, UTC**, as stored in `reviews.jsonl`. Period boundaries are calendar months (§7.1).
- **Verification performed after drafting** (`Temp/62-verify-report.py`, output in §9.6): every review ID cited anywhere in this report was checked to exist in `reviews.jsonl`, and every one of the 45 corpus IDs was checked to appear at least once in this report.
