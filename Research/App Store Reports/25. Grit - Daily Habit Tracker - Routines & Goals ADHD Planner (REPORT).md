# Grit — Daily Habit Tracker (App Store ID 6446997766) — Full Review Analysis

**App:** Grit: Daily Habit Tracker — *Routines & Goals ADHD Planner*
**Developer of record in the corpus metadata:** GrittyApps · **Bundle:** `stoope.Grit`
**Corpus:** 1,309 written reviews · 91 storefronts · 22 May 2023 → 6 September 2026
**Corpus mean rating:** 3.671 · **Extracted:** 8 September 2026 (`itunes MZStore userReviewsRow`)
**Analysis date:** 11 September 2026
**Business model as reviewers experience it:** free download, hard 3-habit cap on the free tier, then monthly / annual / lifetime in-app purchase. No ads.

---

## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Any claim traces back to the exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several themes.
- Signal thresholds used throughout: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.

### ⚠️ Seven warnings before any number here is used

1. **The headline rating is inflated by an in-app review prompt, and the inflation grows.** Reviews with a body of 25 characters or less rise from **7.2% of the 2023–24 era (17/236) to 13.8% of 2025 (67/487) to 33.4% of 2026 (196/586)**, and 150 of those 196 are 5★. Eight reviewers explicitly complain that the app asked for a review *before they had used it* (`12424719394`, `12591004629`, `12876930985`, `12957806563`, `13386578145`, `13411399713`, `13419192067`, `13764701322`). **Every aggregate in this report is therefore also restated for "substantive" reviews (body > 60 characters, n = 801), and the two diverge sharply.** See Part 9.1 — it is the most important methodological fact in this corpus.
2. **This is a monetisation corpus, not a feature corpus.** 298 reviews (22.77%) carry a monetisation-friction complaint, mean rating 1.99. No product theme comes close. A reader looking for nuanced feature feedback will find it, but it sits under a very large paywall argument.
3. **The corpus is small and back-weighted.** 1,309 records across 40 months; 44.8% of them fall in the 8 months of 2026. Only 7 storefronts clear the 50-review bar for individual analysis (820 reviews, 62.64%). Country findings below 50 reviews are labelled `[limited evidence]` without exception.
4. **Theme rates are not equally comparable across languages.** The classifier is regex-based with Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Turkish, Russian and Ukrainian patterns, but coverage is thinner in Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Vietnamese, Indonesian and Arabic. Where a language's coverage is thin, a theme is *under*-counted, never over-counted. Country comparisons lead with **rating distributions**, which are language-neutral.
5. **The free cap is not a constant.** Reviewers variously report 2, 3, 4 or 5 free habits (`11855794951` "2 goals", `12293948829` "solo 2 habitos", `13693237309` "more than one task", `14200651675` "four tasks", `14118452913` "3-5 alışkanlık"). Three is by far the dominant figure. A 2024 complaint about "3 habits" and a 2026 complaint about "3 habits" may be about different builds and different paywall placements.
6. **No `version` field exists in this corpus.** Release attribution is inferred from dates and from what reviewers say about "the last update" (`13150926987`, `14117808358` naming v5.6.3, `13501111818`). It is never asserted from metadata.
7. **"Paid evidence" is self-selecting.** 57 reviewers (4.35%) state explicitly that they paid, bought or subscribed. That group skews toward both evangelism and transaction regret, and it is not a sample of paying customers.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. The 3-habit free cap is the single largest fact in this corpus, and it is the first thing most new users meet.**
**138 reviews (10.54%, high-priority)** complain specifically about the numeric free-tier limit, mean rating **2.13**, **68 of them 1★**. Widen to all monetisation friction — cap, general paywall, upsell pop-ups, price objection, paywalled statistics, survey-then-paywall — and it is **298 reviews (22.77%), mean 1.99, 169 of them 1★ (56.0% of all 1★ reviews in the corpus)**. Evidence: `11344752179`, `11580010213`, `12038603986`, `12418313250`, `12594456123`, `13570261114`, `13653480657`, `13759652099`, `14119383340`, `14452682730`, `14500984459`.

**2. The complaint is not "too expensive" — it is "I could not evaluate the product."** Price objection alone is **55 reviews (4.20%)**. What reviewers say far more often is that three habits is not enough to *test* a habit tracker: "impossibile da capire se effettivamente é utile senza pagare" (`13392619767`), "not enough for people to make a decision" (`13557314925`), "how do you expect me to subscribe without trying anything??" (`13927978729`). Against that, **64 reviews (4.89%, mean 4.31)** actively praise the price as cheap or fair, and **19 reviews (1.45%, mean 4.32)** defend the free cap on principle. **Interpretation: the pricing level is not the problem; the trial design is.** This is the cheapest high-value fix available to the product.

**3. Paid-reviewer satisfaction is collapsing, and that is the business-critical trend.**
Reviewers who explicitly state they paid: **E1 (2023-05→2024-12) n=13, mean 4.23 → E2 (2025) n=25, mean 3.16 → E3 (2026) n=19, mean 2.68.** Within the 57 explicit payers, **8 (14.0%) raise a billing dispute (mean 1.12)**, **5 (8.8%) report losing access to something they already bought (mean 1.40)**, and **5 (8.8%) report reliability failure (mean 1.80)**. Evidence: `12379398566`, `12473304418`, `13178225257`, `13184003965`, `13528421192`, `13599650667`, `13605686247`, `14355199654`, `14132304435`, `14379122842`, `14486983673`.

**4. There is an un-fixed launch/interaction blocker that is the fastest-growing negative theme in the corpus.**
**51 reviews (3.90%, very strong), mean 1.63, 33 of them 1★**: the app freezes on or just after the onboarding screen and the main screen stops responding to touch. Rate by era: **1.3% (E1) → 2.9% (E2) → 5.8% (E3)**. Devices named: iPhone 11 (`14156928722`, `14228794250`, `14269477606`, `13970830961`), iPhone 14 Pro Max (`14102414142`), iPhone 16 Pro / iOS 26.5 (`14065834606`), iOS 26.1 (`13448927787`). One reviewer states the freeze survived two updates over a month (`14147118069`). Further: `13637569981`, `14021915764`, `14112998455`, `14183996590`, `14262738292`, `14308159391`, `14312587468`, `14097129502`, `14336842171`, `14121146392`, `14049857126`.

**5. The product's genuine moat is configurability plus Apple-platform depth, and the corpus states it in unusually specific terms.**
**126 reviews (9.63%, mean 4.20)** praise simplicity/ease, **65 (4.97%, mean 4.49)** praise customisation/groups/colours, **62 (4.74%, mean 4.42)** praise the interface, **32 (2.44%)** name Apple Health, **30 (2.29%, mean 4.43)** the widgets, **21 (1.60%, mean 4.48)** the Apple Watch, **15 (1.15%, mean 4.60)** the built-in timer. **37 reviews (2.83%)** arrive after explicitly abandoning a named competitor — Streaks, Habitify, Habitica, HabitKit, Strides, Done, Me+, Ripples, Tiimo, Habit. The recurring sentence is "I have tried them all and this is the one": `10653626434`, `11656905076`, `12427277456`, `12584418650`, `13191422909`, `13470655832`, `13516169817`, `14342011720`, `14394846065`.

**6. Billing disputes are a dated, growing, and reputationally expensive cluster.**
**44 reviews (3.36%, very strong), mean 1.59, 32 of them 1★.** Rate by era: **0.8% (E1) → 3.9% (E2) → 3.9% (E3)** — a ~5× step change after 2024. The named mechanisms are consistent: a 7-day trial that renews into the *annual* plan rather than the plan the user chose (`13431450922`, `14075475519`, `13749925993`), a charge during the trial window (`13588325969`, `13926135435`, `12470598228`), and a charge with no recollection of consent (`12956024975`, `13424912655`, `14512117424`, `13608769744`, `13860427881`). Two reviewers could not find subscription settings in-app at all (`12250584386`, `12941350904`).

**7. The paywall is also a UX interruption, and it breaks the app's own audience promise.**
**35 reviews (2.67%, meaningful), mean 1.80** describe repeated full- or half-screen upsell pop-ups blocking ordinary navigation: "en 20 secondes j'ai eu 10 popup" (`13469881270`), "jedes Mal mit der Premium Version geworben… erst die App wieder schließen" (`12795905617`), "Eine Gewohnheitsapp die gleich mal mit dark pattern anfängt" (`14130753059`). One reviewer makes the product argument precisely: the explosive pop-ups are "hyper désagréable et malvenu pour des TDAH" (`13493408148`) — in an app marketed to ADHD users.

**8. ADHD is the app's strongest positioning and its strongest satisfaction signal.**
**41 reviews (3.13%, very strong)** self-identify ADHD, autism, Asperger's or OCD, mean **4.44**, **32 of them 5★**. A further **24 (1.83%, mean 4.67)** describe procrastination, forgetfulness, task paralysis or depression without naming a diagnosis. Three arrive via a clinician, including a psychologist who recommends it to clients (`12404912935`, `11680828081`, `13158925619`). But the same segment supplies the sharpest paywall anger: "It is just another app trying to profit off of your disability" (`13742936226`), `11544311590`, `11831643030`, `11809645737`.

**9. The biggest unshipped feature is time-of-day scheduling, and it is requested by satisfied users.**
**29 reviews (2.22%, meaningful), mean 3.41, 17 of them 4★ or 5★** ask for a specific clock time per habit with a notification at that time — not a generic reminder. Evidence: `10975371211`, `11185726438`, `11512786062`, `11830672328`, `12559128903`, `12647535093`, `12682799984`, `12889328356`, `13846488281`, `14092149470`, `14303953237`, `14437910533`. Three further requests cluster with it: one-off non-repeating tasks (**10**, 0.76%), recurring intra-day reminders every N minutes (`13466380366`, `13082959947`, `12152603849`, `13711288403`), and a calendar/agenda view (**22 mention calendar**, 1.68%).

**10. Cheapest unshipped wins, in evidence order:** fix the launch freeze → make the free tier evaluable (time-boxed full access, or ~8–10 habits, and unlock read-only statistics) → make trial-to-paid-plan mapping explicit and put subscription management in-app → fix "already paid, asked to pay again" → per-habit clock time with notification → stop blocking navigation with upsell pop-ups → ship Turkish, Russian, Korean, Chinese, Italian localisation.

## 1.4 Processing method

1. Parsed all 1,309 records programmatically; double HTML-unescaped titles and bodies (the source double-encodes entities such as `&#39;`); collapsed whitespace.
2. Emitted the full corpus as 12 human-readable batches (`id|country|rating|date|title :: body`) and read all 12 in full, building the theme taxonomy inductively from what reviewers actually wrote before any regex was designed.
3. Built a multilingual regex classifier for 52 themes (definitions in Part 12.2) over `title + body`, lowercased.
4. **Validated and corrected.** Four patterns were found to mis-match and were fixed before any number here was used:
   - `free_cap_3` initially fired on any "3 + habit-word" proximity, capturing "my 3 favorite things" (`12889328356`) and "5 days a week" (`12201453432`). Corrected to require a monetisation/limit context word within ±90 characters of the match: **138 (10.54%)**, down from 176 raw hits.
   - `needs_ai` initially matched `\bai\b`, which fires on French *"j'ai"* — producing 73 hits, almost all French and none about AI. Tightened to explicit AI terms: **1 genuine review** (`13516169817`), below the ignore threshold. No AI finding is claimed.
   - `freeze_unresponsive` initially included the bare Spanish token `nada`, inflating it to 72. The reliability cluster was then rebuilt **by hand** (see 5 below): **51**.
   - `adhd` was initially merged with procrastination/forgetfulness/depression language, giving 103. Split into **`adhd_named` (41, explicit diagnosis)** and **`exec_difficulty` (24, described difficulty without a named diagnosis)**.
5. **The reliability cluster was hand-curated, not regex-derived.** A deliberately over-broad regex produced 126 candidates; every candidate was read individually and assigned or rejected. This produced the curated `launch_blocker` (51 IDs, listed in full in Part 12.2) and `data_loss` (12 IDs) sets. This was done because reliability language in this corpus is extremely diverse across nine languages ("se traba", "travou", "friert ein", "зависает", "не реагирует на касания", "tildado", "no funciona el táctil") and no single regex captures it without heavy error in both directions.
6. Split ambiguous surface themes (`widget`, `watch`, `calendar`, `health`, `price`, `lifetime`) into mention / positive-framed / problem-framed subsets rather than reporting one misleading number.
7. Aggregated by rating, country, year, month and era; computed every aggregate **twice** — once over all 1,309 records and once over the 801 "substantive" records (body > 60 characters) — because of the review-prompt inflation documented in 1.5 and Part 9.1.

## 1.5 Known limitations

- **Rating inflation from an in-app prompt is the dominant limitation.** Short 5★ reviews with no content rise from 7.2% to 33.4% of the corpus across eras. The all-records mean (3.87 → 3.66 → 3.60) therefore *understates* deterioration; the substantive-records mean (3.83 → 3.51 → 3.10) is the better signal. Both are given everywhere.
- **43 reviews (3.28%, mean 4.21) are first-impression reviews from people who say they have not used the product yet** — "Aún estoy por probarla" (`13992368997`), "No la he probado pero me está gustando" (`14461664452`), "no la use pero tiene pinta" (`14252109539`), "Just downloaded" (`10930050142`). 31 of them are 5★. They are counted in all denominators and should be read as marketing-response signal, not product signal.
- **Regex classification has error in both directions.** Manual reading of all 1,309 records is the control; the four corrections in 1.4 are the disclosed outcome. Residual error is highest for Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Vietnamese, Indonesian, Thai and Arabic text, where the classifier under-counts.
- **Rating is not a feature preference.** 43 of the 138 cap complaints are 3★ or better, 27 are 4★ or better, and 16 are 5★ — people who like the app and object to the gate.
- **Storefront ≠ nationality ≠ language.** Several `mx` reviews are in French (`13421495866`) and English; several `us` reviews are in Spanish (`13433865029`, `13630801788`, `14368847160`) and Arabic (`13672090254`); `ai` (Anguilla) and `ao` (Angola) reviews are Spanish and Portuguese. Country findings are **storefront** findings.
- **Price claims are not reconcilable.** Reviewers name 30+ distinct figures across 12 currencies, across promotional offers, regional pricing tiers and three plan types, and some confuse currencies outright (`12319455814`, `13290152980`, `14164700184` all ask whether the price is in pesos or dollars). Part 3.3 reports the range and the direction, not a price list.
- **No download, revenue, retention, churn or refund-outcome data exists in this corpus.** Review volume is used only as a disclosed proxy and is never called downloads.
- **No external source was consulted.** No product website, App Store listing, press material or third-party market data was used. Every product and pricing claim in Parts 2 and 3 is attested by at least one review ID. The one external *assumption* in this report is the membership list of the "high-spend markets" group in Part 8.7, which is flagged there.

## 2.2 Monetisation structure as reviewers describe it

Three plan types are named repeatedly and consistently:

- **Monthly.** Named figures: $1.08 (`12230465395`), 40 MXN (`11856825871`), R$5 (`11975068830`), €2.99 (`12830065250`), €1.25 (`13612032880`), $10 (`13405360163`), $13 (`11100933308`), €9.90 with €4.99 wanted (`13493408148`), €10 (`13391520278`), $12.99/yr quoted as the alternative (`11185726438`).
- **Annual.** €35 (`13493408148`, `14132304435`, `14244904942`), A$19.99 (`13605094837`), ₹1,299 with ₹499 wanted (`13927028841`), R$199.90 (`12956024975`), 299.99 MXN (`14164700184`), $33 (`13424912655`), $42 charged against a $24.99 expectation (`12386056078`).
- **Lifetime / one-time.** £25 → £29.99 (`11422195516`, `12407220130`, `13418259698`), $24.99–$25 (`11007220268`, `11461120524`, `12045596500`, `11122925794`) rising to **$49 / 50€ / $29.99 / MXN 500** by mid-2025 onward (`12904556624`, `13660452698`, `13299143006`, `14379122842`, `13582066669`), ~23–30€ promotional (`12478039620`, `12348445268`, `14167301638`).

**The one-time option is a named purchase driver.** 44 reviews (3.36%) mention lifetime/one-time pricing, mean **3.95**; in the positive direction it is cited as the deciding factor: `11248554918`, `12178717768`, `13547124162`, `12199187753`, `12486962813` ("good option that u can just buy the app you dont have to do subscription stuff thank you"), `13547124162` ("falta uma opção para compra do app, só tem opção premium pagando mensalidade" — a reviewer who could not find it).

## 4.2 Cluster 2 — The paywall architecture `[high-priority]`

**Union n = 298 (22.77%), mean 1.99.** Components (non-exclusive): cap 138, general paywall 163, pop-ups 35, price objection 55, paywalled statistics 5, survey-then-paywall 6.

**Era rate: 64/236 = 27.1% (E1) → 117/487 = 24.0% (E2) → 117/586 = 20.0% (E3).** The *share* falls while absolute volume holds — and the fall is largely explained by the E3 flood of short prompt-driven 5★ reviews diluting the denominator (warning 1). Among **substantive** reviews the rate barely moves at all — **57/191 = 29.8% (E1) → 94/339 = 27.7% (E2) → 86/271 = 31.7% (E3)** — which is the cleaner statement: monetisation friction is flat-to-rising among people who write a real review, and only *appears* to fall in the all-records series.

The cluster has four distinguishable mechanisms, and conflating them loses the actionable detail:

**(a) The cap itself — 138 reviews.** Stated the same way in every market: three is too few to *plan a day*, which is what the app's own title promises. "con esas 3 solo puedo poner desayunar, comer y cenar" (`11772475330`); "3 tasks. 3. If a baby could use this app properly, even they'd have at least five" (`12715120271`); "Solo te deja poner 3 hábitos, lo que no es nada" (`13570261114`). # PART 9 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 9.1 Method and the one trend that governs all others

Three eras defined on volume (Part 1.6), plus a substantive-only series. **Because short prompt-driven 5★ reviews grow from 7.2% to 33.4% of the corpus, the all-records series understates every deterioration.** Both series are given for every trend. No trend is claimed from monthly data alone.

## 9.2 Trend 1 — Substantive sentiment is falling three times faster than the headline `[very strong]`

| Series | E1 | E2 | E3 | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All records | 3.87 | 3.66 | 3.60 | −0.27 |
| Substantive (>60 chars) | **3.83** | **3.51** | **3.10** | **−0.73** |
| 1★ share (all) | 16.1% | 22.2% | 26.6% | +10.5pp |
| 1★ share (substantive) | 16.2% | 23.3% | **35.1%** | +18.9pp |
| 5★ share (substantive) | 51.3% | 47.5% | **39.9%** | −11.4pp |

Among people who write more than a sentence, **one in three now leaves one star**. Evidence is the whole substantive E3 subset; see Part 7 for the mechanism.

## 9.3 Trend 2 — The launch blocker is the fastest-rising theme `[very strong]`

1.3% (3/236) → 2.9% (14/487) → **5.8% (34/586)**. Within 2026 it is a high-priority signal. Concentrated May–July 2026 (**27 of the 34**) and in Colombia, Mexico, Germany, France. IDs in 4.1 and 12.2. **This is the first thing to fix.**

## 9.4 Trend 3 — Billing disputes stepped up ~5× after 2024 and stayed `[very strong]`

0.8% (2/236) → 3.9% (19/487) → 3.9% (23/586). The E1 baseline of 2 reviews in 19 months is the important comparator: this is not a constant background rate. Mechanisms in 4.3.

## 9.5 Trend 4 — Paid-reviewer satisfaction is falling faster than anything else `[meaningful; small n]`

Mean 4.23 → 3.16 → 2.68 across 13/25/19 explicit payers. Supported independently by: premium-lost reviews existing only in E2/E3 (0 → 6 → 2), billing disputes (9.4), and support praise halving (3.0% → 1.6% → 0.9%).

| Interface | 8.1% | 5.7% | 2.6% |

**Every praise theme falls, and the platform-integration themes (Health, widgets, Watch) fall hardest — by 80–85%.** Part of this is mechanical (shorter reviews mention fewer features). But the drop is much steeper than the length change alone would produce, and it is *not* matched by a rise in any specific feature complaint. **Interpretation: the corpus's composition is shifting from evaluative users who explored the product to prompt-driven users who never got past the first screens — which is exactly what Trends 2 and 3 would predict.**

## 9.7 Trend 6 — The cap complaint is being replaced by a broader, angrier one `[meaningful]`

Cap-specific complaints fall (17.4% → 11.9% → 6.7%) while general "you can't use it without paying" holds (16.9% → 10.5% → 12.3%) and survey-then-paywall appears only in E2/E3 (0 → 0.2% → 0.9%). Read together with Part 2.3 (editing gated from Nov 2025) and 4.2(d): **reviewers are increasingly describing the whole app as locked rather than describing a habit limit.** Whether that reflects a real tightening of the gate or an accumulation of paywall surfaces cannot be settled from this corpus — it is the highest-priority verification question in Part 11.6.

## 9.8 Trend 7 — Localisation demand is new and growing `[meaningful]`

0% (0/236) → 1.0% (5/487) → **2.7% (16/586)**. Driven by Turkish (9), Korean (3), Chinese (2), Italian (3), Russian/Ukrainian (3), Japanese (1). **And one request was answered**: Turkish arrives ~July 2026 (`14353986952`). That is the corpus's proof that shipping a request changes the review stream.

## 9.9 Trends explicitly NOT claimed

- **No price-increase→objection trend.** Lifetime roughly doubles, price objection does not rise (3.8% → 4.9% → 3.8%). Do not claim the price rise hurt ratings.
- **No data-loss trend.** 1 → 5 → 6 reviews; n too small, rate flat at ~1%.
- **No sync trend.** 2.1% → 0.8% → 0.9%; the apparent improvement is within noise for n=14.
- **No Apple Watch trend.** 21 mentions total across 40 months. The one notable event — `13316944137`, "suddenly they removed the Apple Watch app" (Oct 2025) — is a single report and is recorded, not asserted.
- **No monthly trend.** Nine 2026 months ranging 3.26–3.84 on n=13–135; month-level variation here is noise.
- **No confusing-UX trend.** 0.4% → 2.9% → 2.0%: the E1 figure rests on one review.

# PART 10 — WHAT A COMPETITOR LEARNS FROM THIS CORPUS FOR FREE

1. **A 3-habit free cap on a "plan your whole day" product generates more 1★ reviews than every feature defect combined.** 169 of 302 one-star reviews. Any competitor offering 8–10 free habits can quote this corpus as its positioning.
2. **"I couldn't evaluate it" is a stronger churn driver than "it's too expensive."** Only ~12 of 55 price objections are genuinely about price level.
3. **The comparison set for a gated user is Apple Reminders and a paper notebook**, not other habit apps (9 reviews say so explicitly). A free tier must beat Reminders, not beat Streaks.
4. **There is a clear, named, unbuilt feature with demand from satisfied users: per-habit clock time with a notification at that time.** 29 reviews, 17 of them 4–5★. Whoever ships routine *scheduling* rather than routine *checking* takes the ADHD-planner positioning.
5. **A lifetime purchase option is a stated, repeated reason people buy** (44 reviews; 8 name it as the deciding factor). Subscription fatigue is explicit in German, French and US reviews.
6. **ADHD users are the highest-satisfaction segment and they notice hostile monetisation more than anyone else.** 41 reviews at mean 4.44, and the most cutting paywall criticism in the corpus comes from that same group (`13742936226`, `13493408148`).
7. **Localisation is cheap share.** Turkey, Korea, China, Italy and the Russian-speaking storefronts are asking, and the Turkish case proves the review stream responds.
8. **Statistics behind a paywall removes the motivation loop the category sells.** Five reviewers name it; one explains it exactly: for someone at rock bottom, free statistics *are* the motivation (`13636020247`).
9. **An in-app review prompt at first launch buys a store rating and costs you your own telemetry.** This corpus's headline mean is 0.22 points above its substantive mean, and the gap is widening.

# PART 11 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation cites the findings it rests on.

## 11.1 Immediate — reliability (this is the top of the list)

1. **Reproduce and fix the post-onboarding freeze.** `[4.1, 9.3]` Start with iPhone 11 and iOS 26.x, and with the iCloud-sync-stuck state named in `14097129502`. 51 reviews, mean 1.63, rate quadrupling; Colombia 18%, Germany 7.8%. Every one of these users got zero value from the product.
2. **Add a first-run failure escape hatch.** `[4.1]` Four reviewers state reinstalling does not help and then delete the app. A "skip setup / continue offline" path and a visible error state would convert some of those 51 into support tickets instead of 1★ reviews.
3. **Fix purchase recognition.** `[4.3, 6.5]` 8 reviews over 16 months say a completed purchase — including lifetime — stopped being honoured. Audit the entitlement/restore path. `14355199654` is a clean repro description.
4. **Investigate the three independent "broke right after payment" reports.** `[6.5]` `13299143006`, `13358500440`, `12315242035`.

## 11.2 Immediate — make the free tier evaluable (the single highest-leverage change)

5. **Raise or time-box the cap.** `[3.3, 4.2, 8.2]` The corpus names its own acceptable number: **8–10 habits**, or full access for a fixed window. The argument to answer is not "make it free" — it is "let me see what I'd be buying." 138 cap reviews, 163 general-paywall reviews, and 64 price-praise reviews say the price itself is not the obstacle.
6. **Unlock statistics read-only on the free tier.** `[2.3, 3.2]` Mean 1.40 on the 5 reviews that name it; it is the feature that makes the product motivating, and gating it removes the reason to upgrade rather than creating one.
7. **Never gate editing of habits the onboarding created.** `[2.3]` 5 reviews, mean 1.20, all after Nov 2025. If real, this makes the free tier a demo and is almost certainly net-negative on conversion.
8. **Stop blocking navigation with upsell sheets.** `[4.2c]` 35 reviews, mean 1.80. Cap the prompt to one dismissible surface per session. This is also the clearest conflict with the app's ADHD positioning (`13493408148`).
9. **Move the paywall to after first value, not after the onboarding survey.** `[4.2d]` 6 reviews at mean 1.17 describe spending 5–10 minutes on a questionnaire and being charged at the end of it.

## 11.3 Immediate — billing hygiene

10. **Make trial→plan mapping explicit and let the user pick the post-trial plan.** `[4.3, 6.6]` The most specific repeated claim in the corpus is renewal into the annual plan when monthly was selected (`13431450922`, `13731572727`, `14075475519`).
11. **Put subscription management in the app.** `[4.3]` Two reviewers could not find it and one asked publicly whether its absence is legal (`12250584386`).
12. **Show the local currency unambiguously on the paywall.** `[8.4]` Four Mexican and two other reviewers cannot tell pesos from dollars; this is a trust leak at the moment of purchase.
13. **Confirm the displayed price equals the charged price.** `[4.3]` `12386056078` ($24.99 → $42), `13605686247` (€29.99 + 2×€6.99).

## 11.4 Near-term product

14. **Per-habit clock time with a notification at that time.** `[3.5, 4.4, 5.2]` 29 reviews, 17 from 4–5★ users; the top request in the 4★ band at 9.0%.
15. **One-off, non-repeating tasks in a separate list or tab.** `[3.5, 5.2]` 10 reviews, 7 of them 4★.
16. **Optional intra-day recurring reminders (every N minutes until completed).** `[3.5]` 4 reviews, including a paying user with a medication use case (`12152603849`).
17. **Rework the statistics screen around "why did I miss it."** `[3.5, 8.12]` `13976982513` (detailed, edited down from 5★ to 3★) and `14139271437` (mood/energy tracker proposal) are effectively a free design brief.
18. **Stop order/group reshuffling and make reordering a drag gesture.** `[3.5]` 11 reviews; two long-term users left over it (`12657367016`, `12658607281`).
19. **Preserve history through archive and restore.** `[3.5]` 4 reviews; `12201042880` explains that archive-losing-history defeats the point of archiving.
20. **Fix Apple Health mappings that are demonstrably wrong.** `[3.5]` Weight read as entry count (`13502878041`), steps off by 568 (`11278083369`), running only measurable in minutes (`12918071192`), step counting starting at 04:00 (`12158005240`).
21. **Make over-achievement and bad-habit semantics coherent.** `[3.5]` Exceeding a goal should not pre-complete tomorrow (`14074498392`), and exceeding a *bad* habit limit should not award 200% (`12778092747`).

## 11.5 Localisation

22. **Ship Russian, Korean, Chinese and Italian** (Turkish has shipped). `[9.8, 8.12]` 21 requests, rising; Korean and Chinese storefronts are currently 100% 5★ and effectively ask for nothing else.

## 11.6 Verification questions this report cannot answer, ranked

23. **Did the free tier tighten after ~Nov 2025 to include editing and groups?** `[2.3, 9.7]` Checkable in one minute against the developer's own entitlement config. It determines whether Trend 6 is a real regression or an artefact.
24. **Is there, or was there, a review prompt that blocks progress until a review is written?** `[7]` `12591004629` claims so. If yes, the store rating is not a measurement and should be treated accordingly in any decision.
25. **What is the actual device/OS distribution of the freeze?** `[4.1]` Crash-free-session telemetry by model will settle in minutes what 51 reviews can only suggest.
26. **Does the trial genuinely renew into the annual plan when monthly was selected?** `[4.3]`
27. **Was the Apple Watch app withdrawn in/around Oct 2025?** `[9.9]` One report (`13316944137`); also `12865760230` says the App Store listing showed Watch support that could not be installed.
28. **Does raising the free cap to 8–10 increase or decrease paid conversion?** `[11.2]` The corpus can state the complaint volume but cannot predict the elasticity. This is an A/B test, and it is the most valuable one available.

## 11.7 Experiments worth running

29. **Cap 3 vs 8 vs 7-day-unlimited**, measuring trial start, paid conversion, D7 retention **and** 1★-review rate. `[11.2]`
30. **Paywall after first habit completion vs after the onboarding survey.** `[4.2d]`
31. **Statistics free vs gated**, measuring upgrade rate — the corpus predicts free statistics *increase* conversion; that prediction is testable and counter-intuitive. `[11.2]`
32. **Review prompt at day 7 vs first launch**, measuring store rating, review length and 1★ share. `[7]`

## 12.3 Validation approach and known error risk

- **Control:** all 1,309 records were read in full before the classifier was written, and again in cluster form for the reliability and monetisation themes.
- **Corrections made and disclosed:** 4 (Part 1.4) — `free_cap_3` over-matching numerals (176 → 138), `needs_ai` matching French *j'ai* (73 → 1, dropped), `freeze_unresponsive` matching Spanish *nada* (72 → hand-curated 51), `adhd` conflated with procrastination (103 → 41 + 24).
- **Direction of residual error:** under-counting, concentrated in Japanese (n=6), Korean (4), Chinese (4), Vietnamese (11), Indonesian (11), Thai (2), Arabic and Hebrew text, where the classifier has little or no pattern coverage. Every one of those reviews was still read and is in every denominator.
- **Over-counting risk** remains highest in `praise_simple` (the token `easy`/`fácil`/`easy` also appears in negative constructions — spot-checked: 16 of 126 are 1★, and manual reading confirms those are "easy to use but paywalled" reviews, correctly dual-classified) and `praise_ui` (the token `layout`/`diseño` appears in complaints — 4 of 62 are 1★, same pattern).
- **Not validated by a second classifier.** There is no inter-rater check; a single analyst read the corpus and wrote the patterns.
- **Boundary cases reviewed individually:** all 51 launch-blocker candidates, all 44 billing disputes, all 57 explicit payers, all 12 data-loss reports, all 8 premium-lost reports, all 8 review-prompt complaints, all 5 editing-gated reports, all 19 free-cap defences.
