# HelloHabit — Habit Tracker (App Store ID 6476824223) — Full Review Analysis

**App:** HelloHabit — *Habit Tracker · Tasks, Routines, & Streaks*
**Developer of record:** RightLife, Inc. · **Bundle:** `com.studio.hellohabit`
**Corpus:** 205 written reviews · 28 storefronts · 29 February 2024 → 4 September 2026
**Corpus mean rating:** 4.263 · **Extracted:** 8 September 2026 (`itunes MZStore userReviewsRow`)
**Analysis date:** 12 September 2026
**Business model:** Free download, **freemium with a hard cap on free habits**, plus a 7-day trial and monthly / annual / lifetime tiers. **The free habit cap was cut from 5 to 3 between late January and mid-May 2026** — this is established from review text alone and is the single most consequential fact in this report (§3.3.1).

---

## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Every claim traces to an exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`. **§9.6 is a complete 205-row index** — every review in the corpus, with ID, storefront, star rating, date, verbatim title and assigned themes. Nothing in this report is unreachable from that table.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several. All 205 records carry at least one theme (mean 2.88 themes per review, max 10).
- Signal thresholds: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority. Denominator for every global percentage is **205**.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.

### ⚠️ Eight warnings before any number here is used

1. **This corpus is small (205) and heavily positive.** 142 reviews are 5★ (69.3%) and only 25 are 1★ or 2★ (12.2%). This is the inverse shape of most habit-tracker corpora. **The consequence is that negative themes here have small absolute counts**: the largest negative theme in the report is 14 reviews. Signal labels are applied honestly against the 205 denominator, but a "high-priority" negative at 6.83% here is 14 people, and every one of them is named in §9.3. Read the direction and the *dates*, not the magnitude.
2. **The corpus is short — 2 years 6 months — and the app is young.** First review 29 February 2024, last 4 September 2026. There is no "launch era vs mature era" comparison available. Time analysis in Part 8 uses half-year buckets and is explicit about where n is too small to carry a claim (2026H2 is n=17).
3. **Only one storefront clears the 50-review bar: the United States (n=111, 54.15%).** Canada (16), Great Britain (16) and Australia (12) are presented as **limited evidence** with explicit warnings because they are internally consistent and materially different from the US. Every other storefront is ≤6 reviews and receives no standalone conclusion. **Mexico (n=5) is the one sub-50 storefront given a labelled callout**, because 4 of its 5 reviews are recent, concentrated on one issue, and constitute the clearest evidence for the central finding of this report (§6.7).
4. **Reviews are actively solicited inside the app, and two reviewers documented the mechanism.** `13528988161` (GB, 1★, Dec 2025): *"I don't even star using this app and you ask me for a review so it's my review one start"*. `14237389387` (US, 1★, Jun 2026) names it precisely: *"This app tries to take advantage of [participant reactivity bias] plus peer pressure by showing users a page of positive reviews less than two minutes after I opened the app and am going through the intro."* **27 of the 142 five-star reviews (19.0%) have bodies of 60 characters or less**, and 22 of 142 (15.5%) carry no extractable product signal at all (`low_info`). **Interpretation: the 4.263 corpus mean is inflated by an in-app prompt that fires before the user has used the product. Treat the positive tail as weaker evidence than the negative tail, which is uniformly specific and long-form.**
5. **No review burst or coordinated cluster was found, and none is alleged.** Maximum reviews on any single day is 4; only 5 days in 31 months carry ≥3 reviews; the largest 7-day window is 12 reviews and falls on **5–11 January 2026**, which is New Year's-resolution seasonality, not a burst. Helpful-vote counts are unremarkable (138 of 205 are zero; maximum 11). **This corpus is organically distributed.** Warning 4 concerns a prompt shown to real users, not fake reviews.
6. **Seasonality is extreme and must not be read as a trend.** 48 of 205 reviews (23.4%) fall in a January — 22 in January 2025, 26 in January 2026 — against a 6.6-per-month baseline. Any month-over-month comparison that straddles January is meaningless. Part 8 uses half-year buckets for this reason.
7. **Classification was hand-curated, not blind-automated.** All 205 records were read individually and in full, in their original languages (English, Spanish, German, French, Korean, Chinese, Arabic, Russian). Every theme is a hand-assigned ID list, not regex output. No sampling was used. Boundary rules are at §9.1. The map is saved at `Temp/50-review-classification.py` and validated programmatically (§1.5).
8. **External sources are weak for this app and are used only where labelled.** The App Store and Google Play listings are unreachable from this environment (egress-blocked); only search-engine summaries of those pages were obtainable, and **they contradict the review evidence** — they state a 5-habit free cap and a €2.09–€54.99 IAP range, while five 2026 reviewers from four storefronts report a 3-habit cap. **Every price and limit in Part 2 is therefore sourced to a named review ID unless explicitly marked as a low-confidence external summary.** No finding in Parts 3–8 rests on an external source.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. The free habit cap was cut from 5 to 3 in the first half of 2026, and the review record dates the change to within a 16-week window.**
Fourteen reviewers state the numeric free cap. **Nine say 5** — the last on **26 January 2026** (`13677250370`, ES, 5★). **Five say 3** — the first on **17 May 2026** (`14074582574`, TR, 4★). **There is zero overlap between the two groups.** Two reviewers describe the reduction as it happened to them: *"La versión gratuita redujo de 5 hábitos a 3, lo cual es una basura"* / "The free version went down from 5 habits to 3, which is garbage" (`14455227817`, MX, 1★, 21 Aug 2026); and `14074582574` (TR, 4★), who describes having unlimited habits for a few days and then being told to *"купить подписку либо оставить только три привычки"* / "buy a subscription or keep only three habits". The two groups' mean ratings differ sharply: the 5-cap group averages **3.78**, the 3-cap group **2.20**. **Interpretation: this is the clearest causal chain in the corpus. A pricing change made in 2026 H1 is visible in the ratings that followed it, and the evidence is five independent reviewers in four countries writing in three languages.**

**2. The consequences of that change are concentrated in August 2026 — the worst month in the corpus by a wide margin.**
**August 2026 has 9 reviews and a mean of 2.89**, against a corpus mean of 4.263 and a 31-month monthly mean that only drops below 4.0 in five other months. Five of those nine are 1★ or 2★. Monetization-friction reviews, which ran at **3.1% of the corpus in 2024 H1**, reach **35.3% in 2026 H2** (6 of 17) — an eleven-fold rise across six half-year buckets, monotonic in four of five steps: 3.1% → 9.4% → 6.1% → 13.6% → 18.9% → 35.3%. **Interpretation: the deterioration is recent, is accelerating, and is specific to monetization. It is not a general decline in product quality.**

**3. Paying customers are not the problem — the people who never get to pay are.**
**9 reviewers (4.39%, very strong) give explicit first-person purchase evidence**; their mean rating is **4.11**, and 7 of the 9 are 5★. Adding 4 strongly-implied payers gives 13 (6.34%) at mean **4.23** — statistically indistinguishable from the 192 non-payers (mean 4.27). **Only two payers are unhappy, and both were hurt by billing, not by the product**: `12403255909` (AU, 1★, no trial-end reminder, auto-charged ~$50/yr) and `13653714543` (US, 1★, *"I was charged $30 before my free trial was up"* against an advertised $14.99). **Interpretation: unlike most freemium habit trackers, this product does not generate angry buyers. Post-purchase satisfaction is a genuine asset. The monetization risk here is entirely at the top of the funnel, at the free cap and the trial-to-charge transition.**

**4. Billing correctness is the single most severe defect class in this corpus, and it is a three-year-old, unresolved pattern.**
**3 reviews (1.46%, meaningful, mean 1.00 — every one is 1★)** report being charged an amount that does not match what they were shown: `11673631454` (US, Aug 2024, quoted $14.99/yr, bank declined **$31.01**), `12403255909` (AU, Mar 2025, no trial-expiry reminder, charged ~$50 for a year), `13653714543` (US, Jan 2026, quoted $14.99, **charged $30 before the trial ended**). Two of the three name the same mismatch — roughly $15 advertised against roughly $30 charged — **17 months apart**. A further **2 reviews (0.98%, emerging, mean 1.00)** report being unable to pay at all: `12291537832` (IT, *"The app does not accept any of my cards"*) and `11673631454` again. **Interpretation: this is elevated above its 1.46% share under the threshold policy's severe-harm exception. Three users independently describe a charge that does not match the quoted price; that is a refund, chargeback and App Store compliance exposure, not a satisfaction problem.**

**5. What this product is loved for is breadth plus restraint — and that combination is its differentiator against a crowded field.**
**37 reviews (18.05%, high-priority, mean 4.89) explicitly compare HelloHabit to other habit trackers they have tried and rank it first.** That is the largest single theme in the corpus and 36 of its 37 reviews are 5★. *"I reckon I've tried almost every habit tracking app on the App Store, and this is the one I've settled on"* (`13448518959`, AU); *"I've been trying different habit trackers since 2018 and HelloHabit is the only one that stuck"* (`12781523260`, PH); *"여태껏 생산성 앱 유목민으로서… 이거는 많이 땡김"* / "as a productivity-app nomad until now… this one really pulls me in" (`14178676113`, KR). Alongside it, **20 reviews (9.76%) praise simplicity** and **14 (6.83%) praise flexibility** — two things usually in tension. **Interpretation: the moat is that the app is both deep and uncluttered. Every roadmap decision should be tested against whether it preserves that.**

**6. The journal is not a side feature — it is a primary reason people choose and keep this app.**
**19 reviews (9.27%, high-priority, mean 4.74) praise the built-in journaling / notes-on-habits capability**, and several say it let them delete other apps. *"More importantly I love that there is a journaling feature embedded within it"* (`14009851946`, AE); *"I can put details of the exercise, and then I can review those notes… in a diary-like view. To me this is gold, and the best implementation I've seen of this feature"* (`13448518959`, AU); *"I also love the Journaling feature - it let me delete a couple other apps I used to use"* (`12400743554`, US). **But the journal's text editor is also the most-reported functional bug in the corpus** — see finding 7. **Interpretation: the highest-value differentiator and the worst-quality surface are the same feature.**

**7. The notes text editor is broken in a way three users independently described, and it is the only defect with a repeat pattern.**
**3 reviews (1.46%, meaningful, mean 3.33)** report the same class of failure when typing notes: `12208608692` (GB, 4★, Jan 2025 — keyboard covers the cursor, cursor jumps back to top, *"Would be 5Stars from me if this bug was fixed"*), `13675051713` (US, 3★, Jan 2026 — *"the letters will glitch and suddenly there's always a letter by my cursor and the letter will not leave"*), `13980463198` (US, 3★, Apr 2026 — *"my second sentence somehow gets pieced into my first and then none of it makes sense… since notes on progress is really what i have the app for, it really bothers me"*). **Sixteen months separate the first and last report.** **Interpretation: this is the cheapest rating repair available. All three reviewers rate the app 3–4★ and two of them state that the bug alone is what withheld the fifth star.**

**8. Developer responsiveness is a measurable differentiator, and reviewers cite it as a reason to trust the product.**
**11 reviews (5.37%, high-priority, mean 4.91) praise the developers' responsiveness specifically**, and **8 more (3.90%, very strong, mean 5.00) praise support** — a union of **17 unique reviewers (8.29%)**, two of whom carry both. *"I requested some changes… and the developer implemented what I asked and more - he pretty much filled all the gaps I identified"* (`13448518959`, AU); *"they actually listen to feedback - even going the extra mile, crowdsourcing feature ideas from the community(!!!)"* (`12781523260`, PH); *"Reached out to support and they immediately responded and were able to re-send that offer"* (`14310174846`, US, converted a lost checkout into a sale). One reviewer even documents a fix shipping: `12160005173` (US) — *"be able to reorder habits without scheduling (\*this was updated very soon, thank you)"*. **Only one review is negative on support** (`13835821016`, US, 3★). **Interpretation: support is currently converting revenue and generating 5★ reviews. It is an asset to protect as the user base grows, not a cost centre to optimise.**

**9. The most-requested missing capability is a way to pause a habit without losing the streak — and it is a retention feature, not a nicety.**
**3 reviews (1.46%, meaningful, mean 4.33)** ask for streak freeze / habit pause, and all three explain the emotional mechanism: *"It makes me feel guilty for losing my streaks, but like sometimes your routine has to change for a few days!"* (`13086329598`, US, 4★, sickness/vacation); *"the option to 'freeze' your streaks if you accidentally miss a day… it's much easier for me to continue a streak when it's large rather than from ground zero"* (`13956867783`, CA, 5★); `12605109904` (US, 4★). A fourth asks for skipped days to be visually distinct (`12548136363`, US, 4★). **Two more ask to archive completed habits** (`12250094565`, `12605109904`). **Interpretation: every one of these reviewers is a 3–5★ engaged user describing the exact moment they are most likely to churn. That makes this the highest-leverage feature request in the corpus despite its small count.**

**10. Health-integration gaps are the most common feature request among satisfied US users, and they are all the same request.**
**5 reviews (2.44%, meaningful, mean 4.60 — 4.50% of the US subset)** want data that already exists in Apple Health to flow in automatically instead of being tapped in by hand: weight (`13696241769`, *"it a bit frustrating when i need to put my weight manually while it can be synced from the apple health app"*), mindful minutes (`13089863548`), sleep (`13598469223`), Apple Fitness workout types (`12244752465`), and a third-party fitness app (`11833620378`). Separately **7 reviews (3.41%, very strong, mean 4.71) praise the Health integration that does exist** — *"I love the automatic tracking of steps, calories, etc. via health and Apple Watch especially! I loaded that up and it picked up historical data"* (`10993489379`). **Interpretation: the integration is a proven delight when it works and a specific, named disappointment where it stops. This is extension work on a shipped feature, not new development.**

**11. Two reviewers were driven away by the review prompt itself, and one of them explains the ethics.**
**2 reviews (0.98%, emerging, mean 1.00 — both 1★)** are about nothing except being asked for a review before using the app: `13528988161` (GB) and `14237389387` (US), quoted in Warning 4. **Interpretation: the prompt is a rating liability as well as an evidentiary one. It is producing 1★ reviews from people who had formed no opinion of the product, and the fix — fire the prompt after a streak milestone rather than during onboarding — costs nothing and would also improve the quality of the corpus this report is built on.**

**12. The UI was rewritten in a way that three long-term US users publicly objected to, in a five-week window in early 2025 — and it has not recurred.**
**3 reviews (1.46%, meaningful, mean 2.67)** attack repeated UI redesigns, all between **10 January and 18 February 2025**: `12167788858` (1★, *"the consistent and routine updates that makes the UI worse and worse… Update after update I'm being pushed a little more to uninstall this app"*), `12244698035` (4★, *"it's also counter intuitive to create an app about routine and habits and make your users keep adjusting to new layouts"*), `12326982290` (3★, *"this was perfect as it was when I first downloaded it! It was simple and effective, and now it's messy"*). All three carry `churn_risk`. **No further UI-regression complaint appears in the 19 months since.** **Interpretation: a real, contained incident that appears to have been learned from. Note the specific argument in `12244698035` — for a habit app, UI churn contradicts the product's own premise. That argument is worth adopting internally.**

**13. The cheapest high-value moves, in evidence order:** reverse or grandfather the 5→3 free-cap cut, or disclose it before install (finding 1; 5 reviewers, mean 2.20, all in the last four months of the corpus) → fix the trial-to-charge price mismatch and add a trial-expiry reminder (finding 4; 3 reviewers, all 1★, spanning 17 months) → fix the notes text editor (finding 7; 3 reviewers, 16 months apart, two of whom name it as the withheld star) → move the review prompt behind a usage milestone (finding 11; 2 reviewers, both 1★) → ship streak-freeze / habit-pause / archive (finding 9; 5 reviewers, all 3–5★, all describing a churn moment) → extend Health sync to weight, sleep and mindful minutes (finding 10; 5 reviewers, mean 4.60) → restore the ability to edit or delete a single occurrence of a recurring habit, removed in an August 2026 update (`14453158436`, CA, 4★) → resolve the listing-vs-product conflict on the free cap (Warning 8) and on journal-app sync (`13472233675`, CA, 2★, *"doesn't sync with journal app as advertised"*) and templates (`14386354700`, GB, 4★, *"I hope vote templates are coming as it promises"*).

## 1.2 Schema

Fields present on every one of the 205 records: `review_id`, `app_id`, `app_name`, `country`, `country_name`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `author`, `date`, `vote_count`, `vote_sum`, `is_edited`.

**Fields used in this analysis:** `review_id`, `country`, `rating`, `title`, `body`, `date`.
**Fields inspected and set aside:** `author` (not analysed; no reviewer is named in this report), `app_id` / `app_name` / `country_name` (constant or derivable from `country`).
**Fields inspected and used only as integrity checks:**
- `vote_count` / `vote_sum` — 138 of 205 records (67.3%) have zero votes; the maximum is 11 (`11057451287`). `vote_sum` is the *net* score, so `vote_sum < vote_count` indicates down-votes; this occurs on 22 records (10.7%). Used solely to test for vote-inflated clusters (Warning 5). **None found.**
- `is_edited` — true on only 2 records (0.98%): `12379374379` (US, 1★, *"Decided to give this app another chance but it's still stuck at initializing"* — the edit is visible in the text) and `12160005173` (US, 5★, where the reviewer appended *"\*this was updated very soon, thank you"* to a feature request). Both are noted in-line where they matter.

**Text handling:** HTML entities (`&#39;`, `&#34;`, `&amp;`) are present in the raw `title` and `body` fields and were unescaped before reading and classification. This affects string rendering only. Body length: median **161** characters, mean 242, min **1** (`13472233675`, CA — body is the single character `…`; the signal is entirely in its title), max **2020** (`13927542460`, CA, a detailed premium-user review).

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Parse and reconcile.** All 205 records parsed from `reviews.jsonl`; reconciled against `by_country/*.jsonl` and `manifest.json` (§1.3). Script: `Temp/50-reconcile.py`.
2. **Full-text dump.** Every record written out in full, HTML-unescaped, sorted by storefront then date, with ID, country, rating, date, vote counts and edit flag. Script: `Temp/50-dump.py` → `Temp/50-all-reviews.txt`.
3. **Individual reading.** All 205 reviews read one by one, in original language. No sampling, no keyword filtering, no truncation, no stopping at a context limit. Non-English records (Spanish 5, Korean 2, Chinese 2, Arabic 1, Russian 1, German 2, French 3, plus mixed) were read in the original; translations in this report are the analyst's and are marked with `/`.
4. **Hand-curated classification.** A theme → review-ID map built by hand, 110 themes, saved at `Temp/50-review-classification.py`. No regex or automated clustering was used to assign any theme.
5. **Programmatic validation** (`Temp/50-validate.py`), run to completion before any number in this report was written:

| Validation check | Result |
|---|---|
| Review IDs in the map that do not exist in `reviews.jsonl` | **0 ✓** |
| Duplicate IDs within any single theme | **0 ✓** |
| Records in the corpus with no theme assigned | **0 ✓** |
| Themes with an empty ID list | **0 ✓** |
| Records carrying ≥1 theme | **205 / 205 ✓** |
| Themes per review | min 1 · max 10 · mean 2.88 |

6. **Aggregation.** All counts, percentages, means, rating splits, country cuts, half-year buckets and family unions computed from the validated map (`Temp/50-stats.py`, `Temp/50-stats2.py`, `Temp/50-stats3.py`). Appendix tables §9.2, §9.3 and §9.6 are machine-generated from the same map (`Temp/50-appendix.py`), so no review ID in this report was transcribed by hand.

**Known error risk.** The judgement calls with the widest error bars are: (a) `paid_direct` vs `paid_probable` — four reviews strongly imply payment without stating it, and are segregated rather than merged (§9.1); (b) `low_info` — 26 records carry it, and the boundary against a weak praise theme is a judgement call, disclosed at §9.1; (c) theme assignment for the two Korean and two Chinese reviews rests on the analyst's reading of the original text.

## 1.6 Limitations and known biases

- **Written reviews are not users.** 205 written reviews are a small self-selected slice of everyone who installed this app. Nothing here estimates install base, retention or conversion.
- **The in-app review prompt biases the corpus positive** (Warning 4) — and two reviewers documented it firing during onboarding. The 69.3% five-star share should not be read as a satisfaction measure.
- **No conversion rate is inferable from review text and none is claimed.** 9 reviewers (4.39%) give explicit purchase evidence; that is a self-selected sample of writers, not of buyers.
- **Negative themes have small absolute counts** (Warning 1). Each is enumerated by ID in §9.3 so the reader can check every one.
- **Only the United States clears the 50-review threshold.** CA, GB, AU and MX findings are labelled limited evidence throughout.
- **No version field exists in this schema**, so no finding can be tied to a specific app version. Where a reviewer says "after the update", that is their word, not a version identifier.
- **External verification failed** (Warning 8). Prices and free-tier limits in Part 2 are reconstructed from review text; where an external summary conflicts, both are shown.
- **The corpus ends 4 September 2026.** The 5→3 cap change is only four months old at the corpus edge, and 2026 H2 is n=17. The *direction* of finding 1 is well-evidenced; its eventual *magnitude* is not yet measurable.

## 7.2 Trend 1 — Monetization friction rose eleven-fold · **worsening, high confidence in direction**

**3.1% → 9.4% → 6.1% → 13.6% → 18.9% → 35.3%.** Monotonic in four of five steps; the single reversal (2025H1) is a January-heavy bucket in which new, enthusiastic users dominate.

This is the strongest and most consistent trend in the corpus. It is *not* accompanied by a comparable rise in reliability complaints (9.8% in H1 of the corpus vs 10.7% in H2) or UX complaints (8.8% vs 8.7%), both of which are flat. **Interpretation: the deterioration is specific to money, not to quality.**

⚠️ **2026H2 is 17 reviews and its 35.3% is 6 of them.** The direction is established by the full six-bucket series; the endpoint value is not precise.

## 7.3 Trend 2 — The free cap was cut from 5 to 3 in 2026 H1 · **the causal event, high confidence**

Detailed at §3.3.1. The evidence is a clean temporal partition of fourteen reviewers: last "5" on **26 January 2026**, first "3" on **17 May 2026**, zero overlap, mean ratings 3.78 vs 2.20.

**The two trends line up.** Monetization friction stands at 18.9% in 2026H1 (the bucket containing the change) and 35.3% in 2026H2 (the first full bucket after it). Every 2026H2 monetization-friction review except one (`14248080020`, CA, *"Pay before trying won't help"*) is cap-related.

## 7.4 Trend 3 — August 2026 is the worst month in the corpus · **worsening, medium confidence**

**August 2026: n=9, mean 2.89.** Five of the nine are 1–2★, and four of those five are about money or a functional bug:
`14451148045` (DE, 1★, immediate paywall + €5.99/mo) · `14451696132` (MX, 2★, broken list section) · `14455227817` (MX, 1★, cap 5→3) · `14464763405` (MX, 1★, 3-habit cap) · plus `14441435311` (MX, 3★, 3-habit cap) and `14453158436` (CA, 4★, feature removed in update).

⚠️ **n=9.** Reported because it is the only month with n≥9 and a mean below 3.5, and because its composition matches trends 1 and 2 exactly rather than being a random draw.

## 7.5 Trend 4 — Praise held steady for two years, then fell · **emerging, low-to-medium confidence**

Praise-any: 75.0% → 59.4% → 67.3% → 77.3% → 66.0% → **41.2%**. The first five buckets fluctuate around ~69% with no trend. **2026H2 is the only bucket outside that range**, and it is n=17.

## 7.6 Trend 5 — Reliability is flat, and thin, across the whole period · **persistent, high confidence**

Reliability-family share by half-year: **9.4% → 6.2% → 18.4% → 4.5% → 7.5% → 11.8%** — no direction. Splitting the corpus into equal halves instead gives **9.8% (Feb 2024 – Apr 2025) against 10.7% (Apr 2025 – Sep 2026)**, likewise flat. No individual reliability defect exceeds 4 reviews across 31 months.

The one bucket that stands out is **2025 H1 at 18.4% (9 of 49)** — the corpus's only concentration of defect reports: the UI-regression trio, the first notes-editor report, a Health-sync break after an update, a launch failure, a login failure and a calendar counting bug. ⚠️ These are unrelated defects in a January-heavy bucket, not one incident. **No regression event is claimed.**

**Interpretation: this is a well-built app and it has stayed well-built.** That matters for prioritisation — it means the rating risk identified in this report cannot be engineered away. The exceptions worth naming are the notes editor (3 reports, Jan 2025 → Apr 2026, unresolved across 16 months) and the two 2026 data-loss reports.

## 7.7 Trend 6 — The UI-redesign backlash was a contained five-week event in early 2025 · **resolved, high confidence**

All three `ui_regression_complaint` reviews fall between **10 January and 18 February 2025** (`12167788858`, `12244698035`, `12326982290`) — all US, all carrying `churn_risk`, mean 2.67. **No further UI-regression complaint appears in the 19 months to the end of the corpus.**

**Interpretation: a real regression shipped, users objected loudly, and it did not recur. This is evidence of a team that responds — which the `praise_dev_responsiveness` theme (11 reviews, 5.37%) independently corroborates.**

## 7.8 Trend 7 — Feature requests shifted from "does it exist" to "does it go deep enough" · **persistent, medium confidence**

- **2024 requests were for features that did not exist at all:** home-screen widgets (`11057451287`, Mar 2024), an Apple Watch app (`11931971404`, Nov 2024; `12250249874`, Jan 2025), to-do lists (`11258026649`, May 2024), calendar integration (`11916670042`, Nov 2024). **Widgets and the Watch app both shipped inside the corpus window** — widgets are praised by Nov 2024 (`11969962134`) and the Watch app is reviewed as existing by May 2025 (`12662311740`).
- **2025–2026 requests are refinements of shipped features:** Health sync for weight / sleep / mindful minutes (`13696241769`, `13598469223`, `13089863548` — all 2025–26), recurring journal templates (`13366668018`, `14386354700`), interactive check-off widgets (`13939936171`), streak freeze (all three, 2025–26), "at least N per week" goals (`13618737734`, Jan 2026).

**Interpretation: the product cleared its table-stakes gap list within roughly 18 months, and its backlog is now depth work driven by long-tenure users. That is a healthy maturation curve and it corroborates §3.1 — feature gaps here are a retention lever, not a rating problem.**

## 7.9 What did not change

- **`praise_comparison` is stable across the entire period** — present in every half-year bucket, 37 reviews, mean 4.89. The competitive advantage has not eroded.
- **Payer satisfaction is stable.** The nine `paid_direct` reviews span Mar 2024 → Jul 2026 and their mean (4.11) shows no time trend; the two 1★ payers are Mar 2025 and Jan 2026, either side of six 5★ payers.
- **The billing defect is stable and unresolved** — Aug 2024, Mar 2025, Jan 2026. **Three reports, 17 months, same failure mode.**
- **The notes-editor bug is stable and unresolved** — Jan 2025, Jan 2026, Apr 2026.
- **January seasonality is stable** — 22 reviews in Jan 2025, 26 in Jan 2026.

## 8.2 Structural changes — direction is clear, magnitude is not

**S1 — Reverse, grandfather, or fully disclose the 5→3 free-cap cut. This is the central recommendation of this report.**
Evidence: §3.3.1 · §7.3 · §7.2 · §6.6 · §6.7. Fourteen reviewers state the cap; the nine who experienced 5 average **3.78** and three of them wrote 5★ reviews *defending* it; the five who experienced 3 average **2.20** and none defends it. Monetization friction went 18.9% → 35.3% across the change. Every documented purchase trigger in this corpus (§5.3) runs through sustained free use first — the cap cut attacks the only conversion path the corpus evidences.

Three options, in order of evidential support:
1. **Revert to 5.** The corpus contains an unusual asset: users who publicly argue *for* the cap when it is 5. *"Tienes 5 hábitos por elegir gratis lo que considero perfecto ya que te hace ser prioritario"* (`13677250370`). At 3 that argument disappears.
2. **Grandfather existing free users at 5** and apply 3 only to new installs. This addresses the angriest reviews specifically — `14455227817` and `14074582574` are both people who *lost* capacity they already had.
3. **If the cap stays at 3, disclose it before install.** Four reviewers (§5.5, `deceptive_free`) say they were not told the terms. `14464763405` (MX) states the alternative himself: *"la versión gratuita debería ser funcional y la versión de paga debería agregarle un plus… en ese caso o pongamos de paga desde 0"* / "the free version should be functional and the paid version should add a plus… otherwise just make it paid from zero."

⚠️ **Magnitude caveat.** 5 reviews, 2026H2 is n=17, and the change is only four months old at the corpus edge. **The direction is well-evidenced across four storefronts and three languages; the size of the effect is not yet measurable and should be monitored in the next extraction, not assumed.**

**S2 — Make the lifetime tier discoverable, or reinstate it.**
Evidence: §5.5. The tier existed (`11057451287` Mar 2024, `13058573989` Aug 2025) but three reviewers ask for it as if absent, two of them after August 2025 (`13446856492` Nov 2025, `14032245863` May 2026, `11969620942` Nov 2024). One is a 4★ reviewer whose entire review is *"I would like purchase a lifetime access subscription for this."* **This is demand asking to be met.** It also directly addresses the four `subscription_objection` reviewers (1.95%, mean 2.00), whose objection is to recurring billing, not to paying.

**S3 — Build a referral programme.**
Evidence: §5.5 · §3.4. `praise_comparison` is the largest theme in the corpus (37 reviews, 18.05%, mean 4.89) — thirty-seven people who have tried the competition and evangelise this product unprompted. Two reviewers explicitly ask for a referral mechanism (`13677250370`, `14032245863`, both 5★). **The corpus's strongest asset currently has no capture mechanism.**

**S4 — Treat streak-freeze / pause / archive as a retention feature and ship them together.**
Evidence: §3.5 · §4.2. Five reviewers across three themes (`streak_pause_missing` 3, `archive_habit_missing` 2), all rating 3–5★, all describing the exact moment a habit streak breaks and engagement is at risk: illness, vacation, one missed day, a completed short-term goal cluttering the screen. `13086329598` (US, 4★): *"If those two features were added it would be a perfect 5 stars to me!"* **Small counts, high leverage — these reviewers are describing churn mechanics, not preferences.**

**S5 — Extend Apple Health sync to weight, sleep and mindful minutes, and add Apple Fitness session types.**
Evidence: §3.5 · §6.2. 5 reviews (2.44%, mean 4.60), **4.50% of the US subset** — the joint-largest US feature-request cluster. All five describe hand-entering data that already exists in Health. `13696241769` (US, 3★) says explicitly that adding weight sync would move the rating to 5. Meanwhile 7 reviewers praise the Health integration that exists (§3.4). **This is extension of a proven-delightful shipped feature, not new development.**

**S6 — Reduce the cost of the sign-in gate.**
Evidence: §3.3.4 · §6.4. 2 reviews (0.98%, mean 1.50), both GB, both at account creation: one privacy refusal (*"Why would I want to expose every aspect of my life to data mining in this way? Stay clear."*) and one hard failure (*"unable to login with apple"*). ⚠️ Emerging signal only. But a mandatory account before first use is a single point of failure in front of the entire product, and `12766734680` never saw the app at all. **Consider a local-first trial mode, or at minimum instrument the failure rate at this step.**

## 8.4 Research questions this corpus raises but cannot answer

1. **Did the 5→3 cap cut increase net revenue?** The corpus sees friction only. This is the question the whole report turns on and it requires internal data.
2. **Was the cap change rolled out globally or by storefront?** Zero US reviewers report a 3-cap; all five reports are non-US (TR, AU, MX ×3). This is either a staged rollout, a price-tier-linked policy, or a 111-vs-94 sampling artefact. **§6.6 shows a US-only dashboard would have missed the finding entirely.**
3. **Why is monetization friction roughly twice as high outside the US** (17.0% vs 9.0%; 20.5% vs 10.8% outside the high-spend group)? Local price points, purchasing power, or sample noise?
4. **What is the actual lifetime tier status?** External verification was impossible (Warning 8) and reviewer evidence conflicts across 2024–2026.
5. **What is the install-to-trial-to-paid funnel?** 13 self-identified payers in 205 reviews supports no conversion estimate, and none is offered.
6. **Do the two data-loss reports represent a migration defect or two isolated incidents?** Both are 2026, both Canadian, n=2. Server logs would settle it in minutes.
7. **How large is the ADHD segment really?** 7 self-identifying reviewers (3.41%, mean 4.29), 5 of them US. If that share holds in the install base it is a positioning decision, not a footnote — but self-identification in reviews is not a population estimate.

## 8.5 What not to change

- **Do not add features to raise the rating.** §3.1 is decisive: 40 feature-gap reviewers average **4.38** and supply 2 of the 25 bad reviews. The feature backlog is a depth and retention lever, not a rating lever. The rating lever is monetization (48% of bad reviews) and billing.
- **Do not sacrifice simplicity for depth.** 20 reviewers praise simplicity and 14 praise flexibility — **held simultaneously**, mean 4.90 and 5.00. This combination is the product's competitive position (37 `praise_comparison` reviews). `13627490790` (US): *"Nothing comes close to the ease in navigation, creativity, options, and customizability."*
- **Do not redesign the UI again without a migration path.** §7.7: three long-tenure US users objected inside five weeks in early 2025, all carrying churn risk. `12244698035` supplies the argument to adopt internally: *"it's also counter intuitive to create an app about routine and habits and make your users keep adjusting to new layouts and programming."*
- **Do not treat support as a cost centre.** 17 unique reviewers (8.29%) praise support or developer responsiveness (mean 4.91 and 5.00); one instance (`14310174846`) directly recovered a lost sale. One reviewer negative in 205.
- **Do not paywall anything currently free.** The corpus is unusual in that **more reviewers praise the monetization than complain about it** (31 vs 26) and no reviewer reports statistics, reminders, journaling, widgets, Health or the Watch app being individually gated. That clean one-gate model is worth protecting — and §7.2 shows what happens when the one gate moves.
