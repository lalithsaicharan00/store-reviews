# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

## 1. This is the healthiest product in the set — and its rating is still sliding, for reasons that are all fixable

HabitKit is not a troubled app. 78.46% of the written corpus is 5★, praise is dense and specific, and the top three themes are all *product* themes (simplicity 38.10%, design 34.58%, the developer himself 15.08%). Nothing in this corpus suggests a broken core loop.

But the rating is going down, monotonically, across four annual cohorts:

| Period | n | Mean | 5★ | 1★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 — Nov 2022 → Dec 2023 | 142 | **4.79** | 88.0% | 1.4% |
| P2 — 2024 | 234 | **4.66** | 80.3% | 1.7% |
| P3 — 2025 | 308 | **4.47** | 75.0% | **6.2%** |
| P4 — 2026 (Jan–Sep) | 198 | **4.42** | 74.7% | **8.1%** |

The 1★ rate has **multiplied by ~5.8×** from P1 to P4 (1.4% → 8.1%). The 5★ rate has fallen 13.3 points. This is not noise — it is a consistent four-cohort trend on healthy sample sizes.

**And the cause is almost entirely monetization, not product quality.** Of the 41 one-star reviews in the whole corpus, **21 (51.2%)** are about paywall, price, or the free cap; a further **4 (9.8%)** are people who *paid* and could not get what they paid for. Only **4** are about the app malfunctioning and **3** about it being confusing.

> The product is winning. The packaging is losing. Every point of rating decline traces back to how the free tier is drawn, not to what the app does.

## 2. The 4-habit free cap is the single most damaging decision in the product

- **27 reviews (3.06%, VERY STRONG)** name the free habit cap as a limitation. Mean rating **2.56**. **55.6% are 1–2★**, only 7.4% are 5★.
- **Zero** of those 27 reviewers show any evidence of having paid. The cap does not convert them; it produces a bad review and a churn.
- The cap has been **"4" continuously from Feb 2023 to Aug 2026** — 18 dated reviewer reports, 15 saying 4, two saying 5 and one saying 3 (likely miscounts). This is a 3½-year-old constraint that has never been revisited.
- It is the top 2★ theme by a distance: **8 of 19 two-star reviews (42.1%)** name it.

Evidence: `9589360229`(PL,2★) `10100430683`(FR,1★) `11058226263`(PL,3★) `11333683156`(AR,2★) `11530931211`(CA,2★) `11713771657`(IT,2★) `12224917759`(CA,1★) `12297500345`(TR,1★) `12499305420`(MA,2★) `13030220021`(DZ,1★) `13513014070`(MX,1★) `13677605567`(DE,2★) `13838060940`(US,2★) `14316598205`(PT,1★)

Counter-evidence that matters: **11 reviews (1.25%) name the same cap and are fine with it — all 11 are 5★** (`10842857261` `10882654265` `11602928681` `11714676984` `12068026268` `12238453493` `12273297032` `13727963896` `13813261463` `14407894713` `14463193980`). The cap is not universally hated; it is **hated by people who need 5–8 habits and loved by people who need 3–4**. That is a segmentation problem with a cheap fix (raise the cap to 6–8), not a pricing problem.

## 3. Putting home-screen widgets behind the paywall costs more rating than it earns revenue

- **13 reviews (1.47%, MEANINGFUL) object specifically to widgets being Pro-gated.** Mean rating **2.23**. **53.8% are 1–2★. Not one is 5★** — the only theme in the corpus with a zero 5★ rate.
- Against that: **8 reviews (0.91%) name the widget as the thing that made them buy**, mean 4.88.
- Widgets are simultaneously the app's **best-loved feature** (84 reviews praise them, 9.52%, mean 4.69) and its **most resented paywall**.

The argument reviewers actually make is not "too expensive" — it is *"a widget is an OS feature, you shouldn't charge for it"*: `10771302089`(AU,3★, "something that's built into the operating system"), `12781411049`(US,4★, "isn't the no widgets without subscription thing a bit overkill?"), `13905806444`(PT,1★, "I'm all for pro features being 'add-ons' but the widget?!"), `13575345887`(AU,1★), `12622891075`(CA,3★, addresses the developer by name and proposes free-one/paid-many).

## 5. No cross-device sync is the biggest *product* gap, and it is 2½ years old and unfixed

- **54 reviews (6.12%, HIGH-PRIORITY)** ask for iCloud / cross-device sync. Mean **3.85** vs corpus 4.56.
- Only **33.3%** of them are 5★ — versus 78.46% corpus-wide. Sync requests come attached to a rating penalty.
- It is the **#1 theme in the recoverable 3★/4★ band**: 18.9% of all 4★ reviews and 31.4% of all 3★ reviews mention it.
- Persistent, not decaying: **0.70% (P1) → 7.26% (P2) → 7.14% (P3) → 7.07% (P4)**. Once the userbase got multi-device, the complaint appeared and has held flat for three years.
- It is a **conversion blocker with named willingness to pay**: `12386725682`(AU,4★) *"If you could sync it across multiple devices I would totally be willing to buy the lifetime pass"*; `13473841112`(GB,3★) *"I probably wouldn't have subscribed if I had noticed this sooner"*; `13833569208`(BR,2★) *"for a Pro plan, it should include sync"*.

**[external]** The FAQ states sync "is currently in development." The corpus shows one reviewer (`13346250530`, IN, 5★) already relaying this from the developer — so the roadmap message is reaching some users and buying goodwill.

The tail of this gap is data loss: **5 reviews (0.57%)** lost their history — `10001495366`(GB,3★, reinstall), `12211144791`(UA,1★, new phone), `13499288017`(MX,1★, new phone → switched to Tiimo), `14016661342`(US,5★, accidental delete), `13952801052`(UA,1★, historical entries silently vanished). Mean **2.20**.

## 6. The developer is a top-5 product asset, and reviewers say so by name

**133 reviews (15.08%, HIGH-PRIORITY)** mention the developer, his responsiveness, or supporting an indie. Mean **4.85**, **93.2% are 5★**. Reviewers name him — "Sebastian", "Seb" — and describe reply times in hours to days, features shipped after their email, and personal follow-up.

## 7. What actually makes people love this app: the GitHub grid, and doing nothing else

## 1.4 Upgrade barriers and monetization friction

**Barrier 1 — the €35/$30 lifetime price, specifically.** 15 reviews (1.70%, MEANINGFUL) object to price. Mean **2.73**, 46.7% 1–2★. Every post-2024 instance targets the lifetime SKU:
`11942193233`(DE,3★) *"35€ for lifetime access for (essentially) an automated excel sheet was just too much… I found a great habit tracker app for 6.99€ (lifetime)"* — **an explicit competitive churn at a stated price point.**
`12076054585`(CA,5★) *"making people pay 40$ CA for lifetime usage. It's taking advantage of people. I still paid it because I don't feel like returning to other apps, but that's an abusive amount."* — bought, resents it, 5★.
`14093446153`(DE,3★) *"Ich hab schon bis zu 500€ für Apps bezahlt. Aber hier reden wir über die Simpelste Utility vorstellbar."*
`13621381288`(US,4★, payer) *"The app does not have enough features to justify the $13 pro. $5 or $10 would be more acceptable."*
`12032382845`(US,1★) *"$2 a month. Too pricy for me, twice the cost of same subscription on android."* — **cross-platform price-parity objection.**

**Barrier 2 — no trial.** 7 reviews (0.79%, EMERGING) ask for one before paying. `10332746889`(DE,4★) *"it could be super nice if we could try before purchasing"*; `12181661428`(PE,4★); `11031287982`(BR,5★) *"I wish I could see how the charts are before purchasing"*; `14248083289`(CA,1★) *"Same payment before trying stuff"*; `11920130040`(US,5★). The paid features people most want to preview are precisely charts and widgets — the two things a screenshot cannot convey.

**Barrier 3 — a willing buyer literally could not give the developer money.** Two reviewers in the *same week* (11 Jul 2026) wanted to move from subscription to lifetime and could not:
`14288167581`(US,5★) *"I wanna buy the lifetime subscription but I can't find it anywhere, it only shows month or yearly — it shows on the App Store and in 'in app purchases' that there's a lifetime purchase."*
`14289596763`(ES,5★) *"quería pasar de suscripción a pago único. ¿Ya no existe esa opción?"*
**[external]** The FAQ confirms this is by design: *"upgrading directly from an active subscription to Lifetime is not available in-app. The current workaround is to cancel the subscription, let it expire, then purchase Lifetime."* Two 5★ customers with money in hand were sent away in one week. `[limited evidence]` at n=2, but the mechanism is documented, not inferred.

**Barrier 4 — China storefront cannot buy at all.** 2 of the 4 CN reviews are purchase failures: `11442530087`(CN,3★) *"无法购买会员"* (cannot purchase membership) and `11183018670`(CN,3★) *"怎么开会员 / 怎么设置中文啊"* (how do I buy membership / how do I set Chinese?). `[limited evidence — 4 CN reviews total]`, but with a 100% English-only listing **[external: languageCodesISO2A = ['EN']]** and a third CN reviewer asking for Chinese (`11385868721`,5★), the CN storefront looks unserved rather than under-served.

**Barrier 5 — no Family Sharing.** 2 reviews (0.23%, Weak). `12266571361`(SE,4★, payer) *"after I paid for it I couldn't let my family use it? Paying for Pro Lifetime would have felt much better if my kids could use it too"*; `14057511657`(IE,4★) *"it doesn't support family sharing so I will probably uninstall it and try another."* One lost expansion sale, one stated churn.

## 1.5 Subscription aversion is loud but mostly non-fatal

**55 reviews (6.24%, HIGH-PRIORITY)** mention subscriptions. Mean **3.84** — but the distribution is bimodal: **32 of the 55 are 5★** (people praising the fact that a subscription is *not* required), while 15 are 1–2★. Rising every period: 4.93% → 5.13% → 6.17% → **8.59% (2026)**.

The lesson is not "drop subscriptions." **22 of the 55 (40% segment rate) are confirmed payers** — they subscribed while complaining. The lesson is that the lifetime SKU is doing enormous defensive work and must stay visible and purchasable.

## 1.6 Upsell pressure is small — and that's an asset

Only **8 reviews (0.91%)** complain about upsell/nagging, and one is a hard case (`12253135804`, US, 4★, *"there are wayyyyyyyyyyyyyyyyy too many ads of premium"*) with `14488967268`(KZ,1★) *"Annoying popups"* in Aug 2026. Against that, several reviewers name the *absence* of nagging as a reason they stayed: `13206904375`(US,5★) *"I personally hate when developers force users into buying a subscription… by nagging with constant ads… Habitkit is one of the rare gems among many slop apps"*; `11714676984`(MX,5★) *"no te bloquea todo ni te insiste en hacer el upgrade"*; `14088197028`(FR,5★) *"not forcing ads or notifications on you every hour"*.
**Do not solve the conversion problem by adding pressure. This corpus explicitly rewards the absence of it.**

## 4.1 The two request clusters that are pure upside (zero 1–2★)

**Per-day notes — 16 requests (1.81%), mean 4.81, not one below 4★.** People want to record *what* they did, not only *that* they did it: `13824458972`(TR,5★) *"when I mark my 'Gym' habit as done on Tuesday, I'd like to add a note specifying which exercises"*; `13036578153`(CH,5★) *"on a day you missed your habit, write the reason why — this would help acknowledge mistakes and build self accountability"*; `12607872016`(US,5★) *"I'd be willing to subscribe to pro for that."*
**[external]** Day notes shipped in changelog 1.16.0. `14466920687`(CA,5★, Aug 2026) confirms it landed and that he had proposed it. **Requests span Nov 2023 → Aug 2026, so this is a shipped-feature story; verify the last few requesters were on an older build before treating them as open.**

**Sub-habits / folders / pages — 11 requests (1.25%), mean 4.82, zero 1–2★.** `11526306665`(US,5★) describes it exactly: *"label the habit morning routine, click it to open, add a sub-habit/task like make bed, brush teeth, do skin care, and once you check off all three it will complete that habit."* Also `13692456434`(DE,4★), `13149743389`(RO,5★, wants "pages" to separate his tracking from his dog's), `12727692876`(US,5★, career vs health folders), `11650437167`(GB,5★), `11081763094`(DE,5★).
This is the natural *next* product after categories — and it is what heavy users (10+ habits) ask for, i.e. the paying segment.

## 4.2 The frequency model is the most-misunderstood part of the product

17 reviews (1.93%) want weekly / flexible / every-other-day / weekday-only / skip-day goals. **[external]** The app *has* "X per week / X per month" goals (changelog 1.1, 1.11) and rest days (marketing site). So a large share of this cluster is a **discoverability failure, not a feature gap**:

- `10266075296`(GB,3★) *"Cannot track a habit for a set amount of days per week/month… If incorrect advise me how and will adjust review"* — an explicit offer to raise the rating that nobody took.
- `11800436326`(US,3★) *"I didn't realize until subscribing to pro… that it is only set up for daily habits"* — **a payer who bought and then believed the feature was absent.**
- `13187090517`(FR,2★) *"on ne semble pas pouvoir régler d'objectif par semaine… ce qui la met derrière d'autres apps du même type qui le proposent"*
- `14489689059`(GB,5★) *"All I would say is missing is a weekly tracking option."*
- `13736974190`(DE,4★) *"Finde keinen Modus für Arbeitswochen"* — plus *"Zahnrad öffnet nicht die Einstellungen, sondern das Export-Menu"* (the gear icon opens Export, not Settings).

Adjacent: **streak maths for non-daily goals is not trusted.** `13578452293`(US,4★) *"I'm on a 12 week streak for a habit I started 8 weeks ago"*; `13856682531`(US,4★) *"The numeric streak count for weekly goals is strange, so I don't use it"*; `12189061392`(US,3★) *"The streak is not a true streak"*; `11317982485`(CA,5★, resolved only because the developer explained it personally).

Genuinely missing from this cluster: **skip / rest days without breaking the chain** (`13604363091`,PL,5★ — public holidays) and **every-other-day intervals** (`12962416172`,DE,4★ — *"leider keine Gewohnheiten, die alle zwei Tage anstehen… Sonst hätte ich es mit der App versucht"* — a stated non-adoption).

## 4.4 Notifications: a small, sharp, six-review problem

Mean **2.67**, 50% 1–2★, and the failures are opposite kinds:

- `10334933288`(DE,5★) reminders silently stopped firing. **[external]** Changelog 1.16.1 documents exactly this: *"reminders could go quiet for the rest of the week, depending on the day you last opened the app."*

## 4.5 What is *not* in this corpus (and that is informative)

- **No privacy or data-selling accusations at all.** Zero. In a category where this is common, the offline/no-account posture appears to have completely eliminated the theme.
- **No ad complaints** beyond premium-upsell interstitials (8 reviews). There are no ads.
- **No AI backlash and almost no AI demand.** Two reviews mention AI: `13891792959`(US,5★) praises the *absence* — *"without adding AI bloatware"* — and `14450302981`(IN,5★) proposes adaptive scheduling and natural-language logging. `13177753546`(GB,5★) praises *"not based around some opaque cloudy AI nonsense."* **Adding AI to this app would be arguing with its own fanbase.**
- **No billing-fraud or surprise-charge accusations.** Unlike apps 3, 5 and 6 in this folder, nobody claims they were charged an amount they did not agree to. The entitlement failures in §0.4 are the *opposite* problem: charged correctly, not delivered.
- **Almost no crash reports.** 8 reviews (0.91%) volunteer that the app has *no* bugs and never crashes — mean 5.00.

# PART 8 — WHAT CHANGED OVER TIME

## 8.1 Method

Four cohorts by review date, sized to be comparable: P1 = launch through Dec 2023 (n=142), P2 = 2024 (n=234), P3 = 2025 (n=308), P4 = Jan–Sep 2026 (n=198). Review records carry **no app-version field**, so all release attributions are inferred from the public changelog **[external]** and from reviewers' own dated statements. Where a version cannot be dated, no attribution is made.

Volume note: the corpus has two January spikes (Jan 2025 n=71, Jan 2026 n=36) — New Year resolution seasonality. Sept 2026 is partial (6 reviews, cut off 6 Sep).

## 8.2 Themes that genuinely improved

**Interactive widgets — a clean, closed loop.** Requests to tick a habit *from* the widget run Dec 2023 → Jun 2024 (`10685338852` `10690084425` `10786717280` `10809321944` `10854549103`(SK,3★) `10899303798` `11309161661` `11426923525`), then stop dead. **Widget praise peaked with that release** — 7.04% (P1) → **14.10% (P2)** → 9.09% (P3) → 6.57% (P4). The P2 spike is the interactivity ship.

**Quit-habit mode.** Requested from 2024 (`11762071060`), rising to 2.53% in P4, then shipped **[external: 1.17.0]** and immediately validated: `14470496356`(AU,5★, 25 Aug 2026) *"The quit option work surprisingly well… I'm more motivated because I don't have to open the app to log a missed day."*

**Day notes.** Requested Nov 2023 → 2026; shipped **[external: 1.16.0]**; confirmed by the reviewer who proposed it (`14466920687`, CA, 5★).

# PART 9 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation cites the findings it comes from.

## Immediate — fix before anything else (days, not sprints)

**1. Fix the Pro entitlement / widget-unlock failure. Highest severity in the corpus.**
→ §0.4, §1.2. 8 reviews, mean 2.25, **100% of them paying customers**, unresolved across 15 months, and the single most recent review in the corpus (`14519062888`, 6 Sep 2026) is an instance. Cache entitlement locally and let the widget render optimistically from the last-known-good receipt rather than blocking on a network check — an offline-first app should never gate a widget on a live server call. Then proactively email the eight reviewers.

**2. Put a support address in the app, visibly.**
→ §0.6. `14351190258`(MM,1★) *"I can't find any way to contact customer support"* and `13952801052`(UA,1★) *"the developer doesn't respond to emails."* The developer's responsiveness is a top-5 asset (133 reviews, 93.2% 5★) — the two reviewers who couldn't reach him produced two 1★s. This is a discoverability fix, not a staffing one.

**3. Investigate the Aug 2026 compact-list redesign.**
→ §0.9. 5 reviews in 12 days, mean 2.43, three from payers, one explicit "going to hunt for an alternative app." Ship a text-size / density option, or an opt-out. `14452046441`(UA,5★) gives the precise repro: iPhone 14 with display zoom + large text.

**4. Audit the streak count for "X per week/month" habits.**
→ §4.2. `13578452293` reports a 12-week streak on an 8-week-old habit. Two more reviewers say they simply stopped using the streak number. A wrong number in a trust-based app is worse than no number.

## Monetization — repackage, don't reprice

**5. Raise the free habit cap from 4 to 6–8.**
→ §0.2. The cap produces 27 complaints at mean 2.56, 55.6% 1–2★, **zero conversions among the complainers**, and it is the top cause of 2★ reviews. Meanwhile 11 reviewers explicitly say 4 is enough for them — so the cap is only binding on the 5-to-8-habit user, who is exactly the person most likely to become a power user and eventually pay for folders, colours, and stats. **Move the wall past them, not in front of them.**

**6. Make one widget free.**
→ §0.3, §6.3. The widget paywall is the only theme in the corpus with a **0% 5★ rate**, and reviewers have already designed the fix for you: `12622891075`(CA,3★) *"only making one widget free and making more widgets paywalled, kinda like Widgy."* Widgets are also the app's strongest daily-engagement surface (`14305420985`,GB,5★ runs three home-screen pages of them) — free widgets create the habit that later justifies Pro.

**7. Keep the lifetime SKU prominent, and make subscription→lifetime possible in-app.**
→ §1.3a, §1.4-Barrier-3. The lifetime option is the top named purchase trigger (45 reviews, 35.6% of them confirmed payers). Two 5★ customers in one July 2026 week tried to hand over the lifetime price and could not. This is pure lost revenue with a documented workaround that no user should have to perform.

**8. Review Canadian and emerging-market pricing.**
→ §7.1, §7.2. CA is the worst-rated eligible market with the highest price/paywall complaint rates, on a CA$40 SKU. Outside the high-spend group the cap complaint rate nearly doubles (4.2% vs 2.3%). Consider regional lifetime pricing, or lead with the $12/year SKU in price-sensitive storefronts.

**9. Add a real trial for the two features screenshots cannot sell: charts and widgets.**
→ §1.4-Barrier-2. 7 requests, and the specific ask is always "let me see the charts / use the widget once first."

**10. Do NOT add upsell pressure to solve conversion.**
→ §1.6. Only 8 reviews complain about nagging, and multiple 5★ reviews name the *absence* of nagging as the reason they stayed and paid. This is a defended position; giving it up would trade a durable asset for a short-term lift.

## Product — converts 3★/4★ into 5★

**11. Ship iCloud/CloudKit sync. It is the single largest rating lever available.**
→ §0.5, §2 (4★/3★ bands). 54 reviews, 18.9% of all 4★ and 31.4% of all 3★, three years unfixed, with named willingness to pay attached (`12386725682`, `12139974588`, `13833569208`). It also eliminates the data-loss cluster (§0.5) and the "changed phones, lost everything" churn (`13499288017` → Tiimo).
**Do it without breaking the privacy promise** — 7 reviews praise local-only storage, one (`11241317063`) praises the *absence* of sync, and `13860278748`/`13177753546` explicitly value the no-cloud posture. Ship it as **opt-in, end-to-end, CloudKit private database**, and say so in the release notes.

**12. Make the existing weekly/monthly goal modes discoverable.**
→ §4.2. 17 reviews want a feature the app already has — including a 3★ who offered to change his rating if corrected, and a **payer** who bought and still thought it was daily-only. Surface frequency in habit creation rather than behind "Advanced Options" **[external: changelog 1.11 moved it there]**. This is the highest ratio of rating-gain to engineering-cost in the whole report.

**13. Native iPad layout before Apple Watch.**
→ §4.3. iPad complaints have the worse mean (3.61 vs 4.15) and are a layout problem, not a new product. Watch is asked for more but forgiven more.

**14. More colours, and a colour picker.**
→ §6.8. 13 requests. `13621381288`(US,4★, payer) makes the operational case: 21 colours of which 4 are greys, and 10+ habits become visually indistinguishable. Directly limits the value of Pro's unlimited habits.

**15. Rest/skip days, and a configurable day boundary.**
→ §4.2, §6.14. Small counts (3 + 3) but all 5★ and both are cheap. `13604363091`(PL) wants public holidays not to break a work habit; `12632001002`(CL), `13776739735`(PL), `11670099719`(RU) all want a 4am rollover for late-night logging.

**16. Protect the simplicity. Ship complexity behind opt-in.**
→ §8.3. Simplicity praise fell 12 points across the corpus's life. Every new mode (sub-habits, integrations, AI) should be invisible by default. Reviewers have said this in advance, repeatedly and unprompted.

## Positioning — free rating and revenue, no engineering

**17. Say "ADHD" and "no-guilt tracking" in the store listing.**
→ §3.2, §3.1. ADHD reviewers rate **5.00 across all 13**. The "doesn't shame you for breaking a streak" property is a genuine category differentiator (6 reviews, all 5★) and appears nowhere in the listing **[external]**.

**18. Market the generic-tracker use case.**
→ §3.3. 11 reviews (mean 4.91) track medication, migraines, art projects, pets. The listing says "form new habits or break old ones" — narrower than what users actually do.

**19. Localise the listing, starting with Chinese and German.**
→ §7.2, §7.4. The listing is English-only **[external]**; the app itself supports 15+ languages **[external: changelog 1.2, 1.14, 1.17.2]**. DE is 12.1% of the corpus, and CN cannot even buy.

**20. Lean into the YouTube/Threads discovery channel.**
→ Discovery is named in 12 reviews: YouTube (`12898756160` `12949502025` `13053618099` `14516845857` — the last names "The Studio" channel, which is also cited on the marketing site **[external]**), Threads (`11877901803`), Twitter (`11039862982`), LinkedIn (`10984473093`), Product Hunt (`9980285580`). One of these (`12450937418`,IN,2★) bought an annual plan chasing a lock-screen widget seen in a video **that does not exist** — creator content is driving purchases against features the app doesn't have. Worth a creator-brief.

## Research questions this corpus cannot answer

- **What is the actual free→paid conversion rate?** 75 self-declared payers out of 882 reviewers is a *review-writing* rate, not a conversion rate. Never quote 8.5% as conversion.
- **How many people hit the 4-habit cap and silently leave?** The corpus only contains those who stayed long enough to write. The 27 complaints are a floor, not an estimate.
- **Would raising the cap to 8 reduce revenue?** Untestable from reviews. Needs an A/B test with cohort LTV.
- **Is the P4 rating decline compositional?** More payers write reviews each year (2.11% → 12.63%), and payers rate 0.44 lower. Some of the decline is mix, not deterioration. Store-side cohort data would separate the two.
- **How widespread is the entitlement failure among non-reviewers?** 8 reviews is what surfaced. RevenueCat logs would give the true rate.
- **Which SKU do buyers actually pick?** Reviewers name lifetime far more than yearly, but review-writing is biased toward one-time purchasers who feel good about the transaction.

## 10.3 Method and known error risk

1. **Full read.** All 882 records were dumped chronologically in 10 batches of 90 and read individually, in the original language, before any classification was written. Non-English reviews (178, 20.2%) were read in-language; no machine translation was used.
2. **Taxonomy built from the read**, not from a pre-existing list. 60 themes.
3. **Classification is hybrid.** Broad praise themes (simplicity, design, developer, tried-many, customization, grid, stats, widget) use multilingual regex over `title + body` and were spot-checked. **Every high-stakes theme** — the free cap, the widget paywall, sync, price, entitlement failures, data loss, bugs, churn, notes, notifications, redesign, confusion, and the entire paid cohort — was **hand-curated from an explicit review-ID list** after reading each candidate in context.
4. **Negation was handled by hand, not by regex.** Reviews saying "no bugs", "not complicated", "great that there is no iCloud sync" were manually removed from the corresponding complaint themes and, where meaningful, moved into a praise theme (`reliability_praise`, `praise_no_sync`).
5. **The paid cohort was audited line by line.** 14 regex matches were removed as false positives (people describing the paid tier without buying, or intending to buy) and 15 were added that regex missed. Purchase *intent* ("I'll probably upgrade") is **excluded**. Ten reviews that use Pro-gated features without stating a purchase are held in a separate `paid_inferred_only` set and excluded from all paid-cohort statistics.
6. **HTML entities** (`&#39;`, `&amp;`, `&gt;`) were unescaped before matching.

**Known error risk:** the eight regex-driven praise themes may carry roughly ±3–5% relative error from context misses (e.g. a review saying another app is "beautiful"). Because these themes are all large (≥41) and one-directional, this does not change any label or conclusion. **The hand-curated themes carry near-zero classification error but may under-count** — a review that expresses a theme obliquely, or in a language construction not searched, can be missed. **All small-n themes should therefore be read as floors, not point estimates.**

## 10.4 Known limitations and biases

- **Selection bias.** Written reviews are a small, motivated subset. §0.8 quantifies the gap: written reviews sit 0.07–0.47 stars below tap-only ratings in all eight storefronts checked.
- **No version field.** All release attributions come from the public changelog **[external]**, which is not dated per version. Timeline claims are therefore ordinal, not precise.
- **Prompt-timing bias.** Any in-app review prompt shapes who writes. Its placement is unknown.
- **Language coverage.** The four Chinese/Japanese/Korean-script reviews are few enough that CJK sentiment cannot be assessed; they are counted in all global figures.
- **Survivor bias in the paid cohort.** People who bought, hated it, refunded and left may never write.
- **Recency truncation.** Sept 2026 has 6 reviews (partial month). Nothing in P4 conclusions rests on September alone.
- **The Aug 2026 redesign cluster is 3 weeks old** and could be an early-adopter artefact rather than a durable regression.
- **No developer-response data.** The corpus shows what users wrote, not what was answered.

## 10.5 Counting rules

- A review belongs to **all** themes it expresses. Counts are non-exclusive and do not sum to 882.
- **Global percentages always use n=882.** Rating-band percentages use that band's n (5★=692, 4★=95, 3★=35, 2★=19, 1★=41). Country percentages use that country's n. Segment rates are labelled and carry both denominators.
- "Mean" is the arithmetic mean star rating of the reviews carrying that theme.
- A theme's **direction** is inferred from context, not from the star rating. A 5★ review asking for sync is counted in `want_sync`; a 1★ review saying "no bugs" is not counted in `bug_reported`.
- `paid_explicit` requires a first-person statement of purchase. Intent, recommendation, and inference are excluded.
- Signal labels are applied to the **global** share (n=882) and, separately, to each eligible country's own share.
