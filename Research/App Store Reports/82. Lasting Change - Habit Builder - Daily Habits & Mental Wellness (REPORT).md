# Lasting Change: Habit Builder (App Store ID 6456888481) — Full Review Analysis

**Corpus:** all **175** text reviews in `App Store Reviews/82. Lasting Change - Habit Builder - Daily Habits & Mental Wellness/reviews.jsonl`
**Window:** 2023-09-02 → 2026-08-17 (1080 days) · **Storefronts:** 26 · **Corpus mean rating: 1.880★**
**Developer:** MEDICAL SCORE UAB (trading as "Medical Score", Lithuania) · **Current version:** 2.4.2 (2026-08-21)
**Method:** every review read individually in its original language; 96 hand-assigned theme codes across 16 families; 925 code assignments; 43 integrity checks; 21 absence tests; all external evidence captured 13 September 2026.

---

## How to read this

Every quantified claim carries a **count, a percentage, the denominator, the market scope, the time window, a signal label and exact review IDs**. Review IDs are `review_id` values from `reviews.jsonl`; the bracketed `#n` is the working index (date order) used by Part 10, so any number here resolves to specific reviews and any review resolves to the findings it supports.

Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — a review that reports an unauthorised charge and also calls the app basic is counted in both. Composite unions (`U:` prefix) count each review **once**.

### ⚠️ Ten warnings before any number here is used

1. **This corpus is overwhelmingly negative and the public rating is not.** Corpus mean **1.880★**; the ratings-weighted public mean across the same 26 storefronts is **3.838★** (848 ratings, captured 13 Sep 2026). That is a **1.958-star gap**. The text corpus is **20.64%** of the rating base; **673 raters left no text**. §1.3 and §6.7 treat this gap as a finding, not as noise — but nothing in this report is a population estimate.
2. **Only one storefront reaches the 50-review threshold** — the United States, at 75. Part 6 makes standalone claims for the US only. Every other storefront is reported in aggregate or explicitly labelled limited evidence.
3. **No version history could be retrieved** for this app (see §9.E). Era boundaries in Part 7 are therefore **calendar thirds, not release boundaries**, and **no finding anywhere in this report is attributed to a release**.
4. **The central subject of this corpus is not the app.** It is a **book-funnel purchase and an off-App-Store subscription**. `U:money_harm` = **115 of 175 (65.71%)**. Reviews that criticise the app itself and say nothing about money are **14 (8.00%)**. Read every product finding against that.
5. **No price was obtainable from any external source.** Every price figure in this report comes from reviewers' own text (§2.6) and is labelled as such. The store listing prints a heading called "SUBSCRIPTION PRICING & TERMS" that contains **no price at all**.
6. **Nothing here is a conversion, churn or refund rate.** 125 reviews carry purchase evidence. That describes buyers; it cannot estimate what share of buyers are affected, because people who were charged and noticed are exactly the people who write.
7. **Reviewers' allegations are reported as allegations.** Where a reviewer says "scam" or "fraud", this report records that they said it and counts it; it does not adjudicate it. Where an external capture independently corroborates a factual claim, §2 says so explicitly and shows the capture.
8. **Two reviews carry a star rating that contradicts their own text** (`13757151696` 5★ and `12542916281` 5★ — §4.6). Both are retained in every calculation; both are flagged.
9. **The corpus's positive reviews are thin.** Of 35 reviews at 4–5★, **14 (40.0%) are under 60 characters** and **8 (22.9%) are non-English one-liners**. §4.5 shows the anatomy review by review.
10. **The developer publishes six apps.** Three reviews report being billed under a *different* app's name (`Effecto`, `Medical Score`, `Walking Yoga`). This matters for attribution: some of this corpus may describe charges originating in a sibling product's checkout. §2.5.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

## 0.1 The finding that reframes everything else: this subscription is not sold through Apple

The App Store listing for this app, captured 13 September 2026, carries the offer metadata:

```
"hasInAppPurchases": false,
"hasExternalPurchases": false,
"isFree": true,
"titles": {"standard": "Get"}          <- no "In-App Purchases" subtitle
```

The string `In-App Purchase` **does not occur anywhere in the rendered listing HTML**. This is not a gap in the capture. On the same page, the similar-app lockup *Walking Yoga* carries `"hasInAppPurchases": true` with the subtitle `"In‑App Purchases"`, and **all five sibling apps by the same developer** — Effecto, Mindway, Brainway, Raising Dog, Welcome Baby — also carry `true`. The flag is false for *this app specifically* and true for its neighbours. The Google Play listing for the same bundle shows no "In-app purchases" badge either, while each sibling in its "More by Health Score" cluster shows an IAP price range.

And yet the same listing's description says, under a heading in capitals:

> **SUBSCRIPTION PRICING & TERMS**
> Lasting Change offers flexible auto-renewing plans.
> **PAYMENT & RENEWAL**
> You can download and use the app for free. **Continued access to all features requires a subscription.** Payment is charged to your App Store account at confirmation of purchase…

**The description says payment is charged to your App Store account. The listing's own offer metadata says there are no App Store purchases.** Both were captured from the same page on the same day.

The corpus says which one is true, and one reviewer quotes the company's own terms doing it:

> `12822274987` (#129, 2025-06-27, gb, 3★) — "I checked Direct debits, apple subscriptions etc but **nothing matched**. I eventually narrowed it down **after searching on google**… The ts&Cs I found state **"subscriptions will not appear on apple or google if purchased through the website"**"

That single sentence, quoted by a reviewer from the seller's own terms, is the mechanism behind the largest theme in this corpus. **13 reviews (7.43%, CI 4.4–12.3%) independently report that the subscription does not appear in their Apple subscriptions**, and 4 more state it is a website-only subscription:

| Code | n | % of 175 | CI 95% | Mean ★ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `RAIL_NOT_IN_APPLE` | 13 | 7.43% | 4.4–12.3% | 1.15 |
| `RAIL_WEB_ONLY` | 4 | 2.29% | 0.9–5.7% | 1.50 |
| `RAIL_CARD_DIRECT` (names the card/PayPal/bank charged) | 27 | 15.43% | 10.8–21.5% | 1.15 |
| `RAIL_BLOCKED_CARD` (had to block the card or close the account) | 7 | 4.00% | 2.0–8.0% | 1.57 |

**Everything in Part 3 follows from this one structural fact.** Because the charge is not an Apple transaction:

- it cannot be cancelled in iOS Settings → Subscriptions — **27 reviews (15.43%) say there is no way to cancel anywhere** (`CANCEL_NO_PATH`);
- Apple cannot refund it — **36 reviews (20.57%) were refused a refund** by the seller, and the absence test in §9.F finds **zero** reviews in which Apple refunded anything;
- it does not appear in a purchase history the buyer can audit — **17 reviews (9.71%) discovered the charge from a bank or card statement** rather than from the seller;
- and it is invisible to the App Store's own review-and-ratings context, where the app shows **4.1★**.

## 0.2 The product that gets sold is a book. The app is what gets billed.

The developer's own website leads with *"The Lasting Change: #1 Personalized Self-Growth & Habit-Building **Book**"*. The corpus agrees, and this is the single most consistent narrative in 1080 days:

**`BILL_TIED_TO_BOOK` = 43 reviews (24.57%, CI 18.8–31.5%, mean 1.05★)** — a subscription that arrived attached to a book or journal purchase. It is the largest non-rhetorical code in the corpus.

- `10559324757` (#17, 2023-11-07, gb) — "I had only intended to purchase the Lasting Change book and **ended up with a subscription to this app**."
- `11101438874` (#46, 2024-03-30, au) — "When purchasing the e-book **they add on 3 Months subscription** to the app without being too clear about it."
- `12265926193` (#94, 2025-02-03, us) — "**Had I known I would have to have the app, I would not have purchased the book.**"
- `12329827913` (#102, 2025-02-19, us) — "I purchased the book for **$50.99** which was fine. Along with that purchase is an **undisclosed subscription that is $30.99**."
- `14437712673` (#175, 2026-08-17, au) — "a **$131** purchase has turned into an ongoing financial commitment of **$55 per month**."

And **13 reviews (7.43%)** say outright that the book was the thing they wanted and the app was unwanted baggage (`BOOK_IS_THE_PRODUCT`). The book itself is not the complaint: **`BOOK_PRAISE` = 14 (8.00%)** against **`BOOK_CRITIC` = 8 (4.57%)**. Several reviewers praise the book *in the same breath* as calling the billing a scam — `10822728831` (#26) "no way to contact them and can't find out how to cancel the subscription. **I do love the book though**"; `12822274987` (#129) titles the review "**Love the book, don't fall for the scam**"; `13158666353` (#136) "**The book is GREAT.** The app and company itself is terrible."

**The product people bought is not the product they are complaining about.** That is what makes this corpus unusual, and it is why §8 treats the App Store listing as the primary defect.

## 0.3 Consent, not price, is the complaint

**`U:consent_failure` = 56 reviews (32.00%, CI 25.5–39.2%, mean 1.11★)** — reviews stating the recurring charge was never knowingly agreed to. Against that, objections to the *amount* are rare: **`PRICE_TOO_HIGH` = 4 (2.29%)**.

The strongest single piece of consent evidence is `13710163306` (#160, 2026-02-04, us, 1★, edited), because the reviewer went back and re-ran the checkout to test the company's claim:

> "During the checkout process, **I explicitly unselected the app option** because I was interested in the physical book only. Several weeks later, I was charged $30.99… Customer Service declined to refund me and **repeatedly lied that the subscription details are set forth during checkout (so I went back and ran through the process again; they are not)**… Edited pursuant to the developer's response: the app is not offered as an opt-in during checkout, **you are automatically subscribed to it with a purchase of the physical book**. And they do not describe the payment terms, though **they did add a disclaimer** beneath the consent to the terms **in response to my email back-and-forth** — and then proceeded to lie to me that it had been there all along **when I have screenshots** contradicting that."

That review is the only `BILL_DESELECTED` in the corpus (1 review, 0.57%, Weak-to-Emerging signal — reported here because it is a **direct test of the seller's stated position**, not because the count is large).

Seven reviews (`BILL_FREE_CLAIMED`, 4.00%) report the opposite framing — that the app was presented as *free* or as a trial before the charge:

- `12320369125` (#100, us) — "Said the app came **free**… I am getting charged 30.99 a month for an app. Which was to be included."
- `13711624416` (#162, us) — "offers what appears to be a '**free month of membership**,' but they hide the fact that they will begin charging you monthly **many months afterward**."
- `13738145254` (#166, ae) — "they offer **free subscription** and then they charge you **without your approval**."
- `13535238064` (#146, us) — "it costs $30.99 / month **after the trial period, which i didnt see mentioned anywhere**."

## 0.4 The App Store listing prints a support address at a domain that does not exist

The listing's description ends: *"For support or questions, contact us at **hello@lastingchange.app**"*, and gives Privacy and Terms URLs on the same domain. Checked 13 September 2026:

```
$ nslookup lastingchange.app          -> NXDOMAIN
$ nslookup -type=MX lastingchange.app -> NXDOMAIN
$ curl https://lastingchange.app/     -> could not resolve host
```

**`lastingchange.app` has no DNS record and no MX record. Mail to that address cannot be delivered.** The developer's working domain is `thelastingchange.com` — and the *same listing page* links its "Developer Website" and "Privacy Policy" buttons to `thelastingchange.com`. The page contains both domains, and only the one printed as the support address is dead. Per the developer site's own schema.org block, the real support addresses are `help@thelastingchange.com` and `hello@thelastingchange.com`; **neither is printed in the App Store listing.**

A reviewer hit exactly this, in June 2024:

> `11375486586` (#55, 2024-06-13, gb, 1★) — "I tried to email **hello@** and **was told this was invalid** despite it being given my your information."

This matters because email is the *only* cancellation route the corpus describes: **`CANCEL_EMAIL_ONLY` = 12 reviews (6.86%)**, and there are **zero** reviews describing a working in-app cancel control (§9.F). A cancellation path that runs exclusively through a support address, published at a non-existent domain, is a structural failure rather than a service-quality complaint.

The counter-example proves the point rather than softening it. The one unambiguously satisfied paying customer in this corpus used the **working** address:

> `11605113507` (#73, 2024-08-13, ch, 5★) — "I contacted **help@** in order to cancel my subscription… and got a friendly reply, **confirming the cancellation within a quarter of an hour**."

`help@thelastingchange.com` worked in fifteen minutes. `hello@lastingchange.app`, the address on the store listing, bounces.

| | Corpus (text reviews) | Public rating base |

## 0.8 Six things this corpus proves are *absent*

Tested against all 175 reviews in seven languages (full list and method in §9.F). **20 of 21 claims tested are absent.** The load-bearing blanks:

- **No review describes a working in-app cancel control.** Not one, in 1080 days.
- **No review describes Apple refunding anything.** Consistent with §0.1.
- **No review mentions a free tier or a free-habit limit.** This app has no freemium story in its own corpus.
- **No review reports a price increase over time.** The two price regimes in §2.6 are different *billing periods*, not a rise.
- **No review mentions the app crashing or freezing.** The reliability complaint here is login and account state, not stability.
- **No review mentions ads, battery, accessibility, iPad, widgets, Siri, dark mode, export, Family Sharing, or Android.** For a product in this category that is a striking silence, and §8.6 reads it as evidence that almost nobody in this corpus used the app long enough to form a view of it.

## 1.4 Processing method

1. `82-dump.py` — parse all 175 records, HTML-unescape twice, normalise whitespace, sort by `(date, review_id)`, emit `82-all-reviews.txt` (every review in full) and `82-index.tsv`.
2. **Every review read individually, in full, in its original language, in date order.** No sampling, no keyword filtering, no truncation, no clustering. 17 non-English reviews (Spanish 8, German 4, French 2, Polish 1, Dutch 1, Italian 1) were read in the original; translations in this report are labelled.
3. `82-themes.py` — a 96-code, 16-family taxonomy built **from** the corpus, with explicit definitions and directions, plus 21 composite unions and the era boundaries. **Every one of the 96 codes is used.**
4. `82-cls/001-090.txt` and `82-cls/091-175.txt` — the hand classification. One line per review: working index, codes, and a note quoting the exact words that drove each code. This is the expensive artefact; it encodes 175 reading judgements.
5. `82-build-classification.py` — rebuilds the index→`review_id` map from `reviews.jsonl` and **asserts it field-by-field against `82-index.tsv` (175 rows × 7 fields)** before use, then emits `82-review-classification.py`. Two codes (`META_LOW_INFO`, `META_EDITED`) are assigned **mechanically** from the source record, never by hand, and the build refuses to run if they appear in a hand file.
6. `82-validate.py` — **43 integrity checks, all passing**; 21 of them re-derive a code from literal review text and fail on any disagreement. **Five real classification errors were caught this way and fixed** (§9.C).
7. `82-aggregate.py`, `82-extra.py`, `82-extra2.py` — counts, unions, per-star profiles, storefronts, eras, years, quarters, the paid segment, market groups, the price timeline, absence tests, burst/solicitation checks and the public-rating comparison.
8. `82-tables.py` → `82-gen/` — all 26 generated tables plus `_facts.json`; `82-build-report.py` fills the placeholders; `82-verify-report.py` re-derives every claim after the report is written (§9.M).

## 1.5 Signal thresholds

Applied to the global corpus (denominator 175) and, in Part 6, to the United States (denominator 75) — the only storefront eligible for standalone analysis.

| Share | Label |
|---|---|
| <0.1% | Ignore by default |
| 0.1–<0.5% | Weak signal |
| 0.5–<1% | Emerging signal |
| 1–<3% | Meaningful signal |
| 3–5% | Very strong signal |
| >5% | High-priority signal |

**A caution on this scale at n=175.** One review is 0.57%, which the bands already call an "Emerging signal"; two reviews reach "Meaningful". The bands are applied as specified, but every headline share below also carries a **Wilson 95% confidence interval** so the reader can see the real precision. Where a finding rests on fewer than five reviews, the report says so in the sentence.

## 1.6 Limitations, stated plainly

1. **No version history, no `version` field** → no release attribution anywhere.
2. **No external price data** → §2.6 is built entirely from reviewer statements.
3. **No checkout capture.** §0.1 establishes where the charge is *not* made (Apple IAP). It does not establish what the web funnel displays at the moment of purchase. Reviewer accounts of the checkout (`BILL_CHECKOUT_TRICK`, n=5) are reported as accounts.
4. **Allegations are not adjudicated.** `TRUST_SCAM_WORD` counts the use of a word, not a finding of fact.
5. **The public rating base is a single snapshot** (13 Sep 2026). No time series exists, so §6.7 cannot say whether the 1.958★ gap is widening.
6. **Six-app developer.** Three reviews report charges under a sibling app's name; some corpus content may originate in a sibling checkout (§2.5).
7. **Non-exclusive counts.** Codes overlap by design; unions are provided wherever a once-per-review count is needed.
8. **Small positive base.** 35 reviews at 4–5★, 14 of them under 60 characters. Every positive finding is correspondingly weak, and §4.5 shows exactly how weak.

1. **A personalised printed and/or digital book** (also called a workbook or journal), ordered from a website after a questionnaire. This is the thing people set out to buy.
2. **A companion habit / mental-wellness app** — the subject of this listing — containing habit tracking, mood and stress check-ins, reminders, guided audio, meditations, lessons, challenges, sleep logging and a nutrition log.

## 4.7 What the ratings do *not* show

- **No rating elasticity.** Not one reviewer says they would raise their rating if a named feature shipped. In comparable corpora this is usually present; here the negative reviews are not asking for features, they are asking for their money back.
- **No rating recovery.** No review describes a problem being resolved and the rating being revised upward. All five edited reviews either stay negative or become more so.
- **No defence of the company by users.** No review pushes back on the scam allegations.

That is a customer converting on fear of future cost, not on value — and then, having bought, deciding they preferred a paper bullet journal and cancelling. It is the corpus's clearest picture of what the bundle actually sells.

## 7.3 The rising star average is a measurement artefact, not a recovery

This is the trend most likely to be misread, so it is stated carefully.

Mean rating by era: **1.69 → 1.75 → 2.41**. The 4–5★ share: **16.2% → 13.3% → 36.6%**. On its face, a recovery.

It is not. **Nine of the fifteen E3 positive reviews are under 60 characters**, and the E3 positives include the corpus's clearest rating/text contradiction (`13757151696`, 5★, "this company **scams**… Closing bank account"). Removing nothing and re-checking the negative side: **`U:negative_any` in E3 is still 65.9%**, and `U:money_harm` is **58.5% — its highest of the three eras**.

**Ratings drifted up while the underlying complaint intensified.** Both facts are in the table; only the second is about the product.

## 7.4 Volume is falling

74 → 60 → 41 reviews per era, with 2026 producing 27 reviews in nearly eight months. The last quarter of the window (2026Q2 + Q3) produced **4 reviews**. Three readings are consistent with the data and this report does not choose: reduced acquisition, reduced funnel volume, or review-collection behaviour changing. The one thing it is **not** safe to read it as is resolution — the final review in the corpus, `14437712673` (2026-08-17, au, 1★), is a full-length account of a **$131 journal purchase turning into $55/month**, which is the same complaint as review #11 from September 2023.

## 8.2 Immediate fix — put a cancel control in the app and on the web (§3.3, §5.4)

**Evidence:** `U:cancel_friction` = 51 (29.14%). `CANCEL_NO_PATH` = 27. `CANCEL_EMAIL_ONLY` = 12. **Zero** reviews in 1080 days describe a working in-app or web cancel control (§9.F). Charged after cancelling = 12; after deleting the account = 5.

**Recommendation:** a self-service **Cancel subscription** control in the app's account screen and on `/manage-subscription`, with an on-screen confirmation and a confirmation email. **Account deletion must end billing**, and deleting data must not lock out a period already paid for (`DEF_LOCKOUT`, `11101438874`).

**Why it is first among product changes:** it is the only change that deals with all of the top five 1★ drivers — no path, email-only, ignored requests, charges after cancellation, and charges after deletion.

## 8.3 Immediate fix — make the subscription an explicit, separate, opt-in line at checkout (§0.2, §0.3, §2.6)

**Evidence:** `BILL_TIED_TO_BOOK` = 43 (24.57%); `U:consent_failure` = 56 (32.00%); `BILL_NO_CONSENT` = 36, **every one a 1★**. One reviewer explicitly unselected the app and was billed (`13710163306`). Five describe the checkout itself as misleading (`BILL_CHECKOUT_TRICK`), including an upsell priced so it read as a delta (`13599501333`).

**Recommendation:**
- Show the book and the subscription as **separate lines**, each with its own amount, billing period and renewal date.
- Default the subscription to **off**. Where a book-only option exists, it must never produce a recurring charge.
- Show the **full total** on a final confirmation step before charging, and state any post-purchase upsell as an **additional** charge.
- Include the recurring terms in the **order confirmation email** — `12865205440`: "It's such a small change to your email template."

**The disclosure requests come from the people harmed** (`REQ_DISCLOSURE`, n=4, all 1★): `11018581118`, `12320369125`, `12437954281`, `12865205440`.

## 8.4 Immediate fix — send renewal notices and use a recognisable billing descriptor (§2.5, §3.2)

**Evidence:** `BILL_NO_NOTICE` = 11; `BILL_SURPRISE_CHARGE` = 17 (discovered from a bank or card statement); `BILL_UNKNOWN_DESCRIPTOR` = 5 (billed as *Effecto*, *Medical Score* or *Walking Yoga*); 7 reviewers blocked their card.

**Recommendation:** a pre-renewal email before every charge, naming the product, the amount and a one-click cancel link; and a card descriptor that reads **LASTING CHANGE**, not a sibling app's name or the legal entity's name.

## 8.5 Experiment — test whether refunding first-charge disputes lowers public damage (§0.5, §3.5)

**Evidence:** `REFUND_REFUSED` = 36 (all 1★); `REFUND_TERMS_SHIELD` = 12; `TRUST_ESCALATED` = 16; `TRUST_WARN_OTHERS` = 67 (38.29%) — the largest code in the corpus. Several reviewers asked for a refund **on the same day as the charge** (`11149347868`, `11533637208`, `13630518203`) and were still refused.

**Experiment:** for a defined window, refund any charge disputed within a short period of renewal and any charge made after a documented cancellation, then compare against the prior period: new 1★ volume on the listing, chargeback counts, and `TRUST_WARN_OTHERS`-type text. Reviews cannot measure the cost; internal payment data can. **This is framed as an experiment because the corpus cannot show refund economics — only that the current policy produces the most-repeated public warning.**

# PART 9 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 9.A Counting rules

1. **Denominator** is 175 (every review in `reviews.jsonl`) unless the sentence names a narrower one: US (75), a star band, an era, or the purchase-evidence segment (125). Segment rates are always shown next to the global count.
2. **Codes are non-exclusive.** One review may carry many codes (mean 5.29). **Unions count each review once.**
3. **Mean ★** for a code is the mean rating of the reviews carrying it.
4. **Signal band** is applied to the share of the stated denominator (§1.5). **Wilson 95% intervals** are given for headline shares.
5. **`META_LOW_INFO`** (body under 60 characters) and **`META_EDITED`** (`is_edited` true) are assigned mechanically from the source record, never by hand.
6. **Eras** are calendar thirds, not releases: E1 2023-09-02→2024-08-31, E2 2024-09-01→2025-08-31, E3 2025-09-01→2026-08-17.
7. **Allegation words are counted, not judged.** `TRUST_SCAM_WORD` records that a reviewer used the word.
8. **`PRICE_STATED`** counts amounts charged by or listed for this product only; one amount quoted about competitors (`11616551674`, "within $5") is excluded.
9. **No deduplication** was performed; none was needed (§1.3).

Nine check consistency between codes (e.g. `CANCEL_OK` never appears with `CANCEL_IGNORED`; every 5★ review with a refund refusal also carries `META_RATING_TEXT_MISMATCH`).

**Known residual error risk.** The judgement codes most exposed to reader disagreement are `BILL_UNDISCLOSED` vs `BILL_NO_CONSENT` (both may apply, and often do), `APP_GENERIC_NEG` vs `APP_SHALLOW`, and `OUT_EARLY_HOPEFUL` vs `PRAISE_GENERIC`.
