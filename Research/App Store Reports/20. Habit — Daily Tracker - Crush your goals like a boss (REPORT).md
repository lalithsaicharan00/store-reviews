### Five things to know before using any number here

1. **This corpus is not one product.** It is two: a beloved free habit tracker (Dec 2018 – 26 Jan 2021) and a monetised subscription app (27 Jan 2021 onward). Global percentages average across both and understate both. **Always read the era-relative rate**, given throughout.
2. **The global mean of 3.14 is an artefact of that split**, not a description of any user's experience. Pre-conversion mean = **4.56** (n=2,066). Post-conversion mean = **1.62** (n=1,982). Almost nobody experienced a "3-star" product.
3. **Volume is event-driven, not organic.** 61.5% of the corpus (2,491 reviews) falls in two windows: the Jan–Feb 2021 pricing conversion and the Mar 2025 data wipe. Review volume here measures *anger events*, not usage.
4. **The 2019 volume is partly manufactured.** The app shipped a review prompt whose decline button read *"let the developers be sad."* 26 reviews (0.64%) explicitly say the prompt is why they wrote. The true rate is certainly higher — this inflates 2019's 5★ count and is a disclosed selection bias.
5. **Theme counts are non-exclusive.** One review can carry many themes. 917 reviews (22.65%) carry no theme — these are overwhelmingly short pure-sentiment posts ("Отлично", "Great app") with a mean of 3.80; they are counted in all denominators but add no thematic signal.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**1. A single product decision destroyed this app, and the corpus dates it to the day.** On **27 January 2021** the developer converted a "pay once, yours forever" IAP into an annual subscription, **revoked the entitlements of everyone who had already paid**, and capped the previously-unlimited free tier at 3 habits. Daily review volume went from 2 (26 Jan) to 61 (27 Jan) to 135 (28 Jan). Mean rating for those days: **1.15–1.36**. **615 reviews (15.19%, high-priority)** describe losing a purchase they had already made.

**2. The thing they broke was the only thing that differentiated them.** Pre-conversion, the app's entire market position was *"the one habit tracker that gives you unlimited habits for free, with no subscription."* That is what 5★ reviewers said, unprompted, for two years. Capping the free tier at 3 habits didn't just annoy people — it deleted the product's reason to exist. 63 reviews (1.56%) say exactly this; 99 (2.45%) complain about the 3-habit cap specifically.

**3. The App Store listing still advertised the removed feature — for years.** Reviews from Jan 2021 through Sep 2023 quote the live listing verbatim: *"Unlimited amount of habits. You don't need to pay a penny."* 24 reviews (0.59%, emerging) call this false advertising by name. This is the cheapest fix in the report and it went unmade for over two years.

**4. The paid tier never delivered its headline feature.** The widget — the single most-cited reason people upgraded — did not work. **182 reviews (4.50%, very strong)** report a broken or absent widget; **113 of those (12.4% of all payers)** are from confirmed payers. It was still being reported broken in **2025**, five years after first report, with an in-app FAQ acknowledging it and offering "restart your phone" as the fix.

**5. Support does not exist.** 148 reviews (3.66%, very strong) report contacting support and getting nothing. Among payers the segment rate is **10.7%**. The in-app "Contact Us" link pointed at a dead email address for years; the Instagram account stopped posting. This converts every recoverable bug into a permanent 1★.

**6. In March 2025 they wiped everyone's data.** Version 1.41.0/1.42.x (13–15 Mar 2025) erased multi-year habit histories globally. **233 reviews (5.76%, high-priority)** report data loss; **126 of them (78.3% of that window)** land in the 7 weeks after 13 Mar 2025. Users reported losing 2, 3, 4 and 5 years of tracking. The claimed fix (1.42.1) did not work for many. Backup-to-iCloud was itself paywalled.

**7. There is an active billing-integrity problem, still live in 2026.** 28 reviews (0.69%, emerging by volume — **severe by nature**) report the app advertising a discounted subscription (e.g. $5.99, 499₽, 85% off) and then charging the **full** price ($39.99, 3,150₽). Reports run from Dec 2021 to Apr 2025 across gb/us/ru/de/ua/kz/ca/lt. Below threshold on volume; this is a consumer-protection exposure, not a UX nitpick.

**8. The product that people loved is fully documented and still buildable.** Strip the monetisation story out and the corpus contains an unusually clean specification for a habit tracker people were *delighted* by: minimal (27.45%), beautiful (13.27%), unlimited, ad-free, and — the genuine differentiator — a **non-streak "habit strength" percentage that decays instead of resetting** (0.94% explicit praise, but the highest-affection language in the corpus). That decay model was itself broken by the 2021 rewrite and never restored.

**The transferable lesson:** this is the most complete natural experiment in the category on what happens when you take back what you sold. The app went from a 4.56-mean darling to a 1.62-mean cautionary tale in one release, and never recovered in the following five years.

## 1.4 Processing method

1. Parsed all 4,048 records programmatically; reconciled against `manifest.json` and the per-country files.
2. **Read the full corpus.** All 4,048 reviews were read in date order in 22 batches (title + body, original language preserved — Russian, German, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Polish, Ukrainian, Italian, Turkish, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Vietnamese, Swedish, Dutch, Arabic and others read as-is, not machine-translated).
3. Built a theme taxonomy **from that reading**, not from a keyword guess-list.
4. Implemented a multilingual regex classifier (49 themes) covering English plus Russian, German, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Polish, Ukrainian, Italian and CJK phrasings for the high-volume themes.
5. **Validated by sampling.** Random samples were drawn per theme and manually checked. Three false-positive classes were found and fixed:
   - *Negated praise counted as complaint* — "it **doesn't** constantly bug me about premium" was scoring as nagging. Added a negation guard.
   - *Discount percentage alone counted as billing fraud* — "70% off" is not evidence of a mis-charge. Tightened to require an explicit price-mismatch clause.
   - *Praise-vs-grievance collision* — "one-time purchase" and "unlimited habits" appear in both 5★ praise and 1★ revocation complaints. Split into `P-onetime-praise` / `P-unlimited-free` (praise) and `M-onetime-model` / `N-unlimited-removed` (grievance), gated on rating and co-occurring revocation markers. Post-fix means moved to 4.83 and 4.94 for the praise classes — the expected direction.
6. Aggregated globally, by rating band, by storefront, by era, and by paid/non-paid cohort.

## 1.5 Known limitations

- **Recall is imperfect on low-volume themes.** Short non-English feature requests are the weakest area. Themes below ~0.5% should be read as *floors*, not exact counts. `N-percent-confusion` (0.49%) and `N-checks-disappear` (0.17%) are certainly undercounted — I saw more instances while reading than the classifier caught.
- **`_paid` is a text-evidence flag, not ground truth.** 908 reviews (22.43%) state or strongly imply payment. Real payer share is unknowable from this data. Never read it as a conversion rate.
- **Review-prompt selection bias (2019).** Disclosed in §"Five things", above.
- **Anger-event selection bias (2021, 2025).** Reviews cluster around incidents. Silent satisfied users are systematically absent, especially post-2021.
- **No version field.** Release attribution is inferential.
- **Storefront ≠ nationality ≠ language.** Many `ru` reviews are in English; many `us` reviews are in Spanish or Russian. Country here means *storefront*.
- **Survivorship in the tail.** After mid-2021, most people who would have complained had already left. E3/E4/E5 rates are computed on a shrinking, self-selected base.

## 2.2 The price ladder, as reviewers reported it

**Before 27 Jan 2021 — pay-what-you-want, one-time, non-crippling.** Three tiers, user's choice, all granting identical features. Amounts users report: **$2.99 / $4.99 / $14.99** (`4035547945`), **£4.99 / £6.98 / £14.99** (`4945935338`), €5.49 (`6922843265`), 449₽ / 490₽ / 500₽ (`6926974009`, `7290434920`, `6927207629`), ₹399 (`6956239325`), CHF 5 (`6172057750`).

This model was *itself* a marketing asset. `4945935338` (gb, 5★) describes the tiers as *"£4.99 to say thank you, £6.98 for liking the app and £14.99 for loving it and buy the developers a bottle of champagne."* 35 reviews (0.86%) praise the pricing model explicitly, mean **4.83**.

**After 27 Jan 2021 — annual subscription.** Full price as reported: **$39.99** (us), **£38.99** (gb), **€43.99/44/45** (de/es/nl), **CAD $52.99** (ca), **AUD $65.99/66** (au), **3,150₽ → 3,199₽** (ru), **HUF 15,500** (hu), **PLN ~150–200** (pl), **EGP 600+** (eg). Promo prices offered: $11.99, $19.99, $9.99, $5.99, £11.99, 900–970₽, 499₽, €13.49.

**The multiple is the finding.** For a US user the one-time price of $4.99 became $39.99 **per year** — a ~8× first-year increase and unbounded thereafter. Russian users went from 449₽ once to 3,150₽/year — **a 7× annual multiple**. 84 reviews (2.08%) quote the full annual price; 68 (1.68%) quote the old one-time price; both cohorts have a mean of ~1.5.

## 2.4 Dark-pattern mechanics reviewers describe

These are named repeatedly and specifically enough to treat as product facts, not impressions:

1. **The non-expiring countdown.** A "limited time offer" timer that resets on expiry, forever. Reported continuously Jan 2021 → Mar 2025. `6960081353` (gb): *"When the premium offer ending counter finishes it just resets back to 60 hours."* `7285176283`, `7159264561`, `7016202698`, `9598232878`. Part of the 225-review `N-aggressive-popup` cluster (5.56%, high-priority).
2. **Full-screen interstitial on every launch**, with a small/low-contrast dismiss control. `6927664461` (us) names it: *"Dark patterns make bad UX!"*
3. **Add-habit button routed to the paywall** — including after the user *deleted* a habit to make room. `7266229179`, `7090282099`, `8381455001`, `7266633441`. This is the mechanic that converted annoyance into uninstalls.
4. **Promo price advertised, full price charged** — §7 of the executive summary; 28 reviews.
5. **The review prompt** whose decline option was *"let the developers be sad"* — 26 reviews say it worked on them; `9069346040` and `7133029560` call it emotionally manipulative and dock stars for it.

## 3.2 The trust family dominates everything else

Union of `N-revoked-purchase` ∪ `N-restore-fail` ∪ `N-cancel-refund` ∪ `N-billing-mismatch` ∪ `N-false-advertising` ∪ `N-support-silent`:

**810 reviews = 20.01% of corpus, mean rating 1.39.**

For comparison, the union of every *feature-gap* theme (watch, iPad, sync, streaks, notes, categories, multi-per-day, weekday, export, archive, colours, compact, 7-day, bad habits) is **513 reviews = 12.67%, mean 3.57**.

**Trust complaints outnumber feature complaints by 1.58×, and carry 2.2 stars less.** This is the single most important structural fact in the corpus. This app did not fail on features. It failed on keeping promises.

## 3.4 What the product genuinely did well

These are the assets. They are real, they are large, and they survived until they were taken away.

**Minimalism — 1,111 reviews (27.45%), 42.8% of E1, mean 4.25.** The most-praised attribute by a wide margin. Reviewers consistently frame it *against* competitors: they had tried 5, 10, 20 trackers and found them cluttered or gamified. IDs: `3601977788`, `4423183878`, `4633148866`, `4836612463`, `5097711923`, `5946775063`, `6496027991`.

**Visual design — 537 (13.27%), 20.8% of E1, mean 4.16.** Colour-filling bubbles that grow with habit strength. Repeatedly described as the reason they *open* the app. IDs: `3940095679`, `4121063134`, `4497709123`, `4723686998`, `5202604486`, `5603920265`, `6430207073`.

**Unlimited habits, free — 54 explicit (1.33%), mean 4.94.** The highest-mean theme in the entire corpus. IDs: `3608816066`, `3895739282`, `3999790253`, `4184551703`, `4503098570`, `4723577547`, `4979659555`, `5006450649`, `5146953030`, `6272244857`.

**No ads — 49 (1.21%), mean 4.86.** IDs: `3601977788`, `3904267605`, `3980990590`, `4120408877`, `4321444813`, `4994394779`, `5272119146`, `5953213628`.

**The forgiving habit-strength model — 38 explicit (0.94%), mean 3.95.** The real differentiator, and the most intellectually interesting thing in the corpus. Users describe *why* it works: missing a day reduces your score instead of zeroing a streak, which prevents the "what the hell" abandonment spiral. `6179162507` (us, 5★): *"most focus too much on 'streaks'... missing one day and breaking my streak tends to send me into a spiral where I give up."* `7556821463` (de, 5★) explains it as logarithmic rather than linear and calls it *"much closer to psychological reality."* Also `4249750910`, `5548125389`, `6213055572`, `6815111095`, `7520606500`, `8294993952`.

**Custom reminder text — 25 (0.62%), mean 4.60.** Users write their own motivational line and get it back in the notification. Small feature, outsized affection. IDs: `3892496995`, `4035547945`, `4406637526`, `4760160029`, `5181431978`.

**Documented outcomes — 71 (1.75%), mean 4.17.** Real behaviour change: medication adherence, exercise, study, sobriety, hydration. `8294993952` (ru, 5★) is the most complete: a user credits the app with building the discipline that led to moving country, starting a relationship, and founding a company — *"and it all starts with 'make your bed every day'."*

# PART 4 — RATINGS ANALYSIS

## 4.1 The distribution is bimodal, and the middle is nearly empty

5★ 41.35% · 4★ 9.93% · 3★ 6.45% · 2★ 6.25% · 1★ **36.02%**. 77.4% of all reviews are at one extreme or the other. This is the signature of a product that was excellent and then did something to its users, not of a product with quality variance.

## 4.2 5★ — n = 1,674 (41.35%)

**What earns 5 stars:** minimalism (773 = 46.2% of band), design (346 = 20.7%), documented outcomes (52 = 3.1%), unlimited-free (51 = 3.0%), ad-free (46 = 2.7%).

**93% of 5★ reviews predate the conversion.** This band is essentially a photograph of E1.

**Two caveats.** First, 26 reviews in this band say outright that the review prompt is why they wrote — the true prompt-driven share is higher. Second, and more interesting: **23 reviews in the 5★ band carry `N-revoked-purchase`.** These are not contradictions — they are *amended* reviews. Users who were restored in Feb 2021 came back and raised their score: `6979960168`, `6997900077`, `7003059038`, `7004585831`, `7010504987`, `7024664717`, `7037625817`, `7040631651`.

## 4.3 4★ — n = 402 (9.93%) — the "one thing away" band

Minimalism (132) and design (76) still lead, but this band is defined by a **single named blocker**: streaks (22 = 5.5%), notes (20), multi-per-day (20), broken widget (18), categories (16), iPad (15), sync (15), Apple Watch (15).

This is the most commercially useful band in the corpus: **these people liked the product enough to rate it well and told you exactly what would have made it a 5.** Almost none of those things were ever built.

## 4.4 3★ — n = 261 (6.45%) — the ambivalence band

Where praise and grievance appear in the *same* review: minimalism 43 / subscription objection 41 / design 28 / pop-ups 26 / revocation 26 / broken widget 22. The characteristic 3★ shape is "I love this app, but…". `7041165859` (us) is the archetype: *"Both the Best and Most Annoying App I've Ever Used."*

## 4.5 2★ — n = 253 (6.25%) — the buyer's-remorse band

Subscription objection 64 (25.3%) / pop-ups 41 / revocation 34 / **broken widget 33 (13.0% — the band's peak)** / price 28. Note that design and minimalism still appear (28 and 32). These are people who *still like the app* and are rating it down on commercial conduct alone.

**Paying customers rated this product 2.06 stars lower than non-payers.** In a healthy product this relationship is inverted — payers are usually the most satisfied segment. Here, paying was the thing that exposed you to harm.

## 5.2 What made people pay — named purchase triggers

From reviews that state a reason:

1. **The widget** — by a wide margin the single most-named trigger. `6866660903`, `6898884130`, `6932683997`, `7516808714`, `9525119894`, `12141943563`, `12240298551`. It is also the feature that most often didn't work. **This is the corpus's central commercial irony.**

- **Price/model mismatch.** The most common stated position is *"I'd pay once; I won't rent."* `8537740813` (gb): *"I will NEVER EVER PAY A SUBSCRIPTION FOR THIS APP... If you were to make it a paid app, I would definitely purchase it."* Also `7470777218`, `9459852981`, `10684402507`.
- **Value not demonstrated before the ask.** `7410157966` (ru): *"first the consumer must see the value of the product. And what do you have? Every 2 seconds a 'subscribe' banner."* Also `7181761042`, `8260890448`, `12025762934`.
- **Reviews as deterrent.** People report reading the reviews and not installing: `7411209420`, `7874758944`, `8321275133`, `9909767891`, `12176789557`.
- **Feature parity failure.** By 2022+, competitors offered Watch apps, Home Screen widgets, Health integration and multi-daily tracking at lower prices. `12233815050` (ae): *"literally just a glorified reminder tool... overpriced and under delivers massively."*

## 5.5 Refund, cancellation and post-purchase problems

125 reviews (3.09%, mean **1.28**) concern refunds or cancellation. Three distinct failure modes:

1. **Refund requested, ignored.** Dominant in Feb 2021. Several report Apple refunding where the developer would not — `6943651180` (de), `6955346810` (us), `7039272649` (ca), `6937584026` (se).
2. **Cannot cancel.** Reported 2022–2025. `9371176438` (us) alleges the cancellation path breaks FTC transparency expectations; also `9511675705`, `9568655119`, `9583675777`, `10751290760`, `12425016323`, `9037646067`.
3. **Charged without intent.** Trial-to-paid surprises and accidental Touch-ID purchases during onboarding: `7199706726`, `7712053776`, `7744703720`, `8883167931`, `12463560595`.

**The Feb 2021 remediation, and why it was insufficient.** From ~5 Feb 2021 the developer began responding (users describe a form reply signed "Marlene"), and by ~15–17 Feb an update (users cite 1.14.1) made **Restore Purchases** work again. 30 reviews record restoration; many revised upward. But: (a) it took **three weeks** of daily 1★ inflow; (b) the initial remedy offered was *one free year*, not permanent restoration, which several users rejected as inadequate — `6958039907` (us): *"either update my purchases as originally agreed upon or refund what I paid"*; `6972336692`, `6969229255`; (c) it was never announced — `7026534551` (ca): *"4 stars after premium was restored, but not 5 stars because the devs ignored us for so long and have not acknowledged the issue"*; and (d) **it did not hold**. Revocation reports recur through 2021–2026: `7176715543` (nl, Apr 2021), `8632062766` (ca, May 2022), `10531496818` (ca, Oct 2023), `11696455228` (gb, Sep 2024), `12421451179` (ca, Mar 2025), `12999175131` (at, Aug 2025), `13669398619` (ru, Jan 2026).

## 6.5 Russia — n = 951, mean 3.28 (the corpus's biggest anomaly)

Russia is 23.5% of the corpus and rates the app **higher** than the US, UK, Germany or Australia despite the same product. Why:

1. **Timing.** Russian reviews are heavily weighted to 2019, when the app was free, ad-free and unlimited — and when the "let the developers be sad" prompt was actively harvesting reviews. Russian 5★ reviews are frequently one line: *"Отлично"*, *"Супер"*, *"Ничего лишнего"*.
2. **The widget complaint is almost absent — 0.5% vs 5.6% in the US.** Russian users largely did not buy the widget, so they were not exposed to its failure. This is a *purchase-exposure* difference, not a satisfaction difference.
3. **Price framing differs.** Russian objections are absolute, not comparative: 3,150₽/year is repeatedly called simply unaffordable or absurd for a checkbox app (`6925082543`, `6925476346`, `7183192208`, `7389399093`), rather than framed as a broken bargain.
4. **When Russians *were* exposed, they reacted identically.** Russian revocation complaints (75 reviews) are among the angriest in the corpus and dominate the most-upvoted list — the top two most-upvoted reviews in the entire corpus are Russian revocation complaints (`6918320995`, 39 net votes; `6919032834`, 27).

**Do not read Russia's 3.28 as evidence the product worked better there.** It reflects when those users arrived and what they bought.

## 7.3 Trend 2 — The free tier was gutted at the same moment `[very strong]`

Pre-conversion, unlimited habits was the product's headline. Post-conversion the cap is 3. `N-habit-cap-3` runs at **6.6% of E3**. It registers 0.48% in E1 (10 reviews), but manual inspection shows all ten are praise-context false positives — 5★ reviews thanking *this* app for **not** capping habits while describing competitors that do (`3918690923`, `4225873197`, `4226652382`, `5616772165`, `6169817900`). The genuine E1 rate is effectively zero. `N-unlimited-removed` (63 reviews) is entirely post-conversion.

Worse, the cap behaved as a **lifetime** cap, not a concurrent one: users who deleted a habit to make room could not add a replacement. `7266229179`, `7266633441`, `7090282099`, `7451472900`, `8381455001`, `7436424719`. This turned a pricing decision into a functional dead end.

## 7.4 Trend 3 — Aggressive monetisation UX arrived and never left `[very strong]`

`N-aggressive-popup`: 12.6% of E2 → 8.1% of E3. It registers 0.82% in E1 (17 reviews), but manual inspection shows these too are praise-context false positives — users praising the app for *not* nagging (`4152251500`, `4658115513`, `4907892894`, `6716937579`). The genuine E1 rate is effectively zero. The non-expiring countdown is reported from Jan 2021 to Mar 2025 — **over four years of a "limited time offer."** IDs across the span: `6920136171` (Jan 2021), `7130719014` (Mar 2021), `7696949663` (Aug 2021), `8774978626` (Jun 2022), `9598232878` (Feb 2023), `10403199089` (Sep 2023), `12548894139` (Apr 2025).

## 7.5 Trend 4 — The widget: promised 2020, broken through 2025, never fixed `[very strong]`

Timeline from the corpus:

- **Apr 2019** — a Today-view widget ships as a paid feature and works (`3995542358`, `4474121227`).
- **Sep–Dec 2020** — iOS 14 arrives; users ask for the new Home Screen widget; the listing/store screenshots show one. Purchases begin *specifically* for it (`6460660006`, `6744055213`, `6777922510`).
- **Jan 2021 onward** — the widget is broken or absent for large numbers of payers. The in-app FAQ's first question addresses it; the fix offered is "restart your phone." It doesn't work.
- **2022–2025** — still reported: `8625708859` (May 2022), `9365771044` (Dec 2022), `10034018726` (Jun 2023), `11099954846` (Mar 2024), `12456466846` (Mar 2025).
- **Clarification found in the corpus**: even when present, it was the *Today-view* widget, not a Home Screen widget — `9954684066` (us, 2023) and `9400779376` (us, 2022) both bought expecting Home Screen placement.

**182 reviews, mean 1.94, five years, no fix.** This is the clearest sustained delivery failure in the report.

## 7.6 Trend 5 — Support degraded from responsive to absent `[very strong]`

In 2019 the developer answered reviews and fixed reported bugs, and users noticed: `6319774608` (us) documents a bug being fixed *and* the developer following up; `4702811610` (ru) changed a rating after a developer reply; `6560591296` (cn) praises a same-day fix in Oct 2020.

By 2021 that is gone. `N-support-silent` runs **0.05% of E1 (a single review) → 6.6% of E2 → 8.0% of E3 → 13.0% of E4**. From 2022, users report the in-app "Contact Us" pointing to a **dead email address** with an autoresponder telling them to write elsewhere (`8651373725`, `11655625375`, `11590913646`, `9623996845`), and the Instagram account — the app's other stated support channel — dormant since 2019 (`6937097883`, `11408456954`).

# PART 8 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation ties to a numbered finding above. Split into fixes, product bets, and research.

## 8.1 Immediate — integrity (days, not sprints)

**F1. Honour every legacy entitlement, permanently and verifiably.** (→ §3.2, §5.3, §7.2)
615 revocation reports, mean 1.37, 52% of payers. The Feb 2021 patch was partial and did not hold — recurrences run to Jan 2026. Restore on the *receipt*, not on local state, and make it survive reinstall and device change. Publish a note saying it is done.

**F2. Fix the App Store listing.** (→ §3.1 #39)
The listing promised "unlimited habits… without paying a penny" for at least two years after that stopped being true. 24 reviews (mean **1.21**) call it out. Zero engineering cost.

**F3. Kill the non-expiring countdown and the launch interstitial.** (→ §2.4, §7.4)
225 reviews. A four-year "limited time offer" is not a growth tactic; it is a documented reason people uninstalled. Cap the paywall at one dismissible prompt per session with a durable "no."

**F4. Audit the promo-price-to-charge path.** (→ §3.1 #33)
28 reports of advertised-discount-then-full-price, spanning Dec 2021 → Apr 2025, across 8 storefronts. Low volume, high exposure. Reproduce it or prove it cannot happen.

**F5. Stand up a support channel that answers.** (→ §4.7)
148 reports, mean 1.33 — versus mean 3.63 for people who *were* helped. Fix the dead "Contact Us" address first; even an autoresponder with a real queue behind it is worth ~2 stars per incident.

**F6. Make backup automatic, free, and continuous.** (→ §7.7)
Paywalling the recovery path for a data-loss event caused by your own update is the single most damaging decision in the 2025 window. Automatic daily local + iCloud snapshots, restore available to free users. Ship regression tests on the migration path — the same class of bug fired in Jan 2021 and again in Mar 2025.

**F7. Make the free tier viable again — and at minimum fix the delete-then-add dead end.** (→ §7.3)
The 3-habit cap behaving as a lifetime cap is almost certainly a bug, and it converts a pricing choice into a broken app. Separately: 3 is below the threshold of usefulness for this category. 7–10 free habits with paid depth elsewhere is the defensible line.

## 8.2 Near-term product — earn the paid tier

**P1. Ship the widget, properly, or stop selling it.** (→ §7.5)
182 reviews, five years, the #1 named purchase trigger. Ship a real **Home Screen** widget (iOS 14+) with direct check-off. If it cannot be shipped, remove it from the paywall copy and screenshots immediately and refund on request.

**P2. Add a streak counter *alongside* the percentage.** (→ §3.5)
71 requests, mean 3.72 — and critically, nobody asks to *replace* the percentage. Users want both: the streak for the daily dopamine, the strength score for the honest long-run picture.

**P3. Explain the percentage.** (→ §3.5)
20 classified (undercounted). A one-screen explainer — "a weekly habit completed once shows 4% because the score measures *strength over ~91 repetitions*, not this week's completion" — plus an optional "this period" toggle. Cheapest satisfaction win available.

**P4. Multiple check-ins per day.** (→ §3.5) 54 requests. Water, medication, brushing teeth are the canonical use cases and are named repeatedly. This is table stakes against Streaks and Productive.

**P5. Specific weekdays, not just "N times in M days."** (→ §3.5) 22 requests, plus a large share of the notification complaints. "Gym Mon/Wed/Fri" is not expressible today.

**P6. Notes per day.** (→ §3.5) 79 requests, the single most-requested feature in the corpus, mean 3.66.

**P7. Categories/folders + a compact row option.** (→ §3.5) 58 + 26 requests. These co-occur: both are what happens when a power user exceeds ~8 habits.

**P8. Apple Watch app.** (→ §3.5) 62 requests at mean **4.15** — asked for by the happiest users, and named explicitly in churn-to-Streaks reviews.

## 8.3 Restore the actual differentiator

**D1. Fix the habit-strength decay.** (→ §3.4, `7303014090`, `7330745927`, `7641636642`, `8231849401`, `7464605501`)
Users report that after 2021 the percentage **stopped decaying** — habits sat at 100% after weeks of non-completion. The forgiving-but-decaying model was this product's one genuine intellectual differentiator and the thing its most articulate advocates loved. It broke, and nobody fixed it. `7303014090` (us) begs for it back: *"Please bring that algorithm back!"*

This is the highest-leverage product item in the report, because it is the only feature no competitor has.

## 8.4 Monetisation — repackage around what the corpus actually values

The evidence says three things clearly:

1. **This audience will pay.** They paid voluntarily, at self-chosen prices, when nothing forced them to — and 35 of them wrote 4.83-mean reviews praising the model.
2. **This audience will not rent a checkbox.** The stated willingness-to-pay clusters at **$5–15 one-time**, or **$10–15/year** at the very top end. $39.99/year is rejected in every market, in every language, for seven years.
3. **The price is not the objection — the *broken bargain* is.** Spain, which never lost anything, complains about price (16.9%) but barely about revocation (2.8%). The high-spend markets, which did lose something, complain about revocation at 19.3%.

**Recommendation:** offer a **lifetime unlock** alongside any subscription, priced in the $15–25 band, and grandfather every legacy purchaser into it free. Multiple users say explicitly they would buy this — `7470777218`, `8537740813`, `8053917590`, `9459852981`. It converts the corpus's loudest grievance into the corpus's most-praised historical asset.

## 8.5 What a competitor entering this category gets free from this corpus

1. **The positioning gap is wide open.** "Unlimited habits, one-time price, no subscription" was a winning position that this product abandoned. 26 reviewers walked to Streaks specifically for it.
2. **The forgiving-decay model is unclaimed** and is the most emotionally resonant mechanic in the corpus — particularly for ADHD and perfectionist users, who describe streak-resets as actively harmful (`6179162507`, `6815111095`, `11139240840`).
3. **The feature checklist is written for you:** Home Screen widget, Watch app, streak *and* strength, multi-daily check-ins, weekday scheduling, notes, categories, automatic free backup, iPad. That is roughly 400 reviews of specification.
4. **Trust is the moat here, not features.** A visible, honest pricing promise plus a support address that answers would outperform any feature in this category.

## 8.6 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

- What share of the installed base actually paid, and what share churned versus stayed silent? (Reviews systematically over-represent the angry.)
- Did the subscription conversion increase revenue? It plainly destroyed reputation; the corpus says nothing about the P&L.
- Did the Feb 2021 restoration reach everyone, or only those who complained loudly? Recurrence reports through 2026 suggest the latter, but this cannot be established from reviews.
- Why did the widget fail? Every user-side report is consistent with an entitlement/extension bug, but no diagnostic data exists here.
- What actually happened in March 2025 — migration bug, backend change, or entitlement reset? Users describe symptoms, not causes.
- Is the product still maintained? The last substantive review activity is thin, and 2026 volume (12 reviews) is too small to say.

## 9.3 Validation approach and known error risk

**Method.** After full-corpus reading, a regex classifier was applied and then sampled per theme (10–18 reviews each for the top themes, drawn at random with a fixed seed). Three false-positive classes were identified and corrected (§1.4 step 5).

**Known residual error:**

- **Recall is the weak side, not precision.** Post-fix samples for `N-revoked-purchase`, `N-data-loss`, `N-widget-broken`, `N-habit-cap-3` and `N-support-silent` were clean. Low-volume feature themes in non-English text are under-counted.
- **`N-percent-confusion` (20) and `N-checks-disappear` (7) are firmly undercounted** — I observed more instances during reading than the classifier catches. Treat as floors.
- **`X-widget-mention` (128) is a deliberate abstain bucket**, not a null result. Some of those are genuine defects that lacked an unambiguous negative marker; the true `N-widget-broken` figure is therefore **≥182**, and plausibly closer to 250.
- **`P-simple` and `P-design` are broad by construction** — they capture a family of praise, not a single claim. Their value is directional (what people liked), not precise.
- **Praise-context false positives survive in two themes' pre-2021 tail.** `N-habit-cap-3` and `N-aggressive-popup` each register a small E1 count (10 and 17) that manual inspection shows to be 5★ reviews praising *this* app for lacking a cap or lacking nagging, usually by contrast with competitors. The negation guard (§1.4) catches the English forms but misses comparative English and Russian constructions (*«не выскакивает»*, *«ненавязчивые»*). Global counts for these two themes are therefore overstated by roughly 10 and 17 respectively; both remain correctly signposted in their post-2021 concentration, which is where the finding lives.
- **The 0% claims in Part 7 are stated as "effectively zero" rather than zero** for exactly this reason.
- **`_paid` (908) is a floor**, and is biased toward payers who were unhappy enough to explain that they had paid.

## 9.12 The one-paragraph version

Habit — Daily Tracker was, for two years, the best-loved free habit tracker on the App Store: minimal, beautiful, ad-free, unlimited, and built on a genuinely original non-streak "habit strength" model that forgave missed days. It monetised through voluntary pay-what-you-want donations and its users adored it for that — mean rating 4.56 across 2,066 reviews. On **27 January 2021** it converted to a $39.99/year subscription, revoked the entitlements of everyone who had already paid, and capped the free tier at 3 habits. Reviews went from 2 a day to 135, and the mean went from 4.29 to 1.15 in 24 hours. It never recovered. Over the following five years it added no meaningful features, never shipped the widget it kept selling, stopped answering support entirely, ran a "limited time offer" countdown that reset for four years, and in **March 2025** shipped an update that erased years of user data — with the backup that would have saved it locked behind the same paywall. **810 reviews (20.0%) are about broken trust; only 513 (12.7%) are about missing features.** The product was never the problem.
