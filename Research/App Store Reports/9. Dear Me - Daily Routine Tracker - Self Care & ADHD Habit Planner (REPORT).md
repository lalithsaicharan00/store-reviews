### ⚠️ Read these three things before anything else

**1. This is a hard-paywall product, and money is the single biggest thing reviewers talk about.** 434 of 1,281 reviews (33.88%, HIGH-PRIORITY) contain a monetization theme, with a mean rating of **2.13**. The listing price is Free **[external]**, but the app gates the core loop behind a "Plus Membership" subscription with five price points ($6.99 / $16.99 / $27.99 / $39.99 / $79.99) **[external]** and, per the corpus, offers **no free trial in most storefronts**.

**2. The public star rating is ~1.35 stars higher than what people actually write, and 13 reviews explain why.** Across the eight storefronts checked, the public rating averages **4.86** on ~40,555 ratings **[external]**, while written reviews in the same storefronts average **3.51**. Thirteen reviewers (1.01%, MEANINGFUL) state that the app asked them for a rating *during onboarding, before they had used it* — several of them left 5★ while writing negative text. See §2.6 and §5.6.

**3. The app has a severe, geographically concentrated reliability failure.** 66 reviews (5.15%, HIGH-PRIORITY) report the app loading for minutes, hanging, or never loading. **52 of those 66 are Russian** — 46.02% of all RU reviews, versus 1.20% of all non-RU reviews. RU's written mean is 2.38 against a public 4.65. The v1.1.46 release notes (3 Sep 2026) read *"Things just got a whole lot faster! Enjoy quicker loading"* **[external]** — the developer knows.

# EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**Dear Me is a well-loved product wrapped in an acquisition funnel that is actively destroying its own reputation, sold to a paying cohort that rates it 1.94 stars.**

**Seven findings that should change decisions:**

1. **Paying customers are the angriest users in the corpus.** 226 reviews (17.64%) contain explicit purchase evidence. Their mean rating is **1.94** and **61.1% of them give one star**. They do not complain about price — only 8.4% of the paid cohort mentions price. They complain about refunds (43.8%), regret (22.1%), the app being "just a checklist" (20.8%), cancellation (13.7%) and the app not loading (12.4%). *(§4)*

2. **"Let me look before you charge me" is the single largest actionable theme.** Deduplicated, **159 reviews (12.41%)** ask for a trial, object to the hard paywall, object to the late price reveal, or object to upsell pressure. Their mean is **1.79**. Several state they would have bought. Meanwhile 28 reviewers report the free tier is *already* usable — the paywall's perceived height exceeds its actual height. *(§0.2, §1.4, §7.2)*

3. **The public 4.86★ rating is not a satisfaction signal.** Written reviews across the same eight storefronts average **3.51** — a +1.35 gap. Thirteen reviews document being asked to rate the app *during sign-up, before use*; nine five-star reviews carry entirely negative text, one explaining *"I'm giving five stars so my review appears at the top."* This is also an App Store guideline exposure. *(§2.6)*

4. **Russia is a market in structural failure and nobody would see it in the star rating.** 46.0% of Russian reviews report the app loading for minutes or not at all; 56.6% report some defect; 5.3% say it only works over a VPN. RU written mean is **2.38** against a public 4.65. And 31.9% of Russian reviewers **paid** before discovering this. *(§0.7, §5.1-RU)*

5. **The app markets a coach and ships a checklist, and reviewers name the exact moment they notice.** `G-thin` is 99 reviews (7.73%) at a **1.45** mean — the worst of any major theme. The mechanism is specific: an animated guided-breathing exercise runs once during onboarding and is never available again. 21 reviews in six languages describe it; several call it false advertising and one says it was the reason they subscribed. *(§0.5, §7.3)*

6. **Upsell pressure is the only complaint getting monotonically worse.** `M-nag` runs 2.16% → 4.84% → **10.23%** across 2024 / 2025 / 2026. Over the same window refunds, "just a checklist" and the money-back-guarantee complaint all *fell*. The funnel is being tuned harder while the product improves — and the funnel is now the fastest-growing source of one-star reviews. *(§6.5)*

7. **Two safety issues deserve attention regardless of volume.** The app administers depression and ADHD self-assessments in a 4+-rated Health & Fitness product; one reviewer reports it returned a benign result for a suicidal friend, another calls the ADHD test needless scaremongering at teenagers. Separately, the 2026 AI coach "Mimi" has a free message cap, and one reviewer describes being cut off mid-crisis: *"I was almost having a collapse… the chat limit came and I couldn't continue."* *(§0.6, §6.6)*

**What is genuinely working, and must be protected:** 106 reviews say it organises their day and 101 report a real life change, both at a **4.92** mean. Motivation (32 reviews) and ADHD/mental-health benefit (16 reviews) are the only two themes in the corpus with a **perfect 5.00 mean and zero reviews below five stars**. Turkish localization is a named reason people chose this app over a competitor. The warmth is the moat.

**Two dated regressions the star rating never showed:** a November 2024 onboarding freeze that locked German and French users out of the app entirely (10 reviews in one month), and an April 2025 change that broke chronological task sorting across four countries in the same week. Both appear to have been fixed. Both were detectable within days from written reviews.

**One thing that already got fixed, and is worth saying out loud:** an advertised "3-month money-back guarantee" generated 17 complaints between July 2024 and February 2025 and then vanished from the corpus entirely. Over the same period, refund complaints fell from 9.70% to 3.98%. Removing a promise the business could not keep measurably reduced the worst category of complaint.

**The prioritised ask:** fix Russia's backend; remove the rating prompt from sign-up; build a real support and refund path; ship a genuine trial in every storefront; cap the upsell at one interstitial per session; and make the breathing exercise re-runnable. The first three are damage control. The last three are the growth thesis.

## 0.1 The shape of this corpus is unusual and it matters

A 51.37% five-star share alongside a 26.23% one-star share is a **bimodal** distribution, not a normal one. There is almost no middle: 4★+3★+2★ together are only 22.40% of the corpus. The corpus contains two nearly disjoint populations:

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Reviews carrying at least one money theme | 434 | 33.88% | 2.13 | Paywall, refunds, no trial, upsell pressure |
| Reviews carrying at least one defect theme | 175 | 13.66% | 2.01 | Loading, crashes, onboarding freeze, login |
| Reviews carrying at least one capability-gap theme | 244 | 19.05% | 2.46 | Thin content, can't edit/delete, frequency limits |
| Reviews with no classified theme (short pure praise or noise) | 339 | 26.46% | — | "Çok güzel", "Muito bom", emoji-only |

Of the 339 unclassified reviews, **318 are 5★** — overwhelmingly one- to four-word praise ("Süper", "Amei", "⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️"). This is a large, low-information positive mass sitting next to a large, highly articulate negative mass. Both are real; only one of them tells you what to build.

## 0.3 The refund pipeline is the app's largest reputational liability

`M-refund` is 102 reviews (7.96%, HIGH-PRIORITY) with a mean of **1.73**. This is not a pricing complaint — it is a **support and policy-execution failure**, and it clusters tightly:

- **43.8% of the paid cohort** (99 of 226 reviewers with explicit purchase evidence) mention a refund. That is a segment rate; the global count is 102.
- **57.8% of all `M-refund` reviews are Turkish** (59 of 102), which is 12.53% of all TR reviews.
- 17 reviews (1.33%) specifically cite an advertised **money-back guarantee** that was not honoured. Every one of those 17 was written between **Jul 2024 and Feb 2025** — and none after. The claim appears to have been withdrawn from the funnel around Feb 2025, which is itself a documented product change (see §8.4).
- 34 reviews (2.65%) report that **cancellation itself** failed or was unclear, and 16 (1.25%) report being charged an **amount different from what was displayed** (`11539871453`·TR·4 annual shown as ₺199.99, more taken; `12264639015`·SA·2 "subscribed at 49, they took 114"; `13966657008`·US·2 "supposed to pay 39.99 but it charged me 56"; `12221358308`·FR·1 double-charged on a plan switch).
- 25 reviews (1.95%) report **no support response at all** (`X-support`, mean 1.52) — several after multiple emails (`12178773869`·DE·1, `12571528769`·DE·2, `12754744101`·MX·1, `13860369435`·EE·1).

Many of these people are asking for help *in a public review* because they could not find in-app support: `11695609359`·TR·3 explicitly asks for an in-app support section instead of an email address; `12901421019`·RU·1 writes *"Не увидела нигде никаких контактов службы поддержки, поэтому пришлось писать как отзыв"* — "I couldn't find any support contacts anywhere, so I had to write this as a review."

## 0.5 "It's just a checklist" is the #1 product criticism, and it has a specific cause

The most likely reading is a backend/CDN reachability problem in Russia rather than device-side slowness, and it has been degrading since mid-2025 while RU reviewers continue to pay: **36 of 113 RU reviews (31.9%) carry explicit purchase evidence.**

## 4.3 What triggers a purchase — stated by buyers themselves

Purchase triggers are recoverable from the corpus in five recognisable patterns:

1. **A social/video ad promising a capability.** `12633273226`·TR·1 (*"Reklamlarına aldanarak… bir yıllık üyelik satın aldım"*), `11789743896`·US·1 ("the Facebook video I saw"), `12755295856`·CA·1 (an ad promising MBTI-based routines), `11718643683`·TR·5 ("Sosyal medyadan birkaç yorumun gazına gelip… 1 senelik paket aldım"), `14134826933`·RU·1 ("повелась на рекламу"), `13613553985`·TR·1, `13810246956`·TR·4, `12181667454`·BR·4.
2. **The end-of-quiz "your plan is ready" moment.** `12286738501`·MX·1 describes it precisely: *"después de la última pregunta dicen que tu plan ya está listo, solo tienes que realizar un pago de $700 pesos."* Also `11472460295`·TR·1, `12137044359`·US·1, `12407060265`·CA·2.
3. **The −50% "newcomer discount" timer.** `12755295856`·CA·1 bought "with the newcomer discount"; `12966178467`·DK·1 was offered 50% off 129 DKK; `12650182347`·RU·1 paid with the 50% discount. `11777093103`·FR·1 reports the discount screen itself completed an Apple Pay purchase while they were trying to dismiss it for the *n*th time.
4. **The onboarding breathing demo.** `12645661722`·FR·2 states the subscription was bought *for that feature*. `12238297592`·IT·4 bought premium to evaluate the interactive features seen in the ad.
5. **Genuine early delight.** The healthy path, and it exists: `11805353050`·SA·5 — *"كتبته من أول دقيقة بالتطبيق… لدرجة دفعت حق سنة مباشرة بدون ما أفكر"* (paid for a year within the first minute, without thinking), `14309372488`·TR·5, `12769176601`·RU·5 (*"Первое приложение которое захотелось оплатить"* — the first app I ever wanted to pay for), `13005854395`·TR·4, `13111037970`·RU·5.

Triggers 1–4 are all **pre-experience** triggers. Trigger 5 is the only post-experience one, and it is the rarest. That is the structural reason the paid cohort skews so negative: the funnel is engineered to convert before the product can prove itself, so the product has to be *retroactively* good enough to justify a purchase already made.

## 4.5 Refund and churn mechanics

Four distinct failure modes, all documented:

1. **The advertised guarantee was not executed** (17 reviews, Jul 2024–Feb 2025). See §1.2.
2. **The in-app refund path is a dead end.** `11567513705`·TR·1: *"para iadesi istedim, boş bir kutucuk çıktı"* — I requested a refund and got an empty box. `11480287005`·TR·3 and `11912344269`·TR·1 could not find the option at all. `13065540266`·FR·1: *"impossible de contacter le SAV simplement et de prendre son droit de rétractation"* — invoking the EU right of withdrawal was impossible.
3. **Cancelling stops renewal but does not refund, and users do not understand that.** `11936253199`·TR·5, `12133402020`·TR·5, `11895224084`·BR·1 (*"Se cancelar é apenas para não renovar depois de um ano"*), `12652692645`·TR·3, `13477378855`·TR·5. Many of these are neutral or positive-rated people asking a question in public because they had nowhere else to ask.
4. **Billing errors.** Double charges on plan switches (`12221358308`·FR·1, `11935551837`·TR·1), charges after deletion (`13610891124`·BR·1), a subscription the user says they never authorised (`13565016167`·TR·1), and 16 `M-payfail` cases where money left the account but Plus never activated (`11687224311`·TR·1, `12442228880`·RU·1, `12650182347`·RU·1, `12791514383`·IN·1, `12609778626`·FR·1, `13711157656`·DE·1).

**Support is the amplifier.** 21 of the 25 `X-support` reviews are from paying customers. `12571528769`·DE·2: *"Trotz mehrmaligen Kontaktieren des Supports, erhielt ich keine Rückmeldung. Mein Geld umsonst rausgeworfen!"*

**Counter-evidence worth noting:** 4 reviews (`P-support`) show the support loop working, and one is a documented recovery. `12745580868`·TR·4 originally reported losing their subscription, then *edited the review*: *"Yanıtınıza istinaden uygulamayı tekrar açtım… abonelik hakkım kullanılabilir hale gelmiş, teşekkürler."* Also `12827787443`·TR·5, `13534159613`·RU·2 (refunded via Apple), `13622846328`·US·5. Eleven reviews in the corpus are marked `is_edited` — evidence that developer replies do change minds when they land.

## 4.6 The honest limits of this section

- Review text cannot yield a conversion rate, a renewal rate, a churn rate, or a refund rate. Nothing here should be read as one.
- The paid cohort (226) is negatively selected: satisfied subscribers have little reason to write. The 25 five-star paying reviewers are real but almost certainly under-represented.
- `PAID` classification required explicit textual evidence. Reviewers who paid but did not say so are counted as unpaid, so 226 is a **floor**, not an estimate of the paying population.

# PART 6 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 6.1 Method

The corpus has no `version` field, so trends are computed on `date` only. Three periods are used, chosen to align with the store's release history **[external: first release 27 Jun 2024; current 1.1.46 on 3 Sep 2026]**:

- **P1 — Launch, 14 May 2024 → 31 Dec 2024** (n = 464)
- **P2 — Scale, 2025** (n = 641)
- **P3 — Recent, 1 Jan → 4 Sep 2026** (n = 176)

Half-year buckets are used where the quarterly shape matters. No trend is claimed where the underlying count falls below 5 in a period.

| Period | n | mean ★ | 1★ | 5★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 2024 | 464 | 3.567 | 26.1% | 52.8% |
| P2 2025 | 641 | 3.429 | 27.6% | 49.0% |
| P3 2026 | 176 | 3.648 | 21.6% | 56.2% |

Headline ratings are almost flat. The composition underneath is not.

## 6.3 Trend 2 — Two dated regressions, each visible and each apparently fixed

**(a) The Nov 2024 onboarding freeze.** `B-onboard` = 21 reviews total, and **10 of them were written in Nov 2024 alone** (2.4% of all P1 reviews), concentrated in DE (4) and FR (4), with CH, IT, BR and TR also hit. They describe the same specific screen: a question about procrastination whose "continue" control was off-screen and unscrollable. `11953340146`·DE·5 and `11987084380`·DE·1 both describe *"das Bild mit der Frau und dem Kleiderschrank"* — the picture of the woman and the wardrobe. `11982010915`·FR·1 notes an update did not fix it and identifies an iPhone 6. Reports fall to 9 in all of 2025 and 1 in 2026.

**(b) The Apr 2025 sort-order regression.** `B-order` = 5 reviews, **4 of them in Apr 2025**, across four storefronts: `12548475613`·IN·3, `12571528769`·DE·2, `12571797731`·TR·2, `12577110521`·MX·2. All describe the same change: tasks that used to appear in chronological order started appearing alphabetically or randomly. `12571528769`·DE·2 is an annual subscriber who says it persisted ~6 weeks with no support reply. One late recurrence (`13915239677`·US·1, Apr 2026).

Both are examples of a **cross-market regression that reviewers detect within days**. Neither would have been visible in the star rating.

## 6.4 Trend 3 — The money-back guarantee was quietly withdrawn, and the refund complaint rate halved

`M-guarantee` appears 17 times between Jul 2024 and Feb 2025, then **never again**. `M-trialtrap` follows the same shape (7 in P1, 3 in P2, 0 in P3). `M-refund` overall falls 9.70% → 7.80% → 3.98%.

Two readings are consistent with the evidence, and the corpus supports the second:
- The refund process improved; or
- **The guarantee claim was removed from the purchase flow**, so fewer people bought expecting a refund they would not get.

`12017205465`·DE·1 (Dec 2024) is the pivot: *"Außerdem wird von der Geld-zurück-Garantie nichts mehr erwähnt"* — the guarantee is no longer mentioned. This is a real, dated, positive product-integrity change. **The single highest-severity monetization complaint in the corpus's first year has been eliminated at source.**

`M-nag` has quintupled. In P3, one in ten written reviews complains about upsell pressure. This is the clearest evidence in the corpus of a funnel being tuned harder over time — and, given that `M-refund` and `G-thin` both fell over the same period, **it is the theme that is currently eroding sentiment fastest.** The 2026 reviews are almost formulaic about it: `13808808716`·RU·1, `13810012694`·RU·1, `13813415532`·RU·1, `13917372875`·RU·2, `14276958495`·RU·1, `14413735582`·RU·1, `14451743287`·RU·1, `14497068096`·RU·1, `14503775552`·RU·1, `13753570537`·TR·1 (*"an excellent example of how to show tons of different paywall pages to users and don't let them use the app"*), `14139653785`·TR·1, `13776099933`·DE·3, `13675689954`·IT·2.

## 6.6 Trend 5 — New in 2026: an AI coach, and a safety question with it

`X-ai` = 5 reviews, **all in 2026** (0.39% globally, but 2.84% of P3 — a MEANINGFUL rate within its period). An AI companion called **Mimi** was added, with a hard free message cap:
- `14302044695`·TR·4: *"uygulamadaki Mimi'ye sadece 1 şey sorabiliyoruz, daha sonra pro falan diyor"* — you can only ask Mimi one thing, then it asks for Pro.
- `13810246956`·TR·4: *"Koç ile konuşma sınırımız artsın (bence iyi dertleşiliyor)"* — please raise the coach chat limit; it's genuinely good to talk to.
- `13860369435`·EE·1: the beaver appeared in an update, offers to chat, and cannot be dismissed.
- `14084062489`·BR·3 asks for voice input so an AI can add tasks to the schedule.
- `14507626498`·BR·1 is the one that matters: *"eu estava em um momento muito difícil, fui falar com a mimi e o chat atingiu o limite… eu estava com quase tendo um colapso… estou até agora desabando de choro."*

**Two reviewers say Mimi is genuinely valuable. One reviewer was cut off mid-crisis by a monetization cap.** Rate-limiting an emotional-support conversation, in a 4+ app marketed for ADHD and self-care, is a product-safety decision, not a pricing one.

## 6.7 Trend 6 — Seasonality

New-Year seasonality is real and large. Jan–Feb 2025 alone produced **239 reviews (18.7% of the corpus in two months)**; Jan 2026 produced 47 against a 2026 monthly mean of ~20. The corpus's peak month is Feb 2025 (131). Ratings in the January spikes are not worse than baseline (2025 Q1 mean 3.59 vs corpus 3.51), so the spike is volume, not sentiment — but it means **any funnel change should be measured against a January baseline, not a calendar one**, and that a January release is the highest-exposure moment of the year.

## 6.8 Trends explicitly NOT claimed

- No claim about `G-guide`, `G-freq`, `G-noedit` or any request theme over time — counts per period fall below 10 and the movement is inside noise.
- No claim that the 2026 rating recovery is caused by any specific change. P3's n is 176 and its composition (fewer TR, more RU) differs from P1/P2.
- No claim about `B-ime` beyond "reported in Aug 2024, never again" — 7 reviews, one month, one storefront.

## 7.2 The single highest-leverage change: let people see the product before they pay

This is one recommendation with three parts, and it is supported by more evidence than anything else in the corpus.

**The evidence.** `M-notrial` 52 reviews (4.06%) + `M-wall` 75 (5.85%) + `M-latereveal` 18 (1.41%) + `M-nag` 59 (4.61%). Deduplicated, **159 reviews (12.41%) say some version of "let me look before you charge me."** Their combined mean is **1.79**. Meanwhile 28 reviewers (`P-free`) report the free tier is *already* good enough to be useful — meaning the paywall's perceived height exceeds its actual height. And the demand is loudest in the highest-spend markets (`M-notrial` 7.2% in the high-spend group vs 4.06% globally).

**Part A — Ship a real trial, everywhere.** Not 24 hours; the reviews ask for 3 days to 1 week (`11854298993`·DE·4 "3 Tage", `12523888830`·TR·4 "1 haftalık", `12636034921`·TR·1 "1-2 gün", `12683497729`·DE·2 "1-2 Wochen"). The storefront inconsistency documented by `11836191831`·SA·3 should be eliminated — a trial in the US and none in Saudi Arabia is worse than no trial anywhere, because reviewers compare notes in public.

**Part B — Cap the upsell at one interstitial per session, and add "don't show again".** `M-nag` has gone 2.16% → 4.84% → 10.23%. `12360368631`·US·3 asks for the exact control. Reviewers are counting the pop-ups (3, 4, 6, 10) and quoting the number in one-star reviews.

**Part C — State the free/paid split on a single screen.** 19 `M-unclear` reviews cannot tell what Plus adds; `11878902945`·TR·5 — a product manager writing unsolicited structured feedback — points out that the premium page is one slogan and the profile membership page is blank. `13021325704`·US·2: *"there is no info on what is actually included in the pro subscription — I can't find that info anywhere."*

**Expected effect.** This does not necessarily raise revenue per install. It targets the 30 `M-scam` reviews (mean 1.20), the 50 `M-regret` reviews, and the 102 `M-refund` reviews — i.e. it should reduce refunds and one-star volume, and it converts the "I'd pay if I could look first" cohort (55 reviews) that currently converts at zero.

## 7.4 Positioning — protect what already works

Do not lose these while fixing the above:

- **"It organises my day" and "it changed my life" are the product.** 106 + 101 reviews at 4.92 mean. Every roadmap item should be tested against whether it makes the daily organise-and-tick loop better.
- **Motivation and ADHD are perfect-scoring themes** (32 and 16 reviews, both 5.00 mean, zero sub-5★). The warmth — mascot, colour, affirmations, encouragement — is doing real work. `13860369435`·EE·1's complaint is not that affirmations exist but that they cannot be turned off; make them optional, do not remove them.
- **Turkish localization is a named competitive advantage** (`11647652906`·TR·5 chose Dear Me over a competitor for it). Extend that quality to the other four languages rather than diluting it.
- **The free tier is better than its reputation.** 28 reviewers say so and correct other reviewers in public. Market it.

## 7.5 Experiments worth running

1. **Trial length A/B (3 vs 7 days) in DE, FR, US.** Hypothesis: high-spend markets convert at a *higher* rate with a trial than without, because the current blocker is trust, not price. Measure refund rate and 1★ rate, not just conversion.
2. **Upsell frequency cap.** Hypothesis: capping interstitials at one per session reduces 1★ volume more than it reduces revenue. `M-nag` is the fastest-worsening theme in the corpus.
3. **Onboarding length.** Hypothesis: a skippable quiz raises completion in RU/IN without reducing conversion. `O-long` mean is 1.31 and 15 of 35 are Russian.
4. **Guided-content re-run.** Hypothesis: making the breathing exercise repeatable measurably reduces `G-thin`/`O-mislead` sentiment and 7-day churn. Cheap to build, directly named by 21 reviewers.
5. **Post-purchase onboarding.** Given that triggers 1–4 in §4.3 are all pre-experience, test a "here's what you just unlocked" flow immediately after purchase. Hypothesis: it reduces `M-regret` (50 reviews) and refund requests.

## 7.6 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

- What is the actual trial-to-paid, renewal and refund rate? Reviews cannot say.
- Is the Russia loading failure server-side, CDN, or a sanction-related dependency? Requires telemetry.
- How large is the silent satisfied-paying population? The 226 paid reviewers are negatively selected by definition.
- Did the Feb 2025 removal of the money-back guarantee reduce conversion, or only reduce refunds?
- Do the ADHD/depression screens have any clinical validation?
- Is the free-tier habit cap real, and what is it? Reviewers report 10+ habits with no cap hit (`12468334420`·TR·5) while others report immediate blocking.
- What do the ~40,000 star-only raters think? Given §2.6, that population may be substantially prompt-driven.

## 8.3 Method and known error risk

1. All 1,281 records were parsed, sorted chronologically, and rendered as `index|review_id|country|rating|date|title :: body`.
2. Every record was read in full, in its original language, in sequential batches of 60–120. No keyword search, no sampling, no rating filter, no language filter.
3. Each record was assigned zero or more theme codes from the taxonomy in §8.6. Codes were added to the taxonomy as new patterns appeared and then applied consistently; the final taxonomy has 101 codes.
4. Assignments were written to a flat `index|codes` file, verified for complete 1→1281 coverage with no gaps or duplicates, then joined back to the source records by index and aggregated programmatically.
5. All counts, percentages, means, cross-tabs and per-country and per-period tables in this report are computed from that join — none are estimated or rounded by hand.

**Known error risk.**
- Classification is manual and therefore judgement-bearing. The highest-ambiguity boundaries are: `M-price` vs `M-wall` (a price objection vs an access objection), `G-thin` vs `G-guide` (shallow vs "the guidance vanished"), and `M-refund` vs `M-cancel` (wanting money back vs wanting the subscription to stop). Where a review supports both, both were assigned; theme counts are non-exclusive throughout.
- `PAID` required explicit textual purchase evidence. It is a **floor**. Reviewers who paid silently are counted as unpaid.
- Short reviews ("Güzel", "Bom", "⭐️") were read and deliberately left unclassified rather than forced into a theme. All 339 are retained in every denominator.
- Non-Latin-script reviews (Turkish, Russian, Hebrew, Arabic, Japanese) were read in the original. Glosses in this report are the analyst's translations, marked with an em-dash and quotation.
- Small-count themes (n < 6) are labelled Weak or Ignore and no standalone conclusion is drawn from them, with the two stated exceptions (`X-safety`, `G-a11y`) promoted explicitly under the safety carve-out in the threshold table.

## 8.4 Known limitations and biases

- **Selection bias.** Written reviewers are self-selecting and more motivated than the ~40,000 star-only raters **[external]**. The +1.35 gap in §2.6 quantifies this, but the corpus cannot decompose how much of that gap is ordinary selection versus the documented pre-use rating prompt.
- **Paid-cohort bias.** 226 paid reviewers skew negative because satisfied subscribers rarely write. Their 1.94 mean is not a satisfaction score.
- **Storefront skew.** TR is 36.77% of the corpus but far from 36.77% of the world. RU is 8.82% of the corpus and contributes 78.8% of all loading complaints — global defect figures are pulled upward by one storefront and are stated with that caveat wherever they appear.
- **No version data.** Regressions are dated, not versioned. The Nov 2024 and Apr 2025 clusters are inferred from date concentration plus consistent user description.
- **No cohort or telemetry data.** Nothing about conversion, retention, refund rate or session length can be derived from this corpus, and nothing here claims to.
- **External data is a point-in-time snapshot** taken 9 Sep 2026 and is never mixed into corpus percentages.

## 8.5 Counting rules

- **Denominator.** Every global percentage divides by 1,281. Every country percentage divides by that country's own n. Every period percentage divides by that period's own n. Segment rates (paid cohort, a star band) name their denominator on the line.
- **Non-exclusivity.** A review may carry any number of themes. The most-coded review in the corpus (`12587805273`·RU·1) carries 11. Theme counts do not sum to 1,281 and are not intended to.
- **Rating-band membership** is by the record's own `rating` field, regardless of text sentiment. The 9 `MISMATCH` reviews stay in the 5★ band and are flagged, not moved.
- **`PAID`** is assigned only on explicit textual evidence of purchase, subscription, renewal, a named charged amount, or a refund/cancellation request that presupposes a purchase.
- **Deduplication.** None performed. One exact `title`+`body` pair exists across two distinct `review_id`s and dates; both are retained, both counted.
- **Supergroups.** `MONEY-any` = any `M-*`; `BUG-any` = any `B-*`; `GAP-any` = any `G-*`; `PRAISE-any` = any `P-*`; `ONBOARD-any` = any `O-*`. `PAID`, `WTP`, `ADHD`, `MISMATCH`, `U-conf` and `X-*` sit outside the supergroups and are reported separately.
- **Signal labels** are applied to the share of the stated denominator, per the band table in "How to read this". Safety, legal, security and data-loss findings may be promoted above their band; two are (`X-safety`, `G-a11y`), and both are labelled as such.

## 8.6 Theme taxonomy — full definitions

**Praise (`P-*`)**
`P-org` helps organise the day/routine · `P-outcome` a stated life change or result · `P-motiv` motivating, fun, encouraging · `P-design` design, colour, illustration, animation · `P-easy` easy/intuitive · `P-free` the free tier is usable · `P-presets` the ready-made routines are valuable · `P-remind` reminders work well · `P-adhd` helped with ADHD, anxiety or mental health · `P-tests` the quizzes/tests are valuable · `P-value` worth the price · `P-mascot` the beaver, positively · `P-l10n` local-language support praised · `P-noads` no advertising, praised · `P-notes` journaling praised · `P-support` support resolved an issue

**Money (`M-*`)**
`M-price` too expensive / wish it were free / cannot afford · `M-refund` refund requested or not received · `M-wall` cannot use the app meaningfully without paying · `M-nag` repeated upsell pop-ups, discount timers · `M-notrial` explicitly asks for or laments the absence of a free trial · `M-regret` paid and regrets it · `M-cancel` cancellation blocked, unclear, or ineffective · `M-gated` a specific named feature is locked · `M-scam` explicit fraud/theft/deception accusation · `M-unclear` price, currency, plan length or Plus contents not understood · `M-latereveal` price revealed only after the full onboarding quiz · `M-guarantee` an advertised money-back guarantee was not honoured · `M-overcharge` charged a different, higher or duplicate amount · `M-payfail` paid but the subscription never activated, or payment is impossible · `M-trialtrap` charged despite cancelling inside a trial · `M-onetime` wants a one-time purchase instead of a subscription

**Onboarding & marketing (`O-*`)**
`O-long` the intro/quiz is too long · `O-mislead` advertised or demonstrated features are absent · `O-ads` external advertising mentioned (positively or negatively) · `O-forcedrate` asked to rate the app before using it · `O-notpersonal` the "personalised plan" is not personalised · `O-privacy` personal-data or permissions concern · `O-agegate` age brackets exclude the reviewer · `O-gender` content written for women only · `O-inclusive` objection to inclusive-language forms · `O-signature` the handwritten commitment/signature step

**Capability gaps (`G-*`)**
`G-thin` shallow — "just a checklist", no guidance, few options · `G-content` wants more or deeper routine templates · `G-freq` scheduling model too rigid (sub-daily, any-N-days, weekday choice, skip) · `G-noedit` cannot rename or edit habits, especially presets · `G-nodelete` cannot delete a habit or routine · `G-icons` too few icons, emoji or colours · `G-l10n` translation or RTL quality · `G-group` no categorisation/grouping of routines · `G-guide` the guided exercise appears only in onboarding · `G-widget` widget missing or limited · `G-time` wants time ranges, durations or exact times · `G-notes` wants real journaling/notes/photos · `G-sound` wants an audible completion sound or alarm tone · `G-dup` the same habit appears multiple times · `G-order` cannot reorder the day manually · `G-sub` wants sub-tasks, quantities or counters · `G-sync` iPad, Apple Watch or multi-device · `G-backfill` cannot tick off a past day · `G-future` cannot plan far enough ahead · `G-stats` statistics too shallow · `G-calendar` wants Apple Calendar integration · `G-todo` wants one-off tasks alongside habits · `G-kids` wants a children's version · `G-social` wants shared routines with friends · `G-cal-start` week should start on Monday · `G-dark` wants a dark mode · `G-a11y` accessibility/photosensitivity concern

**Defects (`B-*`)**
`B-load` loads for minutes, hangs, spinners · `B-open` will not open, white screen, crash on launch · `B-onboard` frozen inside the onboarding quiz · `B-create` cannot save a new habit · `B-login` logged out, cannot sign back in, account lost · `B-notif` reminders never arrive · `B-check` a completed task reverts to incomplete · `B-notifspam` too many or badly-timed notifications · `B-ime` Japanese text input (kanji conversion, dakuten) broken · `B-vpn` unusable in Russia without a VPN · `B-ui` truncated text or broken layout · `B-order` tasks stopped sorting chronologically · `B-widget` widget fails to load or renders blank · `B-dataloss` routines or history disappeared · `B-cal` wrong weekday displayed · `B-sig` the signature field does not accept input · `B-discover` the Discover tab errors out · `B-tablet` iPad layout unusable · `B-offline` requires a network connection to plan · `B-rtl` Hebrew interface renders reversed

**Other**
`PAID` explicit purchase evidence · `WTP` states willingness to pay without having paid · `ADHD` mentions ADHD/neurodivergence (neutral marker) · `U-conf` cannot find or understand a feature · `X-support` no support response · `X-anxiety` the app increased the reviewer's anxiety · `X-safety` clinical or psychological-safety concern · `X-ai` mentions the AI coach "Mimi" · `X-redesign` a change or update made things worse · `X-copycat` calls the app a copy of another · `X-mascot` rejects the mascot · `MISMATCH` the star rating contradicts the review text
