## How to read this

Every number here resolves to named review IDs. Part 10 lists all 41 reviews with the themes
assigned to each, so any figure can be walked back to exact source text, and any review can be
walked forward to the findings it supports. Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can
carry many codes, and most do (154 code assignments across 41 reviews, mean 3.76 per review).

### ⚠️ Seven warnings before any number here is used

1. **The corpus is 41 reviews.** This is a small-sample report. One review is 2.44% of it.
2. **Therefore the signal-threshold table in the spec is close to useless here.** Its lowest
   meaningful band ("Weak signal", 0.1–0.5%) cannot be reached: the smallest non-zero share
   possible is 2.44%, which already lands in "Meaningful signal", and three reviews (7.32%)
   already reads as "High-priority signal". **Bands are still reported as the spec requires, but
   they are not the right instrument at this N.** Every headline finding in this report is
   therefore also given a Wilson 95% confidence interval, and no single-review theme is promoted
   to a roadmap recommendation. §1.6 covers this in full.
3. **No country reaches the 50-review threshold.** The largest storefronts are France and Germany
   at 10 each. **There are no standalone country claims in this report** — Part 7 is explicitly
   limited-evidence throughout.
4. **Nobody in this corpus states that they bought the product.** One reviewer says they used a
   trial. Every monetization finding below is about *reaction to the paywall*, not about buyer
   behaviour. No conversion, renewal, refund or churn rate is derivable, and none is claimed.
5. **Text reviewers are not the rating base.** These 41 text reviews sit on a public base of 451
   ratings across the same 18 storefronts, whose ratings-weighted mean is **4.67★** against this
   corpus's **4.00★**. Text reviewers here skew roughly 0.67★ more negative. Treat every
   complaint share as a share *of people who chose to write*, not of users.
6. **The paywall's introduction date is not externally verifiable.** Apple exposes no release
   before v1.31.0 (2025-01-30). The date used here is inferred from review text and is a
   *latest-possible* bound, not a ship date.
7. **Translations are mine.** Original-language text is quoted first throughout; 24 of 41 reviews
   (58.5%) are not in English.

# PART 0 — READ THIS FIRST

## 0.1 The product is liked. The pricing *structure* is what people write about.

This is not an app with a quality problem. **36 of 41 reviews (87.80%, Wilson 95% CI 74.5–94.7%)
contain explicit praise** — including 9 of the 12 reviews that complain about money, and **4 of
the 5 one-star reviews.**

The one-star reviews are the clearest statement of the situation. All five of them:

| 12 | `12293956311` | fr | 2025-02-10 | "L'app est **bien** mais il aurait pu prévenir que c'était payant" — *The app is good but they could have warned it was paid.* |

| 33 | `13802989713` | fr | 2026-03-01 | "pour la version gratuite on ne peux avoir que 5 choix se stupide" — *in the free version you only get 5 choices, stupid.* |

**5 of 5 one-star reviews (100%) are about money and nothing else.** Not one names a bug, a
missing feature, a crash, a data loss or a support failure. Four of the five praise the product in
the same sentence in which they rate it one star.

That is an unusually clean diagnosis: **the rating damage in this corpus is not being caused by
the product. It is being caused by where the free/paid line sits and how it is disclosed.**

## 0.2 The specific lever is the free habit-count cap — and raising it did not work

The single most consequential and *datable* fact in this analysis:

> **On 2025-10-21, v1.56.0 raised the free tier from 3 habits to 5.** The release note reads:
> "Build More Habits: **You asked, we listened. Free users can now track up to 5 habits (up from 3).**"
> (external source S3, §9.D.)

The corpus straddles that change, and the review text tracks it exactly — three reviewers report
the cap as 3 before it, two report it as 5 after it, and no review ever reports both:

| Reported cap | n | Dates | Review IDs |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 habits | 3 | 2025-01-26, 2025-04-14, 2025-08-19 | `12232453094`, `12541530832`, `13036592730` |
| 5 habits | 2 | 2026-03-01, 2026-03-21 | `13802989713`, `13871805750` |

**The concession did not buy back sentiment.** Comparing the era before the change with the era
after it:

| | E2 — paywall, 3-habit cap | E3 — 5-habit cap | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Window | 2024-09-19 → 2025-10-20 | 2025-10-21 → 2026-07-15 | |
| Reviews | 18 | 17 | |
| Mean rating | **4.28★** | **3.53★** | ↓ 0.75★ |
| Money-negative share | 6/18 = 33.3% | 6/17 = 35.3% | flat |
| 1–3★ share | 3/18 = 16.7% | **7/17 = 41.2%** | ↑ 24.5 pts |
| Cap objections | 2/18 | 2/17 | flat |

And the objection text did not soften — it hardened. The two pre-change cap complaints are 3★ and
4★ and politely worded ("which is unfortunate… other than that, I would recommend!",
`13036592730`). The two post-change cap complaints are **both 1★** and openly hostile ("stupide",
`13802989713`; "Pff franchement c est soulant", `13871805750`).

**Read honestly:** at n=17 and n=18 these differences are *not* statistically separable — the
money-negative confidence intervals overlap almost completely (E2 16.3–56.3%, E3 17.3–58.7%). What
the corpus supports is the **negative** claim, and it is the useful one: *there is no evidence in
this corpus that moving the cap from 3 to 5 reduced monetization complaints.* Anyone proposing to
move it to 7 should know that the last move bought nothing measurable.

## 0.3 The disclosure gap is externally corroborated

Two reviewers say they were not told (`12293956311` fr 1★; `13606058398` de 2★ — "Kann sein das
ich etwas falsch verstanden habe😭 aber man muss Geld ausgeben um die App zu benutzen" / *Maybe I
misunderstood but you have to spend money to use the app*).

I checked the listing against that claim. **They are right.**

- The App Store description (S1, accessed 13 Sep 2026) **never states a habit-count limit.** It
  says "Add, edit, and organize your habits in seconds" with no number anywhere.
- The description's monetization paragraph names **only subscriptions**: "Subscription options are:
  1 month and 1 year with 7 days of free trial."
- The listing nevertheless sells **nine SKUs from $1.99 to $79.99**, and **six of the nine carry no
  period in their name** ("Premium", "Premium access"), including a **"Premium Lifetime" at $79.99**
  that the description does not mention at all (S2).

So the two things users complain about most — *that there is a cap* and *that the cap is at N* —
appear nowhere in the material they read before installing. This is a fixable disclosure defect,
not a pricing defect, and it is the cheapest item in this report.

## 0.4 Price level is a smaller problem than price *structure*

Separate the two objections, because they call for different responses:

| **Level** — the amount is too high | 4 | 9.76% | 3.50 | `11740258271`, `12169201015`, `13237903873`, `14305265486` |

Only **one** reviewer names an actual figure — `13237903873` (us, 5★): "I would download it then
delete it once I realized it was like **11 Dollars**." That matches the $11.99 SKU on the listing.

And one reviewer argues the opposite, with a benchmark — `14095584857` (de, 5★): "I think the price
for the premium **as well as the features of the normal version** are both **very reasonable**
considering that some apps charge up to **$70 a year**."

**One review is not a finding** (Wilson 95% CI for 1/41 is 0.4–12.6%). But the *shape* is
consistent: the people objecting are overwhelmingly objecting to being stopped, not to the number.
The most constructive version says so explicitly — `13871805750` (fr, 1★): "faite le gratuit ça et
mettez des trucs premium en plus" — ***make that part free and put premium things on top.***

## 0.7 The widget arc — request → ship → regression

This corpus contains a complete, datable product loop, and its ending is a warning:

| Stage | Dates | n | Review IDs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asked for a widget (none existed) | 2024-07-15 → 2024-08-25 | 3 | `11497210404`, `11554294446`, `11651421884` |
| Confirmed the widget exists and is used | 2025-05-31 → 2026-02-13 | 3 | `12718618757`, `13415065399`, `13744942868` |
| **Regression — the earlier widget was better** | 2026-02-13 | 1 | `13744942868` |

Three of the first six reviews the app ever received asked for a widget. It shipped. Then
v1.57.1 (2025-10-26) "polished up the design" (S3) — and on 2026-02-13 a 5★ reviewer wrote:
"earlier version of widget was very attractive. **light screen and fire icon can you please bring
that back**" (`13744942868`, in). The redesign appears to have removed something users valued.

Widget demand has not stopped; it has moved up the stack — completing a habit *from* the widget
(`12718618757`), the streak on the **Lock Screen** (`13591582179`), and milestone-based widget
art (`14130958475`). **8 of 41 reviews (19.51%) touch the widget.** For a habit app this is the
retention surface, and it is where the engaged users are concentrated: the widget cohort's mean
rating is **4.75★**.

## 0.9 The nine decisions this corpus supports

1. **Put the free-tier limit in the App Store description.** It is the #1 complaint and it is
   currently unstated. Zero engineering cost. (§0.3, §3.3-N2)
2. **Do not raise the cap again as a sentiment fix.** 3→5 produced no measurable improvement. (§0.2, §8.2)
3. **Rename the six period-less "Premium" / "Premium access" SKUs** so a shopper can tell what
   recurs. (§0.3, §3.3-N3)
4. **Restore the pre-v1.57.1 widget look, or make it an option.** A named regression from a 5★ user. (§0.7, §3.6-R1)
5. **Ship backfill** — marking past days complete. The only request in the corpus that is
   demonstrably *causing* disengagement. (§3.6-R2)
6. **Fix the streak counter.** The one defect that attacks the core promise. (§3.7-D2)
7. **Reconsider what the free tier gates.** The best-argued complaint asks to gate *features*, not
   *habit count*. (§3.3-N1, §8.4)
8. **Answer the two reviewers who asked a direct question.** Both are still unanswered. (§3.8)
9. **Instrument what reviews cannot tell you** — conversion, refunds, churn. This corpus contains
   zero purchase evidence and cannot substitute. (§6.1, §8.6)

## 1.5 Processing method

1. **Parse and order.** All records loaded, HTML-unescaped, sorted by `(date, review_id)`, and
   assigned a stable working index 1–41. The index table was asserted field-by-field against
   `reviews.jsonl` before use.
2. **Read.** All 41 reviews read in full, in original language, in date order.
3. **Classify by hand.** A 68-code, 12-family taxonomy was built **bottom-up from the text** — no
   preset theme list, no automated clustering, no LLM-assigned labels applied unreviewed. Each
   review's codes were written on one line together with the quoted phrase that drove the
   judgement. That file (`Temp/75-cls/001-041.txt`) is the auditable artefact; the
   `review_id → [themes]` map is generated from it and never hand-edited.
4. **Validate.** 25 automated integrity checks, all passing (§1.8).
5. **Aggregate.** Counts, percentages, Wilson intervals, signal bands, unions, per-rating,
   per-storefront, per-era and per-language breakdowns, all recomputed from the map.
6. **Corroborate externally.** iTunes Lookup, the US listing's IAP table, the 25-entry version
   history, and public rating bases for 20 storefronts. Kept separate and labelled S1–S4 (§9.D).
7. **Verify after writing.** Every review ID cited in this report re-checked against
   `reviews.jsonl`; every count re-derived (§1.8).

**Classification error risk.** 41 reviews classified by one reader is small enough to be fully
re-read, and it was — twice. The judgement calls that could reasonably have gone the other way are
listed individually in §9.C rather than buried.

## 1.6 Limitations — read these before citing any figure

1. **N = 41.** Everything else follows from this.
2. **The spec's signal bands do not discriminate at this N.** One review = 2.44% = "Meaningful
   signal"; three reviews = 7.32% = "High-priority signal". The bands are reported because the
   spec requires them, but **at n=41 a band label carries almost no information** — a theme
   mentioned three times cannot honestly be called a high-priority signal on that basis alone.
   Wilson 95% intervals are given alongside for every headline figure, and the report leans on
   *direction, dating and corroboration* rather than on band labels.
3. **No country is eligible.** The 50-review threshold is met by none of the 18 storefronts (max:
   fr and de at 10). Part 7 makes **no standalone country claims**.
4. **No purchase evidence exists.** Zero reviewers state that they paid. Conversion, renewal,
   refund and churn rates are **not derivable** and are not estimated.
5. **Selection bias is measurable here and it is large.** Corpus mean 4.00★ vs 4.67★ ratings-
   weighted public mean across the same 18 storefronts (S4) — text reviewers skew ~0.67★ negative.
   In France the gap is 1.34★ (corpus 3.40 vs public 4.74).
6. **No version field.** Reviews cannot be attributed to a build. Era assignment uses review date
   against externally dated releases, which is an approximation — a user may review weeks after
   updating.
7. **The paywall introduction date is inferred, not sourced.** Apple exposes no release before
   2025-01-30. The earliest review complaining that the app "est devenue payante" is 2024-09-19
   (`11740258271`), so the paywall existed **by** then. It may have existed earlier.
8. **Era comparisons are underpowered.** E1/E2/E3 hold 6/18/17 reviews. Confidence intervals
   overlap on nearly every cross-era comparison; §8 says so at each claim.
9. **Language attribution is mine**, assigned by reading. 10 reviews are written in English from
   non-English storefronts (§7.5), so storefront ≠ language ≠ nationality.
10. **Small-country arithmetic is misleading.** Thirteen storefronts hold exactly one review. A
    single 1★ makes such a storefront "1.00★ mean". Part 7 shows these but never reasons from them.

## 1.7 Counting and segment rates

- Theme counts are **non-exclusive**; a review carrying seven codes is counted in seven themes.
- **Unions** (`U:*`) count each review **once** even when it matches several member codes. Use
  unions, not sums of codes, for any "how many people" statement.
- The global denominator is **41** everywhere unless a **segment rate** is explicitly labelled as
  such — e.g. "8 of the 11 reviews at 1–3★ (72.7%, segment rate; 8/41 = 19.51% globally)". Segment
  rates never replace the global figure.

# PART 5 — PAID-USER AND UPGRADE ANALYSIS

## 5.1 The finding that governs this entire part

> **No reviewer in this corpus states that they purchased anything.**

Across 41 reviews there is **not one** statement of the form "I bought", "I subscribed", "I
upgraded", "I renewed", "I paid", or "I got a refund" — in any of the six languages present.
This was tested by reading, and by regex across all titles and bodies.

What does exist:

| Evidence type | n | IDs | What it actually establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Used a trial / test option | 1 | `12169201015` | "i use i option test" — trial usage, then declined ("i dont have money to subscribe") |
| Reports a specific price | 1 | `13237903873` | Saw "$11 Dollars" on the paywall. **Did not buy** — "I would download it then delete it" |
| Evaluates the price as fair | 1 | `14095584857` | An opinion about the price, from a reviewer who says "I have **just downloaded** this app" — i.e. almost certainly not yet a payer |
| Says the free tier suffices | 3 | `11545888660`, `12541530832`, `14095584857` | Explicit non-purchase, satisfied |

**Consequences, stated plainly:**
- **No conversion rate, trial-to-paid rate, renewal rate, refund rate or churn rate is derivable
  from this corpus, and none is given anywhere in this report.**
- There is **no post-purchase experience data at all** — no reports of a purchase failing to
  restore, of a subscription being hard to cancel, of premium features underdelivering, or of
  billing problems. That is a genuine blank, not a clean bill of health.
- Everything below is **reaction to the paywall**, i.e. pre-purchase behaviour.

This is itself worth acting on (§8.6): the developer cannot learn from reviews what only
instrumentation can tell them.

## 5.2 What the paywall encounter looks like

Reconstructed from the 12 monetization-negative reviews (29.27%, mean 2.58★, Wilson 17.6–44.5%):

1. **The user installs believing the app is free.** It is listed as Free, and the description
   states no limit (S1). Two reviewers say explicitly they were not warned (`12293956311`,
   `13606058398`).
2. **They set up habits and like the product.** 9 of the 12 monetization complainers volunteer
   praise (`U:praise_any` ∩ `U:money_negative` = 9/12): `11740258271`, `12169201015`,
   `12293956311`, `13036592730`, `13237903873`, `13421279860`, `13606058398`, `13871805750`,
   `14305265486`.
3. **They hit the cap, or a premium prompt, and read it as "the app is paid."** Four describe the
   product as pay-to-use (`13250332545`, `13421279860`, `13606058398`, `13753692081`) — a
   perception the free tier's actual generosity contradicts.
4. **They rate, and they rate on the money, not the product.** Mean rating of this group: **2.58★**
   against a corpus mean of 4.00★.

The most compressed statement of the whole sequence is `13421279860` (vn, 1★): likes the look,
hits the paywall, **deletes at ten minutes**, and titles the review "Tư bản" — *Capitalist*.

## 5.3 What would make someone pay — inferred, and labelled as inference

No purchase evidence exists, so the following is **inference from stated intent**, not measured
behaviour:

| Signal | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Users want **more habit slots** — the only premium benefit anyone names | `13036592730`, `13802989713`, `13871805750`, `13250332545` | Direct, but they are declining to pay for it |
| One user finds the price fair **because the free tier is good** | `14095584857`: "the price for the premium **as well as the features of the normal version** are both very reasonable" | n=1; but it is the only positive purchase-intent logic in the corpus, and it runs through free-tier satisfaction |
| Retention precedes willingness | `12541530832` reached a one-month streak on the free tier and rated 4★ | n=1, consistent with (2) |

**The pattern these three suggest — and it is a hypothesis, not a finding:** the people most likely
to pay are the ones who got value free first. The current structure caps them early, at the point
of *setup*, before the streak that creates the attachment has had time to form. §8.4 turns this
into a testable experiment rather than a recommendation.

# PART 7 — TIME-TREND ANALYSIS

## 7.1 Method

The corpus spans **25 months** (2024-07-02 → 2026-07-15) at a steady 1–5 reviews per month. Eras
are **anchored to monetization events**, not cut into equal thirds, so that each era means
something:

| Era | Window | Anchor | n | Mean ★ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E1 — Free era** | 2024-07-01 → 2024-09-18 | App release (S1) to the day before the first "it's now paid" review | 6 | **4.50** |
| **E2 — Paywall, 3-habit cap** | 2024-09-19 → 2025-10-20 | First paywall complaint (`11740258271`) to the day before v1.56.0 | 18 | **4.28** |
| **E3 — 5-habit cap** | 2025-10-21 → 2026-07-15 | v1.56.0 raises the free cap 3→5 (S3) to the last review | 17 | **3.53** |

E1's boundary is inferred (§2.4); E3's is externally dated to the day.

| Era | Window | n | Mean ★ | Money-negative | Defect | Request | Widget |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **E1 — Free era** | 2024-07-01 → 2024-09-18 | 6 | 4.5 | 0/6 = 0.0% | 1/6 = 16.7% | 3/6 = 50.0% | 3/6 = 50.0% |
| **E2 — Paywall, 3-habit cap** | 2024-09-19 → 2025-10-20 | 18 | 4.28 | 6/18 = 33.3% | 3/18 = 16.7% | 5/18 = 27.8% | 1/18 = 5.6% |
| **E3 — 5-habit cap** | 2025-10-21 → 2026-07-15 | 17 | 3.53 | 6/17 = 35.3% | 1/17 = 5.9% | 3/17 = 17.6% | 4/17 = 23.5% |
**Power warning.** At 6/18/17 reviews, almost no cross-era difference here is statistically
separable — the money-negative intervals for E2 (16.3–56.3%) and E3 (17.3–58.7%) overlap almost
entirely. Trends below are labelled by what the evidence can actually carry.

## 7.2 The one clean cause-and-effect in the corpus

Not a rating trend — a **vocabulary** trend, and it is the strongest single piece of evidence here:

| Date | Event | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2025-01-26 → 2025-08-19 | Three reviewers independently report the free cap as **3** | `12232453094`, `12541530832`, `13036592730` |
| **2025-10-21** | **v1.56.0: "Free users can now track up to 5 habits (up from 3)"** | S3 (external) |
| 2026-03-01 → 2026-03-21 | Two reviewers report the free cap as **5** | `13802989713`, `13871805750` |

No review reports both numbers; every "3" precedes the release and every "5" follows it
(validation check 20). This is the corpus confirming an externally dated product change without
being told about it — which is also the strongest available evidence that the hand classification
is tracking reality.

## 7.3 The five trends this corpus supports

### T1 — Monetization complaints appeared with the paywall and never receded — *persistent*

| Era | Money-negative | Cap objections |
|---|---|---|
| E1 (free) | **0/6 = 0.0%** | 0/6 |
| E2 (cap 3) | 6/18 = 33.3% | 2/18 |
| E3 (cap 5) | 6/17 = 35.3% | 2/17 |

**E1 is the control group, and it is clean: zero monetization complaints in the six reviews written
before the paywall existed.** After it, roughly a third of every era. The most recent monetization
complaint is `14305265486`, 2026-07-15 — **the last review in the corpus.** This is a steady state,
not a spent event.

### T2 — Ratings declined across the three eras — *worsening, but underpowered*

4.50 → 4.28 → 3.53. The 1–3★ share is the sharper version: **16.7% (E1) → 16.7% (E2) → 41.2% (E3)**.
By calendar year: 2024 = 4.22★ (n=9), 2025 = 4.24★ (n=17), **2026 = 3.60★ (n=15)**.

Both cuts agree on direction. **But the E3 decline is not attributable to the cap change**, because
cap objections stayed flat (2 → 2) while *general* paywall complaints and defect-free negativity
rose. What changed in E3 is that more reviewers arrived at a low rating **by more routes** —
"everything is paid" (`13753692081`, `13421279860`), confusion (`13606058398`), the streak bug
(`13608628754`), price (`14305265486`). Direction: **worsening. Cause: not established.**

### T3 — Raising the free cap produced no measurable benefit — *no effect detected*

The single most decision-relevant trend, and it is a **negative result**: across the v1.56.0
boundary, money-negative share was flat (33.3% → 35.3%), cap objections were flat (2 → 2), and mean
rating fell (4.28 → 3.53). Meanwhile the *tone* of cap complaints worsened from 3–4★ with volunteered
praise to 1★ with "stupide" and "soulant" (§3.3-N1).

**Correctly stated:** this corpus provides no evidence that the 3→5 change improved sentiment, and
it is too small to prove it did not. What it does establish is that **the change did not solve the
problem** — the objection survived the concession in identical form, at the new number.

### T4 — The widget went request → shipped → regressed — *shipped, then partially lost*

Requests clustered in E1 (3 of the first 6 reviews ever written). Confirmations run 2025-05-31 →
2026-02-13. Then the v1.57.1 "fresh look" (2025-10-26, S3) is followed on 2026-02-13 by a 5★ user
asking for the **old widget back** (`13744942868`). Widget demand in E3 (4/17 = 23.5%) is back near
its E1 level (3/6 = 50%) after nearly vanishing in E2 (1/18 = 5.6%) — consistent with a feature
that shipped, satisfied people, then changed.

### T5 — Defect reports fell and stopped — *improving*

1 (E1) → 3 (E2) → 1 (E3). **No defect has been reported since 2026-01-08**, across the last eight
reviews and six months. The blocking crash (2024-07-29) and the free-tier limit bug (2025-01-26)
were both one-off and neither recurred. At n=41 this is weak evidence, but it points the same way
as the rest: **reliability is improving while monetization sentiment is not.**

## 7.4 What persisted unchanged across all three eras

- **Simplicity praise.** Present in E1 (`11448026753`, 2024-07-02) and in E3 (`13926014051`,
  2026-04-05, "simple y sin ruido"). The differentiator has not eroded.
- **Fit praise.** E1 `11554294446` "the one I have been looking for" → E3 `13951751980` "So eine App
  hat gefehlt!" (2026-04-12).
- **Feature requests from happy users.** 3/6 in E1, 5/18 in E2, 3/17 in E3 — mean rating of the
  request cohort stays at 4.64★ throughout.
- **Zero complaints** about privacy, sync, accounts, ads, data loss, battery, support or
  accessibility — in every era (§0.8).

## 7.5 What this corpus cannot say about time

- **When the paywall shipped.** Bounded only to between 2024-07-28 and 2024-09-19 (§2.4).
- **Whether v1.57.1's Insights screen landed.** Shipped 2025-10-26; **no review mentions it.**
- **Whether v1.70.0's habit search landed.** Shipped 2026-04-02; no review mentions it.
- **Whether any defect was actually fixed.** Absence of repeat reports at n=41 is not a fix.
- **Whether ratings fell because of the product.** Volume is too thin, and E3's decline has several
  candidate causes.
- **Any seasonality.** 1–5 reviews per month cannot support it. Note only that **January is the
  busiest month in the corpus** (2025-01: 2; 2026-01: 4) — consistent with New Year habit-forming
  intent, but far too thin to assert.

# PART 8 — PRODUCT IMPLICATIONS

Every recommendation names the findings it rests on. **Immediate** = the evidence supports doing it
now. **Build** = the evidence supports scheduling it. **Experiment** = the corpus raises the
hypothesis but cannot settle it. **Research** = the corpus explicitly cannot answer it.

## 8.1 Immediate — close the disclosure gap (cost: hours, not sprints)

| # | Action | Rests on |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **State the free-tier habit limit in the App Store description and in the first onboarding screen.** It currently appears in neither (S1). Wording: "Track up to 5 habits free, forever." | §0.3, §3.3-N1/N2/N3, §5.2 |
| 2 | **Rename the six period-less SKUs.** "Premium — $11.99" and "Premium access — $22.99" tell a shopper nothing. Every SKU should state its period, as "Premium Monthly" and "Premium Annual" already do. | §2.2, §5.4 |
| 3 | **Reconcile the description with the SKU list.** The description names only subscriptions; the listing sells "Premium Lifetime — $79.99". One of the two is wrong. | §2.2 |
| 4 | **Show what Premium unlocks besides more habits.** No reviewer in 41 can name a single premium capability. If there are others, they are invisible; if there are not, the offer is a quantity gate and reads as one. | §2.3, §3.3-N3 |

These four are the highest return-on-effort items in this report. They cost no engineering, and
they address the theme behind **8 of the 11 reviews at 1–3★**.

## 8.2 Immediate — do not raise the cap again as a sentiment fix

Raising it 3→5 produced no measurable improvement in monetization sentiment and coincided with the
worst-rated era in the corpus (§7.3). If the cap is changed again, change **what it gates** or
**when it is hit**, not the number — and instrument it, because reviews will not tell you whether
it worked (§8.6).

In evidence order:

## 8.4 Positioning and monetization strategy

**What the evidence supports saying about the product:**
- Lead with **simplicity as a comparison**, not as an adjective. Reviewers already write the
  copy: "what sets this apart from the rest is its simplicity", "super simple", "simple y sin
  ruido", "remplit mieux mes attentes que toutes les autres."
- Lead with the **Duolingo streak, for anything**. Two users independently describe arriving with
  exactly that model (`12541530832`, `13415065399`), and one supplies the differentiator: "except
  super simple and **you can use any habit you want**."
- Lead with **outcomes**, which 5 reviewers supply for free: a novel written, a daily life changed,
  a first month of streak.

**On the paywall structure** — the corpus's best-argued complaint asks for a specific change:
gate *features*, not *habit count* (`13871805750`: "faite le gratuit ça et mettez des trucs premium
en plus"). Three independent observations point the same way:
1. The two reviewers who call the free tier sufficient are the ones who stayed
   (`12541530832`, `14095584857`).
2. The only fair-price argument in the corpus runs **through** free-tier satisfaction
   (`14095584857` praises "the price for the premium **as well as the features of the normal
   version**").
3. The "everything is paid" reactions all come from users who bounced within minutes
   (§3.3-N3, §5.2) — i.e. before a streak could form.

**This is a hypothesis, not a finding** (the supporting reviews are n=1 to n=3, Wilson intervals
2.5–19.4%). It is stated as a hypothesis and tested in §8.5, not shipped on this evidence.

## 8.5 Experiments — hypotheses the corpus raises but cannot settle

| # | Hypothesis | Test | Success measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Gating **features** rather than **habit count** converts better and rates better | A/B: unlimited habits with premium features gated, vs the current cap | Conversion rate; 1★ rate; D30 retention |
| E2 | The cap is hit **too early** — before the streak creates attachment | A/B: cap enforced at day 14 rather than at setup | Conversion; 10-minute abandonment rate |
| E3 | Disclosing the limit up front **raises** ratings even though it costs installs | Ship §8.1 item 1, then compare 1★ rate and install→D1 retention before/after | 1★ rate; D1 retention |
| E4 | The widget is the retention surface | Compare retention for widget-installers vs not | D30 retention delta |
| E5 | Backfill fixes a first-week leak | Ship it; measure D7 retention of users who backfill | D7 retention delta |

**E3 is the one to run first.** It is nearly free, it directly tests the report's central claim, and
the current listing makes the honest version of it impossible to test at all.

## 8.6 Research questions this corpus cannot answer

These are blanks, not oversights. **Reviews are structurally incapable of answering them**, and
this report refuses to estimate them:

1. **Conversion rate** — trial start, trial→paid, free→paid. Zero purchasers in the corpus (§5.1).
2. **Which of the nine SKUs people actually buy**, and whether the period-less names cost sales.
3. **Refund, cancellation and renewal rates** — no events of any kind in the corpus (§5.5).
4. **Whether the paywall is hit at setup or later.** The whole of §8.4 turns on this and it is not

5. **Whether Insights (v1.57.1) or habit search (v1.70.0) are used.** Neither is mentioned by any

6. **What the 410 people who rated but did not write think.** They rate 0.67★ higher on average

7. **Why gb and jp produce ratings but no text reviews.**

## 8.7 What to do first

1. **§8.1 items 1–4** — disclosure. Hours of work; addresses the theme behind 8 of 11 low ratings.
2. **§8.3 items 1 and 4** — restore the widget look; fix the streak counter. One is a reversal, one

## A. Counting rules

1. **Denominator is 41** for every global percentage, without exception.
2. **Theme counts are non-exclusive.** 154 code assignments across 41 reviews (mean 3.76 codes per
   review, range 1–11). Codes must not be summed to produce a headcount.
3. **Unions count each review once.** Use `U:*` figures for "how many people" statements.

   0.4–12.6%; 3 → 2.5–19.4%; 5 → 5.3–25.5%; 12 → 17.6–44.5%.
7. **Signal bands** follow the spec's table and are reported for completeness. **§1.6.2 explains
   why they carry little information at n=41** and should not be quoted without it.

## C. Judgement calls and known error risk

Disclosed individually, because at n=41 a single reclassification moves a percentage by 2.44 points.

1. **`12472757189` (mx, 5★)** — "Porque deshabilitado la opción de pulsar para check el hábito."
   Read as a **defect report** (`BUG_CHECK_DISABLED`). It could instead be a paywall gate
   misread as a bug, or a disliked deliberate change. The Spanish is ambiguous and the title ("Está
   bien") contradicts the body. **If it is a paywall gate, `U:money_negative` rises to 13 (31.71%)
   and `U:defect_any` falls to 4 (9.76%).**
2. **`13250332545` (mx, 5★)** — "Me gustaría que fuera gratuito." Read as **monetization-negative**
   on the strength of the body and the author handle "Gratuito porfa" (*free please*). A stricter
   reader could treat it as a wish rather than an objection, which would drop
   `U:money_negative` to 11 (26.83%).
3. **`12169201015` (st, 5★)** — coded both positive and monetization-negative. The reviewer praises
   the app warmly and states they cannot afford it. Both codes are carried deliberately.
4. **Author handles were read as evidence exactly once** — item 2 above. This is the single
   exception to rule A.5 and is flagged here rather than left implicit.
5. **`12232453094` (de, 3★)** — carries both `BUG_FREE_TIER_LIMIT` and `MON_CAP_3`. It is primarily
   a bug report about the free tier under-delivering, not a pricing objection; it is therefore
   **excluded** from `U:money_negative` and **included** in `U:cap_objection`. Counting it as a
   pricing objection would raise `U:money_negative` to 13 (31.71%).
6. **`11545888660` (de, 5★)** — "For a free app without adds" is dated 2024-07-28, before the
   paywall. It is coded `MON_FREE_TIER_OK` for what the reviewer experienced at the time; it is
   **not** evidence about the current model.
7. **Era boundary E1/E2 is inferred**, not sourced (§2.4). Moving it earlier would not change any
   count, since E1 contains no monetization reviews at all.
8. **Language assignment is by reading**, not by a detector. The six languages present (en, fr, de,
   es, it, vi) are unambiguous in every case.
9. **`X_LOW_INFO` (3 reviews)** is a judgement about informational content, not quality. All three
   are 5★ and all three are included in every positive count they qualify for.

**Aggregate error exposure:** the disclosed judgement calls could shift `U:money_negative` between
**11 and 13 of 41 (26.83%–31.71%)**. Every headline conclusion in this report holds across that
