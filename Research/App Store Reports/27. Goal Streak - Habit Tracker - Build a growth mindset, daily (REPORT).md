## How to read this

- Every quantified finding gives **count, percentage, denominator, scope, period, signal label, and review IDs**. Any claim traces back to the exact record via `review_id` in `reviews.jsonl`.
- Theme counts are **non-exclusive** — one review can carry several themes. 138 of 138 records (100%) carry at least one theme.
- Signal thresholds used throughout: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority.
- **Facts** are what reviewers wrote. **Interpretation** is labelled as such.

### ⚠️ Six warnings before any number here is used

1. **The corpus is small: 138 records.** This is the single most important fact about every percentage below. At N=138, **one review = 0.72%**, which the prescribed threshold table classifies as a "meaningful signal". That is a threshold artefact, not a discovery. **Throughout this report the raw count is printed first and is the number you should reason with.** Any theme below ~4 reviews should be read as "a named individual said this", not as a rate. The signal labels are applied as instructed but are flagged `[THIN]` wherever n < 5.
2. **No storefront reaches the 50-review threshold.** The largest is the US at 43. **This report therefore makes no standalone country conclusions at all.** Every country observation in Part 7 is explicitly labelled limited evidence. This was the correct call for this corpus and is why only a global report exists.
3. **Classification was manual, not automated.** All 138 records were read in full, individually, and assigned to themes by hand. There is no regex classifier and therefore no classifier error rate — but there is assignment judgement, and the boundary rules are written out in Part 10.1 so a second reader can reproduce or dispute each call.
4. **The corpus is heavily positive and heavily recent.** 109 of 138 (79.0%) are 5★; 97 of 138 (70.3%) are from 2025–2026. The app accumulated only 12 reviews in its first 19 months. Early-era rates (n=12) cannot support trend claims on their own and are never used alone.
5. **A review-prompt mechanism exists but is barely visible.** Exactly one reviewer discloses being prompted: "Saw the message and had to rate" (`12023192028`, US, 5★). That is 1/138 = 0.72%. Unlike some peers in this category, there is **no evidence of reward-for-review**. Rating inflation from prompting is possible but unquantifiable and appears low.
6. **There are zero paying customers in this corpus, by construction.** No reviewer claims to have paid, subscribed, trialled, renewed or requested a refund for Goal Streak — a regex probe for `I paid / I bought / I subscribed / my subscription / refund` returns **0 matches across 138 records**. All money language in the corpus (52 records, 37.68%) is about the *absence* of a price, or about competitors' prices.

**1. "Free with no in-app purchases" is not a pricing detail here — it is the product's primary stated reason for existing, and it is the largest theme in the corpus.**
**47 reviews (34.06%, high-priority, mean 4.89)** volunteer that the app is free / has no IAP / no subscription / no paywall. The free tier is the acquisition channel.**

**2. A further 13 reviews (9.42%, high-priority, mean 4.92) name the competitive paywall explicitly, and 13 (9.42%, mean 4.85) say they came from other trackers.**
Price contrast: "Don't get streaks for $6. 👎 Get this!" Era rates: **33.3% (2022-23) → 44.8% (2024) → 41.8% (2025) → 28.6% (2026)**. **4. The single largest *quality* event in the corpus is a September–November 2025 update-and-iOS-26 regression, and it accounts for almost every bad rating the app has ever received.**
**All 4 one-star reviews, the only 2-star review, and 4 of 6 three-star reviews fall between 22 September 2025 and 17 January 2026.** Window rates: **2025-09-01 → 2026-01-31: n=37, mean 4.162, 10 sub-4★ reviews. **2026-02-01 onward: n=35, mean 4.829, zero 1★, zero 2★, one 3★.** Crash mentions: **7.3% of 2025 (4/55) → 0.0% of 2026 (0/42)**. Regression mentions: **9.1% of 2025 (5/55) → 0.0% of 2026 (0/42)**. **Interpretation: the iOS 26 break was real, cost the app its entire negative-rating history, and was fixed. **6. That same update also *removed* things users valued, and three reviewers said so independently.**
**Regression after update: 5 reviews (3.62%, very strong, mean 2.60)**, of which 3 are feature-loss rather than crash: the one-tap note export was removed and the day-record popup changed — "更新后整体使用感差的不是一点两点" / "the overall usability after the update is worse by more than a little" (`13178799590`, CN, 2★); the single-screen annual overview moved behind a per-habit tap and backfilling from the annual grid stopped working (`13077199701`, CN, 4★); and "I loved it before the previous update" with a request to choose the 365-dot view (`13215630472`, GB, 5★ — note the 5★). **Interpretation: the year-at-a-glance grid is a signature feature (praised in `8819961417`, `10477776521`, `10787472094`, `12944050597`). Demoting it cost goodwill even from people who still rated 5★. Feature removals in this app are more expensive than feature absences.**

**7. The widget is the app's most consistently under-delivering surface — and the only surface that produced a 1★ on its own.**
**Widget gap or defect: 9 reviews (6.52%, high-priority, mean 4.33)** against **5 reviews (3.62%) that praise it**. In a streak app, the widget is the retention surface. This is the highest-value engineering gap in the corpus.**

**8. The second-largest unmet need is a monthly view — requested five times over four years, never shipped.**
**5 reviews (3.62%, very strong, mean 4.00)**: "希望能增加月视图… 增加一个月的所有习惯打卡情况" (`8918509405`, 2022), "Wish it had a full month view though" (`13155678708`, 2025), "Please add a monthly review option in addition to the existing weekly and yearly reviews" (`13463476864`, 2025), "I can't summarize by week or by month" (`13642789952`, 2026), "it would be nice if there was a monthly overview of your progress" (`14239313970`, 2026). **Two of these are 3★ — the only feature request in the corpus that repeatedly costs stars.** The app has daily, weekly and yearly views; the month is the missing rung.

**9. Data durability is an unspoken anxiety, and one reviewer has already left over it.**
**4 reviews (2.90%, meaningful, mean 3.75)**: `13444084049` lost all progress in the November 2025 crash and said "guess its time for a new app"; `13491588986` asks outright "if my device gets reset all the data will be gone or I can recover it?"; `11762567349` will not reorder habits because "I think I will lose my data if I do that"; `13481955817` found moving to a new phone "a bit tedious". Three separate reviewers across 2024–2025 either fear data loss or ask for cross-device sync (`11937614517` iPad, `12900579642` "wish my goals were synced across my devices"). The feature is probably shipped and definitely under-communicated.**

**10. There is demand to pay — voluntarily — and demand *not* to be charged, and both come from the same people.**
**Tip jar / donation requests: 2 reviews (1.45%, meaningful) [THIN]** — "Keep the free app, consider adding a tip jar for users who are thankful" (`10787472094`) and "it would be nice to donate to the dev since it's a free app" (`11393669723`). **Pleas to stay free: 4 reviews (2.90%, meaningful)** — "i urge you to please keep the app free as long as possible" (`11937614517`), "please try to keep it that way as long as possible" (`12944050597`), "Please keep this free" (`14383016940`), plus `10787472094` again. **11. The solo developer is part of the product.**
**10 reviews (7.25%, high-priority, mean 5.00)** thank or celebrate the developer personally: "Thanks Emily" (`10787472094`, `13136686944`), "The story of how this app was built is really wholesome and relatable" (`9983242833`), "It's clear that someone made this with joy, not for money" (`12936194802`), "built/maintained by an individual female developer— THANK YOU" (`13933124385`), "Definitely designed by a woman, love the themes" (`13919778285`), "you are a hero" (`13989106663`), also `11393669723`, `12146188261`, `13583086435`. **Every one of these is 5★.** **Interpretation: the origin story in the listing is doing measurable work. ## 1.4 Deduplication

**No records were removed. The denominator for every global percentage in this report is 138.**

A check for repeated `(title, body)` pairs across the corpus returned **zero** matches. There are no near-duplicate or bot-pattern reviews. One record is a title-equals-body repeat within a single review (`11699207962`, "Simple and straightforward. And no BS subscriptions. I love it.") — that is one reviewer's formatting, not a duplicate record.

## 1.5 Classification method and error risk

**Method: exhaustive manual assignment.** Each of the 138 records was read and assigned to zero or more of 33 themes. Theme definitions and boundary rules are in Part 10.2. Themes are non-exclusive; the mean number of themes per record is 2.3.

Because assignment was manual and exhaustive rather than pattern-matched, there is **no recall gap from language coverage** — the seven non-English records (`8882537119`, `8918509405`, `9631112670`, `10639973409`, `10765907509` is English, `10780603796` DE, `10966990397` DE, `12999527055` NO, `13077199701` CN, `13178799590` CN, `13855189292` RU, `14179666010` ES-MX) were each read and translated in place rather than skipped.

**Known error risk, stated honestly:**

| Risk | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Assignment judgement | The largest risk. Boundary calls on `praise_simplicity_minimal` (52 records) and `praise_free_no_iap` (47) materially move the two biggest numbers. Both were applied **conservatively**: a review counts for `praise_free_no_iap` only if it names free/no-IAP/no-subscription/no-paywall/price explicitly. Reviews that merely *imply* it by contrast with expensive rivals (`13571505007`, `13919778285`, `13974276382`) were **excluded** from that theme and counted only under `competitor_price_contrast`. Both headline numbers are therefore floors. |
| Small-n instability | At n=138, each record moves any rate by 0.72pp. Themes with n<5 are flagged `[THIN]` and should be read as anecdotes. |
| Positivity skew | 79.0% of records are 5★. App Store reviews are self-selected; silent churn is invisible. The only churn actually observed in the corpus is `13444084049` ("time for a new app"). |
| Collection gaps | Two months inside the active period have zero reviews: **June 2025** and — with an extraction date of 8 September 2026 — the corpus ends 25 August 2026. July 2026 has only 1 record and August 2026 only 2. Do not read the 2026 tail as a decline. |
| Era imbalance | 2022–23 carries only 12 records (8.7%) against 55 for 2025 and 42 for 2026. All era comparisons lead with the later, better-populated eras. |

## 1.6 Limitations

- **No country is individually analysable.** The 50-review threshold is met by zero storefronts (max: US, 43). Part 7 contains observations, not conclusions.
- **No paid-user analysis is possible.** The app has never had a paid tier. Part 6 is therefore a forward-looking monetisation-risk analysis built from what reviewers say about price, not a post-purchase analysis.
- **No support-quality analysis is possible.** Not one review mentions contacting support, receiving a reply, or a developer response. This is a genuine absence, not an omission.
- **Version attribution is inferential.** Where this report says "the September 2025 update", it means "the update reviewers refer to in reviews dated September–November 2025". No build number is asserted.
- One external source was consulted (the App Store listing) and every claim drawn from it is labelled `[LISTING]`.

## 7.3 Six defensible trends

**Trend 1 — A reliability collapse in autumn 2025, and a complete recovery by 2026. [Strong evidence]**
The window **2025-09-01 → 2026-01-31** holds **37 reviews, mean 4.162, with 10 of the corpus's 11 sub-4★ reviews**. Every other period combined holds **101 reviews, mean 4.822, with 1 sub-4★ review**. From **2026-02-01 onward: 35 reviews, mean 4.829, zero 1★, zero 2★.** Evidence: `13169445559`, `13178799590`, `13240874103`, `13243690627`, `13338573061`, `13414248154`, `13444084049`, `13498254162`, `13608165763`, `13642789952`. **This single window explains the entire negative history of the app.**
*Interpretation: an update landing around September 2025, colliding with the iOS 26 transition, broke launch on some devices and removed or relocated several features. It was fixed within roughly four months. A solo-developer app with no in-app support channel pays for such a break entirely in public rating.*

**Trend 2 — The 2025 update was a net feature *restructure*, and reviewers reported both sides of it. [Strong evidence]**
Gained, per reviewers: multiple check-ins per day (`13077199701`, Aug 2025 — "每天多次打卡功能很好用" / "the multiple check-ins per day feature is very handy"; `13987574053`, Apr 2026 — "I can adjust and see as much times I can do something in the day"); flexible weekly frequency (`13903934801`, Mar 2026 — "ability to have tasks that can be completed a few times a week rather than everyday"); habit reordering (`12973708986`, Aug 2025). Lost, per reviewers: one-tap note export (`13178799590`), the day-tap record popup (`13178799590`), the single-screen annual overview (`13077199701`), backfill from the annual grid (`13077199701`), and a selectable 365-dot view (`13215630472`). **The same reviewer (`13077199701`) reports a gain and a loss in one review: "最近的更新很酷… 不过，我发觉之前可以在同一界面内完成的年度总览消失了" / "the recent update is cool… but I noticed the annual overview that used to be on the same screen has disappeared".**

**Trend 3 — Requested features do get shipped, on a 6–18 month lag. [Moderate evidence]**
- Per-weekday flexibility requested July 2025 (`12944050597`) → weekly-frequency praise March 2026 (`13903934801`).
- Habit reordering complained about Sept 2024 (`11762567349`) → praised as shipped Aug 2025 (`12973708986`).
- Monday week start asked twice in Jan–Feb 2024 (`10795403293`, `10966990397`) → **never asked again in 29 months**; plausibly shipped, not confirmed.
- Goal end date asked Dec 2025 (`13498254162`) → `[LISTING]` the store description now lists "countdown visualizations for goal end dates" and habit archiving (accessed 11 Sep 2026); plausibly shipped after that review.
*Caveat: absence of repeat complaints is weak evidence of a fix in a 138-review corpus. Each of these should be verified against the changelog.*

**Trend 4 — The year-grid, once a headline differentiator, has stopped being praised. [Moderate evidence]**
`praise_long_range_view`: **16.7% (2022-23) → 3.4% (2024) → 1.8% (2025) → 0.0% (2026)**. Absolute counts 2, 1, 1, 0. The two earliest reviews in the entire corpus are about it: "I love how you can see the progress of an entire year, helps me keep to my goals!" (`8819961417`, the corpus's first review, June 2022) and "Most apps does not let me see the progress of my habits over the months" (`10477776521`). By late 2025 it appears only as a loss: `13077199701`, `13215630472`. **Counts are small; treat as a hypothesis.** *Interpretation: the "Goal Streak Calendar" year grid — the thing the bundle ID is still named after (`goalStreakCalendar`) — is being quietly de-emphasised, and the users who valued it most are the ones who noticed.*

**Trend 5 — The competitive-escape narrative is strengthening. [Moderate evidence]**
`switched_from_competitor`: **0.0% → 10.3% → 5.5% → 16.7%**. `competitor_price_contrast`: **8.3% → 6.9% → 7.3% → 14.3%**. 2026 reviews increasingly open by describing the alternatives: "I must have tried almost all the habit trackers on the App Store" (`13903934801`), "I've tried dozens upon dozens of apps and this is literally unmatched" (`13989106663`), "so many habit apps are expensive and require an account" (`13974276382`), "ive been through so many other apps but they all have ads or limited usage that's behind paywall" (`14037309039`), "In a world where literally every service is locked behind a pay wall" (`14118395021`), "Most apps like this are really bad" (`13846689652`), "unlike other apps it's free" (`14281981627`). *Interpretation: as the category monetises harder, Goal Streak's position sharpens without the app changing. This is market drift working in its favour — and it is entirely contingent on staying free.*

**Trend 6 — Reviewers increasingly report actual behaviour change, not just app quality. [Moderate evidence]**
`outcome_behaviour_change`: **0.0% → 3.4% → 7.3% → 11.9%**. The 2026 examples are the strongest in the corpus: two years of use producing "a much much much better person" (`13761980153`), "the only way i can actually be consistent" (`14099500769`), "it holds you accountable" (`13923424034`). *Interpretation: this is a retention signal. The cohort that has been using the app for years is now writing about outcomes rather than features — the review base is maturing, not churning.*

## 8.4 Research questions the corpus raises but cannot answer

1. **Did the September 2025 update cause the crash, or did iOS 26?** Two reviewers blame the update (`13240874103`, `13444084049`), two blame iOS 26 (`13338573061`, `13169445559`). Crash logs would settle it, and the answer changes the release-process fix.
2. **Is iCloud sync actually working for everyone?** `10655126869` calls it seamless (2023); four later reviewers behave as if it does not exist. Is it opt-in, silently failing, or just invisible?
3. **Did the Monday week-start, goal end date, and habit archiving ship?** The listing suggests end date and archiving now exist. The changelog would confirm; reviews cannot.
4. **Why does China rate lowest (4.00, n=7) while writing the most technically detailed reviews?** Hypothesis: power-user expectations around export and annual views. Needs a larger Chinese-storefront sample.
5. **Would a tip jar cannibalise goodwill?** The two requests are both Canadian, both from 2024, and none has appeared since. A limited rollout with rating monitoring is the only way to find out.
6. **What does the silent majority think?** 138 written reviews over four years, against a growing user base, is a thin public record. The 79% 5★ rate reflects who chose to write, not who uses the app.
7. **Is the year-grid de-emphasis deliberate?** If so, the three reviewers who noticed (`13077199701`, `13215630472`, plus the four early praisers) suggest the trade-off was under-priced.

## 8.5 What not to do

- **Do not add gamification.** Two reviewers name its absence as the reason they chose this app (`13807267873`: "There's not pets to take care of, there's no flashy graphics"; `13933124385`: "it removes a direct emphasis on urgency and/or self-competition"), and 52 reviews praise minimalism.
- **Do not add ads**, even a single interstitial. 12 reviews (8.70%) praise their absence by name.
- **Do not require an account.** 4 reviews praise its absence; `[LISTING]` the no-data-collection privacy label is part of the positioning.
- **Do not remove features in updates without an opt-out.** The 2025 restructure produced the corpus's only 2★ and cost goodwill from 5★ users (`13215630472`).
- **Do not over-build.** `13807267873`'s "this app does everything you need it to do with very little drama" is the product spec. Ship the widget, the month view and the sync assurance; resist the rest.

# PART 9 — EVIDENCE APPENDIX

## 9.1 Counting rules

1. **Denominator.** Every global percentage uses **138**, the full corpus. No review was excluded, deduplicated, or down-weighted for any reason — not length, not language, not age, not generic content.
2. **Non-exclusivity.** Themes are non-exclusive. A review carrying praise and a request is counted in both. Mean themes per record: 2.3. Because of this, theme percentages do not sum to 100%.
3. **Segment rates.** Where a rate is computed against a narrower denominator (e.g. "% of the 109 five-star reviews"), it is labelled a **segment rate**, both denominators are shown, and it never replaces the global threshold classification.
4. **Signal labels.** Applied exactly as prescribed: `<0.1%` ignore · `0.1–0.5%` weak · `0.5–1%` emerging · `1–3%` meaningful · `3–5%` very strong · `>5%` high-priority. **At N=138 a single review is 0.72% and lands in the "meaningful" band.** Every theme with n<5 is therefore flagged `[THIN]` and the count is printed before the percentage.
5. **Mean rating per theme** is the arithmetic mean of the `rating` field over the reviews assigned to that theme.
6. **Direction.** `positive` / `negative` / `mixed` describes how the theme functions in the review, not the star rating. A negative theme can appear in a 5★ review (e.g. `13111651177` asks for a widget at 5★).
7. **Eras** are calendar years, with 2022 and 2023 merged (n=5 and n=7). Era rates use the era's own record count as denominator.
8. **Quotation.** Excerpts are verbatim from the `body` field, including the reviewers' own spelling and typographic errors. Non-English excerpts are given in the original with a labelled English gloss. HTML entities present in the source (`&#34;`, `&#39;`) are rendered as the characters they encode.
9. **External claims** are marked `[LISTING]` and are never mixed with review-derived counts.
