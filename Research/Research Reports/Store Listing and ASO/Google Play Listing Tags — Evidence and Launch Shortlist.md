## 1. What Google confirms, and what it does not publish

Google permits **at most five tags**. Relevance should be evident from the listing or initial use. Its help recommends revisiting tags after substantial functionality/content changes and provides a searchable picker with a category filter. It also says propagation can take 24 hours. The public table on that page is examples of **categories**, not a full tag inventory. [Google: Choose a category and tags](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859673?hl=en), undated; retrieved 15 Oct 2026.

The picker path is **Grow users → Store presence → Store settings → App category → Manage tags**. Do not interpret the category filter as evidence that every tag outside Productivity is forbidden; the documentation offers both search and other tags. Same Google source and retrieval date as above.

### Why public “tags” claims need checking

[AppTweak's own tag study](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/google-play-store-tags-what-how-to-use-them), updated **8 Apr 2024**, observed public tag displays, including Google-assigned labels absent from developers' selectable options. It also reports a client losing Similar Apps visibility after tag changes and advises that two or three relevant tags can be preferable to filling all five. This is dated provider evidence, not a current habit-tracker experiment or a causal guarantee.

Therefore the blanket assertion that tags are never public is too strong historically; the useful narrower rule is that a public chip does not establish the developer's saved selections. The inspected current habit-tracker web pages did not expose a dependable five-tag set.

Other search hits were rejected as selection evidence:

- [MindScout's HabitKit page](https://mindscout.net/apps/habit-tracker-habitkit), undated; retrieved 15 Oct 2026, uses its own matching labels such as “free,” “routine” and “habit tracker.” It does not establish Console selections.
- [GameLoop's HabitNow page](https://www.gameloop.com/game/productivity/com.habitnow), undated; reports app update 26 Jul 2026, retrieved 15 Oct 2026, labels it “Productivity | Board.” That is not authenticated Play Console evidence.
- [Mobbo's Routinery page](https://mobbo.com/Android/App/com.alt.goodmorning/19386000), undated; retrieved 15 Oct 2026, contains an “Add Tag” input. An analytics-tool input is not a disclosure of selected Google tags.

## 3. How tags relate to discovery, ranking and policy

Google says it understands apps using developer information, its own app analysis and user feedback. Ranking combines relevance, quality and other contextual signals; the balance varies by surface. Search emphasizes query relevance, while charts emphasize popularity. Google does not publish a numerical weight or guaranteed uplift for the five tags. [Google: App Discovery and Ranking](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9958766?hl=en), undated; retrieved 15 Oct 2026.

Google's curated benchmark groups use its review-team tagging systems. That statement does **not** establish that a developer's five selections solely determine the peer group. [Google: Peer benchmark groups](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10771707), undated; retrieved 15 Oct 2026.

A particularly fresh first-hand source is Google's **13 Oct 2026** announcement: it advises keeping descriptions, tags and promotional events current for new functionality and discovery surfaces. It describes Ask Play using listings/reviews and Gemini matching needs to listing features. It gives no complete taxonomy and no tag-specific rank coefficient. [Android Developers Blog, Paul Feng](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/10/new-tools-for-discovery-and-engagement.html). Read this freshness advice together with the more specific tag-selection help; do not infer a reason to rotate tags weekly without a product change.

The official documentation supports a discovery role. The specific claim that changing tags can alter Similar Apps has the dated AppTweak observation above; the reviewed Google pages do not disclose that carousel's tag algorithm. Neither “tags have zero search effect” nor “these tags will rank us above HabitNow” is established.

**Policy:** Google's Metadata policy prohibits misleading or irrelevant metadata; its publishing guidance says misleading listing information can be rejected. Neither page reviewed gives a tag-only automatic suspension rule. Applying the general policy to a materially misleading tag is a reasonable interpretation, while claiming every imperfect tag causes rejection, suspension or a quantified ranking penalty would go beyond the evidence. [Google: Metadata](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842?hl=en) and [Helpful publishing tips](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15191715?hl=en), both undated; retrieved 15 Oct 2026.

**Download risk, inferred:** an irrelevant label may attract people whose expectations the app does not meet, reducing useful discovery or conversion. That is a sensible reason to avoid it, not a measured Often Enough effect.

## 4. Launch shortlist and exclusions

The strict official-only verification condition remains open. The following is a **provisional recommendation using corroborated reported fixed-list names**, not an assertion that this research independently saw the current official picker:

| Priority | Exact label to verify | One-line reason and evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Productivity** | Habits, one-off/repeating tasks and routine execution are the app's core organizing work; label appears in both inventories, and the eight observed Productivity competitors support the category fit, not their saved tag choice. |
| 2 | **Self-help** | Building and quitting habits toward personal goals is a central self-improvement use; label appears in both inventories; fit is reasoned from the user's brief, not a claim that a competitor selected it. |

Tag-name evidence for both rows: [Appeak Pro, 28 Sep 2026](https://appeak.pro/aso-guide/os-algorithm/google-play-tags), corroborated by [AppTweak, 19 Sep 2024](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/complete-list-of-available-google-play-store-tags). Priority is our order of relevance; no official tag-order ranking bonus was found.

I would leave the other three slots empty at launch. Lifestyle is broad enough to be plausible but adds less precision. Calendar is not justified merely by schedule/history views; Clock, alarm & timer is not justified merely by habit reminders and embedded timers. Activity tracker should not be stretched from general habit logging into a physical-activity promise. Health & fitness, Meditation, Sleep, Workout and Weight loss should not be selected merely because a person can create habits with those names. These exclusions are our relevance judgments based on the brief, not Google's app-specific ruling.

No verified picker evidence was found for the tempting names “Habit Tracker,” “Daily Planner,” “Task Management,” “Goal Setting” or “Reminder.” Do not enter them into a recommendation as confirmed selectable tags. In particular, **Time management appears in AppTweak's game list**; its occurrence there is not evidence of an app tag for productivity software.

Treat his keyword advice as separate ASO work, within Google's metadata policy.

The newer [Sonar article](https://trysonar.app/blog/play-store-app-tags), published **11 Jun 2026**, updated **1 Oct 2026**, suggests the labels “Self Improvement,” “Health” and “Daily Planner” for a habit app. Those spellings were not verified in the checked inventories. A recent date and an ASO title do not make them official options. I did not adopt that example, its traffic-uplift anecdote, or its categorical algorithm claims.

This is why the recommendation uses fewer tags and retains the Console verification limitation instead of inventing five precise-sounding terms.

## 6. Concrete next step when creating the first listing

At the Manage tags step, inspect the exact labels in Suggested tags and the full searchable list. Confirm Productivity and Self-help, including cross-category search for the latter. If either is absent, do not substitute an imagined synonym; record the visible alternatives and reassess. Preserve the final chosen set and its date as first-hand evidence.

Once published, record a baseline of search/browse visitors, acquisitions and conversion in the available acquisition reports, along with release and promotion dates. Compare sufficiently long periods before interpreting movement. A first launch has no pre-launch organic baseline; initial changes cannot isolate a tag effect from release growth. No claim is made that Play offers a controlled five-tag experiment or a stable tag-specific Similar Apps metric.

Research is complete to the stated public-evidence limit. The live taxonomy and saved launch selections remain unverified until that publishing step. No account access, developer outreach, paid ASO subscription or store edit is needed to read this report.
