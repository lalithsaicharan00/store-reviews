# The Habit Day Sheet — Wording, Hierarchy and Actions

Written by Codex, 4 October 2026.

**Handoff status after the user's later reviews:** this report documents the accepted *overall layout and interaction direction*, including the changes to native-size equal-footprint buttons, note/Skip placement, skip recovery, removal of the bottom day pager, goal-aware CTA emphasis, larger outside spacing and the Close icon. The 21 [Markdown-renderable wireframes](<Day Details Wireframes.md>) and [decision history](<Decision History — Why the Layout Changed.md>) show every state and why it changed. The Figma pop-ups are **representations, not final iOS designs**: implement native SwiftUI buttons, menu, `xmark`, note preview, typography and accessibility instead of copying their glyphs or pixel dimensions (U1/U9/U18). The user found the revised composition better for the intended experience; app implementation and device/usability validation are still pending.

## Decision to test

Call the surface **Day details** in product and design documentation. In the sheet, use the selected day as the short context title: **Today**, **Yesterday**, or **Sat, 3 Oct**. The habit name appears once in the identity block below. This gives the sheet a stable purpose without repeating “Today · Coffee” over “Coffee.” A one-time task uses its planned date.

The sheet answers three questions, in order: **What happened on this day? What can I correct here? What can I do with this habit?** The row’s round control remains the fastest logging path; opening the row is chiefly for inspection and correction. The current implementation answers these with the same “Result / Add Entry / Today's Entries” sequence for nearly every kind. That uniform wording exposes the database model instead of the user’s action. This is the user’s observed problem, confirmed in `iOS/Habits/Today/DaySheet.swift` and `DayEntriesSection.swift`.

This is a design proposal, not a validated user preference. The eight supplied screenshots are evidence of the current UI, not evidence that users prefer a specific replacement. Existing review research found that people expect a row tap to show the habit and its notes, and that editing should be easier than deleting: [Today's Rows — Tap, Swipe, the Day Sheet and Delete](<../Today's Rows — Tap, Swipe, the Day Sheet and Delete.md>). Its review counts are scoped to the reviews read there; this report does not turn them into new counts.

The [editable Figma study](https://www.figma.com/design/Ncccsm1l2O62GJ5xLSInqk/Design?node-id=370-2031) contains 21 variants beside the [current screenshots](https://www.figma.com/design/Ncccsm1l2O62GJ5xLSInqk/Design?node-id=366-2039). It covers the main tracking types, done/undone, a past day, weekly and monthly checks, skipped binary and amount days, a paused state, a limit above its threshold, a running timer, a recorded slip, light mode and three task states. The unused centered-header comparison was removed after the user's decision to use leading-aligned identity throughout. The proposal uses editable Figma text and frames; the supplied screenshots were references, not imported into it.

**Revised after the user's subsequent reviews on 4 October:** the oversized logging controls are now 48 pt specimens for the native regular-size intent. Quick and manual controls have equal height and width, while style conveys their priority. The optional note has a labelled, text-area-like preview field; tapping it opens the existing separate note editor. Skip is a separate, 44 pt bordered button. When tapped, that same button becomes **Undo skip** in the same position; logging controls stay visible but disabled, while saved logs and notes remain visible. Bottom day paging has been removed from the proposal. These dimensions document the prototype, not a fixed SwiftUI size: implementation should use native `controlSize(.regular)` and adapt to Dynamic Type.

## Existing user evidence reused

- [Undo and Fixing Progress — What People Expect](<../Undo and Fixing Progress — What People Expect.md>) read 2,677 matched App Store and Play Store reviews in September 2026; 1,084 were on topic, including 715 habit-app reviews. Of those habit-app reviews, 445 described an accidental log and 47 wanted to correct one amount/time record instead of clearing the day (for example, Habit Tracker review `9466621749`). This directly supports keeping individual log correction visible. It does not prove which label to use.
- [Today's Rows — Tap, Swipe, the Day Sheet and Delete](<../Today's Rows — Tap, Swipe, the Day Sheet and Delete.md>) read 1,700 matched reviews in October 2026; 794 were on topic. Its row-tap group included 16 reviews expecting a tap to show the habit and four expecting notes there (for example, Habit Tracker review `10410891202`). This supports showing the note and a clear route to the habit page. The groups overlap and are not a population-wide preference estimate.
- The same row-actions report records the competing delete concerns: easy accidental deletion and difficulty finding Delete. Its Productive review `3655095954` describes editing as too hard while deleting is too easy. Together with Rulebook U14, this supports grouping management in the familiar ⋯ menu, with Delete last and confirmed. The exact visual placement still needs usability review.
- [Habit Notes and Day Notes — What People Ask For](<../Habit Notes and Day Notes — What People Ask For.md>) hand-read note-related review hits from a 1,487,223-review scan in September 2026. Its 56 reviews in 25 apps wanted or praised a note on a habit's day; Way of Life's notes drew 174 positive reviews among its 7,764 reviews. Reviewers also complained about forced prompts after a check or skip (for example, Loop `6303e9f0-0e10-40ff-9685-fe1e9d8709b7`). This supports an accessible, optional note entry point and a visible saved note, without giving Add note the same weight as logging. It does not establish a preferred pixel position in this sheet.
- The earlier [Today row and Day sheet report](<../Today's Rows — Tap, Swipe, the Day Sheet and Delete.md>) proposed copying Today's `‹ day ›` bottom control into the sheet (its recommendations 2 and 3). That was a consistency inference, not a finding that reviewers navigated between dates *inside* a Day sheet. The [past-day calendar study](<../Filling In a Past Day From the Habit Page.md>) supports tapping a date in the habit calendar to open and correct that day; it does not establish a need for an additional pager once the sheet is open. Past-day editing remains essential, but its in-sheet paging shortcut has weak evidence.

## External principles checked

- Apple describes a [sheet](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets) as a focused task in the current context. The selected day and habit provide that context; the sheet should show the most relevant information first. Apple also describes medium detents as a way to show the most relevant content first, although a full-height sheet can be right when editing several records.
- Apple’s [toggle guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toggles) says a toggle represents two opposing states and its state should be visually clear without color alone. A binary check can use one clearly labelled state control; a repeated count cannot be represented by that same switch without hiding how many checks exist.
- Apple’s [menu guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus) puts frequent commands first and groups related commands. It supports a familiar ⋯ menu for edit, pause/resume, archive and delete, ordered by frequency and consequence. Apple’s [context-menu guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/context-menus) places destructive actions last. The exact menu arrangement below is a design inference, not an Apple mandate.
- Apple’s [button guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons) calls for at least a 44 × 44 pt hit region, recommends one or two prominent actions per view, and says equal-size buttons signal a coherent choice set. It explicitly recommends using **style, not size**, to distinguish the preferred choice among equal-size buttons. SwiftUI’s [regular control size](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/controlsize/regular) is the default. It does not specify one universal iPhone button height; the implementation should use native styles and Dynamic Type rather than hard-code the mockup height.
- Apple’s [text-view guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-views) describes a text view as suitable for longer, potentially editable notes. [Reminders on iPhone](https://support.apple.com/en-lamr/guide/iphone/iphec7a1de82/ios) gives an item an **Add Note** affordance in its detail context. Together with this app’s existing separate `NoteSheet`, these support a labelled note field/preview that opens the editor on tap. This is a reference pattern, not evidence that every habit user wants the note at a specific vertical position.
- Nielsen Norman Group’s [match with the real world](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/match-system-real-world/) and [visibility of status](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visibility-system-status/) principles support plain state labels and unit-specific actions rather than the abstract “Result” and “entry.” These are heuristic arguments, not usability-test results for this app.

## Spacing and grouping (4 October)

The 21 variants previously gave nearly every top-level element the same 14 pt gap. That made status, logging, logs, note and Skip read as one undifferentiated stack. The user asked for **space between elements**, especially around the day activity and logs, rather than more padding inside their cards.

The [Nielsen Norman Group proximity principle](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-proximity/) explains why: close items are perceived as related, while a larger gap signals a separate group. Its examples place a heading closer to its own content than to the preceding group. Apple’s [UI design guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/tips/) likewise advises keeping controls close to the content they modify and aligning elements to show relationships. These principles support a hierarchy of gaps; they do not prescribe the exact point values below. The values are Figma specimens to check on an iPhone with Dynamic Type (U1/U9).

| Relationship in the sheet | Figma gap | Why |
|---|---:|---|
| Toolbar → habit identity | 14 pt | The two establish where the person is; the toolbar's own height already gives its title breathing room. |
| Habit identity → **This day** activity | **28 pt** | Separates permanent habit identity/navigation from the selected day's state and records. This is an outside margin, not added card padding. |
| **This day** status → its logging control or checklist steps | **10 pt** | The control changes the state directly above it, so these stay close. |
| Quick log → manual log | **8 pt** | They are two routes to the same recording job, at equal native-size footprints. |
| Logging controls → **Today's logs** subgroup | **18 pt** | Separates adding progress from inspecting/correcting saved records while keeping both inside day activity. |
| **Today's logs** heading → its records | **8 pt** | The heading clearly belongs to that list; the same applies to checks and slips. |
| Entire day activity → day note | **28 pt** | Gives the status, controls and logs a clear bottom margin before the optional context note. |
| Day note → Skip/Undo skip | **24 pt** | The note field and the day-state action have different jobs; the larger gap prevents them looking like one control set. |

The same nesting is applied to binary, checklist, amount, duration, limit, quit, task, paused and skipped variants. When there are no saved records, there is no empty logs group or artificial spacer. An exposed management-menu preview and a task-date row also start after a 28 pt section gap. The mockups use nested Figma auto-layout frames so these are **inter-element margins**; card heights and internal text padding were not inflated to simulate separation. In SwiftUI, preserve the relative rhythm with semantic stacks and system-appropriate spacing, then inspect it at larger text sizes instead of copying point values blindly.

## Close icon in the Day sheet (4 October)

For this **inspection sheet**, use an icon-only close control at the top trailing edge rather than the word **Close**. This sheet has no unsaved draft to submit: its logging and day actions commit when tapped, and its note opens a separate editor. An `xmark` therefore communicates dismissal without implying another save step. Apple’s [toolbar guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars) recommends the standard Close symbol for a modal view instead of a text label saying “Close.” Its [sheet guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets) distinguishes dismissal from **Done** after completing a task. This is a stronger platform rationale than visual neatness alone.

The 21 Figma variants now show a simple `×` proxy for the iOS SF Symbol **`xmark`**, centred in a 44 × 44 pt invisible hit region. The leading ⋯ has the same hit region, so the selected-day title remains visually centred. In SwiftUI, use a semantic button such as `Button("Close", systemImage: "xmark")` displayed icon-only; its accessible name remains **Close** for VoiceOver, as described in Apple’s [SwiftUI Button documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/button). Keep **Cancel**, **Save** or **Done** text where a separate editor actually has a draft or completion step, including the note editor. Do not treat the icon swap as a change to Skip, note or log behavior.

## Proposed information hierarchy

1. **Toolbar:** selected day as title; ⋯ on the leading side; an icon-only `xmark` with accessible name **Close** on the trailing side. Show **Today**, **Yesterday**, or an unambiguous date for an older day. The date is context, not a second copy of the habit name. Remove the redundant bottom date picker and previous/next controls from this proposal; open another day from Today or the habit calendar. This changes the earlier research proposal's in-sheet paging shortcut, under Rulebook U5, without removing past-day editing.
2. **Habit identity:** icon, name and plan. The whole identity row, including a labelled “View habit” affordance and chevron, opens the habit page. Do not make the name alone a hidden touch target. A task has no habit-page link.
3. **Day status:** one prominent, literal statement such as “Not done,” “Done,” “5 of 20 min,” “2 of 8 glasses,” “0 of 2 cups · within limit,” “2 of 3 steps,” or “No slips recorded.” For period goals, show the selected day’s state and a separate smaller period line, such as “2 of 3 checks this week”; never imply the weekly total is today's count. A limit is never styled as a completion target to fill.
4. **Day activity and correction:** type-specific control, then only the records or steps that can actually be changed. For an empty binary day there is no empty “entries” card. Where both quick and manual logging exist, show them as an adjacent pair with the **same native regular control height and width**. Use style to show priority: a prominent quick action for positive target habits, a standard bordered manual option; both bordered for a limit. For quantitative tracking, show logs immediately after those controls when present, with amount, time and source; tapping one edits that one. Keep status, controls and logs together as the **This day** activity region. The controls stay in a stable position as records appear; logs are the correction surface below them. Do not add a blank logs card.
5. **Note and day action:** a note belongs to the selected habit **and selected day**, so place it after that day's activity rather than directly beneath the habit identity link, which could imply a permanent habit description. Use one full-width, labelled note field/preview: show the existing note text or **Add a note…** when empty. Tapping it opens the separate note editor; this sheet does not suddenly start typing inline. The note is available even when the day is skipped. Never prompt for a note after logging or skipping. **Skip today / Skip this day** is a real, labelled, native-size bordered button below the note, aligned to the same width, with **no chevron** or navigation styling. It remains in place and changes its label to **Undo skip** while skipped. Show a neutral paused/skipped explanation where needed.
6. **Habit management:** place **Edit Habit**, **Pause/Resume**, **Archive**, and **Delete Habit…** together in ⋯, with a separator before Archive/Delete. The identity row supplies navigation to the habit page. The menu can include “View Habit” as a redundant labelled route for accessibility/discoverability, but the sheet itself has only one visible habit-page row. Delete remains confirmed, with Archive Instead, under U14. This resolves the current split between inline edit/pause and menu archive/delete while avoiding an entire screen of management rows.

The user suggested a centered icon/name/plan. The mockups should explore this against a compact leading-aligned identity row. Centering can look calm on a short sheet, but a leading-aligned row gives long names and two-line plans a stable scan edge and makes the chevron clearer. Do not declare either better until inspected at small screen and large Dynamic Type.

## What Skip does

For an eligible day-based habit, tapping **Skip today** (or **Skip this day** while viewing an earlier day) immediately marks only the selected day as **Skipped**. The day status becomes neutral. The **same button in the same position** changes to **Undo skip**; it does not take over the primary logging slot. Existing logging controls stay visible, labelled as before, and disabled while the day is skipped. An existing note remains visible and editable; the empty note field still opens the editor. Existing logs remain visible and saved rather than being silently deleted. Undo skip restores logging without moving the controls or recreating records. The current `HabitStore.setSkipped` stores only the day flag; Rulebook U15 now records this stable sheet behavior, alongside U14's row Undo model. Do not insert a confirmation or ask why the person skipped.

`HabitStore.canSkip` excludes limits, quit habits, and weekly/monthly/yearly total goals. The revised mockups therefore remove Skip from those states. A one-time task does not use the habit Skip control in the current sheet. The skip action should not be shown where the app cannot carry it out. **Implementation gap to fix later:** the current `DaySheet.swift` disables the quick logging controls on skipped days but its generic **Add Entry** button is not disabled for ordinary skipped habits; apply the same disabled rule to every new-log path during implementation. Preserve the existing saved-record inspection/correction path.

## Why the note is below day activity

Placing the note immediately below the habit identity makes it highly visible, but visually couples a dated note to the permanent habit-page link and pushes the current state down. Placing it after logs keeps the sequence **state → correct this day → context about this day**. The saved note itself is displayed in full enough to scan and edit. The empty field remains one tap away and has a full touch target. Its label **Note for this day** makes the day scope explicit; its preview surface is distinct from the action buttons. This is an inference from the sheet's job and the note reviews, not a direct A/B result.

## Action and wording matrix

| Type / state | Prominent state and action | Correction content | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Once-daily check, incomplete | “Not done” + **Mark done** | None until checked | Add Entry; empty Today's Entries |
| Once-daily check, complete | “Done” + **Undo done** | Optional completion time only if useful, never a one-item entries section | A second Done switch beside the same state |
| One check on a weekly/monthly goal | “Not checked today” / “Checked today”; small **2 of 3 this week** or **5 of 12 this month** | Mark/undo this day’s check; period total stays separate | Presenting the period goal as today’s target |
| Repeated check in a day | **2 of 3 checks today** + **Add a check** | Show today's checks only when present; each has time and named Undo | Done switch that marks the whole day and Add Entry beside it |
| Checklist | **2 of 3 steps done** | Named step controls themselves; checked steps can be unchecked | Today's Entries; Add Entry |
| Amount target | **2 of 8 glasses** + prominent **Add 1 glass** | Same-size bordered “Log amount manually”; **Today's logs** only when records exist, each editable | A blank logs card; generic “Result” |
| Amount limit | **0 cups today · limit 2** + bordered **Add 1 cup** | Same-size bordered “Log amount manually”; exact logs; if over limit, factual “3 cups · limit 2” with no shame color/copy | A filled primary Add 1 cup or a progress bar that implies reaching 2 is the aim |
| Time target | **5 of 20 min** + prominent **Start timer** | Same-size bordered “Log time manually”; logged sessions editable, including seconds and source | Add Entry without saying it opens manual time |
| Quit habit | **No slips recorded** or **1 slip recorded** | Bordered **Record a slip**; show slips only when present, tap to edit time/undo | “No entries yet,” “relapse,” or treating a slip as success progress |
| One-time task | **Not done** / **Done** with one Mark done/Undo done control | Date and “Do Tomorrow” when applicable; note; Edit Task in menu | Entry list or day paging |

For a past day, replace “today” in headings and buttons with **this day** or the selected date; permit editing only when the current code considers the day editable. A future or pre-start day is read-only with a direct explanation. A skipped day shows **Skipped** with the usual logging controls disabled, its saved logs and note visible, and the same day-action button labelled **Undo skip**. A paused day shows **Paused** and a clear route to Resume; historical logs remain visible. The menu’s actions apply to the habit, not just the viewed day, so label them accordingly.

## Why “entry” is sometimes right

“Entry” is reasonable as an umbrella noun in **History**, where the user is looking at dated records across time and can add or edit one. The user's feedback explicitly preserves History’s Add Entry action (Current Work Checklist item 22; History contrast is item 26). In the **Day details** sheet, “entry” is less useful than the concrete object: check, step, glass/cup amount, timed session, or slip. For quantitative records, **Today's logs** is an intelligible list heading; for a past day use **Logs for Sat, 3 Oct**. This naming is proposed and should be checked with users, especially whether “log” is understood for water and coffee. “Log amount manually” and “Log time manually” already align with Rulebook U11 and the design rules.

## Prototype acceptance checks

Inspect at least: binary check before/after, weekly check and monthly check on a past day, repeated daily checks, checklist partially done, amount goal empty and with multiple logs, amount limit at/over limit, timer with several sessions, quit without/with a slip, one-time task, skipped binary and skipped amount days with existing logs and a note, paused day, long two-line goal, and light/dark plus accessibility text sizes. Specifically verify that Skip and Undo skip occupy the same position, every new-log control is unavailable while skipped, and saved logs and note remain reachable. A person should identify the day, current state, correction path, note and management path within a short scan. Test the labels with users before treating the copy as final.

Implementation must preserve individual editing and named undo (U5/U14), stable Skip and Undo behavior (U15), goal-aware action priority (U16), meaningful section spacing (U17), the labelled Close symbol (U18), native iOS controls and Dynamic Type (U1), neutral tone (U3), and the source-of-truth day rules (D7). A code change would also require the relevant UI labels/tests (T3), speed scenario (S2/T4), and real-iPhone review (U9). These mockups do not satisfy those implementation gates.
