# Editing One Habit Log — Scope, Fields and Recovery

Written by Codex (OpenAI), 4 October 2026. Research and editable Figma proposal for [Current Work Checklist item 35](<../../../../iOS/Docs/Checklists/Current Work Checklist.md>). This is a design proposal; `EntryEditView` has not been changed. The user's later review prompted the explicit tap-to-type duration interaction and the slip's Date **and** Time fields. The [10 Markdown-renderable wireframes](<Entry Editor Wireframes.md>), [decision history](<Decision History — Why the Layout Changed.md>) and [native implementation contract](<Native Implementation Contract.md>) record the completed handoff. The user found the overall editor direction good, but these pop-ups are **representational wireframes, not final native iOS designs or validated screens**. Implement native controls, keyboard, compact pickers, toolbar and confirmation; do not ship Figma glyphs or geometry (U1/U9/U19).

## The decision in brief

The Day sheet answers **what happened on this day**. Tapping one saved quantitative log should open a focused editor for **that one log**, with its value as the first editable field. A single done/undone check, a checklist step and a one-time task already have simpler named controls in the Day sheet. Giving those a second screen containing only “Times 1” adds work and ambiguity. A quit slip is a record whose meaningful correction is **when it happened — date and clock time together**, not how many times it happened.

Use **Edit log** for amounts, time sessions and a multi-check record; **Edit slip** for a quit record. The Day sheet may still call the collection “Today's logs.” History's existing **Add Entry** terminology is a separate, open item; this proposal does not rename it. These labels are design hypotheses to test, not claims that users prefer one noun in a measured study.

## What was inspected

- The supplied [Entry screenshot section](https://www.figma.com/design/Ncccsm1l2O62GJ5xLSInqk/Design?node-id=400-2095) contains five image examples: Read duration, Call family check, Water amount, Social media timer duration, and Take vitamins check. They show the current `Edit Entry` screen, not editable Figma UI.
- `iOS/Habits/Today/DayEntriesSection.swift` opens `EntryEditView` for every non-step, non-task log. The current form spends its first section on habit/date/source; the amount field omits the habit's unit; checks say “Times”; duration has a large hour/minute picker plus a separate Seconds field; a quit slip edits its timestamp. `Save` changes only the selected record. `Delete Entry` removes it immediately.
- `HabitStore.editEntry` preserves the record ID, tracked day, slot, source and time zone. It can change a value, and can change the timestamp **only for a quit slip** while enforcing the original local day, no future time, and the habit-start lower bound. `undoEntry` removes one exact record. The revised slip mockup intentionally goes beyond this current behavior by allowing its occurrence date to change; **implementation must first add an atomic record-day move**. Amount and duration date/time remain read-only context in this proposal.
- The existing [Undo and Fixing Progress research](<../Undo and Fixing Progress — What People Expect.md>) hand-read 2,677 candidate reviews in September 2026; 1,084 were on topic, including 715 reviews from 84 habit apps. Among the habit-app set, 47 reviews in 22 apps wanted to fix one amount or time record, and 54 in 30 apps wanted to change a past day's result. These counts support **individual correction and access through day/history**, not a specific field arrangement or a claim that every type needs an editor. That report also notes 382 reviews in 52 apps unable to undo mistaken logging. Review IDs and methods remain in the original report.

## External design principles checked

- [Apple's Entering data guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/entering-data) recommends clear labels, prefilled known values, appropriate input methods and dynamic validation. That supports showing the saved number, its unit and an immediately understandable invalid state instead of a generic blank “Amount.”
- [Apple's Pickers guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pickers) warns that a picker can add visual weight, suggests keeping it in context, and says a compact date picker is appropriate where space is constrained. Apple's countdown picker represents hours and minutes, not fractional seconds. For precise corrections to an existing duration, three small numeric fields keep the original seconds visible; that choice is our inference, not an Apple requirement. For a slip's occurrence, compact native date and time pickers are a more direct fit.
- [Apple's Text fields guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-fields?changes=_7) recommends appropriately sized fields, logical focus order, number formatting and validation. [SwiftUI FocusState](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/focusstate/) supports moving focus deliberately among Hour, Minute and Second fields. This supports an explicit number-pad interaction instead of a large wheel plus a disconnected Seconds row.
- [Apple's Sheets guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets?changes=_1_1) puts Cancel/Back and Done/Save in the expected top-toolbar positions and calls for handling unsaved changes on dismissal. The editor is pushed within an existing sheet, so a leading Back and trailing Save are appropriate; Back must discard the draft, and a dirty draft needs a discard choice if dismissed by gesture.
- [Apple's Alerts guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts) says to avoid alerts for readily undoable common actions, but confirm an uncommon destructive action that cannot be undone. The current record deletion has no persistent recovery control, so the proposal shows a specifically worded confirmation until a reliable one-record Undo exists.
- [Nielsen Norman Group's form grouping research](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/form-design-white-space/) says labels and their fields should be closer to one another than to the next group. [Its proximity principle](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-proximity/) supports a compact value group separated from secondary provenance and the destructive action. Exact spacing below is a mockup choice to test on device.

No competitor screen was adopted just because another app uses it. The review study establishes the need to fix individual records; native guidance and the app's own Day-sheet model inform this layout.

## Which habit records open a separate editor?

| Saved record | Proposed action from Day sheet | Editor content | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amount toward a positive target, e.g. Water | Tap that log → **Edit log** | Amount with visible unit; read-only day/source context; Save; Delete log | A wrong one-glass or manually entered amount should be corrected without resetting the whole day. |
| Amount under an at-most limit, e.g. Coffee | Tap that log → **Edit log** | Same amount fields and equal affordance; neutral copy | The recorded cups are factual, not progress toward a target. Do not make adding or saving consumption a celebratory primary action. |
| Duration, whether manual, timer or routine player | Tap that session → **Edit log** | Tap-to-type Hours, Minutes, Seconds together; source and day; Save; Delete log | Each session can be corrected independently. Preserve fractional seconds already stored by the app. |
| Repeated check with a **multi-check value in one record** | Tap that record → **Edit log** | Integer count with the habit's check unit; Save; Delete log | `setDayDone` or a manual log can create a record representing more than one check. Editing its count is meaningful. |
| Repeated check with a **single-check record** | Direct named **Undo check** on that Day-sheet log; no form | None | There is no smaller positive integer to edit. Removing that specific check is the correction. Keep any other same-day checks. |
| Single done/undone check, including a single check toward a weekly or monthly frequency | **Undo done / Undo this day's check** in Day sheet; no form | None | “Times 1” is a second representation of the same binary state. The period goal remains separate from the selected day. |
| Checklist step | Toggle that named step in Day sheet; no form | None | The named step is the unit of correction. Do not expose a generic count editor. |
| One-time task | Mark done / Undo done in Day sheet; no form | None | It is a binary task state, not an editable log amount. |
| Quit slip | Tap that slip → **Edit slip** | **When it happened:** compact native Date and Time pickers, recorded time zone, Save; Delete slip | A wrong date is a correction to the occurrence, not a reason to delete a real slip and create another. Never show “Amount” or “Times 1.” |

The current `DayEntriesSection` gives all non-step, non-task records a chevron. Implementing this matrix requires routing and explicit per-record Undo labels. Do not merely hide a screen while leaving a dead row. Where the existing Day sheet already has a direct undo for a binary day, reuse it; where several checks exist, the undo target must identify the selected record, not erase the whole day (Rulebook U14).

## Proposed screen hierarchy and behavior

1. **Toolbar:** a native Back target on the leading edge, “Edit log” or “Edit slip” centered, Save on the trailing edge. Save is enabled for a valid draft; an unchanged draft may simply dismiss. Back returns without changing the record. The exact label “Day details” can be provided as the accessible destination even if the narrow mockup shows “Back.” Keep a discard confirmation for a dirty draft on a swipe or Back, rather than silently losing typed work.
2. **Compact identity/context:** habit icon and name, then the selected day and record's source. This confirms **which log** is being edited without a large introductory card. Treat source as provenance, not a control. The existing `.daySheet` source label “History” may be clearer as “Added from History” in this context; this is display copy only.
3. **The editable fact:** show one large, clearly labelled value group. Amounts keep their unit beside the input, with singular/plural handled by app copy rules. Duration has small, adjacent **Hours · Minutes · Seconds** numeric fields, including decimal seconds for sub-minute timer logs. A count record has “Checks in this log” and its unit. A slip has **Date** and **Time** rows under “When it happened,” both native compact pickers for one occurrence timestamp. Do not place the goal or day total inside the input; this edit changes a single saved record, not the plan or whole day.
4. **Scope hint and metadata:** a short “Only this log changes” hint when other logs exist; source and tracked day remain readable. For a backfilled log, `createdAt` is the recording timestamp and may be later than the tracked day, so avoid calling it “Time completed.” A slip's saved recording time zone remains visible, especially when it differs from the phone's current zone. The selected Date and Time use that zone so the same displayed values identify the same occurrence.
5. **Destructive action:** “Delete this log” or “Delete slip” is a separate, less prominent red row below the form with generous distance from Save and the numeric field. It removes only the selected record. Since current deletion has no durable Undo, confirmation names the exact amount/session/slip and tracked day, with **Cancel** and **Delete log/slip**. If implementation later provides reliable, reachable one-record Undo, the confirmation can be reconsidered under Apple's alert guidance.

Use the existing Day-sheet light/dark colors and SF Pro/system typography. Mocked controls are a visual approximation: implementation should use regular native SwiftUI controls, Dynamic Type, a usable keyboard Done affordance, and at least 44-point touch targets (Rulebook U1/U18). Group label-to-field spacing around 8–10 pt; separate identity, field and destructive action with roughly 24–28 pt outside gaps. These are proposed relationships, not fixed coordinates at all text sizes (U17).

## How a duration is actually changed

The three adjacent values in the initial mockup were directionally right but did not show what tapping them does. **On entry, keep the keyboard closed** so someone can read the saved session or delete it without an unsolicited keyboard. Tapping one value selects that entire value and opens the system keyboard. Hours and Minutes use the number pad; Seconds uses the locale-appropriate decimal pad. The keyboard accessory says **Next** from Hours → Minutes → Seconds, then **Done** from Seconds; Save stays in the top toolbar. If someone taps Seconds directly, they can edit only Seconds and tap Done. No scroll/type mode switch is needed in this correction screen.

For example, a saved `0 h · 5 min · 00 sec` session becomes `0 h · 12 min · 00 sec` by tapping **Minutes** and typing `12`. A timer record of `0 h · 0 min · 1.23 sec` keeps `1.23` in the Seconds field and uses a decimal pad if edited. The current `ProgressValueDraft` and locale-aware `GoalNumber.parse` already hold separate text values and accept up to two decimal places. Keep each draft field in its own small view (Rulebook S11); do not redraw the whole form per keystroke. The focused field and keyboard must remain visible above the keyboard on small phones and at larger Dynamic Type sizes (U6). Do not silently clamp 75 minutes to 59 or round away fractional seconds: show the reason near the field and disable Save until corrected.

This direct-typing recommendation is a task-specific inference from the review evidence about fixing wrong times and Apple's input guidance. The existing large Scroll/Type `DurationInput` remains a separate habit-goal/add-flow decision; this proposal only changes **editing an already saved session**.

## How a slip's date and time are changed

“Slipped at” means an **instant**, not merely a clock time on an immutable day. The revised editor shows a **Date** row and a **Time** row in one “When it happened” group. Tapping Date opens the native compact calendar picker; tapping Time opens the native time picker. Editing one keeps the other value. Both display in the record's saved time zone, shown directly below. Save validates that the instant is not in the future or before the permitted start, preserves the slip's ID/source/time zone, and recomputes the app's tracking day from the new instant and configured day-start rule (D7). A changed date must not be applied by removing the old slip and adding a new one: that would disturb identity and create a gap or duplicate if persistence fails.

The date correction is a response to the user's review of the slip mockup and the meaning of an occurrence timestamp. The review counts above do not measure demand for changing a slip across days; this interaction needs device and user validation before release.

**Implementation gap:** the present `HabitStore.editEntry` rejects a slip timestamp on a different tracked day, and the repository update writes only value and `createdAt`. The Day-sheet per-day indexes likewise assume the record stays in its original bucket. Before this revised mockup can ship, add one atomic update of `createdAt` **and** `entry.day` for the same record ID, move the in-memory record between day buckets, persist/sync the new day and timestamp together, then recalculate quit runs and affected day summaries. Test travel time zones, day-start boundaries and daylight-saving changes. When Save moves a slip to another tracking day, return to the **destination Day sheet** so the edited record remains visible. The prior day's note stays on its original day; a note is attached to the day, not the slip. Until that storage path exists, keep the current date read-only in the app rather than showing an apparently working Date picker that cannot save.

## Edge cases the design must preserve

- **Zero, negative, nonfinite and over-maximum values** cannot be saved; checks are positive whole numbers; amounts retain up to two decimal places; duration minutes and seconds remain below 60 and total time must be positive. Show the invalid reason near the field and disable Save; never silently coerce the user's input.
- **Fractional timer seconds:** a 1.23-second session must reopen as 0 hours, 0 minutes, 1.23 seconds and survive Save. The proposed numeric row makes precision visible; the current `ProgressValueDraft` already stores seconds to two decimals.
- **Quit slips:** show and edit the occurrence's date and time in the saved time zone; validate the actual instant, not merely displayed calendar components. A permitted date change can move the record to another tracking day, with an atomic same-ID move and destination-day navigation. Do not permit a future instant or one before the permitted start. Deleting a slip changes the quit run, so the confirmation must say what will be removed, without shame language.
- **Multiple records:** saving or deleting one ID leaves the others, note and skip flag untouched. The Day sheet must recalculate the status and reflect the new value immediately. A skipped day can still inspect/correct existing logs under U15; new logging remains disabled.
- **Past days and sources:** the tracking day remains read-only context for amount, duration and count logs. Changing those logs to another day is outside this proposal and would need its own move-record interaction. The date picker in the revised mockup applies **only to quit slips**, whose factual occurrence time is already their defining value.

## Figma review set

The [editable board beside the supplied Entry screenshots](https://www.figma.com/design/Ncccsm1l2O62GJ5xLSInqk/Design?node-id=408-2071) shows: Water amount; Coffee at-most amount; Read manual duration; Social media fractional timer duration; a multi-check record; a quit slip with **date and time** in its saved time zone; a deletion confirmation; and a light-mode amount variant. Two added interaction states show the minute field focused with a number pad and a slip whose occurrence date/time has changed in the draft. The adjacent note specifies focus, decimal seconds, native pickers, validation and destination-day behavior. A routing note names binary checks, checklist steps, one-time tasks and single-check records as **Day-sheet correction, no separate editor**. All ten mockups are editable Figma layers, rather than flattened screenshots; the board reuses the existing Day-sheet light/dark color variables.

**Before build:** test on an iPhone and with larger text: (1) can a person identify the one record; (2) can they tap a time field, edit 1.23 seconds and 2.5 glasses without losing precision; (3) can they distinguish Save from deleting; (4) does Back preserve the original when cancelled; (5) does deleting one of several logs leave the others; (6) can a slip's date change across a day-start or time-zone boundary without losing its ID or note; (7) are light/dark, VoiceOver labels, time zones and keyboards correct. Code changes require U9/T3/S2/T4 checks later. The current research and mockups do not claim those checks have passed.
