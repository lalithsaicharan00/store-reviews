# Decision History — Why the Layout Changed

Written by Codex (OpenAI), 4 October 2026. This records the full Day-details and one-record editor design conversation, including decisions made after the initial research and mockups. The [handoff index](README.md) links the evidence reports and every exported state.

## Status of the decision

The user said the revised overall Day-details layout looked good and later said the Entry-editor version looked good, then asked for specific final refinements. We retained the resulting **interaction and hierarchy direction** because it answered the user's repeated problems: the selected day's result can be scanned, existing records can be corrected, note and skip have distinct jobs, and habit management has one predictable place. The user asked that this rationale and the complete revision trail be documented. **These are wireframes for implementation, not final screen designs, a completed code change, or a substitute for iPhone/usability validation.** Treat exact Figma styling, widths and glyphs as illustrative; preserve the intent through native iOS controls (U1/U9).

## Revision trail

| Stage | What was wrong or uncertain | What changed | Why this was retained |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current app audit | The sheet repeated the habit name in the title and card, used “Result,” “Goal,” “Add Entry” and empty “Today's Entries” for unrelated habit types; direct logging already existed on the home row. Edit/Pause were visible while Archive/Delete were in ⋯. | Defined **Day details** as the selected day's inspection and correction surface. Toolbar names the date, identity row names the habit once and opens its page, type-specific status and actions replace the generic rows, management is grouped in ⋯. | One scan now answers what happened, what can be corrected, and where the habit-level actions live. This follows the user's purpose for the pop-up and U5/U11/U14. |
| First mockups | A uniform “entry” mental model made a binary check or checklist look like repeated amount logs. | Built variants for binary daily/periodic checks, repeated checks, checklist, positive amount, at-most amount, timer, quit, task, past day and other states. Show saved logs only where individual records exist; no empty log card. | The sheet keeps a stable structure while each habit uses its natural action and status. The exact nouns are proposed copy, not measured winners. |
| Button-size review | The first logging controls looked oversized for an iPhone; quick and manual logging looked unequal. | Reduced specimens to native regular-size intent and made quick/manual controls the same footprint. Styles, not size, show priority. | The user approved the general design but wanted it to feel native. Apple button guidance supports coherent equal-size choice sets; SwiftUI must choose actual native control sizing (U1/U16). |
| Note and Skip review | A note looked like another action button. Skip looked like a navigation row and, after tapping, the mockup replaced the main CTA with Undo and removed progress/note. | Made the **Note for this day** surface a labelled, text-area-like preview that opens the existing separate editor. Made Skip a real bordered button; on tap **that same button becomes Undo skip in the same position**. Keep saved logs/note visible and editable; keep new-log controls visible but disabled. | The recovery action stays where the person acted, and skipping only changes that day's status. Existing information does not disappear. The user explicitly rejected the earlier behavior (U15). |
| Day navigation review | Bottom `‹ date ›` controls repeated the date, took space and could cover content. The earlier proposal inferred consistency from Today but had no evidence that people often page *within* an open sheet. | Removed the bottom pager. The toolbar says Today, Yesterday or a dated label. Open a different day's sheet from Today or the habit calendar; a top-title date picker remains a future test only if usage warrants it. | Past-day access remains while the sheet has room for content and no redundant navigation. See the Day-sheet report's evidence discussion (U5). |
| Action priority review | A filled Add 1 cup on an at-most-two-cups habit suggests consuming more is the goal; every habit cannot share the same visual encouragement. | Positive-target quick actions may be prominent (Mark done, Add 1 glass, Start timer). Limit logging and slip recording remain available in standard bordered style. Quick/manual pairs retain equal native-size footprints. | Priority follows the meaning of the goal without hiding factual logging or creating a different layout model (U16). |
| Spacing review | Equal gaps made status, action, logs, note and Skip feel like one long section; adding card padding would not solve grouping. | Nested selected-day status, controls and logs with small internal gaps. Added larger **outside** gaps before and after the whole activity; separated note from Skip. The report records 8/10/18/24/28 pt Figma relationships. | The user liked the revised content and asked for stronger grouping. Proximity and alignment make the hierarchy faster to scan; exact point values are prototypes for Dynamic Type review (U17). |
| Close-control review | A word **Close** used more toolbar width than needed for an inspection sheet with no unsaved draft. | Replaced it in mockups with an `×` proxy. Implement with native SF Symbol `xmark`, an accessible **Close** label and a 44 pt target; balance it against ⋯. | It matches the sheet's dismissal semantics and the user's native-iOS direction. Save/Cancel/Done remain text in actual editors (U18). |
| Entry-editor scope review | Tapping any record opened one generic `Edit Entry` form, including a binary “Times 1.” Its selected-record value was not visually dominant, units were weak and deletion was immediate. | Separate **Edit log** for amount/duration/multi-check values and **Edit slip** for a quit occurrence. Single checks, checklist steps and tasks correct in Day details. Show one selected value, compact source/day context, native Back/Save, and a distinct Delete-this-record confirmation. | The user asked for the same focused research as Day details. Only records with an independent editable fact need another screen; one-record correction remains clear (U19). |
| Duration interaction review | Three time fields alone did not explain whether the person types, scrolls or picks; the current large wheel feels heavy for changing one saved session. | Kept Hours/Minutes/Seconds visible together. Tap one value to select and type; number pad for Hours/Minutes, decimal pad for Seconds; Next/Done moves focus. Keyboard stays closed on initial open. Preserve 1.23-second precision and show errors rather than silently clamping. | It makes correction direct while retaining the exact original value. This is an inference from Apple's input guidance and the app's fractional-second model, not a measured comparison (S11/U6/U19). |
| Slip-occurrence review | A slip records **when it happened**, not only the clock time on a permanently fixed day. | Added separate native compact Date and Time picker rows, saved time-zone context, and a draft state that moves the slip to the destination day. Documented an atomic same-ID `createdAt`/tracked-day update before shipping. | Deleting and re-adding a true slip merely to fix its date would be confusing and risks identity/data consistency. Current code cannot move the day yet, so the picker is a proposal until D7/U19 storage work is complete. |
| Entry-editor Delete placement | The red text action below a divider read like a loose form row, not a button; the user wanted Delete at the bottom and a confirmation pop-up on tap. | Replaced the form-row treatment in all ten editor states with a full-width, bottom bordered destructive button, removed the divider above it, and retained the separate alert state reached by tapping it. The alert names one record and offers Cancel plus the destructive action. | The button's shape communicates an action, its bottom position separates it from correcting and saving the value, and the alert prevents an accidental immediate removal. This is the user's reviewed direction; native SwiftUI button/alert behavior and a real-iPhone check remain required (U1/U9/U19). |
| Task-note and alignment follow-up | It was unclear whether tasks need Add Note. The centered-header exploration could imply an alternate layout despite the user's leading-aligned decision. | Checked task-note requests in the review corpus and the app's current note storage. Kept an optional, secondary **Note for this day** surface in unfinished and done task states, added a task-with-saved-note state, and removed the centered-header comparison from Figma and Markdown. | Reviews show concrete use for instructions and follow-up, while one explicitly says most tasks need no note. A note stays available without becoming a required step or rival completion CTA. Leading alignment keeps one scan pattern across the Today sheet. Current task notes are date-scoped; moving a task does not migrate them, so the wireframe does not claim a task-wide note (U5/D7). [Evidence and decision](<Task Notes in Day Details — Evidence and Decision.md>). |

## Why the final arrangement is the handoff baseline

The user repeatedly reviewed concrete Figma variants and accepted the latest overall composition, while identifying the last issues in button size, grouping, note/Skip placement, Close and time/slip editing. The final wireframes incorporate those corrections rather than reverting to the initial generic sheet. This is a sound **design baseline for native implementation**, not a claim that every aesthetic detail is final. The remaining validation is a real iPhone at small/large text sizes, VoiceOver, light/dark, keyboard exposure, past-day and skipped states, correct record persistence and speed (U1/U9/S2/T3/T4).
