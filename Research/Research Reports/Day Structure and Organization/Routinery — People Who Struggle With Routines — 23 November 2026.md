# Routinery: People Who Struggle With Routines — What They Need and Why the App Works for Them

Written by Claude (Claude Code), 23 November 2026, at the user's request: read every review from the biggest group of Routinery users, people who say they struggle with routines, and work out from first principles what they are trying to do, why they struggle, why they chose this app and how it helps.

**Scope.** Routinery (now listed as *Routine Planner, Habit Tracker*; App Store `1450486923`, Google Play `com.alt.goodmorning`), reviews from 2019 to 2023, both stores. The labelled reviews are in `Review Labels/Routinery/<year>/`. The group is every review whose `who` field describes the person as struggling with routines (procrastinating, lazy by their own account, distracted, forgetful, always late, giving up after a few days, and so on), found with the grouping used for the user-type counts (`Research/Temp/routinery-strugglers/who_groups.py`). That gives **310 reviews**: 2020: 32 · 2021: 106 · 2022: 114 · 2023: 58 (2019 had none). 182 are from Google Play and 128 from the App Store; 212 are in English, 63 in Korean and 35 in eight other languages.

**Method.** All 310 were read in full, in their own language, and each was coded by hand (`Research/Temp/routinery-strugglers/classification.py`; the text is in `strugglers.txt`). The coding was checked by script: every review has codes, every code is defined, no review has a code twice, and every review has at least one struggle code. A review can carry several codes, so percentages add up to more than 100. **All percentages are of the 310.** Many reviews are short ("I'm lazy and this helped"), so a count shows how many people *said* something, not how many people it applies to. Everything below is "users show…" (review evidence) unless marked "reasoned from first principles".

**One thing to keep in mind.** This group is very happy: average 4.74★, with 254 of 310 (82 %) giving 5★ and only 6 giving 1–2★. People who struggle with routines and found something that finally works write to say so. That makes the group good evidence for *why it works* and weak evidence for *who it failed*: people it didn't help mostly left without saying they struggle.

## 1. The short answer

- **What they want:** to get through the same few daily sequences, every day, without them falling apart: the morning, getting out of the door on time, the evening, the chores, basic self-care. They aren't after big goals. They want to stop being late, stop forgetting their meds and teeth, stop losing the morning to their phone, and stop feeling bad about themselves at the end of the day.
- **Why they struggle:** a routine asks four things of a person, and these people fail at one or more of them. They can't **start** it (35 %), they **lose the thread halfway** through distraction, forgetting or overwhelm (35 %), they can't **feel time passing** so everything runs long (19 %), or they can't **keep it up** past a few days (15 %). Underneath all four: they already know what they should do. Planning isn't the problem; *doing it in the moment* is.
- **Why Routinery works for them:** it moves the routine out of their head and **runs it for them like a playlist**. It shows one step, puts a clock on it, tells them the next one, calls them back when they drift, says when they'll finish and praises them at the end. In effect it does in the moment what their own attention, memory and sense of time don't. Other apps they'd tried (11 % say so) helped them *plan* or *record* a routine; this one helps them *carry it out*.

## 3. Why they struggle

275 of the 310 reviews (89 %) say *why* they struggle. The other 35 only say they struggle. Read from first principles, a routine needs four things from a person, and each review's struggle falls under one or more of them:

| Where it breaks | Reviews | % of 310 | Codes inside it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Starting** | 108 | 34.8 % | can't start or procrastinates (44), calls self lazy or unmotivated (30), groggy or blank in the morning (26), phone eats the time (13) |
| **2. Staying on track mid-routine** | 107 | 34.5 % | forgets steps or the routine (42), gets distracted or sidetracked (40), disorganised or doesn't know what's next (27), overwhelmed (17) |
| **3. Time** | 59 | 19.0 % | no sense of time, dawdles, one task takes too long, always late |

54 reviews (17 %) name two or more of these. Separately, **49 (16 %)** put their struggle down to a condition: ADHD most often, then autism, depression, anxiety and OCD.

### 3.4 Keeping it up: the "three-day" pattern

- 33 say they always gave up after a few days. In Korean this is *작심삼일* (resolutions that last three days), which comes up again and again: "someone with no persistence whose resolutions last three days" (#4, `28c8b64f-74f3-4a52-85ee-dcd3ff744c6d`). ## 7. What it adds up to (reasoned from first principles)

1. **The problem is doing, not knowing.** These people already have the plan, often an elaborate one. What fails is turning it into action at 7 a.m. with a foggy head and a phone in reach. Tools that help you *plan* or *record* (to-do lists, habit trackers, paper planners) don't touch that moment, and many reviewers had tried them.
2. **A routine has four weak points: starting, staying on track, time, keeping it up.** Routinery covers each one: a one-tap start and a running clock for starting; one step on screen, the next step chosen for you, and a call-back when you drift for staying on track; per-step time, real durations and an end time for time; forgiving skips and progress that isn't all-or-nothing for keeping it up.
3. **The app does what a helpful person would.** Someone standing beside you saying "now brush your teeth, you've got two minutes, then shoes" is exactly what these reviews describe. That's why the tone matters ("still waiting :)" hurt one reader), why reliable reminders matter more than new features, and why a voice saying the next step was "pure gold" to one reviewer (#279).
4. **Pressure works for most of them, but it has to be kind.** Most like being rushed by the clock, a few are stressed by it, and nearly all value being able to pause, skip or run over without the routine "failing". Discipline and forgiveness together are what they praise (#237).
5. **The real outcome is self-respect.** The words they use are "I feel like a super person now" (#267), "it helps with my self esteem" (#147) and "I didn't know I could be a Miracle Morning person too" (#273, translated). Being on time and having a clean room are how they get there.

**Limits.** This is one app's happy reviewers, many writing in their first days. It shows clearly why the format works for people who struggle with routines. It doesn't show how many keep using it or how many people it didn't help.
