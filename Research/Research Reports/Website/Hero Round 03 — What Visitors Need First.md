## Answer

For a prospective downloader, the hero must quickly answer: **What is this app, will tracking feel easy, can I see useful progress, and how do I get it?** Keep the descriptive store headline. Replace “Make room for your habits” with **“Know what to do today. See your progress over time.”** Show the actual app clearly, with one download path and a short, factual offer line: **“5 habits free. Buy Plus once.”** The longer one-time-purchase wording clipped at 320px and was shortened after visual review.

App reviews show motivations for choosing and keeping trackers; they do not measure why visitors arrive at oftenenough.com. New prospects comparing apps, people already intending to install, and returning users looking for another device or Help are reasoned task groups. We have no visitor survey or traffic data ranking their shares. The website's download objective makes prospective users the primary hero audience. Download and Help navigation keep the other tasks available.

## Homepage guidance and prioritization

[NN/g homepage principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/) calls for immediately clear purpose, informative imagery, specific actions, priority hierarchy and simple presentation. Applied here: product category in the headline, concrete user jobs in the subtext, actual app output, prominent Get the app, quieter See features, and no decorative panel or distracting motion. This is an application of guidance, not proof this layout will convert.

| Visitor question | Hero answer | Where further detail belongs |
|---|---|---|
| What is Often Enough? | Habit tracker for your real life | Features below |
| Will it fit my daily use? | Real Today screen: checks, amounts, timed reading, weekly family calls and quit/cut-down examples | Flexible goals/routines sections |
| Can I see whether I am making progress? | Concrete progress benefit in the subtext; actual progress values already visible in Today | Native Progress assets in its later section |
| What does trying it cost? | 5 habits free; Plus is a one-time purchase | Plus comparison and localized in-app price |
| Can I get it on my device? | Get the app leads to Download | Availability, planned Android/desktop and Apple device details there |
| I already use it and need an answer | Help remains in navigation | Help/Support, backup and account routes |

Simplicity does not mean hiding the offer. The short free/Plus line answers repeated uncertainty; “Android planned” and a widget annotation do not answer the core prospective user's question and leave the hero. Specific goal schedules are demonstrated in the real UI and explained below, rather than using “at your own pace” as the only value.

Copy alternatives considered: “Make room…” is warm but vague; “One tap for every habit” overclaims because some actions need forms; “Build better habits” promises a broad result without explaining the product; a long feature list creates scan work. The proposed two short jobs describe things the app actually helps the person do.

## Visual and device decisions

Remove the floating widget completely from this hero. Its raster includes a native gallery tray/background and light falloff; a native capture can still be a poor standalone marketing asset. Removing it also lets the core app screen be read unobstructed. The store screenshots and app widgets are unaffected (U28). Future widget sections need a clean isolated asset or faithful editable reproduction, not this crop.

Remove the rounded gray staging plane and widget caption. Keep gray website canvas, store type, seedling and exact tonal logo: they identify the product rather than introduce a competing illustration. Use one larger, upright phone, preserve screen aspect ratio, and leave its native UI pixels intact.

Asset research included the available Figma iOS 26 kit, official [Apple design resources](https://developer.apple.com/design/resources/), Bezel Mockups, DesignDeck, CC0 PommePlate, Wikimedia SVG and [Mockify](https://github.com/Mecury-Labs/mockify). The Figma search returned controls but no matching device frame. PommePlate's older models and the colored Wikimedia vector do not fit this capture. DesignDeck uses a checkout/download gate; no checkout performed. Bezel requires a plugin installation; no plugin installed.

The official Apple DMG download reset after a partial transfer; it was not usable. The chosen transparent iPhone 16 Pro Max Black Titanium PNG comes from Mockify's public asset repository, which attributes the files to Apple Design Resources. This origin is the provider's claim, not a byte match against the incomplete official package. Source frame: [PNG in repository](https://github.com/Mecury-Labs/mockify/blob/main/public/devices/iPhone%2016%20Pro%20Max%20-%20Black%20Titanium.png), Git blob 75acb0659211d370ebea5a5f77676cf3458a6881; 1470×3000, RGBA, 382,572 bytes. Its central screen and exterior are transparent and its hardware/sensor detail remains intact. Native app source is 1320×2868, matching this screen class; display dimensions are fitted without stretching.

The incomplete 261,688,779-byte archive was discarded from this task's scratch folder. No installation or execution of downloaded third-party code was needed. The small transparent PNG is reused directly in Figma, over the existing native app image.

[Apple's marketing guidance](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/) specifies intact product images with true app screen content. The new sourced bezel remains upright, whole, unshadowed and unobstructed; no hardware recoloring, added device shadow or animation. Code's MIT label is not treated as a license override for Apple-origin artwork. Public release must use the current appropriate official asset and associated terms; the design review does not publish anything. Third-party source artwork stays in ignored scratch, with provenance retained (D11).

## Feasibility and validation boundary

The hero needs ordinary text, links and two image layers inside one product component. Native app/seedling screenshots are intentional content expressly authorized by the user, while website copy and controls remain editable. The bezel uses a sourced transparent PNG instead of a hand-drawn casing. No native screen repainting, image generation, WebGL or continuous animation. Desktop/tablet/mobile/320px require a visual and structural check after composition.

This is design work. Current website runtime, DNS, Cloudflare and app behavior are unchanged. Asset encoding, low-memory real-phone checks and conversion validation happen after user design approval (S19/U9). No claim that Figma proves performance, or that earlier competitor praise validates our release.
