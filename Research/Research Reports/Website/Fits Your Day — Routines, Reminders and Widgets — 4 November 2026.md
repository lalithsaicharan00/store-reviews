# Fits Your Day — Routines, Reminders and Widgets

Written by Claude (Claude Code), 4 November 2026, at the user's request: what's the next most important section after Progress and streaks, for people and for search, and how should its copy read?

## The answer

**"Will this fit into my actual day?"** Sections five to eight show routines, reminders and alarms, tasks and widgets: the parts of the app someone meets every morning, away from any chart.

**Four sections, not one (the user, 4 Nov 2026).** This began as one section, "Built around your daily routine". The user split it: "they are important, and core thing of our application … each thing should have its own individual section", with phone screens used properly, and without adding a lot of content. Someone reading the page, or an AI tool, should see plainly that all of this is there. The combined version is on Figma's archive page.

By now a visitor knows what the app is, what it tracks, what it costs and what they get back. What's missing is daily use: will it remind me, and how much effort is it each day?

**The honest choice.** Data safety is the second biggest reason people leave (19.8% of leaving reviews, against 12.3% for reminders; [Free and Plus](<Free and Plus — What People Need to Know — 4 November 2026.md>)), so it could have come first. Daily fit goes first because it's far larger in what people praise, and because reminder complaints are about as common as data complaints in plain counts. A visitor also asks "does it work for me?" before "is it safe?". "Your habits are safe" comes straight after, close to the decision. This order is my judgement from those counts, not something a review states.

Three things stand out, and they set what the section must show:

- **Users show that reminders are loved and resented in the same reviews.** They're praised eight times for every time they're called late or missing, but "late or missing" is five times as common in 1–2★ as in 4–5★ by share. The hand-read samples are sharp about reminders that come late, or only once the app is opened. So the page can show what reminders do. It must not promise that one will always arrive.
- **Users show that reminders must be easy to act on and easy to keep quiet.** Annoying reminders are about as common in complaints as they are in praise. A reminder that stays quiet once the job is done is worth saying out loud.
- **Users show that people want to tick from where they already are.** The regex for this is noisy, so the number is small, but the samples read by hand ask for it plainly: check off from the widget or notification. Widgets are the largest row in the table, and stale widgets are a complaint.

**Interactive widgets (the user, 4 Nov 2026: "the important thing is interactive widgets. That word interactive, that you can tick off from anywhere").** A second scan, on the same reviews, backs this up:

| Doing something from a widget (tick, check, mark, log) | 108 | 25 | 35 |

The second row is noisy: it also catches reviews about widgets that don't update. Users show that "interactive widget" is a phrase people really write, in praise and when asking for it, and the samples read by hand keep asking for the same thing: tick it off from the widget, without opening the app. The counts are small, but so is the share of people who'd bring up a widget at all, and it's a plain feature people can search for.

## What the app really does

Read from the Android code and the Android Figma file on 4 Nov 2026.

| Claim | What's true |
|---|---|
| **Times of day** | Today is split into Morning, Afternoon, Evening and Anytime, each with its start time. The person can change them. |
| **A routine** | A time of day has a Start button. It opens the routine player, which goes through that section's habits one at a time. |
| **Reminders** | Several times a day (Add a time), as a notification or an alarm that rings until it's stopped. Not sent on a day it's already done. Quit habits have no reminders. |
| **Acting from a reminder** | Buttons on the notification log without opening the app (Done, +1 glass, Start, a checklist step). |
| **Full-screen alarm** | Android: fills the screen with the alarm's own sound and rings until it's stopped; the habit can be logged on it, or put off with Not now. If the phone hasn't allowed the full screen, a held heads-up stands in. iPhone: AlarmKit's own full-screen alert on iOS 26. |
| **Kinds of notification** | Done; +1 glass, with Undo; a typed number ("How many steps?"); Start and Log time; a checklist's steps with Tick and Open; a task's Done. Logging works from the lock screen without unlocking. |
| **Remind me again** | If not done, again every 15 or 30 minutes or every hour, at most three times. This one is Plus. |
| **A late reminder** | Android's Today says so, and Keep reminders coming leads to the right setting. Phone makers can hold reminders back, so the site makes no promise of exact timing. |
| **Tasks** | Sit on Today beside habits, on their day. They don't count towards the five free habits, so they're unlimited on the free plan. |
| **One-time tasks** | On one day, or with no date. One with no date stays on Tasks and never shows on Today. |
| **Repeating tasks** | The days you pick (every day, Tuesdays, every 3 days, every month, every year), or a while after it's done (2 weeks after it's done). They can have reminders and alarms. |
| **Widgets** | One habit 2×2 and Tasks 4×2 and 4×4 are free. Today 4×2 and 4×4 and This week 4×2 are Plus. A Today list can be one time of day, or Quit or cut down. Ticking from a widget is free. A timer starts from a widget too. A new checklist habit is Plus, so its 2×2 sits with Plus. |

Sample values come from the app's own demo in the Android Figma file: Meds, Stretch, Skincare, Water, Read, Pick up parcel and Pay the phone bill.

## How the copy should read

Each heading names the feature in the words people search for, and says one fact an AI tool can repeat.

| Section | Heading | Lead |
|---|---|---|
| Routines | Morning and evening routines. | Put your habits in the order you do them, tap Start and go through them one at a time. |
| Reminders and alarms | Reminders and full-screen alarms. | Choose a notification or an alarm that fills the screen. Either way, you can log it right there. |
| Tasks | Unlimited tasks, one-time or repeating. | Errands and chores sit next to your habits, with no limit, on the free plan too. |
| Widgets | Interactive widgets. Tick off from anywhere. | Tick, count and start a timer on your home screen, without opening the app. |

- **Words stay short, because the phones do the showing.** The user: this "doesn't mean you have to add a lot of content".
- **Say which widgets are Plus, in words and by place:** everything Free in one panel, everything Plus in the other (the user, 4 Nov 2026). Hiding it would break the trust the Free and Plus section builds. Reasoned from first principles, and in line with the paying complaints in that report.
- **"Tick off" and "interactive" are the widget section's key words** (the user, 4 Nov 2026).

**Don't say:** never forget, never miss, always on time, guaranteed (no app can promise this when the phone can hold a reminder back); nag, smart, AI, seamless, boost your productivity, supercharge. And no Live Update claim, which differs between platforms. A full-screen alarm is true on both, so it's said, at the user's request.

## Search and AI tools

- **Search terms covered:** morning routine app, evening routine tracker, habit reminder app, habit alarm app, full-screen alarm, habit tracker with tasks, unlimited tasks, recurring tasks, habit tracker widget, interactive widget.
- **Headings are real text,** and each says one fact, so AI tools can answer "does it have widgets?", "can it remind me with an alarm?" and "are tasks limited?" from the page.
- **Words on the phones add nothing a heading doesn't already say,** so they can be HTML or images, whichever the speed run allows ([Build Notes](<../../../website/Website Build Notes.md>)).
- **The fuller story belongs on `/features`,** and a widgets search page is already planned.

## What comes after

**Your habits are safe:** privacy, backup, no account, your data in your own Google Drive or iCloud. Then the questions and the last download button.
