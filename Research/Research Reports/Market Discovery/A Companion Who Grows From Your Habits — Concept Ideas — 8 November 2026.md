# A Companion Who Grows From Your Habits: Concept Ideas

Written by Claude (Claude Code), 8 November 2026, at the user's request: "a character in a different world, where it
grows, and learns, and it kind of simulates our real world itself … challenges should feel like real … if someone don't
want it … they should be able to [use it] like a normal productivity tracker … do some thorough research … and try to
come up with some ideas."

**This is brainstorming backed by research. Nothing here is decided.**

**What it rests on:**
- **Games and apps where something grows from what you do in real life:** Finch, Focus Friend, Forest, Habitica,
  Pokémon Sleep, Pikmin Bloom, Zombies, Run!, Tsuki's Odyssey and Neko Atsume. Also games known for kind design: Hades,
  Celeste, Animal Crossing, Stardew Valley and Sky.
- **Research on confidence and motivation:** Bandura's self-efficacy, Amabile's progress principle, self-determination
  theory in games, and the Tamagotchi effect.
- **Our own review folders:** 332,567 reviews of 21 gamified habit and focus apps (`Research/Temp/growth-games-themes.py`).
  It's a machine count, and about 90 reviews were read by hand.
- **The two Finch reports from today:** [Why Finch Earns So Much](<Why Finch Earns So Much — 8 November 2026.md>) and
  the earlier [Finch report](<../../App Store Reports/10. Finch - Self-Care Pet - Daily Journal & Habit Tracker (REPORT).md>).

## 1. What makes these products work, under the surface

Each product, read for its core idea and for what people actually see.

| Product | What people see | The core idea underneath | What it proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Finch** | A bird gets energy when you tick goals and goes on adventures | **Someone depends on you:** you'll do things for them that you won't do for yourself | Care for another is a stronger pull than self-improvement. But its adventures are random, so the bird says nothing about *your* life |
| **Focus Friend** (Hank Green) | A bean knits while you stay off your phone, and unravels if you don't | **Your restraint becomes their craft.** You earn socks to decorate its room | A small, kind character with one clear link to one behaviour went to #1. When the room was finished, motivation died: "Since decorating my room, I have run out of things to work towards" (`536be1a5-a521-41b1-98e5-46b661a15699`) |
| **Forest** | A tree grows while you focus, and dies if you leave the app | **Your time becomes something living** that you can look back on | Visible growth works. But with no character and slow unlocks, people feel no progress: "To be able to buy all of the trees I'd have to use this app regularly for 13 YEARS" (`7034038406`) |
| **Habitica** | You're an RPG hero. Missed tasks hurt you, and you can die and lose gold or levels | **Real tasks are game moves** | Its "challenge" is punishment, and that drives people away. "Very unforgiving … I lost all my accumulated gold and skills as soon as I died. uninstalling!" (`8dbb4df4-112e-401e-bd0d-b4e62228a6a4`). People also say they had to lie on a snow day (`1873a6d2-8b9c-41be-b3a4-375bf168bbda`). It makes ~$15–35K a month |
| **Pokémon Sleep** | How you slept decides which Pokémon gather round Snorlax in the morning | **Something you do anyway, unconsciously, becomes a surprise to wake up to** | ~$235M in three years (AppMagic, via PocketGamer.biz). Its COO's aim was "a game that makes you look forward to waking up". Money comes from events and growing the Pokémon |
| **Pikmin Bloom** | Walking grows Pikmin and plants flowers along your real path | **Your real route becomes a beautiful trace** | Its revenue has grown every year for five years, to $36M in year five and ~$139M lifetime. There's no competitive pressure, and most purchases only speed things up |
| **Zombies, Run!** | You're a runner in a story. Your real run moves the story on | **Effort becomes a chapter.** The run is "worth it" because of the story | Made for beginners, not for people who are already fit (co-creator Naomi Alderman). A story beats badges and statistics |
| **Tsuki's Odyssey, Neko Atsume** | A rabbit lives his own life in a village; cats visit your garden while you're away | **A life that goes on without you,** which you check in on | You can feel attached with no duty and no guilt. Players call them comfort games |
| **Hades** | You die again and again, and each death moves the story on | **Failure as progress.** "Take the sting out of failure"; "every run counts" (writer Greg Kasavin) | A game can be hard without punishing. A setback becomes a scene, not a loss |
| **Celeste** | A very hard climbing game, with an Assist Mode you can turn on at any time | **The struggle is the meaning, and its level is yours to set, without judgement** | The creator renamed "Cheat Mode" because it felt "judgmental", and removed the marker on assisted saves |
| **Stardew Valley** | You restore a run-down community centre room by room by bringing things in | **Your effort visibly brings a place back to life** | Long, satisfying goals made of many small ones |

**What they share** (reasoned from the table):
1. **One clear link** from a real act to a visible change.
2. **Someone or something to care about.**
3. **Something new to see or collect,** which keeps people opening it.
4. **No real loss when you slip.**

The ones that fade lack an arc. In Finch the bird grows up and then nothing changes, and people ask for "another baby
bird" (`9965243208`). In Focus Friend the room gets finished. In Forest unlocking is far too slow. Our reviews show this
again and again.

## 2. Challenge: how difficulty builds confidence

**The research:**
- **Bandura's mastery experiences.** Confidence ("I can do this") grows most from having done it yourself, especially
  something that took effort and persistence. Early failures before any success can undermine it.
- **Amabile's progress principle.** Of everything that lifts motivation in a day, the strongest is progress on
  something meaningful, even a small step. Setbacks weigh more than wins.
- **Self-determination theory in games.** Players enjoy a game, and feel better after it, when it meets three needs:
  - competence (I'm getting better);
  - autonomy (my choice);
  - relatedness (someone else).

**The trap to avoid** (users show): most habit "games" make the challenge *not missing a day*. Habitica takes your
health, gold or levels. Duolingo's owl guilts you about your streak. In Habitica's reviews:
- "the penalties for dying are too harsh, i lost interest" (`a489babf-fc7b-4fd6-821d-d8847b033159`);
- "it's absurd to lose your LEVEL" (`14134662711`).

The difficulty is artificial, and it punishes exactly the people who are struggling.

**What a real challenge looks like** (reasoned from the research):
- **It's your own real difficulty,** not one the app invents. The thing that's hard for you is the challenge.
- **It waits.** It never expires, and it never hurts you or anyone else.
- **You choose it and set how hard it is,** as in Celeste. Changing it carries no shame.
- **Overcoming it is marked and remembered,** because a remembered mastery is what builds confidence.
- **A slip becomes part of the story,** as in Hades, not a loss.

**People value what was hard to earn** (users show). A Finch user was upset when the pets they'd "worked hard for by
checking in everyday" became easy for everyone (`10222704218`). A Forest user wanted a cheated tree to look different
(`1160236020`). And the character can make people honest: "I feel too guilty to try and cheat the system by lying about
completing an activity" (`13422232737`).

## 3. The concept: a companion whose life is shaped by yours

**In one line:** a companion lives in their own world and walks a long journey. **Your real habits become their
strengths, your real difficulties become their obstacles, and every obstacle you overcome together is remembered.**

They aren't a pet who depends on you to survive. They're a fellow traveller who can go further because of you, and who
is never hurt by waiting.

### 3.1 Your habits become their strengths

Every habit feeds one of a few strengths. The character visibly learns them:

| Strength | Fed by habits like | What the character can then do |
|---|---|---|
| **Body** | Walk, run, gym, stretch | Climb, carry, cross rivers |
| **Mind** | Read, study, learn a language | Read old signs and maps; open libraries and ruins |
| **Calm** | Meditate, sleep, journal, rest days | Settle storms, see through fog, rest at camp |
| **Care** | Medicine, water, meals, chores | Tend their camp; heal and grow things on the way |
| **Bond** | Call family, see friends | Meet and befriend the people of the world |
| **Craft** | Practise an instrument, draw, make things | Build, mend, play music at the fire |

- **Each habit gets its strength from its icon and name.** A habit already stores every name its icon goes by (A111).
  The person can change it in one tap. A quit habit feeds **Resolve**, and a slip never takes it away.
- **The character's world takes the shape of your life** (reasoned). Someone who mostly exercises finds mountain
  passes. A reader finds cities of libraries. A carer gets a camp full of growing things.
- **Two people's journeys never look the same.** That's what makes it theirs, and what makes it worth sharing.

### 3.2 A day, a week, a season

- **A day:**
  - The person ticks habits as they always would. The list doesn't change.
  - A thin strip above the list shows the traveller moving on, and what they're doing reflects what was just done.
    After a walk, they're on a trail. After reading, they're under a tree with a book.
  - At night there's a short **campfire moment**: a few lines about the traveller's day, written to mirror yours.
    "It was a long climb today, but the view was worth it." It's ready whenever you open it, never pushed at you.
- **A week:** a stretch of road ends somewhere: a village, a ruin, a lake. Something is found and kept.
- **A season** (real seasons, by hemisphere): a new region, with its own look, music, people and things to collect.
  A season's things return the next year, so nothing is "now or never".
- **Years:** the traveller grows older and wiser. Their camp becomes a home, and the map fills in. The arc never runs
  out, because it's made of your life, not of a finite shop.

### 3.3 Challenges that are real: the Crossings

This is the heart of it, and what no product above does.

- **The app knows what's hard for you.** It sees a habit you keep missing, one you've just started, or one you came back
  to after a long break.
- **The next obstacle on the traveller's road is made from it.** If moving your body has been hard lately, a mountain
  pass appears. If reading has slipped, there's a locked library. The obstacle says plainly what it's waiting for: "Three
  walks this week opens the pass."
- **It waits as long as it takes.** No timer, no damage, no sad face. The traveller camps at its foot, cooks and meets
  people. Their life goes on.
- **Getting through is a moment.** The crossing is drawn as an event, and a page is added to the **Book of Crossings**:
  "14 November: the High Pass. Three walks in a week, after two months away." Over time this book becomes proof that
  you've overcome hard things before, which is exactly what Bandura says builds confidence. On a bad day, the app can
  show it.
- **You can choose your own Expeditions.** "I want to run 5 km", "a month without sugar", "write every morning". The
  traveller takes on a matching expedition, and you set how hard it is, then change it any time, as in Celeste's Assist
  Mode. Easier never means lesser.
- **What's hard changes as you change.** When a habit has become easy, done steadily for weeks, the traveller
  **masters** that strength. It becomes part of who they are, and the road moves on to what's still hard. Graduating is
  celebrated. It's never treated as losing a user. A Focus Friend user "begun to focus without the app"
  (`2ab7fcc2-5a86-4fcd-8832-1c7fd23d478a`): that's the win.

### 3.4 Slips become story, not loss

- **Missed days hurt nothing.** The traveller had a quiet day of their own: "Rained all day. Stayed in and mended my
  boots."
- **Coming back is celebrated.** "You're back!" is a scene, not a reproach. Starting again is a mastery too: the Book of
  Crossings counts returns.
- **Rest days count, and they're Calm.** A planned rest day gives the traveller a rest scene and fills Calm. Resting is
  something people do, not a gap.
- **No streak to lose, no health to lose, no leaderboard to cheat.** Forest's reviews complain that leaderboards are
  "full of people who cheat the system". The only person to fool is yourself, and the traveller makes honesty feel
  natural.

### 3.5 A relationship without guilt

- **They have their own life** (as in Tsuki and Neko Atsume). They're never sick, never sad at you, never dying.
- **They need your strength to go further, but never to be safe.** This keeps Finch's pull ("I do it for them") without
  Focus Friend's guilt ("If you mess with your phone, you're the bad guy", a 5★ review that likes the guilt, and others
  don't).
- **They know you.** They remember the crossings, the habits you've kept for a year, the day you came back. Those
  memories are computed from your own records, on your phone.
- **They speak in short, warm lines, not chat.** No AI conversation: nothing leaves the phone, and nothing pretends to be
  a person.

### 3.6 It's grown-up and cozy, not childish

Picture a painted, calm world in the spirit of cozy games (Animal Crossing, Stardew, Sky), with quiet music. The
traveller is a small, gentle figure. People choose their look: an animal, a little person or a spirit. Finch is cute in
one way, and some adults find it childish. This look aims to suit a 35-year-old manager as much as a student. The art
direction should be tested with real people before anything is drawn in full.

### 3.7 The world never dies

**The whole world is worked out from your habit records** plus a few choices you make (their look, where they turned at
a fork). Nothing about the world needs saving on its own, so it can't be lost if your logs aren't:
- iCloud, Google Drive, a backup file and a new phone all bring the traveller back exactly;
- this follows our data rules: streaks are computed, never stored.

Finch's angriest reviews are lost birds. "Your companion can't be lost" is a promise we can actually keep.

### 3.8 Beautiful enough to share

- **A postcard from each Crossing.** "Crossed the High Pass after two months away".
- **The Year Map:** the route your life drew, through mountains, libraries and gardens.
- **What a card shows is the person's choice.** No habit names unless they add them, so sharing never reveals anything
  private.

Finch and Focus Friend both grew from what their users posted. A map made of your own year is something people will
want to show.

### 3.9 Turned off, it's a normal tracker

- **One switch,** in onboarding and in Settings: *Show the journey*. Off means no strip, no campfire, no world. Today
  is the same list as now, and every functional feature works the same.
- **Turning it on later builds the world from the history already there.** "Your traveller has already walked 214
  days." Nothing was lost by starting plain.
- **The journey is never in the way.** The habit list stays the first thing on screen, and the speed rules (S) apply:
  ticking a habit is instant, and the world catches up behind.

## 4. What people would pay for

**Rule:** every functional feature is free. Paid things are wants, never needs.
- **No randomness for money:** no loot boxes, no paid re-rolls.
- **No currency to buy:** everything has a known price in real money.
- **Nothing sold against fear:** no "now or never", and nothing that shields a streak.
- **No sponsored content, no ads, no data sold.**

**What people pay for in games like these** (research): self-expression and identity, belonging and novelty, and
supporting makers they like. Finch's payers pay for more of the bird and the monthly season, not for tools.

**Ideas, from most to least promising** (reasoned):
1. **Regions.** Each season's region can be explored for free on the main road. Plus opens its side paths, extra
   people and stories, and its collectibles. It's like a game expansion, and people already accept paying for
   meaningful new content.
2. **Wardrobe and camp.** Clothes, a tent that grows into a home, lanterns, instruments: Finch's best seller, with
   known prices.
3. **Companions.** A second figure who joins the journey, with their own look and lines.
4. **Keepsakes.** A printed Year Map or a small book of your Crossings. Real objects, later, through a print partner.
5. **Pay it forward.** Buy Plus for someone who can't afford it. Finch shows this works and earns goodwill.

**This changes the Plus plan.** Today's plan is one-time Plus that sells only sync. Sync is a need, so it hits the
"function ceiling" from the Finch report. To earn like Finch, Plus would hold the wants above, and sync could simply be
in it.

One-time versus yearly is still open. New regions every season argue for yearly, or for a one-time Plus with paid
seasonal regions. That's for the user to decide.

## 5. Why this isn't a copy

| | Finch | Habitica | Forest, Focus Friend | **This concept** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Who it is | A pet who needs you | You, as a hero | A tree, a bean | **A fellow traveller with their own life** |
| What drives growth | Any ticked goal, converted into energy | XP and gold | Focus time | **Each habit feeds a matching strength** |
| What the world shows | Random adventures | Fantasy quests | A field, a room | **A route shaped by the shape of your life** |
| Challenge | None | Damage, death, losing levels | Losing the tree | **Your real difficulties become obstacles that wait** |
| A slip | Nothing happens | Punishment | Lost progress | **A quiet day in the story; coming back is celebrated** |
| Long arc | The bird grows up, then stops | Level cap | The room or shop is finished | **Seasons, regions, mastery, the Book of Crossings, for years** |
| Can it be turned off? | No | No | No | **Yes: a plain tracker underneath** |
| Can it be lost? | Birds have been lost | Server account | Server account | **Rebuilt from your own records; never lost** |

## 6. The hard parts

- **Art and writing cost money.** The plan only works with art that scales: pieces that combine, regions built from a
  shared kit, and campfire lines written as many small templates that combine. Finch has a team for this, and we'd need
  a smaller, cleverer system. **Biggest risk.**
- **Mapping habits to strengths has to be invisible.** A wrong guess has to be easy to fix and never feel like a quiz.
- **The Crossings must never feel like the app is judging you.** Its words should say what the road needs, never what
  you failed at. A test with people who have depression and ADHD is essential before building.
- **Speed.** The world can't slow down the list (S rules). It draws only when on screen and catches up after taps.
- **Positioning.** The store keyword is still "habit tracker". The journey is what makes the screenshots and videos
  different. It's the reason someone picks this tracker, not a different product.
- **Both phones.** The world has to look and behave the same on iPhone and Android (the user, 11 Oct 2026), and be
  built from the same records in the shared backup file.

## 7. Decisions for the user

1. **The frame.** A traveller on a journey is the main idea here. Other frames for the same system:
   - an apprentice in a workshop, who learns crafts;
   - a lighthouse keeper whose light reaches further;
   - someone restoring a forgotten valley.

   The system (strengths, Crossings, Book, slips as story) works with any of them.
2. **How much world.** Just the strip and the campfire, or also a full world page to explore?
3. **What Plus is.** Wants plus sync, and whether it's one-time, yearly, or one-time with paid seasons.
4. **The first test.** Make a clickable prototype of the strip, one Crossing and the Book of Crossings, and show it to
  ten people: some with ADHD or depression, some who only want a plain tracker. Do this before any art is commissioned.

## What can't be known from here

- **Nobody has built this, so whether it works is a bet.** The parts are proven separately: a character to care about
  (Finch), a visible trace of real action (Pikmin Bloom, Forest), story as the reward (Zombies, Run!), failure as story
  (Hades), difficulty you control (Celeste). The whole is not proven.
- **The machine counts from our 21 gamified apps are word matches.** They show which problems come up, not how often
  exactly. The hand reading was about 90 reviews.
- **Revenue figures for the games are third-party estimates.**
