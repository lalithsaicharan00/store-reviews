# Fabulous — iOS Release and Download Growth

Written by Codex, 13 October 2026. Research order: 1, the first app in the inventory's iOS download-supported section.

**App:** [Fabulous: Daily Habit Tracker](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fabulous-daily-habit-tracker/id1203637303), by Fabulous. Apple ID `1203637303`; bundle ID `co.thefabulous.app`.

The clearest specific growth lead is **New Year 2023**: a provider reports roughly **36,000 US iOS downloads in the highest week of Q1**, while a separate ad-monitoring provider records **US Apple-targeted TikTok ads across 30 December 2022–3 January 2023**, promoting routines and decluttering. That makes paid seasonal promotion a plausible contributor. It does **not** establish how many installs it caused. The separately accessible Kickstart 2023 creative was first observed later in January. Earlier launch visibility and later paid-growth operations are also documented. The exact causes of the other observed peaks remain unknown.

## 3. What actually happened around the most useful peak

For **early January 2023**, Pipiads records individual ads with country **US**, platform **Apple**, and install/App Store actions. A morning-routine ad is dated **30 December 2022–2 January 2023**; a decluttering ad is dated **30 December 2022–3 January 2023**. The same archive includes messaging inviting people to begin 2023 with healthy routines. Its captured ad text, dates and target labels support a real, contemporary advertising lead. [Pipiads](https://www.pipiads.com/tiktok-ads-examples/fabulous-tiktok-ads-1753583265073185).

**Inference:** paid seasonal acquisition may have contributed to the reported January download increase. This is stronger than merely noticing that the app advertises today because the **date, country and Apple platform align**. However, the archive has no attributed install counts, spend series, organic baseline or campaign experiment for that peak. Impression figures are not downloads. It is not established that the ads caused all, most, or a particular percentage of the peak. Seasonal organic demand could also contribute.

The origin of the recalled 65% could not be established; the nearby popularity score must not be substituted for it.

The concrete acquisition lead is advertising that promises help with morning routines and decluttering, then directs the viewer to install or visit the App Store during the New Year period. A match between that promotion and the estimated January peak is plausible, but there is no measured conversion rate or causal install share. No exact feature, localization or ad-triggered explanation for the peak has been established.

**Video access:** clicking the dated morning-routine card's viewing link redirects to a Pipiads login page. Its original 2022–2023 video was not available through the public page. This illustrates a creative approach, but **its campaign date and identity were not verified against the historical TikTok record**. It cannot establish what caused January 2023 downloads. The closing frame of the inspected video asks the viewer to take a three-minute quiz. Its funnel is a relatable procrastination problem, a promise of improved routines, then a quiz invitation. ## 4. Confirmed growth mechanisms, with their limits

**Store and launch visibility:** Product Hunt exposure in December 2017, App of the Day near the January public launch, and Apple's December 2018 self-care selection are documented above. They could help acquire users. No source here quantifies their individual install contribution. Early-2018 acquisition should not be described as purely organic or purely paid without that split.

**Paid social scaling:** [Birch's customer case](https://bir.ch/case-studies/the-fabulous) identifies Fabulous as a customer since **2025**, names CMO Filippo De Rose, and describes automated budget, bid and stop-loss management across **Meta, TikTok and Snapchat**. It reports **50% more campaign scaling while holding the target CPA**. That is a vendor's campaign-scaling claim, not a 50% increase in downloads; the baseline, experiment period and operating-system split are not published. The case confirms a paid-growth operation, but cannot explain the earlier 2021–2024 peaks by itself.

**Measurement to manage paid growth:** [Adjust's Fabulous case](https://www.adjust.com/resources/case-studies/fabulous/) describes support for Apple's SKAdNetwork, media mix modeling and incrementality. It quotes the CMO about spending efficiently. It contains no dated Fabulous download peak or numerical install lift; it is evidence of marketing infrastructure rather than a causal explanation of a particular surge. Its public article was readable through web retrieval; direct scratch capture returned HTTP 429.

**Features:** launch-time journeys/challenges/coaching, the May 2018 digital-balance journey and later coaching/community additions are dated product facts with the scope stated above. A feature being useful, or launching before growth, does not prove it caused acquisition. No feature-triggered iOS download spike was established.

**Localization:** no dated, app-specific localization release was matched to an iOS download peak. The presence of several languages today is insufficient evidence of a historical localization-driven surge.

## 5. Research conclusion

Fabulous reached early iOS traction during a launch period with public promotion and editorial visibility. Its later growth includes paid acquisition; the strongest time-matched clue is the **New Year 2023 US Apple TikTok activity alongside the reported US iOS download peak**. This is an evidence-backed hypothesis, not proven install attribution. The exact reasons for the March 2021, July 2022 and Brazil January 2024 peaks were not found.

The next evidence that would resolve the January cause is a dated install-attribution or incrementality result matched to the observed campaign and US iOS weekly series. This report finishes the requested public research on the first app; it does not move to the next app or infer a strategy for our product.
