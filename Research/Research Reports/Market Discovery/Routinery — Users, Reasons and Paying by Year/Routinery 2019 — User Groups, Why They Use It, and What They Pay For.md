# Routinery 2019: User Groups, Why They Use It, and What They Pay For

Written by Claude (Claude Code), 26 November 2026, at the user's request. Reworked the same day: the first version
grouped people by language and country, which isn't what a user group is. This one groups them by the **problem
they're trying to solve**, as UX user research does.

**What this is.** Routinery's target users as its 2019 reviews show them: which groups of people use it, the pain
point that brought each group, whether each group pays and for what, and which features each group likes. It's the
first year of a year-by-year series ([2020](<Routinery 2020 — User Groups, Why They Use It, and What They Pay For.md>)).

**How it was done.** From the reviews alone, as research for a new product or a full rebuild. No product of ours, its
features or its code was used to choose, read or group anything.

**Source.** The checked labels in `Review Labels/Routinery/2019/labels.jsonl`: `who` (what people say about
themselves), `why_chose` (the need or struggle that brought them), `chose_for` and `liked` (features), `paid`. Each
person goes in a group by the pain point in their own words; one person can be in more than one group.

---

## The short answer

- **2019 has only 11 reviews** (0.1% of the app's 9,644). In 2019 the app was a Korean morning-routine app (its
  developer's replies call it 굿모닝, "Good Morning"); English came late in the year.
- **Only 3 of the 11 people name a pain point.** None says who they are (job, life stage, condition, family). So 2019
  shows the outline of the target users and no more:

| User group (the pain point) | People | Share of 11 | Why they use it | Features they like | Paying |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Mornings go badly** | 2 | 18% | Want their mornings to change, a "successful morning" | None named; they praise the change itself | 0 say anything |
| **Can't make themselves do it, and blame their own willpower** | 1 (also in the morning group) | 9% | *"I used to blame my willpower a lot, but now I can feel my mornings changing"* | None named | 0 say anything |
| **Nothing else they tried worked; searched for years** | 1 | 9% | *"exactly what I've been looking for for years"*; used it through a translator before English came | None named | 0 say anything |
| No pain point given | 8 | 73% | Bugs, language requests or short praise | Cute pictures (1), simplicity (1), the English version (1) | 0 say anything |

- **Paying: none of the 11 mentions it.** No review and no developer reply mentions a price, subscription, premium or
  trial. So the share who pay, and what they pay for, can't be told for 2019, and this data doesn't show whether
  there was anything to buy. Paying starts showing in 2020.
- **The same three groups are the biggest ones in 2020**, from 1,122 reviews: bad mornings, can't start or keep going,
  and people who tried everything else. 2019 is where they first show up.

## 1. The groups

### Mornings go badly (2 people, 18%)

- *"그동안 의지탓을 많이 했었는데 이제는 아침이 달라지는게 느껴지네요"*, "I used to blame my willpower a lot, but now I
  can feel my mornings changing" (`4004808264`, App Store Korea, 5★, title *"신선해요.."*, "It's refreshing").
- *"성공하는 아침시간 만들어봐요!!"*, "Let's make a successful morning!" (`50ef48f9`, Play, Korean, 5★).

**Why they use it:** to make the morning work. **Liked:** no feature named. **Paying:** not said.

### Can't make themselves do it, and blame their willpower (1 person, 9%)

The same `4004808264`. The pain is a feeling rather than a task: they had tried before, failed and put it down to
themselves, and now credit the app's structure. **Liked:** not named. **Paying:** not said.

### Nothing else worked; searched for years (1 person, 9%)

- *"Really excited about this app its exactly what I've been looking for for years!! Can't wait for it to be offered
  in English language but for now I will use translate!"* (`edbc0ce4`, Play, English, 5★).

**Why they use it:** the long search ended here; it mattered enough to use a translator. They don't say which
feature. **Liked:** not named. **Paying:** not said.

### No pain point given (8 people, 73%)

| Review | ★ | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| `4004841199` (App Store Korea) | 5 | *"그림 귀여워요"*, "the pictures are cute"; wants more cute icons |
| `5140123045` (App Store US) | 5 | *"simple is powerful.."* |
| `0797b00d` (Play, English) | 4 | Updated to the English version, *"it looks good"*; setup screens repeat after setting the wake-up hour |
| `7a86faad` (Play, Korean) | 5 | Thanks the developer for a fix |
| `2a443a10` (Play, Korean) | 2 | The app resets after the phone locks and unlocks |
| `34427bee` (Play, German) | 1 | *"Nicht auf deutsch..."*, "not in German" |
| `c659c5ea` (Play, English) | 1 | *"dont use it unless u kno the language"* |
| `51e81536` (Play, English) | 1 | *"Needs English version!"* |

These say nothing about the person or their need, so they're not in any group. The three 1★ reviews are only about
the app not being in their language.

---

## 4. Limits

- **11 reviews is too few to size any group.** The shares above describe these 11 people, not the app's users in
  2019.
- **Two possible early-reviewer reviews:** the two App Store Korea reviews were 13 minutes apart on 12 April
  (`4004808264`, `4004841199`), both 5★.
- **Usernames aren't counted:** two names hint at a group ("1일 1습관", "one habit a day"; "addhd-"), but a username
  isn't someone describing themselves.
