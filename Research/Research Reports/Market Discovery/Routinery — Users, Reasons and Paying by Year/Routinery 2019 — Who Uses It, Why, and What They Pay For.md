# Routinery 2019: Who Uses It, Why, and What They Pay For

Written by Claude (Claude Code), 26 November 2026, at the user's request.

**What this is.** Every Routinery review from 2019, read one by one in its own language, to answer four questions:
which groups of people use the app, why each group uses it, whether each group pays (and for what), and which features
each group likes. It's the first year of a year-by-year series on the same app.

**How it was done.** Purely from the reviews. No product of ours, its features or its code was used to choose, read or
group anything; the question is what a brand-new routine app (or one rebuilt from nothing) could learn from these
people.

**Source.** `Review Labels/Routinery/2019/`: `reviews.jsonl` (the reviews) and `labels.jsonl` (one label per review,
checked). Collected 8 September 2026 from both stores; App Store ID `1450486923`, Google Play
`com.alt.goodmorning`. I read all 11 reviews in full myself (Korean, English and German) and compared each with its
label; where I describe a review more exactly than its label, the appendix says so.

---

## The short answer

- **2019 is a tiny year: 11 reviews**, 0.1% of the app's 9,644. Every number below is out of 11 and is weak evidence.
  Read it as the app's starting point, not as proof of who its users are.
- **Nobody says who they are.** 0 of 11 reviews mention a job, life stage, condition, family or anyone they use it for.
  So groups by *identity* (students, parents, people with ADHD …) can't be counted for 2019: the count for each is 0,
  and that means "not said", not "not there".
- **What the reviews do show is two groups by language and need:**
  - **Korean users building a better morning (5 of 11, 45%).** The app was then a Korean morning-routine app (its
    developer replies call it 굿모닝, "Good Morning"). The two who say why they use it want their mornings to change;
    one had always blamed their own willpower.
  - **People outside Korea who found it before it spoke their language (6 of 11, 55%)**, five of them asking for
    English, German (and, from the developer's reply, Spanish). Three of those five gave 1★ only for the language; one
    used it through a translator because it was "exactly what I've been looking for for years".
- **Paying: nobody mentions it.** 0 of 11 say they paid, tried a trial, were charged, refused to pay, or that it's
  free; no review or developer reply mentions a price, subscription or premium. **The share who pay can't be worked
  out for 2019**, and whether the app had anything to buy that year isn't shown by this data.
- **Features liked:** cute pictures (1), simplicity (1), the new English version (1). Everything else said is about
  language or bugs.

### Group A: Korean users building a better morning (5, 45%)

**Why they use it.** Two of the five say why, and both are about the morning:

- *"그동안 의지탓을 많이 했었는데 이제는 아침이 달라지는게 느껴지네요"*, "I used to blame my willpower a lot, but now I
  can feel my mornings changing" (`4004808264`, 5★, title *"신선해요.."*, "It's refreshing").
- *"성공하는 아침시간 만들어봐요!!"*, "Let's make a successful morning!" (`50ef48f9`, 5★). The developer's reply calls
  the app 굿모닝 ("Good Morning") and speaks of a "successful morning habit".

So the need is **a morning that goes better, and an end to blaming themselves for it**. The willpower line matters
most: it says the person had tried before, put the failure on themselves, and now credits the app's structure
instead.

The other three don't say why; they give feedback as people already using it:

- *"그림 귀여워요"*, "the pictures are cute", and *"귀여운 아이콘 많이 추가해주세욥"*, "please add lots of cute icons"
  (`4004841199`, 5★).
- *"업데이트 해주셔서 감사합니다 … 잘 쓰겠습니다"*, "thanks for the update … I'll use it well" (`7a86faad`, 5★); the
  developer's reply says an issue was fixed and released straight away.
- With the app running, when the screen goes dark and they unlock the phone, *"초기화가 되어 있습니다"*, "it's been
  reset"; *"이 문제만 해결되면 정말 좋을것 같습니다"*, "if only this were fixed, it would be really good"
  (`2a443a10`, 2★). The developer asked for steps and screenshots.

**Paying.** 0 of 5 say anything about it.

**Features liked.** Cute pictures (1 of 5). Nothing else is named; the two morning reviews praise the change in their
mornings, not a feature.

**Problems.** The app resets after the phone is locked (1); wants more icons (1).

## 6. What 2019 can and can't tell a new product

Drawn only from the 11 reviews above, and weak because there are 11.

**What it suggests:**

1. **The morning was the reason to come.** The app started as a morning routine app, and the only reasons Korean users
   give are about mornings. The strongest line (*"I used to blame my willpower"*) is a feeling, not a feature: people
   who came having failed before, and felt the change was the app's structure rather than their effort.
2. **People outside the app's home market found it before it spoke their language, and wanted it.** More than half
   the year's reviews (6 of 11) come from outside Korea; five ask for their language, and their reviews hold 84% of
   the year's helpful votes. One used it through a translator. For a new product, the language a person first meets
   the app in decides whether they stay long enough to rate anything but the language.
3. **The first minutes have to work.** One of the two bugs breaks the very first setup (setting a wake-up hour), the
   other resets a running session when the phone locks: both are moments a routine app can't afford to lose.
4. **Small delights were noticed:** cute pictures (with a request for more), and simplicity.

**What it can't tell:**

- **Who the users are.** Nobody says. Later years will have to answer this.
- **Whether anyone pays, how much, or for what.** Nobody mentions it.
- **Which feature drew people in.** Nobody names one as the reason they chose the app (`chose_for` is empty for all
  11).
