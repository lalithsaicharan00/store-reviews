# ADHD App: What to Support, What to Do, and Free and Plus

Written by Claude (Claude Code), 7 November 2026.

**The question:** the features are chosen and task breakdown at launch is a library. So what must the app support overall, what must it always do and never do, and what goes in Free and what goes in Plus?

**How to read it:** this does for the ADHD app what the [Feature Ledger](<../Feature Ledger.md>) did for the habit app, using only reviews from people who say they have ADHD. "Users show" means review evidence. "Reasoned" means there is too little evidence and it is a judgement. It builds on [What ADHD Users Pay For](<What ADHD Users Pay For — Features and Reasons — 7 November 2026.md>), which holds the feature counts.

## The short answer

1. **The line between Free and Plus is this: anything that stops someone forgetting or falling behind today is free. Having more of it, seeing it all together and making it yours is Plus.**
2. **Free must be a real app, with no time limit.** Unlimited to-dos, repeating tasks, every reminder, every timer, the whole step library, My Day, 5 habits, 2 full routines, one widget, dark mode, backup.
3. **Plus is one payment for six things:** more habits and routines, your calendar in My Day, reminders that keep going until it's done, all the widgets, every look and sound, and everything added later. No subscription and no trial, because the free app is the trial.
4. **Reminders and repeating tasks stay free.** 20 reviews across 6 apps object to locked reminders and 13 across 5 apps to locked repeating tasks. Only 3 and 4 people are happy paying for them.
5. **The calendar is the fairest thing to charge for.** 11 reviews across 5 apps are glad they paid for it, all 5 stars, and only 2 object.
6. **A limit on routines is both the loudest complaint and the clearest reason to buy.** One free routine reads as a trick. Two reads as fair, because two is morning and night.
7. **The biggest thing to avoid is the way ADHD apps sell.** In the people's own words it is "predatory". 1,514 of 2,965 one and two star reviews (51%) mention charges, trials, cancelling or subscriptions. Our model removes most of that by itself: no card, no trial, nothing that renews.
8. **After money, the top reason for a low rating is too much.** Too many screens, too many questions, too much to set up. So the first minute matters more than any feature.

**Limits on this evidence:**

- One reader coded this, from sentences rather than whole reviews (the 1,285 payers in the earlier report were read more fully).
- Word searches miss people who say it another way, so every count here is a floor.
- People who object to a lock are mostly people who left. None of the 20 who object to locked reminders is in the payer group. People who are happy are mostly payers. So these counts show how each side feels, not how many of each exist.
- Nothing here shows how many people would buy. No review can show that.
- Counts of apps matter more than counts of reviews. A row with one app in it may be about that app.

**How sure each rule is:** **Strong** means many apps, and it agrees with the Feature Ledger. **Moderate** means several apps. **Thin** means few reviews, so it is partly reasoned.

## Must do

| # | Rule | Evidence | How sure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Every reminder arrives, every time.** This comes before any new feature | Reminders are named as missing or broken by 29 of 861 payers, tied highest. 20 of 150 who quit say so. 17 of the 330 low-star sample. Ledger C039 | Strong |
| 2 | **Be useful in the first minute.** Open straight into the app, with no quiz, no sign-up, nothing to fill in | Too much is 55 of 330 in the low-star sample (17%), in 28 apps. Quizzes and sign-up add 13 in 10 apps. “the onboarding process shouldn't feel like a never ending chore” (Fabulous, 1★, review `12534202096`) | Strong |
| 3 | **Say the price and the limits before any setup.** On the store page, and before someone builds anything | “make your first routine before it tells you about its monetization strategy” (RoutineFlow, 1★, review `08482512-af38-4731-bed4-f7f42856409f`). Ledger C236 | Strong |
| 4 | **Let people use it for as long as they like before paying.** | 15 reviews in 10 apps used the free plan and then bought, all 5 stars. “I can do everything I need in the free version but I subscribed” (TickTick, 5★, review `11084131366`) | Strong |
| 5 | **Promise it in writing: what's free stays free, and what's paid stays paid.** Then keep the promise | 15 reviews in 10 apps are about something moved to paid, average 2.1★. Tiimo removed timed routines, and 36 of its 67 payers quit or regret paying. Ledger C001 | Strong |
| 6 | **Give a way in when someone is stuck.** The step library, starter routines and a tiny first step | Breakdown is credited by 34 payers. A word search finds 64 reviews saying some form of not knowing where to start | Moderate |
| 7 | **Make a reminder hard to lose, when the person wants that.** | 8 ask for it in 6 apps and 7 praise it in 5. “if I open my phone once, the notification goes away” (Tiimo, 3★, review `7596045697`) | Moderate |
| 8 | **Be kind.** No guilt, no red piles, no lectures | 8 of the 330 sample. “stop being lazy” (Rabit, 2★, review `2b6ecd51-eda3-46d6-9037-fa4fb8b87829`). Ledger C157 | Moderate |
| 9 | **Keep the daily path short through every update.** Count the taps, and never add one without a reason | 17 of the 330 say an update ruined the app, in 11 apps. Forest alone gives 6 of those, mostly for added taps and a heavier app | Strong |
| 10 | **Answer people.** One clear way to reach a person, inside the app | 13 of 150 who quit say no support. Ledger C036 | Strong |
| 11 | **Let people change anything the app suggests.** | 15 of the 330 say they can't edit, add their own or change times, in 13 apps | Moderate |
| 12 | **Make it plain who made it, honestly.** | 12 praise an app made by people with ADHD. 9 say one clearly wasn't, averaging 1.1★. Whoever fronts the app must not claim what isn't true | Moderate |

## Must not do

| # | Rule | Evidence | How sure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Never use a trial that turns into a charge, or ask for a card to try the app.** | 285 surprise-charge complaints among 18,180, and 1,514 of 2,965 low-star reviews mention charges, trials, cancelling or subscriptions. ADHD people say they will forget to cancel. Ledger C213 | Strong |
| 2 | **Never put a paywall before the app.** | 80 of the 330 low-star sample are about having to pay or the price, in 34 apps, and that sample had charge words removed. “unlike some apps that make it impossible to properly try until you pay” (TimeFinder, 5★, review `ca66b5bc-a776-4fc7-8ea3-5d7e500e801b`) | Strong |
| 3 | **Never lock a reminder or a repeating task.** | See the table below. Ledger C008 | Strong |
| 4 | **Never limit to-dos, or count ticks.** | 13 reviews in 8 apps, average 2.4★. Ledger C200. [Tasks in the Free Plan](<../Business Model and Monetization/Tasks in the Free Plan — Limit, Count or Plus.md>) found the same | Strong |
| 5 | **Never lock dark mode, backup or export.** | 8 reviews on dark mode. Ledger C262 and Rulebook D10 on data | Strong |
| 6 | **Never interrupt with pop-ups, countdown offers or ads.** No upsell to someone who has paid | 15 of the 330 in 13 apps. “IMMEDIATELY throws popups asking you to pay” (Dear Me, 1★, review `82d8d147-8189-4bbd-b94c-78fe17f5415e`). Ledger C127, C180 | Strong |
| 7 | **Never push another app.** | 11 complain of bundles and sister apps, and 6 Fabulous payers are angry about them | Moderate |
| 8 | **Never make people sit through anything.** No slow intro, no animation that can't be skipped | 9 reviews, nearly all Clarify, average 1.6★. “no option to skip the animations” (Clarify, 1★, review `ce7ca4db-9f8a-4ab2-b405-a78d38a35784`) | Moderate |
| 9 | **Never give generic advice as if it were ADHD help.** | 14 of the 330 in 11 apps, and "not as promised" is the top reason payers quit (37 of 150) | Strong |
| 10 | **Never promise what the app can't do.** Medical claims, cures, or an ad showing something else | 37 of 150 quit for it. Ledger C218 | Strong |
| 11 | **Never let a reward get in the way of the task.** | 6 of the 330 say the game itself distracts | Thin |
| 12 | **Never use AI art, AI support replies or an AI therapist.** | 38 dislike AI-made art and text, averaging 1.3★. 6 complain of AI support replies | Moderate |
| 13 | **Never abandon one-time buyers.** No new app that asks them to pay again, and no later subscription for what they own | 3 reviews, all 1 star. Few, but it is the exact risk of a one-time price | Thin |

## What people accept paying for, and what angers them

This table carries the Free and Plus decision. It counts hand-read reviews on a feature that was locked or limited.

| Feature | Object to the lock | Happy to pay, or fine with the limit | Reading |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Reminders** | 20 (6 apps, 2.9★) | 3 | Keep free |
| **Repeating tasks** | 13 (5 apps, 2.3★) | 4 (all Structured) | Keep free |
| **Number of to-dos** | 13 (8 apps, 2.4★) | few | Keep free and unlimited |
| **Dark mode** | 8 (3 apps, 2.5★) | 2 | Keep free |
| **Number of habits** | 7 (5 apps, 2.1★) | 9 (with paying for more) | A fair limit works, if it isn't tiny |
| **Number of routines** | 24 (2 apps, 2.7★) | 14 (7 fine, 7 paid) | A fair limit works, and it sells |
| **Looks, colours, icons, sounds** | 5 (5 apps, 2.2★) | 10 (6 apps, 4.9★) | Plus, but basic colours stay free |
| **Widgets** | 3 (3 apps) | 2 | Too few to decide. Ledger C009 says basic ones are free |
| **Calendar** | 2 (2 apps) | 11 (5 apps, 5.0★) | **The clearest Plus feature** |

**Three things stand out:**

- **Fair limits turn on what the number means.** Routines show this best. 11 reviews on a limit of one average 2.4★, and 10 on a limit of two average 3.5★. Six of the 7 who are fine with the limit are talking about two: “that's enough for your morning & night routine” (Routine Planner, Habit Tracker, 5★, review `13503431066`). Two gives a whole day's shape. One doesn't.
- **People pay for "more" of what already works for them.** 27 payers name going past a free limit, the top named unlock. They pay after the thing has helped, not before.
- **Calendar is a clean case.** “Unlocking the calendar sync really made this worth every penny for me” (Structured, 5★, review `13797908107`). It is useful to people with full lives, it isn't needed to start, and locking it stops nobody from using the app.

## The plan

### Free, for as long as anyone wants

| What | Limit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| To-dos, one-time and repeating, steps inside, quick capture | None | Must not 3 and 4 |
| Reminders on anything, with Done and Snooze on the notification | None | The app's first job. Must do 1 |
| Timers | None | Timers are credited by 68 payers. 2 Focus Keeper reviews object to basic timer settings being locked |
| My Day with your own tasks, missed tasks moving on | None | The picture of the day is the reason to come. Ledger C296 says free must show what makes the app different |
| Step library and starter routines | None | Costs nothing to run, and it's the thing the channel can show. 7 reviews object to templates that are poor or paid (Me+) |
| Habits | **5** | Agrees with [Free Habit Limit](<../Business Model and Monetization/Free Habit Limit — 5, 6, 7 or More.md>). “the free version allows for maximum 5 habits, which is great” (TickTick, 5★, review `e880a94c-70e2-4941-91a5-c4e1b8f0239e`) |
| Routines | **2, with the whole player** | Morning and night. Voice, total time, screen on and how long it took are all included, so free people feel what they would be buying more of |
| Recording how long things take | None | History is never held back. Rulebook D10 |
| Light rewards | Default set | Motivation is credited by 153 of 861 payers |
| One small widget, "Next up" | 1 kind | Seeing the next thing without opening the app is the job. Ledger C009 |
| Dark mode, text size, screen reader, sound and motion off | None | Must not 5 |
| Backup, restore, export | None | Must not 5 |
| Undo, skip, rest day, fix a past day | None | People shouldn't pay for making mistakes or having bad days |

### Plus, one payment

| What | Why people would pay | How sure |
|---|---|---|
| **Your calendar in My Day** | 9 payers say they paid for it, and 11 reviews are glad | Strong |
| **Habits and routines without limit** | The top named unlock (27 payers) | Strong |
| **Keep reminding until it's done** | The feature people buy Due for. Nagging is credited by 11 payers, and 8 say it's missing | **Moderate, the closest call** |
| **All widgets** | 7 payers say they paid for a widget | Moderate |
| **Every look: themes, colours, icons, sounds, app icons** | 18 payers paid for customising, the second most named unlock. 10 reviews are glad | Strong |
| **Everything added later**, including capped AI if it ever comes | Buyers keep what they bought | Reasoned |

Plus also carries a reason of its own: **support the person making it.** 33 of 1,285 payers give that reason. “completely usable without premium but I pay for it just out of appreciation” (Tasks, 5★, review `01e9f0ec-fe93-4247-b799-c7889b3ca8b0`). A small tip button can sit beside it.

**The closest call: keeping on reminding.** Ordinary reminders are free. Should the repeat-until-done version be Plus? For Plus: it's a real extra ability, Due sells on it, and only 1 review objects to paying for repeat alerts. Against: forgetting is the main problem, so it may feel like paying not to forget. My recommendation is Plus, with one free follow-up reminder so free people still get a second chance. Often Enough's Plus already has "If not done, remind me again" (A73).

### How Plus is offered

- **Nothing appears by itself.** There is one quiet Plus row in the menu, and an offer only at the moment someone taps something Plus.
- **The limit is visible early:** "2 of 2 routines" sits on the Routines page, before anyone starts building a third.
- **Hitting a limit never loses work.** Ledger C204.
- **The page says the whole deal:** one payment, no subscription, no renewal, what's included, and that free stays free.
- **Nobody who paid sees an offer again.** Ledger C127.
- **Later, a way to ask for help with the price.** 7 reviews praise this kind of help (Finch, Structured, Habit Tracker), all 5 stars. It could be as simple as "Can't afford it? Write to us".

This is a check on the market. The reasons for our plan are the reviews above.

## What's still open

- **Reshape Often Enough or keep two apps?** This plan fits either, and it needs a decision before design starts.
- **The price.** This report sets what's in Plus, not what it costs. It should be decided against live prices and what the channel shows.
- **The 5 habits and 2 routines are starting points.** Reviews can show fairness, not sales. Once the app is live, real numbers decide, but a limit can only ever go up, because of Must do 5.
- **Who fronts the app.** Must do 12 depends on the open question of whether the presenter has ADHD.
