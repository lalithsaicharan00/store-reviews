# Habits That Do Real Good: Doing It for Someone Else

Written by Claude (Claude Code), 8 November 2026, at the user's request: "We need a mechanism that helps the user to do
something even if they don't care about them, they do for someone else … that shouldn't be like creating the same streak
pressure in a character form. Something for which the subscription should become justifiable."

This follows [A Companion Who Grows From Your Habits](<A Companion Who Grows From Your Habits — Concept Ideas — 8 November 2026.md>),
which the user found too complicated, and a simpler companion idea the user found too thin or too close to Finch.
**Nothing here is decided.**

> **Rejected by the user, 8 November 2026:** "They sound good. They sound very noble. But they're not going to work.
> We are not going to make a profit out of it … we can donate something … But it's not how we should do it." The
> people this serves (anxious, depressed, ADHD) need an external being who gets something when they act, as Finch's

## 1. The idea in one line

**Every habit you keep does real good for someone real: a tree planted, a meal for a child, a day's care for a rescued
animal.** It's paid for by Plus, so paying for Plus is itself doing good.

The "someone else" isn't a character in a game. It's real, and it's outside the app.

## 2. Why this, and why now

**People work harder for others than for themselves, at small stakes.** In Imas's 2014 study (*Journal of Public
Economics*), people put in more effort when it earned money for charity than for themselves, but only when the amounts
were small. A habit is exactly that: a small act, many times.

**Giving makes people happier, most of all in three conditions.** Spending on others predicts more happiness than
spending on yourself (Dunn, Aknin and Norton, *Science* 2008). The amount, $5 or $20, made no difference. Their 2014
review says it helps most when people:
1. **see the impact;**
2. **choose** where it goes;
3. **feel connected** to someone through it.

The design below is built on those three.

**It's proven in our own reviews:**
- **Forest plants real trees** with coins earned by focusing.
  - 1,928 of its 80,649 iPhone reviews (2.4%) mention real trees, rated 4.75 against 4.68 for the app.
  - On Android it's 860 reviews.
  - People say it makes them "want to work hard for long periods of time" (`3777737794`) and gives "a common goal for
    people to share" (`1912648502`).
- **Forest users' complaint is that it's too rare.**
  - It takes 2,500 coins per tree; people would "rather be planting real trees more often!" (`4138933168`).
  - Some bought premium expecting a tree every session (`045acfeb-3ca2-44b7-9f11-b960ff96d98d`).
  - Some were stopped after five trees (`12171523107`).
  - One recommends buying the app "so you can use your coins for planting real trees" (`d2f6f87c-896b-4d31-90dc-e5a38970382d`).
- **Finch's Guardians pay so others can have Plus.**
  - 642 reviews mention them, rated 4.78.
  - A mental health professional cried visiting the Hall of Guardians (`13499497367`).
  - A man recovering from brain surgery "literally cried" when a Guardian gifted him Plus (`14149514750`).

**Outside our folders:**
- ShareTheMeal (UN World Food Programme) lets people tap to give a child's meal for about $0.80. It raised over $28M in 2022.
- Charity Miles pays charities per mile walked, funded by sponsors.
- Ecosia turned web searches into trees, and people switched search engines for it.

**What exists in habit apps is the opposite:** donate when you *fail*. Givr asks for a donation when you skip; Pattern
pledges money per missed session; Beeminder charges you. That's punishment with a charity label. **Our version gives
when you succeed and never fines anyone.**

## 3. How it works

**For the person, it's three things:**
1. **Choose who your habits help** (once, changeable any time): trees, meals or animals. Choosing is one of the three
   conditions.
2. **Keep your habits as usual.** The list doesn't change. Each kept habit fills a small jar. Every 10 habit-days
   (a number to tune) releases one real unit: one tree, one meal, one day of an animal's care.
3. **See it.** Each month brings a short update from the partner: where the trees went in the ground, a photo, a count.
   Your total only grows, like "You've planted 37 trees", and it's easy to share.

**What makes it honest, never guilt:**
- **The money waits for you.** If you have a hard month, your share isn't lost or kept by us. It waits until your habits
  release it. If you cancel, or a year passes, anything still waiting is given anyway.
- **Every cent of the giving share is always given.** Your habits decide when, and let you say "I did that".
- **Nobody goes without because you skipped,** and the app never says otherwise. Words it never uses: "they're
  waiting for you", "don't let them down".
- **Rest days count as kept.** Pauses and skips are left out, as everywhere else in the app.
- **No streak.** Ten habit-days can come from any habits, on any days.

**One switch** turns it off. The app is then a plain tracker, and the money still goes, all at once each month.

## 4. Why Plus becomes worth paying for

Plus today is sync, which is a need, so it hits the ceiling on what people will pay for function (see
[Why Finch Earns So Much](<Why Finch Earns So Much — 8 November 2026.md>)).

**With this, Plus is:**
- **everything extra** (sync, and the extras that come later);
- **a fixed share of every Plus that goes to the person's chosen cause,** released by their habits;
- **Plus gifted to people who can't afford it,** paid from Plus. A "can't afford it?" link sends people to support,
  who grant a store offer code by hand, as Finch does.

**The pitch on the Plus page:** "Plus syncs your habits across your phones, and part of every Plus does real good. Your
habits decide when."

**The cost, as rough arithmetic.** Say Plus is about $30 a year and the store takes 15–30%. A giving share of
about 15% is roughly $4–5 a year per payer. At $0.25–$1 a tree, or $0.80 a meal, that's about 5–20 units a year per person.
That's visible, and far more often than Forest's one tree per 2,500 coins. The share has to be set so the business
still makes money.

**Free users** keep every functional feature. The first tree can be on us (about $0.25–1 per new person): "We plant a
tree for everyone who joins." It's cheap, it's honest, and it shows the idea on day one.

## 5. What it needs that we don't have today

- **A partner for each cause,** with proof (locations, photos, receipts). Charity partners and tree-planting services
  with certificates exist. Which ones to use, and their terms, need checking.
- **An exact count, to stay honest.** To pay exactly what habits released, the company needs one number a month: how
  many units were released. No habits, no names, nothing else.
  - **That's a tiny server,** and today's rule is "we run no server at all" (D16). **That's the user's decision.**
  - **Without it, the honest version is weaker.** The full share is given every month, and the app says "your Plus
    planted 2 trees this month", not "your habits planted them".
- **Store rules.** Apple and Google have rules on in-app charity and on claims about where money goes. Our words and
  flow must follow them. That needs checking before building.
- **Both phones.** The same jar, the same numbers, the same backup file.

## 6. Risks

- **Moral licensing.** Small good acts can make people feel they've "done their bit" (Imas's later work). This is why
  the unit stays small and steady, not one big gesture.
- **It could feel like a gimmick** if the impact is vague. Real places, photos and counts are what keep it from being one.
- **Charity fatigue, or a cause someone dislikes.** Choosing and changing the cause keeps it the person's own.
- **It doesn't replace a good tracker.** It's the reason to pay, not the reason to use the app.

## 7. Decisions for the user

1. **The causes:** trees, meals, animals, or a different set.
2. **The size of the giving share,** and so how often a unit is released.
3. **The count:** a tiny anonymous server so habits really release the giving, or no server and the weaker wording.
4. **Whether the companion stays,** for example as the one who brings back the monthly update. It isn't needed for this
   to work.
