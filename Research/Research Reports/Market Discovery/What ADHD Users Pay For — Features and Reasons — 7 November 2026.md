# What ADHD Users Pay For: Features and Reasons

Written by Claude (Claude Code), 7 November 2026.

**The question:** across Tiimo and every ADHD app we could find, which features do people with ADHD pay for, why do they buy, and which features are must-haves?

**The rule for counting:** a review counts only if the person says they have ADHD **and** says they paid. Everything here comes from what those people wrote. Reviews from anyone else are marked as "not a payer".

## The short answer

1. **People with ADHD pay for help with two things most of all: not forgetting, and getting through the day on time.** Of 861 payers read in full, 224 (26.0%) credit a feature that stops them forgetting and 209 (24.3%) credit routines and time features. A further 172 (20.0%) credit the app being simple and calm.
2. **The must-haves are plain.** They are reminders that always arrive, a simple app with little setup, step-by-step routines with timers, a list and calendar in one place, a way to see time, and being on the home screen. None of these is new. What people pay for is that they work every day.
3. **Rewards, task breakdown, lessons, focus music and app blocking each sell well, but in their own kind of app.** They are reasons to choose one app rather than features every app needs.
4. **The price model is itself a reason to buy.** "One-time" is the most common reason people give for paying: 78 of 1,285 payers (6.1%) say so directly. ADHD makes people forget subscriptions, and they know it.
5. **What loses payers:** the app wasn't what was promised (37 of 150 who quit), bugs (32), too much to take in (30), a feature taken away (22) and reminders that failed (20). Tiimo shows the cost of the fourth: it removed timed routines, and 36 of its 67 paying reviewers now say they quit or regret paying.

## The must-haves

This ranking is my judgement from the numbers above. A feature is a must-have if many payers credit it across many apps **and** people complain or leave when it's missing or fails.

### 1. Reminders that always arrive

71 payers credit reminders across 21 apps. 29 say they're missing or broken, and 20 of the 150 who quit left over them. For someone with ADHD a failed reminder is the whole product failing.

- Users show the need goes beyond one ping: a Due payer wanted it to "continue to remind me until I had actually completed it" (#1579). Nagging is credited by 11 and asked for by 8 more.
- The failures are named plainly: "the notifications never work" (#1394) and "Reminder notifications do not appear" (#643).
- A HabitNow payer puts the value in one line: "Now I start and the app remembers for me!" (#6557).

### 2. Simple, with very little setup

83 payers credit it, across 31 apps, more apps than any other feature. 29 say the app failed at it, 17 of them on Fabulous, and 30 of the 150 who quit left because it was too much.

- "My ADHD said run the other way." (#166, a Fabulous payer.)
- Effort counts as much as looks: "a chore which to an ADHD brain means I wont use it" (#1236).
- Pop-ups and offers for other apps count as overwhelm. 13 of those who quit name ads or upselling after they'd paid: "so they can try to sell me their other apps?" (#872).

### 3. Step-by-step routines with a timer on each step

74 payers credit routines and 68 credit timers. Payers credit routines in only 7 apps, mainly RoutineFlow, Fabulous, Routinery, Tiimo and Brili.

- The job is **moving on without deciding**: each step has a time, the next one starts, and a voice can say so: "a voice that tells you when it's time to move on to another task" (#1040).
- It is what people came for: "I invested in Tiimo specifically for the routines feature" (#1376).
- The timer must stay visible: "Visual timer is pointless if screen goes dark" (#1391).

### 4. Seeing time

40 credit a visual timeline, 24 seeing how long things take, and 46 planning the day. Payers credit a timeline in only 4 apps, and Structured, Tiimo and Sectograph carry it. Nobody in the 861 complained that it was missing.

- "Being able to see my day broke down by the time it takes" (#1176, a Structured payer).
- "The visual map of how far away events are really helps." (#5779, Sectograph.)
- Time blindness is named directly: "If you suffer from time blindness" (#1118).

### 5. A list and calendar in one place

Lists are the most credited single feature (85). Calendar is credited by 26 and is missing or broken for 13, and repeating tasks by 21, missing for 8. TickTick shows how far this goes: 52 payers and only 1 quit, because habits, tasks, calendar and timer sit in one app.

### 6. On the home screen

Only 25 credit widgets, but 14 more say a widget is missing or poor, and 7 name it as why they bought. "I paid so I could have the checklist widget on my home screen" (#676). The reason is that ADHD users forget the app itself: "So I never forget" about it (#607).

### 7. It keeps working and never loses anything

Bugs drove 32 of the 150 who quit, more than any single feature. Lost data hits hardest: "Completely deleted all my previous data." (#1663). People also spend a long time setting an app up: "i spent so long adding my own pictures and crafting my own routines" (#1375).

### Also needed, in fewer words

- **Missed tasks shouldn't punish.** Few mention it (5 missing or broken, 6 who quit), but the feeling is strong. One Tiimo payer disliked "having to manually shift over undone items from the day before" (#1331). A Todoist payer relied on a feature where "Todoist would just spread out my overdue tasks over the next week", and was angry when it was removed (#1736).
- **Gentleness** is credited by 37, mostly on Finch and Fabulous.

## Features that sell in their own kind of app

These are strong reasons to pay for one specific app. They aren't things every ADHD app needs.

| Feature | Evidence | Where it works | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rewards and a pet | 64 and 25 credit them | Finch (93 payers, 3 quit), Forest (62 payers), Focus Friend. "the only app my ADHD brain has managed to continue using" (#380) | Rewards that get harder to earn feel like a trap (#1621) |
| Breaking tasks down | 34 credit it | Goblin Tools (44 payers), neurolist. "I get stuck in ADHD paralysis because I don't know where to begin." (#570) | Thin results get called out (#557) |
| Lessons and coaching | 47 and 18 credit them | Inflow (60 payers), Clarify. "a year of this was less than 2 months of my medication costs" (#5300) | Price against content: "This is essentially a $200 ADHD book." (#774). 16 of Inflow's 60 quit |
| Focus music | 22 credit it | Brain.fm (28 payers, 2 quit) | One sound doesn't work for everyone (#1593) |
| Blocking apps | 19 credit it | Forest, Focus Friend. "got the paid version so I can select exactly which apps to allow" (#1624) | Choosing allowed apps is the part people pay for |
| Body doubling | 14 credit it | dubbii (23 payers), Inflow | Credited in only 2 apps |
| Wake-up missions | 12 Alarmy payers | See the next section | |

**Little evidence for:** AI (16 credit it, and it put off a few long-time payers: #1229, #1378), streaks (5), sharing (7), mood (8). Low counts here mean few payers mention them, so they aren't proof that these features don't matter.

## Alarmy

You asked about Alarmy, so here it is on its own. All of its Play reviews were collected, in every language, and none were cut off by a page limit.

- **182 reviews mention ADHD** (88 App Store, 94 Play). **12 are payers, and none quit or regret it.** That is a small group, so read it as a pattern and not as a share.
- **Why they pay:** the alarm forces them out of bed, when ordinary alarms don't. "Having ADHD makes waking up and staying awake the hardest part of my day." (#7). One payer says it plainly: "that's why I paid for it!" (#5).
- **They use it beyond waking up.** People set it for medicine ("I use it for an alarm for meds", #1813) and for chores and health tasks, using the photo and step missions (#8). So what they're buying is **a reminder that can't be ignored**, which is the same need as must-have 1, solved harder.

## Why they buy: the money

Only 320 of the 1,285 (24.9%) give any reason for paying at all, and only 137 (10.7%) tie it to a feature. The rest of the reasons are about money and trust, so this part counts as much as the feature list.

| Stated reason for paying | People (of 1,285) |
|---|---:|
| One-time price | 78 |
| To support the maker | 33 |
| Low price | 29 |
| More than the free limit allows | 27 |
| Tried it free first | 18 |
| It visibly worked for them | 15 |
| Paid at once, on impulse | 10 |

- **One-time beats subscription, because of ADHD itself.** "I end up forgetting about monthly subscriptions" (#1110). "Not renewing my subscription at this time because I forget about it" (#6699). Structured and RoutineFlow are credited for this most.
- **They pay after it has worked.** A trial has to be long enough: "a cruel joke to expect someone with ADHD to evaluate an app" (#779) in 7 days.
- **What the upgrade unlocks matters.** The most named unlocks are more routines or habits (27), customising (18), the calendar, the widget, and notifications. Non-payers get angry when a basic is locked: "Reminders should never be a "Premium Feature"" (#6810, not a payer) and repeating tasks on Any.do (#1779, not a payer).
- **Payers also fund a tool they feel is on their side.** 33 say they paid to support the maker, 10 of them on Goblin Tools.

**Trust is a selling point of its own.** People notice when an app guards them against their own forgetting. The first three below are from people who hadn't paid:

- dubbii's alert when you pay but don't use it: "Theyll text you if you subscribed but stoped using it." (#5603, also #5621)
- Brili: "no cancellation is needed for the 30 day free trial" (#5631)
- NowNextLater: "an actual trial that cancels on its own is genius" (#5731)
- Finch emails a month before it renews (#6160, a payer)

**The opposite does real damage.** 285 reviews read by hand complain of a surprise charge or trouble cancelling: Fabulous 142, Clarify 53 and Inflow 34 make up 80% of them. Countdown offers shown before anyone sees the app draw the sharpest anger: "taking advantage of the impulsivity of the people with adhd" (#834).

## Why payers quit

150 people who paid and then quit or regretted it were read in full. A person can have more than one reason.

| Reason | People | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Not what was promised, too basic, nothing new | 37 | 24.7% |
| Bugs, crashes, lost data, can't log in | 32 | 21.3% |
| Overwhelming, confusing, too much setup | 30 | 20.0% |
| A feature was removed or changed | 22 | 14.7% |
| Reminders failed or don't exist | 20 | 13.3% |
| Billing or cancelling trouble | 14 | 9.3% |
| Ads or upselling after paying, or asked to pay again | 13 | 8.7% |
| No reply from support | 13 | 8.7% |
| Too expensive | 7 | 4.7% |
| Stopped using it or forgot it | 7 | 4.7% |
| Punished for missed tasks | 6 | 4.0% |

Only 7 blame themselves for not using it. Nearly all blame the app, so most of this can be fixed.

**Tiimo is the clearest case.** It is built for ADHD, and 36 of its 67 paying reviewers (54%) quit or regret paying. Of 28 read in full, 12 name a removal or change, most often timed routines: "One of the absolute core features for me was the timed routines." (#5035). Reminders failing (6) and bugs (5) come next. People who bought a lifetime plan for a feature felt it most (#1376). Users show that taking away something people have built their day around costs more than never having it.

## What this can and can't show

- **It covers people who wrote a review.** Most payers never write one, and those who do skew happy: 915 of the 1,285 (71%) gave 5 stars. Shares here describe reviewers. They don't describe all ADHD users.
- **"Credit" isn't proof of why they bought.** Only 137 people tie the purchase to a feature in words. The main ranking shows what paying users value, which is close to the question but not identical.
- **Not every ADHD review was read.** 14,561 of the 18,180 weren't read by hand. 13,749 of those had no payment wording that passed the filter. The other 812 are low-rated reviews with charge words: in a sample of 60, about half were surprise charges and about 5% were genuine payers, so roughly 40 payers may be missing there.
- **Two depths of reading.** The 424 payers found in the wider read were read only around their money sentence, so they're in the 1,285 but not in the feature counts of the 861.
- **ADHD is self-stated.** Nobody's diagnosis was checked.
- **One reader coded this.** The features and quit reasons were coded by hand by Claude, with no second coder.
- **Reviews span years.** Some describe features or prices that have since changed.
- **"Quit" comes from what people wrote,** not from store data.
