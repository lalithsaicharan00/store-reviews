# Why Finch Earns So Much

Written by Claude (Claude Code), 8 November 2026, at the user's request: "I want to understand why Finch is earning that
much … What is the things that is driving this revenue … it's a subscription. How people are still paying for it and
why?"

**What this rests on:**
- Finch's own help pages and App Store page, read today.
- Our earlier [Finch report](<../../App Store Reports/10. Finch - Self-Care Pet - Daily Journal & Habit Tracker (REPORT).md>) (70,041 App Store reviews, May 2021 to Sept 2026, every review read).
- A fresh machine count of how payers talk (`Research/Temp/finch-pay-reasons.py`).
- A hand reading of 75 payers' reviews: 45 picked at random from the 4–5★ group and 30 from the 1–2★ group.
- The revenue figures from [Following the Money](<Following the Money — Which Apps in Our Lists Earn the Most — 8 November 2026.md>).

Where a point is reasoned rather than shown by reviews, it says so.

---

## 1. The short answer

Finch is not really sold as a habit tracker. **It's a cute pet game that makes self-care feel rewarding.** It's free to
play, and there's a new season of things to collect every month. Plus is an optional extra for people who already love it.

**Its money comes from four things, all large at once:**

1. **A very large number of people use it every day.** About 2.6 million people use it each month, and it has had 50 million
   installs. It has 773,000 US ratings at 4.9, Editors' Choice, and sits at #15 in Health & Fitness today. Most of this
   came from people recommending it, not from ads.
2. **It helps the people who use it, and they say so.** Its users are mostly people with anxiety, depression or ADHD, and
   many of them have tried everything else. Two of every three payers we hand-read pay *because it helped them*.
3. **Plus sells more of what they already love, not a gate.** The free app is complete. Plus adds more outfits, more
   event rewards, more sounds, more prompts. People don't pay to get past a wall. They pay because they want more of the
   bird, and to thank the makers.
4. **The price is yearly, low per day, and comes in many sizes.** It's $69.99 a year on paper. Many pay $39.99–$49.99
   after a discount. There's a gift option sold on the web and a "can't afford it" raffle. A year is paid all at once, and
   the decision to pay again comes only once a year.

**Why they keep paying for a subscription:** something new arrives every month, and Plus guarantees that month's
outfits. The bird and years of collected items become theirs. And it's a small decision once a year, not a bill every
month.

**Payers are less happy than they were.** Their average rating fell from 4.41 in 2022 to 3.05 in 2026. The causes are
sponsored movie events (from June 2026), removed features, bugs that lost people's birds, and billing surprises. The trust
that made people pay is being spent.

## 2. How big it is

| Measure | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Store revenue, both stores | ~$1.7M a month (~$20M a year, before Apple's and Google's cut) | Sensor Tower bands for October 2026 (iPhone $1M–10M, Android $500K–1M) and ScreensDesign (iPhone $1M) |
| People using it each month | ~2.6 million | AppGoblin |
| Installs | ~50 million; ~700,000 a month | AppGoblin |
| Ratings | 773,000 in the US at 4.9; about 1 million worldwide | App Store page, read today; AppGoblin |
| Rank | #15 Health & Fitness (US), Editors' Choice | App Store page, read today |
| Ads in the app | None. 100% of revenue is in-app purchases | AppGoblin's SDK scan |
| Company | Finch Care, a Public Benefit Corporation, started by two engineers, Nino and Stephanie, after their own struggles with anxiety and depression | [Google's #WeArePlay profile](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2025/07/weareplay-app-game-founders-across-the-us.html), Finch's FAQ |

**Rough arithmetic (an estimate, not a measurement):** take $20M a year, and assume the average payer pays $40–70 a year.
That gives about **300,000–500,000 paying people**, or roughly **one in six to one in nine** of its monthly users. That's
a high rate for a free app. It fits the reviews: people who stay, love it.

## 3. What drives it

### 3.1 People come for free, and bring others

- **Recommendation is one of its biggest themes:** 9,765 of 70,041 reviews (13.9%) tell others to get it. Clinicians
  recommend it too (381 reviews).
- **People bring the people around them.** A partner bought a year after seeing it work (`11088520407`). A friend brought
  in a sister (`12349069084`). A mother pays for her daughter's (`13065046498`). Friends send each other good vibes and
  gifts, and so they keep each other coming back.
- **TikTok:** Finch's own channel is on its App Store page. A third-party analysis says most of its growth came from what
  users made on TikTok, and that its ad spend is small. That's not confirmed by Finch, so it's a lead, not a finding.

### 3.2 People open it every day because of the bird

- **People do for the bird what they won't do for themselves.** "I would do anything for this bird" (US App Store page
  review). "The more I care about my pet in the app, the more I'm kind of forced to take care of myself" (`8396607649`).
- **It doesn't punish.** A missed day doesn't hurt the bird and loses nothing (`9088749274`). For someone who is depressed,
  this is the difference between coming back and not.
- **A new season every month** (today: Hidden Hollow, with Winter the Weasel to earn by doing self-care in November). There's
  always something to finish this month.

### 3.3 It helps a group that badly needs help

In our earlier report, 12.0% of all reviews mention mental health and 7.4% ADHD or neurodivergence. 3.8% say it changed
their life. **Payers mention the benefit more than everyone else:** 32% of payer reviews mention mental health or say it
helped (26% of other reviews). Of the 41 happy payers we hand-read, **27 (66%) give the help as their reason**:

> "I've been using Finch religiously for about 4 months now and I was so happy with it I purchased a year
> subscription." (`12147311831`, neurodivergent; the sleep breathing and purring soundscape are Plus extras)

Many say they're not people who pay for apps: "Honestly I hate a mf subscription … Worth it" (`9699036962`). Others
say "I'm not a person who spends money on an app" (`13028107758`) or are a "very broke student" (`12752902343`).

### 3.4 Plus is more of the fun, not a wall

Finch's own page says the core self-care features "are (and will always be) free". **What Plus adds** (Finch help
centre, read today):

| Area | With Plus |
|---|---|
| Shops | A discounted item every day; more rare-item slots (clothes, furniture, colours) and travel spots |
| Monthly events | Extra rewards every day; the event's pet on day 20, not 25; **every outfit guaranteed** if you take part daily |
| Goals | Any emoji for a goal |
| Friends | Every kind of good vibe to send |
| Reflections, soundscapes, breathing, movement, timers, quizzes | Every option and length |
| Newsletter | Five a week, not one |

In the hand reading:
- **13 of the 41 happy payers (32%)** pay for the bird's side: outfits, colours, event rewards, the pet.
  "I've got finch plus, cause you can get things also for free like season offers" (`10382923888`).
  "The monthly reward system … has me looking forward to every month" (`9573393325`).
- **Only 3 name a self-care tool**, such as the soundscapes or custom goals (`8494944241`).

So the money is in the **game layer on top of a real benefit**. The benefit makes people stay, and the game makes them
want more.

**The free app being generous makes people trust it, and that trust makes them pay** (users show). People say:
- "even before I got the plus it was helpful because it didn't hide everything behind a paywall" (`13697963626`)
- "This app provides you so much for free, i truly believe the creators of this app want to help people … more than
  happy to pay for" (`9403873180`)
- "I'm willing to pay for but not for the features. Just to help the creators" (`8824164141`)

About one in seven happy payers (6 of 41) gives this reason.

### 3.5 The price fits people's pockets, and they get to try it first

- **A free Plus preview after three days of use, with no card and no charge when it ends** (Finch help centre). People
  get used to the extras, then lose them. That's when many decide. Reasoned, not counted: the loss of something already
  had is a stronger push to buy than a promise.
- **A 7-day trial that rolls into a yearly plan** (earlier report). It sells, but it's also the biggest source of anger
  (see §5).
- **Many prices.** Apple lists nine Plus prices from $5.99 to $69.99, plus a Guardian purchase (App Pricing Lab, March 2026).
  In reviews, the most-quoted price is **$40** (129 mentions), then $70 (49) and $50 (32). People find the daily cost
  small: "$40/year (less than $3.50 a month)" (`12287555537`).
- **Gifts and Guardians.** A year of Plus can be bought as a gift through Stripe, on the web, outside the stores. Guardians
  pay into a fund that gives Plus by monthly raffle to people who can't afford it. Someone who said they couldn't afford
  it any more was offered three lower yearly prices after a Guardian's month ran out (`10996484081`). Another, a high
  schooler, was kept on Plus after winning (`9262507001`). This brings in money from people who can afford it twice, and
  goodwill from people who can't. 7 of the 41 happy payers mention price, a discount or Guardians.

## 4. Why people keep paying a subscription

Reasoned from the evidence above, with the reviews that show each:

1. **Something new every month.** A subscription feels fair when the app keeps changing. Finch runs like a game: a
   season, a pet and outfits each month, guaranteed with Plus. "if you miss a lot of days, you'll miss the best event
   prize" (`10327041988`).
2. **One decision a year.** Most pay yearly, all at once. Between renewals there's nothing to decide. People who
   renew say it simply: "It's been a year and I'm happy … I'm renewing" (`13092629310`). Others say "I've had it almost 3
   years now. Paying for 'Plus' is worth every penny" (`14496640623`).
3. **What they've built is theirs.** A bird that's hundreds of days old, years of outfits and furniture. Losing it "feels
   like a pet died" (`9583838100`). Reasoned: this makes stopping feel like a loss, not a saving.
4. **They feel good about who gets the money.** A small team, a Public Benefit Corporation, a generous free app, a fund for
   those who can't pay. Paying feels like supporting, and people say so.
5. **Some renewals aren't chosen.** Of payer reviews, 8% complain about a charge, a refund or an auto-renewal, and 1 in
   4 of the unhappy payers. "I did get caught up by forgetting about the free trail" (`12918231601`): this one was glad,
   most aren't. Some of the revenue is people who didn't mean to pay.

## 5. Where it's breaking

**Payers are less happy every year.**

| Year | Payer reviews | Average rating |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2022 | 87 | 4.41 |
| 2023 | 78 | 4.23 |
| 2024 | 106 | 4.20 |
| 2025 | 137 | 3.31 |
| 2026 | 102 | 3.05 |

Of 30 unhappy payers read by hand:
- **Bugs or a lost bird:** 8. "it deleted nearly all of my progress … Everything I had paid for the subscription for was
  gone" (`12912434324`). The pet data got "corrupted" (`12925432031`, `13732070192`).
- **Billing, cancelling or support that doesn't answer:** 7. Example: `8516576961`, `12029787765`.
- **Removed features and constant changes:** 5. Journeys went (`12917119050`), and "a glorified to do list" (`14260797252`).
- **Sponsored events, seen as ads in a paid app:** 5. June 2026's Supergirl month is named in 70 reviews. "I just
  canceled my subscription because of the supergirl theme" (`14186200268`). "I won't be renewing because even with plus,
  Finch has begun collaborating with brands … I'm done paying" (`14467821531`).
- **Not enough for the price:** 3. "For 70$ I find it wild I can't buy from all of the clothes" (`13145906888`).
- **Notifications that won't stop:** 1. Example: `13664421236`.
- **Not a payer:** 1.

**The pattern:** people paid because they trusted Finch. Each of these breaks that trust, and a payer who loses it stops
renewing.

---

## 6. What it means for us

Within our hard rules (no ads, no selling data, nothing that breaks trust):

- **The big lesson is the order:** help first, a generous free app, then sell more of what people love. Finch's payers
  pay for the benefit and the bird, not to get past a wall. This fits a free habit app with an optional Plus.
- **Finch earns from a game layer** (outfits, seasons, collecting) that a plain habit tracker doesn't have. Without
  something people *want more of*, the same rate of paying won't happen. Our Plus is sync, which is a need, not a
  want. Reasoned: expect a lower share of people to pay.
- **What not to copy:**
  - a trial that quietly rolls into a year;
  - no reminder before it charges;
  - sponsored content inside a paid app;
  - a free tier that gets smaller.

  These bring money now and cost trust later, and Finch's falling payer ratings are what that looks like.
- **What fits us:** Finch's no-card preview (try Plus, then it ends with no charge) and its pay-for-someone-else fund
  are both honest ways to sell.

---

## 7. What can't be known from here

- How much is yearly versus monthly, how many renew, and how much comes from gifts on the web: Finch doesn't publish
  these.
- The 300,000–500,000 payers is arithmetic on estimates, not a figure.
- Our reviews are App Store only. Finch's Android reviews aren't in our folders.
- **How the counts were made:**
  - The payer count is a word match. It found 516 reviews (0.74%) that say the writer pays. The earlier report's wider
    definition found 1,381.
  - "Worth the money" is one of the words that finds a payer, so the "worth it" share is partly circular and isn't used
    as a finding.
  - The hand reading is 75 reviews, enough to show which reasons lead, not to measure them exactly.
