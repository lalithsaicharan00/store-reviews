# Today Screen, Question 4: Where the View Switcher Sits

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 24 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion.

**Screen.** Row 3 holds a segmented control, **List · Week · Month · Year**. Picking Week changes the title above it from "Today" to "This week". Month and Year do the same.

**The question.** A tab or segmented control should change only what is below it. If it changes things above it, it is in the wrong place. Is that rule right? Where should the switcher go? And can the view choice be combined neatly with the title?

**Basis.**
- **Reviews:** all 1,487,223 screened for view-switching wording.
  - Read all 97 strong habit-app matches (switch / toggle / default / remember a view, view buttons, views being hard to find).
  - Read a random 200 of the other 745 habit-app mentions of a "week / month / year / list / calendar view".
  - Relevant: 140. Reviewers rarely talk about control placement, so the rule itself rests on the guidelines below.
- **External:** Apple's Human Interface Guidelines (segmented controls, toolbars), Nielsen Norman Group on tabs, and the 49-app Home census.

---

## What to do

1. **The rule holds.** A switcher should sit above everything it changes. Ours changes the title above it, so it is in the wrong place.
2. **Make the switcher the top row, in place of the big "Today" title.**
   - The tab bar already names the screen, and Apple allows an empty title when it would repeat the tab.
   - Row 1 becomes the four-way control plus the **+** button. There is no separate title row any more, so this also removes a row.
3. **Call the first view "Day", not "List".** The four options are then four time spans: **Day · Week · Month · Year**. This matches the Health app's D · W · M · Y.
   - "List" describes a layout, and layout is a separate setting (Question 7).
4. **Put the period line directly under the switcher.** It is the heading of whatever the switcher shows:

   | View | Period line |
   |---|---|
   | Day | "Today · Thursday 24 September" |
   | Week | "21–27 September" |
   | Month | "September 2026" |
   | Year | "2026" |

   Everything that changes when you switch is now below the switcher. This period line replaces the date line from Question 1, and tapping it opens the date picker from Question 3.
5. **The day strip belongs to Day view only.** Week, Month and Year show their own grids, so the strip never sits above them.
6. **Remember the last view**, and let people choose which view the app opens on.
7. **Don't hide the views in a title menu.** One tap to switch is the thing people praise. A menu makes it two taps, and views hidden behind menus draw complaints (see findings 2 and 3).

```
Now:     Today  Thu 24 Sep · 14:30          ◔ 5/11   (+)
         [strip]
         [ List | Week | Month | Year ]   (Now) (▦)
         [All] [Health] [Mind] [Home]

Better:  [  Day  |  Week  |  Month  |  Year  ]       (+)
         Today · Thursday 24 September            ← period line (tap = calendar)
         [strip: Day view only]
         …habits
```

## Key findings

**1. The rule is right: the guidelines say controls sit above the content they switch.**
- **Nielsen Norman Group:** "Position the Tab List Above the Panel". Tabs are "supposed to keep users on the same page while alternating between related views". Tab and panel are tied together by "common region" and "proximity". A title *above* the control that changes breaks that tie: it is outside the panel yet acts as if it were inside.
- **Apple HIG:** a segmented control is for "closely related choices that affect an object, state, or view", and "no more than about five segments on iPhone". Apple's own example is Health, where the time-range control sits above the chart and the date range under it changes, not the screen title.
- **Apple HIG** on titles: "If titling a toolbar seems redundant, you can leave the title area empty."

**2. One-tap switching is the valued part (27 reviews, mean 4.85★).** Hiding the switch is punished (5 reviews, mean 2.4★).
- "the availability to switch between three different views is exactly what I needed." `A7#748`
- "no ability to tap to switch from week ti calender view." `A76#3704`
- Our navigation report found the same with Google Calendar's month view hidden behind a menu (13 complaints).

**3. Preferences are split, so keep all four views and remember the choice.**
- Among reviews that name a favourite: month 28, week 17, day or list 5, year 3.
- 49 more ask for a view their app lacks.
- 10 want to set the view the app opens on: "can i set the default view to be monthly view rather than weekly one?" `P130#175`

**4. Accidental switches worry people.** A view that changes by swiping, without an obvious control, confuses them:
- "the horrible possibility of accidentally swiping off of it into a different view without noticing." `A13#13651`
- "Swiping on a habit to mark it as completed would often switch the view from All Day to Morning." `A13#12636`

So swiping is kept for moving between days (Question 3), not for changing views.

**5. People describe the first view as a "day" view.** Reviewers ask for a "daily view", "single day view" or "current day view" (`A76#7263`, `P3#13933`). Calling it "Day" matches the other three spans and the way people talk.

## What other apps do

**View switch on Home:** only 11 of the 49 Home screens have one.

| Pattern | Apps | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Tabs or segments below the header | 6 | Productive, Routinery, ShineDay, MyRoutine |
| Menu in the title | 2 | Way of Life "This Week ▾", ThatGirl "Day ▾" |
| In the tab bar | 2 | Dots, HabitGrid |

In HabitGrid, the tab bar sets the view and the large title simply names it ("Year"). The title follows the control, not the other way round.

**Apple Health:** the control sits directly under the title; the heading beneath it changes and the screen title does not.

---

**Limits.**
- **Principles, not reviews:** reviews almost never discuss where a control sits. The placement rule comes from the guidelines, and the reviews support the parts around it (one-tap switching, remembered default, no accidental switching).
- **Sample:** the view preference counts come from a sample of 200 and show direction only.
- **Choice not tested:** "Day" instead of "List" follows from the reviews' wording and the Health app, not from a naming test.
