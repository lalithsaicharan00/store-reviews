# Today Screen, Question 5: Always Visible or On Demand (Group Chips and Views)

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 24 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion.

**Screen.** Above the first habit there are four rows of controls:
1. Title row
2. Date strip
3. **List · Week · Month · Year** switcher, plus the **Now** and **grid** buttons
4. Group chips: **All 5/11 · Health 57% · Mind 0% · Home …**

**The question.** Things used often should always be visible. Things used rarely should not take permanent space. Are the group chips used often enough to deserve a row? Is the view switcher? Do we need everything on show at once?

**Basis.**
- **Reviews read:**
  - all 141 App Store and Play reviews about filtering by category, tag or group;
  - a random 250 of the 779 habit-app reviews that mention clutter together with a screen element (buttons, tabs, top, filters, menus, Home);
  - the 297 view-switching reviews from Question 4.
- **Relevant:** 328.
- **Earlier evidence:** the Day Structure whole-corpus coding of 2,228 group reviews.
- **External:** Nielsen Norman Group's guidance on progressive disclosure.

---

## What to do

1. **The view switcher stays on screen.** It becomes the top row, replacing the title (Question 4). One tap to switch is the valued part, so it costs no extra row.
2. **Replace the chip row with a single filter button.**
   - The button appears **only when there is something to choose**: at least one group, or the "Due now" and "Hide done" options from Question 6, which exist whenever day sections are on.
   - It sits at the right end of the period line and reads **"All ▾"**.
   - Tapping it opens the group list, and each group's progress ("Health 3 of 5") moves into that menu.
3. **Make an active filter impossible to miss.**
   - The button fills in and reads **"Health ✕"**.
   - The period line adds **"· showing 4 of 11"**.
   - One tap on ✕ returns to All.
   - The app always opens on **All**, as the Day Structure report already requires.
4. **Keep the "Now" and grid buttons out of this row.** Question 6 covers Now and Question 7 covers the grid. The short answer is that both move off Home's top.
5. **Result:** the top goes from **four rows to three**:
   - switcher and +;
   - period line and filter;
   - day strip.

   Every control left is one people use daily or that shows today's state.

```
Now:     Today  Thu 24 Sep · 14:30          ◔ 5/11   (+)
         [strip]
         [ List | Week | Month | Year ]   (Now) (▦)
         (All 5/11) (Health 57%) (Mind 0%) (Home …)

Better:  [  Day  |  Week  |  Month  |  Year  ]       (+)
         Today · Thursday 24 September        [All ▾]
         [strip]
         ANYTIME …
```

## Key findings

**1. Clutter is expensive (91 reviews, mean 2.6★).**
- In the random clutter sample, 89 of 250 reviews complain about clutter; they average 2.6★.
- 47 praise an uncluttered app; they average 4.74★.
- The named causes: too many buttons, tabs and options, and extras stacked above the habits.
  - "five tabs while only one is actually used" `A4#19515`
  - "I wish i could uncheck a box somewhere to hide all the stuff i dont want." `P24#10993`

**2. Groups are wanted, but as a tool you pick up, not a permanent row.**
- Demand is real: 933 requests for groups in the Day Structure coding, and 260 for filtering by group.
- But filtering is used to *focus sometimes*, not every time. The everyday view is everything:
  - "It does allow you to tag goals with categories and filter by category, but I don’t find the filtering useful because I want to see all the goals at once." `A31#4696`
  - When Streaks put a filter at the top, a long-time user asked: "The filter at the top seems odd to me. Do users have so many tasks, and if so, does it need to have a prominent placement like that?" `A23#1464`
- Group demand also grows with list length. People with many habits ask for groups to stop scrolling: "having everything in one long list is visually overwhelming!" `P3#8301`
- So the filter should appear once there is something to filter, not before.

**3. A forgotten filter hides habits, which is the costliest failure.**
- "some habits weren’t showing up on the list. I then found out that it was because of the selected time of day filter on the top of the screen. This isn’t very intuitive." `A33#1164`
- "New drop down option at the top- I can’t figure out what it does though!" `A23#76`
- The Day Structure coding found the same thing: "All did not show all", and untagged habits disappeared.
- That is why the active filter must be loud: a filled button, a count on the period line, and a reset to All on open.

**4. The view switcher is different.** One-tap switching is praised (27 reviews, 4.85★) and hiding it is punished (5, 2.4★; Question 4). Question 4 has already moved it into the title's place, so it keeps its visibility without taking an extra row.

**5. Guideline support.** Nielsen Norman Group on progressive disclosure: "Initially, show users only a few of the most important options. Offer a larger set of specialized options upon request."

## What other apps do

- **Filters on Home:** 13 of the 49 competitor Home screens have one.
  - chips: 6 (Goal Streak, MyRoutine, ShineDay, Do Habits)
  - tabs: 4 (Habitify, Habit Check Calendar)
  - one dropdown, as recommended here: 3 (Habit Tracker "All ▾", Habit Hub "All ▾", MyStreaks' title "All Streaks ▾")
- **No filter at all:** the other 36, including Streaks, HabitKit, Loop and Everyday.

**Limits.**
- **Sample:** the clutter figures come from a random sample of 250 and show direction and cost, not totals.
- **No usage data:** reviews can't measure how often a control is used. "Occasional" for the filter is inferred from how people describe using it, not from analytics. It should be checked with in-app analytics after launch.
- **One coder:** Claude coded every review.
