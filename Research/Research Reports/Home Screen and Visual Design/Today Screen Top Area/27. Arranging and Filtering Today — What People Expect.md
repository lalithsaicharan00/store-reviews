# Today Screen, Question 27: Arranging and Filtering Today — What People Expect

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 3 October 2026, at the user's request (item 5 of the
> [Next Up list](<../../../../iOS/Docs/Checklists/Current Work Checklist.md>); checklist:
> [Today — Filter, Times of Day and Order](<../../../../iOS/Docs/Checklists/Today — Filter, Times of Day and Order (item 5).md>)).

**Status: research and one recommended design. Nothing is built until the user decides** (the decisions are listed
at the end).

**The user's question, in their words.** "Two things should be very clearly indicated": that you can filter by group,
and that "you can edit these [times of day] if you want to, add a new section, edit existing ones, and you can also
sort the habits within them". Sorting by reminder time is wrong as a default, "because most people don't add
reminders; they just put it into the time of the day". "Design everything according to the user mental model… don't
over-complicate things."

**Basis.**
- **Reviews:** all **1,487,223** reviews screened in about 14 languages; **483 habit-app reviews read in full and
  hand-coded** (298 of them 1–3★), every cited ID and quote checked against the data (`verify.py`). Reused: the Day
  Structure whole-corpus coding (5,438 relevant reviews) and Today Top Area questions 5, 8, 13, 17, 24.
- **Conventions:** Apple's Human Interface Guidelines, Apple's own iPhone apps (Reminders, Notes, Files, Mail,
  Photos, Health), usability research (Nielsen Norman Group, Baymard), each read in full and cited by URL.
- Labels follow the research rules: **users show** (reviews), **iPhone convention** (what people already know from
  their phone), **reasoned from first principles**.

---

## The short answer

1. **Two buttons for two jobs.** The **filter** (⊜) only *shows less*: a group, or habits not yet done. **Edit**
   arranges the day. Nothing that changes your setup lives in the filter.
2. **Edit turns Today itself into the arranging view.** Times of day become tappable (name and start time), habits
   get drag handles, and **+ Add Time of Day** sits at the end. Done goes back. You change the real list, in place.
3. **Your order, kept.** Inside each time of day, habits stay in the order *you* put them. A new habit goes at the end
   of its time of day. Nothing reorders itself (except done habits settling, as today). Reminder times are shown,
   never used to shuffle.
4. **Sorting is a one-off action, not a mode:** "Sort by Reminder Time" or "Sort A to Z" for one time of day, from its
   ··· menu in Edit. Dragging afterwards just continues from there.
5. **One tip, once:** "Make Today yours: put habits in your own order, and rename or add times of day", pointing at
   Edit, gone after first use.

## 1. The mental model (what people believe, and the evidence)

**1. "Today is my day, in my order, and it stays the way I put it."** *Users show.*
- Of 179 reviews about order (random draws, 46 apps), **72 (40%) want their own order or the order they do things
  in**; **24 (13%)** want time order; **only 2 (1%) ask for reminder-time order**. 151 of the 179 never mention
  reminders at all, including 60 of the 72 who want their own order.
- The biggest complaint is not the wrong rule but **an order that won't stay**: 35 of 179 (20%) say the app reshuffles
  their list after a new day, a sync or completing something, so they redo it every day.
- The earlier Day Structure study agrees: of 291 ordering reviews, **81% ask to drag into their own order**.
- "I don’t always do things at the same time of day BUT I like to do them in a certain order. Even if I choose
  morning, afternoon, or evening, I can’t change the order." `A43#244` (Habit Hub, 3★)
- "Seria bom se a gente pudesse organizar a ordem da lista (arrastando) sem precisar colocar horário." *(It would be
  good to arrange the list order by dragging, without having to set a time.)* `P11#11668` (Dear Me, 4★)
- "Please stop moving my tasks." `A10#3549` (Finch, 3★)

**2. "Morning, afternoon and evening are *my* parts of the day."** *Users show; iPhone convention.*
- People want to change them: more sections (47 of 125), different start times (28), the day's start (20), renaming
  (9) (Day Structure). They expect sections to stay in time order on their own; nobody asks to drag sections.
- iPhone Reminders' own Today list already has Morning, Afternoon and Tonight; dragging an item between them changes
  its time. People expect the same here.

**3. "I change a thing where I see it."** *Users show.*
- People look for Edit and Add **on the section, the tag or the filter itself**, and are stuck when it isn't there:
  "There's an option to filter on tags, but no obvious way to add tags." `P8#9946` (Habitica, 3★); "The only thing I
  can’t figure out is how to add to the afternoon and evening lists" `A24#38347` (Fabulous, 5★); one app has five
  separate reviews asking how to delete a group (`A1#45221`, `A1#50103`…).
- Earlier: 174 of 286 relevant reviews couldn't find how to edit, delete or rearrange something (Question 8).

**4. "Filtering is a temporary look, not a change."** *Users show; iPhone convention.*
- People want to narrow by group (19 of 77 filter reviews) and, slightly more, to hide what's done (23 of 77).
- A filter left on looks like lost data (8 of 77): "some habits weren’t showing up… because of the selected time of
  day filter… This isn’t very intuitive." `A33#1164` (Habitify, 5★)
- On the iPhone, filtering is its own visible button that shows when it's on, with one tap back (Mail, Photos);
  arranging is a separate act, through dragging or an **Edit → handles → Done** view (Reminders, Files, Health).

**5. "Hidden gestures don't exist for me."** *iPhone convention; usability research.*
- Apple: a touch-and-hold menu is "hidden by default, so people might not know it's there"; "Always make context menu
  items available in the main interface, too." Hiding controls roughly halves how often they're found (NN/g, 179
  participants). So touch and hold can only ever be a shortcut.
- Apple: Edit is the one toolbar action that should be a **word**, not a symbol.

**6. "A to Z isn't how I think about my day."** *Usability research.* People "rarely think A–Z"; lists ordered by an
inherent logic (here, the day) beat alphabetical ones (NN/g). Lists that rearrange themselves are disliked.

## 2. The design, part by part

### A. The filter: only "show less"

![The filter](<27. Arranging and Filtering Today — Images/2-filter.png>)

- **Show:** All Habits (✓), then each group with today's count. One choice at a time.
- **Hide Completed:** on or off (asked for slightly more often than group filtering).
- **Edit Groups…** at the bottom: groups are edited where they're chosen (mental model 3). This is the only "edit" in
  the filter, and it edits the filter's own choices.
- **When something is filtered,** the button fills in and the top of Today says so, with one tap back:

![Filtered](<27. Arranging and Filtering Today — Images/3-filtered.png>)

### B. Edit: arranging the day, on Today itself

![Edit Today](<27. Arranging and Filtering Today — Images/4-edit.png>)

- **Edit** (a word, top right) turns Today into **Edit Today**. One plain sentence at the top says what's possible:
  "Drag habits into the order you do them, or into another time of day. Tap a time of day to rename it or change when
  it starts."
- **Habits:** ≡ handles; drag to reorder inside a time of day, or drop into another one to move it there (its history
  stays with the habit).
- **Each time of day:** tap its name and time to edit it; its **···** menu holds the rest:

![A time of day's menu](<27. Arranging and Filtering Today — Images/5-section-menu.png>)

- **Rename or Change Time…** opens a small sheet; **Delete** never deletes habits, and says so:

![Editing a time of day](<27. Arranging and Filtering Today — Images/6-time-of-day.png>)

- **+ Add Time of Day** at the end: a name and a start time; it takes its place by time automatically.
- **Done** goes back to Today. (Every change also has the app's usual Undo.)

### C. Shortcuts and other ways in (never the only way)

- **Touch and hold a time-of-day header on Today** opens the same menu (plus "Edit Today"), for people who know the
  gesture. Everything in it is also in Edit.
- **≡ → Times of Day** stays, for people who look in settings first.
- **The bottom of Today keeps only "Note for the Day".** "Edit Times of Day" moves into Edit, which also fixes item 6
  (the squeezed row).

### D. Order rules

1. **Each time of day keeps the person's own order.** It never changes by itself: not on a new day, a sync, an edit or
   adding a reminder.
2. **A new habit goes at the end of its time of day**, and Today flashes it there (as now), one drag from anywhere.
3. **Reminder times are shown on the row** (e.g. "15 min · 7:00 PM") **but don't move it.**
4. **Sorting is a one-off action** for one time of day: "Sort by Reminder Time" (shown only when habits there have
   times) or "Sort A to Z". It rewrites that time of day's order once; dragging afterwards continues from it.
5. **Done habits settle as today:** they move down together after a pause and finished parts fold (decided in
   *Ticking Off, Folding and Small Settings*); the order underneath is untouched, and Hide Completed is there for
   people who'd rather not see them.
6. **Moving to the new rules changes nothing on screen:** the order people see today becomes their saved order.

### E. Teaching, without a tutorial

- **One tip on Edit** (Apple's TipKit), shown once the person has two or more habits in one time of day and has opened
  Today a few times; withdrawn as soon as they use Edit. No chain of tips, no tutorial.
- **The Edit Today sentence** explains everything while it's in use.
- **An empty time of day** says how to fill it: "Add a habit with +, or drag one here in Edit."

## 3. What it deliberately leaves out (to stay simple)

| Left out | Why |
|---|---|
| A permanent "sort by" mode (with a checkmark) | It would fight the person's own order: new habits and drags would land in surprising places. A one-off action gives the 13% who want time order what they need |
| A different order per group or per filter | One order for the day. Separate orders per view are where other apps get complicated |
| Dragging habits on Today outside Edit | Today's touch and hold already opens a habit's menu; mixing in a drag would make taps misfire. Edit is one visible place for all arranging |
| Reordering times of day by hand | They follow their start times; nobody asked to drag them (Day Structure) |
| Filtering by time of day, or by several groups at once | Today already shows the whole day under its headers; one group at a time covers the requests |
| A tutorial or a chain of tips | People skip them and don't remember them (NN/g) |

## 4. Decisions for the user

1. **Default order: the person's own order, new habits at the end of their time of day.** This replaces two current
   rules: "A to Z until the person drags" (Design Rules, Groups) for habits, and "timed habits sorted by reminder
   time" inside a time of day. Groups themselves keep A to Z until dragged (Question 24). **Recommended: yes.**
2. **Edit as a word in the top bar, next to the filter and +.** Three items top right, following Apple's guidance
   that Edit is a word. **Recommended: yes.**
3. **Hide Completed in the filter,** alongside the existing settling of done habits. **Recommended: yes** (asked for
   more than group filtering), but it can wait if you'd rather keep the filter to groups only.
4. **The one-off sort actions** in a time of day's ··· menu. **Recommended: yes** (small, and it serves the people who
   use times), or drop them for an even simpler first version.

## 5. The evidence in numbers

**Which order people want** (179 on-theme reviews, random draws, 46 apps; a review can carry several codes):

| | n | % |
|---|---|---|
| Their own order or the order they do things | 72 | 40% |
| By time of day or scheduled time | 24 | 13% |
| ↳ by reminder time, explicitly | 2 | 1% |
| A choice of sort modes | 16 | 9% |
| Priority | 7 | 4% |
| Alphabetical, wanted | 1 | 1% |
| **What goes wrong:** no way to reorder | 76 | 42% |
| Reordering clumsy or buggy | 37 | 21% |
| **The order changes by itself** | 35 | 20% |
| Where new habits land (top, bottom, creation order) | 12 | 7% |
| Done habits: sink to the bottom / stay put | 6 / 3 | |

**Finding how to edit** (67 reviews): couldn't find it 40 (groups 15, sections 10); it didn't exist 21; found late or
by accident 2. **Filtering** (77 reviews): hide completed 23; filter by group 19; a filter mistaken for missing habits
8; filter bugs 9.

**Keyword counts across the whole corpus** (habit apps, not read by hand, floors): own or drag order 882; can't
reorder 151; by time 110; alphabetical 68; by reminder time 13; order changes by itself 61; hide completed 42; filter
by group 90.

## 6. Tasks in a time of day (added 3 October 2026)

Added by Claude (Claude Code), 3 October 2026, when building Arrange Your Day. The user's guess was "habits first, then
tasks, and only habits can be reordered"; they asked for research to decide. 254 reviews were hand-read in full
(evidence: `Today Screen Evidence/Q27/tasks/`).

**Answer: one order per time of day, habits and tasks together, and tasks can be dragged too.** A new task goes at the
end of its time of day, like a new habit, so adding one never pushes a habit down.

- **One list for the day.** *Users show.* Of 52 relevant reviews about mixed lists, 15 want habits and tasks in one
  list or on one screen and 4 want tasks in a separate tab. **None ask for a fixed habits-first or tasks-first order**;
  the 6 who say how a mixed list should be ordered want their own order.
  - "I really wish there would be a way to have the habits list and the to-do list all together in one list, or at least on the same page." `P61#276` (Hizo, 5★)
  - "I want to sort tasks and habit manually." `P2#11778` (HabitNow, 4★)
- **One-time tasks are expected to be dragged.** *Users show.* Of 61 reviews that say how tasks should be ordered,
  **34 (56%)** want or praise a manual order or dragging; 16 want an automatic sort; 5 want timed tasks in time order
  (the one-off Sort by Reminder Time covers them).
  - "I just wish you could manually drag and reorder your tasks... I don't need specific times, nor do I want to bother with that level of granularity in setting my tasks, but I do want them in some kind of order." `P126#28295` (To Do List, 4★)
- **Clutter** complaints are about recurring habits cluttering a to-do list (7 of 10), mostly praise for a habit-only
  app; none say tasks pushed habits down.

*Limits:* few habit apps mix habits and tasks on one day view, so the mixed-list counts are small: a direction, not a
rate. The ordering counts lean on to-do apps.

---

## Limits

- One coder; samples weighted toward 1–3★; two targeted draws describe what people say when they raise a topic, not
  how common it is. Keyword counts are floors.
- A few apps dominate the ordering complaints (Me+ 36 of 226; 16 of 44 "order changes by itself").
- "Doesn't use reminders" is inferred from the review not mentioning them (151 of 179); 2 reviews say it outright.
- iPhone conventions describe what people are used to, not proof that it suits a habit app; where they and the
  reviews agree, the design follows both.
- Not tested with people yet: check that first-time users find Edit and tell the filter from arranging.
