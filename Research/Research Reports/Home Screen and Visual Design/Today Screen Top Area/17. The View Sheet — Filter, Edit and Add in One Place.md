# Today Screen, Question 17: The View Sheet — Filter, Edit and Add in One Place

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 24 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion. Round 3.

**Screen (round 2).**
- The sheet lists Time of day chips and Group chips.
- Editing sits apart, in an "Edit" block at the bottom.
- There is no way to add a group or a section from there.

**The feedback.**
- Put **Edit** right beside each heading.
- Consider **Add a new group** and **Add a new section** right there.
- Make it obvious that this is a filter, *and* where you edit and add.

**Basis.**
- **Read in full:** 47 new matches on creating groups, tags or sections, and 15 on empty groups.
- **Reused:**
  - round 1's editing findability (174 of 286 relevant reviews couldn't find edit or delete);
  - the Day Structure report's 474 grouping-friction reviews.
- **External:** Habitify's area bar, and Baymard's filter research.

---

## What to do

1. **One heading row per part, with the action on the same line:**

   ```
   DAY SECTIONS                              Edit
   (All)(Anytime 3)(Morning 3)(Afternoon 2 · Now)(Evening 3)(+ Add)

   GROUPS                                    Edit
   (All)(● Health 5)(● Mind 1)(● Home 1)(○ Reading –)(+ New group)
   ```

2. **Three looks for three jobs:**
   - **Filter:** solid chips. A filled chip means it's selected.
   - **Create:** a **dashed** chip with "+" at the end of the row.
   - **Manage:** a plain text **Edit** at the right of the heading. It opens that part's editor.
3. **Adding right there, yes.**
   - "+ Add" under Day sections opens the section editor on a new section, with name and start time.
   - "+ New group" opens a small new-group form: name, colour, and pick habits.
   - Both return to the sheet with the new chip selected.
4. **The first group.** When there are no groups yet, the Groups part shows one line: "Group habits by area, like Health or Work, then filter by them here." Below it is the **+ New group** chip.
   - Groups stay invisible on Home until one exists, but this is where people discover them.
5. **The sheet is called View.** Its two parts read **Layout** and **Show** (Question 15), so "this is a filter" is said in plain words: *Show*.

---

## Key findings

**1. Editing is hard to find when it lives somewhere else.**
- Round 1: 174 of 286 relevant reviews couldn't find how to edit, delete or rearrange something.
- For groups specifically:
  - "an option to edit the category in the long-tap menu would be nice, right now you have to go into a habit first" `P9#160`
  - "Easier to manage categories directly" `P2#7573`
- The Day Structure report found **110** grouping-friction reviews: can't edit, delete or rename groups.

**2. Creating groups is also hard to find.**
- "I couldn't figure out how to add tags in-app." `P8#10040`
- Another reviewer lost the chance to set up areas after onboarding: "I cannot set up areas (work, creativity, etc.)" `P8#9911`
- Habitify puts creation in context: "Swipe left the area bar and choose the + button at the end", then "New Area" (Habitify help). But its *editing* lives in Settings, which is the split this design avoids.

**3. Actions must not look like filters.**
- If "+ New group" looks like a group chip, people tap it expecting a filter.
- A dashed outline and a "+" are the usual "add one" look. Edit is text on the heading line, so it can't be mistaken for a choice.
- Baymard's rule for filters is that people should know what a tap will do "before they click".

**4. Empty groups get in the way.**
- "I have many empty groups which won't delete automatically and which get in the way of the groups I actually do use." `A1#50937`
- So empty groups go last, look quieter, and are one tap from Edit (Question 18).

---

**Limits.**
- **Small counts:** direct reviews on creating groups are few (4 findability complaints this round). The in-context "add" leans on Habitify's pattern and on the general findability evidence.
- **Test:** check first-time users can tell the three looks apart: select, add, edit.
