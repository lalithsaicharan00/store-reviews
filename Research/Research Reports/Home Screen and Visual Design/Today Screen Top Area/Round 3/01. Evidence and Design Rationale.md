**New verification:** 32 selected original reviews underlying prior reports were read in full, with source path, one-based line, exact ID and complete raw record saved in [Verified Prior Review Sources](<Evidence/Verified Prior Review Sources.json>). Selection is purposive, not representative.

**Additional retrieval:** all 1,238,784 canonical records under App Store Reviews and Play Store Reviews were screened with English proximity queries for empty/zero groups, section/group counts, and contextual add/edit. All 51 matched records were read in full and individually assessed in [Targeted Review Assessment](<Evidence/Targeted Review Assessment.jsonl>). This includes adjacent task/school/workout apps, labelled when used; Native Store Reviews were not included in this new scan. The 51 matches are retrieval results, not 51 votes supporting a feature. **External research was used for gaps:** local evidence establishes real needs and failures, but not the winning position of a Layout button, whether zero-result filter options should be disabled, or the best label in a naming test. ## 2. Q1 — Card layout is separate from date navigation

**User requirement:** Day / Week / Month / Year are habit-card layouts. They must leave the calendar sheet. Tapping Today must still open the calendar.

**Observed evidence:**

- `14251019776` (A23#4099, Streaks) asks to always open the monthly calendar. `07a2f620-8d5d-428b-8cdf-0d1a9f8c488c` (P130#175, Way of Life) asks for a monthly default instead of weekly. These establish preferences and persistence, not exact switching frequency.
- `6384710442` (A33#3440, Habitify) objects to extra taps to change dates and inaccessible history. `9564885641` (A31#3258, Do Habits) objects to replacing a simple day swipe with calendar → date → confirm. Keep everyday date movement visible.
- Top Area report 10 corrects report 4: most of its earlier “switching” praise was about having several views. Its proposed title/calendar combination, however, conflates the two jobs under the user's clarified card-layout model.
- Apple recommends a labelled pop-up for mutually exclusive view choices in limited space; the label should predict its effect. [Apple: Pop-up buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pop-up-buttons?language=objc).
- Todoist locates list/board/calendar selection in Display, demonstrating a content-layout control separate from date selection. This is precedent, not proof our exact control will win. [Todoist: List layout](https://www.todoist.com/help/todoist/features/use-the-list-layout-in-todoist-AMAhHMVRH).

**Round 3 decision:** Keep one compact **Layout** button in the existing header next to Filter and Add. Open **Card layout** with four labelled visual examples: Day — checklist; Week — seven-day rows; Month — calendar per habit; Year — heatmap per habit. Show the selected option and remember it. No permanent four-segment row; no layout options inside Filter or the calendar. This adds one small, explicit entry point, not another row of choices.

The Today title opens **Choose date**, with month navigation and a Today shortcut only. Selecting a date changes the date context; it does not reset the layout. Changing layout keeps the selected anchor date and active filters. In wider-period layouts, the title names the period containing that date, following report 11. Keep Day's week strip; avoid a duplicate strip in the other layouts. Row size remains the separate existing Appearance preference.

**Alternatives considered:** an ellipsis menu saves a word but hides the purpose and adds a navigation step; putting layout in Filter mixes presentation with inclusion; keeping it in the calendar violates the user's explicit model. The exact compact button and previews are design inference, medium confidence; first-click testing remains needed.

## 3. Q2 and Q7 — Day sections, Your day, and one day-start boundary

**Evidence:**

- `13005403096` asks for more than three periods, including four-hour blocks for shift work (Chinese original). `6443527252` asks for custom commute and lunch-break periods (Japanese original).
- `1230639082` separates after-work evening habits from a Before bed routine. `1771112848` asks for wakeup/lunch/dinner/bedtime slots rather than only three periods. These are meaningful parts of a person's day, not necessarily conventional clock labels.
- `7807522940` asks for clearer time-section names (Chinese original). This supports editable, understandable names, not one universally preferred English umbrella label.
- `12403094681` and `11811576801` describe late-night routines counting for the next day. `11348827617` separately objects to forcing Morning to begin at midnight.
- `7302708097` explicitly describes a day from 03:00 to 02:59, then discovers an existing Start day at setting. It directly supports one visible rollover control and a concrete explanation.

**Naming decision:** Use **Day sections** in Filter, with familiar options Anytime, Morning, Afternoon, Evening and custom names. Use **Your day** for the editor that combines sections and day start. Actions: **Edit**, **Add section**, **All section habits**. Keep **Groups**, **Add group**, **All group habits** for the independent life-area dimension. In first use, helper copy says “Filter by a part of your day.”

Why revise “Time of day”? It is understandable for filtering by clock periods and remains natural explanatory language. But “Add a time of day” is awkward for custom Before work/Night shift sections, and “Times of day” poorly explains the whole-day rollover setting. “Day sections” names the visible list structure; “Your day” names the broader editor. This is a model-based copy recommendation, **not a claim that reviews prove these words outperform Time of day**. Report 13's phrase frequency does not settle that question.

**Day start interaction:** In Your day, show **Day starts at 04:00** near the top, separately from the section list. Supporting copy: **“Before 04:00 counts toward the previous day.”** A detail sheet illustrates **“Friday 03:59 → Thursday”** and **“Friday 04:00 → Friday.”** There is one editable time and no Day ends at input. “Your day ends just before the next one starts” explains the derived boundary; internally it is a continuous boundary, not an extra minute gap.

04:00 is the existing wireframe's customized example, **not a newly selected global default**. This research does not establish the best default. Changing the boundary must preserve past history; apply prospectively with an effective-date explanation. Morning can still start at 06:00 or 09:00. The day-start setting remains available if visible section grouping is turned off. A section window organizes habits; it is not a completion deadline or an automatic notification.

The previous report's cyclic windows have a potential gap when rollover is 04:00 and Morning begins 06:00. For this iteration, explain that 04:00–05:59 belongs to the new tracking day and the previous named time window can remain current until Morning begins; it does not change dated attribution. Anytime remains untimed. Do not claim section windows and tracking-day boundaries are the same operation.

**Current-source correction:** A historical Habitify review praises shifted days; current Habitify documentation says its daily reset is midnight and distinguishes filtering, reset and actual log timestamps. Do not cite that historical praise as evidence of Habitify's current rollover capability. Our custom boundary is supported by user need and the existing product model. [Habitify: Time of Day](https://intercom.help/habitify-app/en/articles/7990118-manage-the-time-of-day).

## 4. Q3 — Filter first, with contextual edit and creation

**Evidence:** `713191f6-d782-4e9a-8e6e-84b5f80c3ac7` says editing/deleting should be as findable as Add. `b110efd7-de7b-48e0-9525-aa477aa8ec9f` specifically wants category editing from a contextual menu rather than entering a habit first. `86c8713c-0d74-4fae-a735-3cd26c49aacd` and `79ac3d35-287f-420f-88bc-f5f2087a70ff` object to uneditable/misnamed tags. These support control and proximity but do not prescribe exact coordinates.

Habitify documents Add Area from the area chooser, with optional member selection. This supports creation at the point of organization. [Habitify: Custom Areas](https://intercom.help/habitify-app/en/articles/6113636-use-area-to-categorize-habits).

**Decision:** Keep the title **Filter**. Place **Edit** at the right of each Day sections / Groups heading. Keep selection chips visually primary; place **+ Add section** or **+ Add group** immediately under its own choices, with a separate **All section habits / All group habits** browse link. Remove the detached generic Edit block at the bottom of Round 2.

Selecting a chip filters. Edit opens the appropriate manager; Add opens a labelled name/start-time or name/member form; the browse link opens assigned habits. Edit/Add/Browse do not silently toggle a filter. Preserve filter selections while entering and leaving those flows. Saving a new group/section returns to Filter with a confirmation; it becomes selectable but is not auto-applied. Cancel returns without creating anything. Existing history stays attached to habits when membership or names change.

**First group:** Keep a small Groups block inside Filter with “No groups yet” and **Add group**. Do not require group creation or seed fake groups. This slightly revises the older “invisible until used” rule: optional organization needs a visible creation path once the user deliberately opens Filter; it does not earn permanent Home chips.

## 5. Q4 — Scheduled count, assigned total and completion are three facts

**Evidence:** `4428439161` asks for the number of habits in each time category (Chinese original). It does not specify today's count versus total. `9653787577` values remaining progress by day part. `13350914040` objects to losing streaks for unscheduled weekdays. `12153222646` objects to a 100% day circle ignoring an outstanding weekly goal. That last original is about **omitting a weekly goal**, not proof that all weekly goals should be excluded — an important nuance in report 2's interpretation.

External filter research recommends showing result counts to make choices predictable. [NN/g: Applying filters](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/applying-filters/), [Baymard: Filter UI](https://baymard.com/blog/ecommerce-filter-ui).

**Decision for this iteration:**

| Surface | Number / label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Filter chips | Morning **4**, Health **7** | Unique habits scheduled on the selected tracking date, before other filters and before Hide completed. One sheet helper explicitly names that scope. |
| Filter sheet footer | **Show 2 habits** | Unique visible matching habits after both selected dimensions and Hide completed. This responds to combined choices. |
| All-habits group/section list | **7 today · 9 total** | Scheduled on the selected date versus all assigned non-archived habit records, including those not due. Paused entries remain in assigned inventory with a Paused label. |
| Group/section detail | **Scheduled today (7)** / **Not scheduled today (2)** | Explicit partition of assigned inventory. Changing filters does not change membership. |
| Day/section progress | Existing done-of-due count | Completion, not inventory. Keep the existing progress system separate. Multiple scheduled slots may give a different denominator from distinct habit counts. |

Chip count helper: **“Numbers = habits scheduled today, including completed.”** For past/future dates, substitute the short selected date: “Numbers = habits scheduled Wed 23 Sep.” When another filter is selected, keep these base counts stable and show the combined result in the footer; annotate “Show” as the actual intersection. This intentionally favors readable scheduled-vs-total semantics over unlabeled dynamic facets. It is a design inference to test, not a demonstrated user preference.

“All” in All groups means no group restriction, including Ungrouped. “All sections” means no section restriction, including Anytime. Neither overrides the selected date's schedule. For Week/Month/Year card layouts, keep **the selected tracking date as the filtering anchor**, state it in the filter helper, and show surrounding history on each matching card. Changing card layout alone must not change the set of selected-date habits. Period-wide inventory exploration belongs in All habits; do not silently redefine “today” counts as weekly occurrences.

Do not display ambiguous **4 of 7** inside filter chips: it currently reads as completion, but could be mistaken for assigned inventory. Inventory totals belong in the explicit browse surface. A habit appearing in multiple sections counts once within each section's unique count and once overall; section counts need not sum to the overall unique-habit count. Sub-steps and glasses/minutes are not additional habits.

## 6. Q5 and Q6 — Zero scheduled is not an empty collection

**Direct new evidence:**

- `14385871874` (A1, source line 50938): unused empty groups clutter the top row; the user cannot delete or organize them. This supports relegating genuinely empty groups, plus full editing. It is one case, not proof every zero-today option should disappear.
- `5303610660` (A13, source line 13734): automatic time filtering repeatedly opens an empty screen. Do not auto-switch to Now or an empty section.
- `c891cd74-64bb-43e6-9a17-29331bb733f7` (P2, source line 4285): wants categories to display their entries, not just their edit page. This directly supports a browse route.
- `5224462090` (A33#1164): a forgotten time filter looked like missing habits. `d54f2e84-cd0d-4152-bc89-32a3233eadb7`: untagged tasks could not be found. Keep active filters visible and Ungrouped accessible.
- `14265126334` (A23#1958) and `8322360433` (A31#3753) want non-due habits out of the daily checklist. `3810462929` (A53#1553) wants access to record extra sessions on off days. The new scan also found a contrary review, `5292f344-fd2e-4062-a6bc-2fc27dcc277d`, opposing off-day logging. Access should be explicit and should not silently reschedule a habit.

NN/g's empty-state guidance supports a precise reason and a direct next action; Baymard supports visible applied-filter summaries and recovery from zero results. Neither establishes the exact habit-tracker treatment below. [NN/g: Empty states](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/), [Baymard: Applied filters](https://baymard.com/research-articles/how-to-design-applied-filters).

**Decision:**

| State | Filter treatment | All-habits treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Assigned habits and some scheduled on selected date | Normal selectable chip with scheduled count | Show scheduled and total counts; tap to browse members |
| Assigned habits, none scheduled on selected date | Stable disclosure **Not scheduled today (n groups/sections)**; expanded option names **0 today · 3 total** | Keep fully accessible; show next due dates and **View all 3 habits** |
| No assigned habits | Inside the same secondary disclosure, explicitly **No habits yet**; no misleading completion percentage | Allow add existing habits, edit or delete empty group |
| Selected filter becomes zero after a date change | Keep its active chip visible; never drop the selection silently | Empty state names the date/filter and offers Clear filters / View all habits |
| Two individually nonempty filters have no intersection | Footer says **Show 0 habits**; allow deliberate selection | Explain “No habits match these filters”; offer removal of the specific filter |
| Hide completed removes everything | **All matching habits are completed** + **Show completed** | Distinguish from not scheduled or no membership |

Zero-scheduled options are not disabled: users may deliberately inspect another date or preserve a filter, and the recovery path is explicit. They are secondary to reduce clutter, not deleted. Keep the chosen zero option visible even when its disclosure is collapsed. Preserve user ordering; do not reorder options every time counts change. For long lists, let the sheet scroll with its header and result action fixed; use the All-habits manager for search and organization.

**Access path:** Filter → All group habits → Health → full inventory with Scheduled today / Not scheduled today. Same for sections. From a zero-result Home state, **View all 3 habits** goes directly to that group's inventory. Back returns to the previous filter/date/layout, not a reset Home. The existing Not due today row can coexist; browsing inventory does not add off-day habits to the due count or silently tick them. Any bonus log is an explicit habit-detail action under the existing Non-Scheduled Days recommendation.

## 8. What is supported, inferred, or still untested

**Strong enough to act:** separate date and layout per user requirement; preserve alternate card views; contextual edit and optional custom groups/sections; no accidental hidden habits; explicit scheduled-vs-total distinction; late-night day boundary; access to non-due members.

**Design inference:** exact Layout placement and preview shape; Day sections / Your day labels; collapse zero-scheduled options rather than always show all; stable base counts with a separate intersection footer; first-group creation inside Filter. Each follows observed problems plus known UI patterns, but no source proves the exact design is optimal.

**Usability checks after this design iteration:** Ask a new user to change the card to Month without changing the selected date; find a not-due Learning habit; explain “7 today · 9 total”; create a first group; edit Morning without changing the filter; explain where a Friday 03:59 log belongs. Test large groups, long names, larger type and zero intersections. Review evidence cannot replace those checks.

## 9. Corrections and retained boundaries

- Round 2 report 10's title-plus-layout sheet is superseded for this iteration by the user's clarified model.
- Report 13's vocabulary frequency does not establish a winning label or prove filter use is rare. New review `10747457174` specifically asks for faster time filtering: frequency varies by user.
- “Visible chips remove the risk” is too absolute. They reduce hidden-filter risk; they do not guarantee users notice.
- Today scheduled habits and all assigned habits must not be treated as the same list. Optional inventory access reconciles both needs.
- 04:00 stays a customized example; this work does not settle a default, monetization, group limits, routine-runner design or global scheduling policy.
- Source report “all apps leave off-days out” and other broad claims were not independently revalidated here and are not used as universal facts.
