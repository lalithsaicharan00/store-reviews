# Today Screen, Question 42: What's on Today — The Whole Day at a Glance, at Any Habit Count

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 25 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion. This is step 2 of the Today-screen jobs work: it goes deep on the first job, "See what's due today". The step-1 board, "Today screen — what people come to do", is Figma section 130:479.

**The question.**
- In the morning, people open Today for a quick look at what's on.
- What are they actually looking for?
- How do we answer that quickly, without scrolling, whether they have 3 habits or 25?

**Basis.**
- **Screened:** all 1,487,223 reviews (App Store, Google Play and the native-app corpora) for four families of wording:
  - glance or overview ("at a glance", "plan my day", "what's on today", "一目了然", "한눈에", "auf einen Blick");
  - long lists (scrolling, "long list", "fits on one screen", overwhelm);
  - focus (hide completed, collapse, "only what's left", "next habit");
  - counts ("how many left", icon badges).
- **Matched:** 5,177 reviews.
- **The reading set was 3,256 reviews:** all 2,833 habit-app matches, plus 423 from adjacent apps (Reminders, Google Tasks, Microsoft To Do, calendars and to-do apps). The adjacent 423 are the 173 focus/count matches plus a random 250 of the rest (seed 20260925).
- **Read in full:** 2,668 reviews.
- **Coded by rule instead:**
  - 203 where "scroll" was not about the list (TikTok, the clothes shop and so on);
  - 385 one-line Chinese or Korean "clear at a glance" praise: 104 about stats or records, 281 generic. Part of that set comes from one app's review-for-membership burst ("发评论获取会员", `A1#5248`), so it carries no weight here.
- **Relevant:** 995 reviews coded by hand. Every quote was checked against its review by script.
- **External:**
  - Nielsen Norman Group on chunking, progressive disclosure and working memory;
  - Apple's WWDC widget principles ("glanceable, relevant, personalizable");
  - product documentation and reviews for Apple Reminders, Things 3, Todoist, Structured, Tiimo and Streaks;
  - the 49-app Home census (Question 1);
  - the saved Dribbble references (Theme Pack report).
- **Dribbble and Pinterest could not be browsed live.** Both are blocked in this session's browser. Their search pages also return no content to a plain fetch, because they render with JavaScript.

Citations such as `A23#4439` point to line 4439 (counting from 0) of `App Store Reviews/23. …/reviews.jsonl`. `P` means Play Store and `N` means the native apps.

## The short answer

- **Each morning look answers three questions at once:**
  1. **How much is on today?**
  2. **In what order does it come?**
  3. **Is anything unusual today, or anything I might miss?**
- **It is not "read me every habit".** People track the same habits every day, so they *recognise* them (icon, colour, position) rather than read them.
- **So the screen should always show the whole day on one screen, as its parts, with details on demand:**
  - Each part of the day (Morning, Afternoon, Evening, Anytime) is one line. It shows the icons of its habits and how many are left.
  - The part of the day you are in is open, with its full cards. The other parts stay folded to their one line.
  - Done habits get out of the way. They slide to the bottom of their part and turn slim. A part where everything is done folds to "All done".
  - The day's count stays in the week strip ("5/11").
- **Then it works at any size:**
  - At 5 habits, everything is open.
  - At 12, 25 or 40 habits, the day is still one screen of 5 lines, plus the part you're in.

**How to read the table:**

1. **The strongest signal is the whole day on one screen** (153 reviews in 49 apps). Twelve reviewers say plainly what happens without it: habits below the fold get forgotten.
2. **"All of it" and "overwhelming" are not in conflict.** The overwhelm reviews describe *one long, flat, undivided list*:
   - "no rhyme or reason" `P12#8457`;
   - "just in a big long list" `P14#95`.

   The relief they name is *structure*, not hiding the plan. One reviewer says: "All those goals don't feel daunting when I can see them all on one tall screen" `A48#3740`.
3. **Done habits should step aside, but not vanish without a trace.** 85 want them out of the way and 14 want them visible. Many land in between: "dropped to the bottom … but still be visible" `P84#15957`.
4. **The count is expected, most loudly in adjacent to-do apps.** 24 of the 49 count reviews are from Reminders, Google Tasks and Microsoft To Do users, who call a missing count a "deal-breaker" (`N6#756`).
5. **"At a glance" means history as often as today:** 115 reviews mean progress over weeks and months, and 110 mean today's list. These are two different jobs. History belongs to the Week, Month and Year views and the Progress tab, not the morning glance.

**People with many habits.**
- 80 reviews say they track many. The stated counts run from 6 to 100, with a median of about 18:
  - "I have ~50 habits and can only see 5 1/2 at a time" `P37#40`;
  - "about 40 … cluttered with anything past 20" `A41#94`.
- What they ask for, in order:
  - denser rows (18);
  - groups (16);
  - all on one screen (15);
  - done out of the way (9);
  - filters (7);
  - collapsing (6).

## Why a plain list can't do it (reasoned, with our current board)

The Day board (Figma 120:4) on a 390 × 844 iPhone:
- The header uses about 154 pt.
- Each card is 64 pt plus a 5 pt gap.
- Each section header is about 40 pt.
- The tab bar floats over the last 80 pt or so.

That leaves about 600 pt for the list, which holds:

| Habits today | Plain list with 4 sections | Rows at 52 pt ("Compact") | One line per part of the day (proposal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | fits | fits | fits (all open) |
| 8 | about 6 visible, 2 below the fold | fits | fits |
| 12 | about 6 visible; half below | about 8 visible | fits: 4 folded lines + the part you're in |
| 25 | about 1 screen in 4 | about 1 in 3 | fits: 4 folded lines + the part you're in |
| 40 | about 1 in 6 | about 1 in 5 | fits (icons show "+N" past 8 per line) |

- Shrinking rows only buys a few habits. Text size also sets a floor: large Dynamic Type makes rows taller (C171, accessibility).
- The only way to keep "the whole day on one screen" at 25 habits is to **stop drawing every habit at full size at once**. Summarise the parts you are not in, and expand the part you are in.

This is the standard guidance, applied here:
- **Chunking:** people take in about 3–6 chunks, so group the list into meaningful chunks ([NN/g, chunking](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/chunking/)).
- **Progressive disclosure:** show the most important things first and the rest on request, with no more than 2 levels ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)).
- **Glanceable:** understood in a fraction of a second; relevant to the moment ([Apple, Principles of great widgets](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10048/)).

## The design this points to

**1. Parts of the day become the overview.**
- Each part of the day (Quitting, Anytime, Morning, Afternoon, Evening, or the user's own) is **one line when folded**. The line has:
  - the name;
  - the **icons of its habits**, in order: done ones filled or dimmed, left ones outlined, up to 8 and then "+N";
  - **"3 left"** or **"All done"** (Question 38).
- Tap the line to open it; tap the header to fold it again.
- This extends Question 38's folded header by adding the icons, so a folded part still shows what is in it. Nothing is hidden, only summarised.

**2. Which parts are open by default:**
- **If the whole day fits on the screen open, everything is open.** A user with 5 habits never sees a fold.
- **If it doesn't fit, the part you're in (Now) is open and the others are folded.** In the morning, that means the Morning part plus four one-line summaries: the whole day on one screen.
- Anytime counts as a part like any other. It is open when it is the only part, or when you open it.
- A part you open or fold stays that way for the rest of the day. At the new day (the "New day starts at" time), the default comes back.
- *Reasoned from first principles, and to test:* the rule is predictable (it follows the clock and the Now badge) and always visible (every folded part shows its icons and count). Show a tip the first time a part folds by itself.

**3. Done habits step aside:**
- After the Undo bar disappears, a done habit **slides to the bottom of its part and becomes a slim done row**.
- When every habit in a part is done, the part **folds to "All done"**. That is also the finished look Question 30 asked for.
- The first undone card in the Now part is always **what's next**. This closes Question 30's open item "what's next" without a badge.
- *This revises Question 30's open item 2* ("done cards stay where they are"): 85 reviews want done out of the way and 14 want it visible. Sinking and slimming serves both groups.

**4. Only today, and the count once:**
- Habits not due today stay in the closed "Not due today (n)" row (Non-Scheduled Days report).
- The day's count stays in the strip's today card ("5/11"). No extra summary banner.

**5. Density is the user's choice:**
- Compact rows remain a setting in Me → Appearance (Question 7).
- Suggest Compact once, at about 15 habits, as a tip, never automatically.

## What this does not settle

- **The folding threshold:**
  - "Fits on one screen" depends on text size and device.
  - The rule should be computed, not a fixed number.
  - Test whether people understand a part that folds on its own.
- **The order of the folded lines:** by time, the same as open. Past parts that are not finished stay in place, not sent to the bottom, so a missed morning habit is still seen.
- **Users with no day sections** (everything in Anytime):
  - The overview then relies on done-sinking and Compact rows.
  - Groups (Question 24) could act as the parts. That needs its own check.
- **Icons carry the folded line,** so every habit needs a distinct icon. Recognising by icon and colour is how users tell habits apart at a glance (Home Screen Cards report, section 4).
