# Today Screen, Question 50: The Progress Fill — Do People Actually Want It?

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 25 October 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision.** The user decides. This checks the evidence behind the Android Today list's row fill: a habit's row fills from the left in a light tint of its colour as today's progress grows, and is fully tinted when done (Android A32, A34; no fill on cut-down rows, A41).

**The question (the user, 25 Oct 2026).** Do we really need the progress fill? Is it what people are looking for, or something we read into a few reviews and kept? How many people like it and how many don't? Is it a must-have, a nice-to-have, or no value at all?

**Basis.**
- **Every review in both stores:** 205 apps, 1,238,784 reviews (App Store and Play Store corpora).
- **Retrieved:** reviews that mention a fill, progress bar, ring or circle filling, "turns green", tint or shade, together with a habit or list word; plus every bar, swipe or slide review of the two apps whose rows fill (App Store 1, Habit Tracker; App Store 31, Done). **1,427 reviews, all read individually.**
- **Coded:** 610 on the topic. The other 817 use the words in another sense ("fills me with joy", "fill in the form", Finch's bird colours) and are listed in the evidence.
- **Reused:** Question 34 (where the fill came from) and Question 49 (how it should look).

---

## The answer

1. **It wasn't invented, but it was adopted on thin evidence.** Question 34 (24 Sep) took the fill from two apps the user pointed to, and counted 34 reviews praising it, all in those two apps. It didn't look at apps without a fill, or for people who dislike one. This study does both.
2. **People who have a fill like it, almost nobody is against it, and people without one ask for it.** Users show:
   - **66 reviews praise a filling row or bar** (4.79★), in 11 apps. 27 more praise a round button that fills (Streaks, Atoms).
   - **1 review asks to remove a fill.** 8 more are confused by one or find it wrong for a case (limits that "light up" when exceeded, a week goal that never fills).
   - **29 ask for one where it's missing**, in 15 apps. 27 of the 29 want to see **in-between progress** ("5 of 8"), not just done.
   - **29 complain when it stops working** (2.59★): they notice and mind.
3. **But it's a small topic, not a reason anyone picks or leaves an app.** In the two apps whose rows fill (61,839 reviews), 37 praise the fill; 432 mention streaks and 1,038 widgets. People asking for it rate the app 4.17★ on average: happy users asking for polish. No review leaves an app for lacking a fill.
4. **So: a nice-to-have that does a real job.** The job is what people need; the fill is one good way to do it.
   - **Seeing what's done at a glance** is needed: 27 praise a clear colour change when done, 14 complain when done is hard to see or ask for it to be stronger. Any clear done state does this.
   - **Seeing how far along a count, time or checklist habit is** is the fill's own value. That is where the requests come from.
   - **For a single check habit, the fill adds nothing beyond the done colour.** Empty, then full.
5. **Recommendation: keep it, keep it cheap.** It's already built, it's liked, nobody asks for it gone, and it doubles as the done state. Don't spend more on it: no fill-style setting, no extra motion. Keep the "5/8" text beside it (Question 34: the fill alone isn't enough). If it ever has to go, keep a clear done state and the text, and move in-between progress to a ring on the button.

## What users show

### 1. Where the fill exists, people like it

| What people say | Reviews | Mean ★ | Apps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Like the fill on a habit row, card or bar | 66 | 4.79 | 11 |
| Like a round button or ring that fills (Streaks, Atoms, Loop) | 27 | 4.78 | 6 |
| Like a clear colour change when done | 27 | 4.74 | 14 |
| Like filling elsewhere: calendar squares, heatmaps, the day's ring | 91 | 4.81 | 33 |
| Like "visual progress" without saying which kind | 157 | 4.87 | 40 |

- **Satisfaction is the word people use.**
  - "v satisfying to see the full bars when I have completed them" `A1#10698299844`
  - "For some reason whenever you fill up the bar, it's satisfying." `A1#8080428489`
  - "It is so satisfying to click on the bars and see them become colorful." `A31#8279286648`
- **An empty bar can pull people in.** "seeing an empty bar each morning can motivate you to get started" `A1#9435708638`
- **Some prefer it to a number.** One reviewer, after trying 40+ apps, lists "progress bar instead of a fraction" among ten must-haves `A31#11121493895`.
- **Whole-row colour beats a thin strip.** "on the Apple Watch when you complete a habit the whole item isn't coloured in like it does on the phone app, it's only a small bar along the bottom… harder to feel the accomplishment" `A1#10774310023`
- **Concentrated in three apps.** 46 of the 66 are App Store 1 (22), Done (15) and Strides (9). 8 are from a school planner and a to-do app; **58 are from habit apps.**
- **The pleasure of filling is broader than rows.** 91 reviews love filling calendar squares and heatmaps ("MUST. FILL. BOXES." `A34#13882752696`). Filling works as a reward wherever it appears.

### 2. Almost nobody is against it

| What people say | Reviews | Mean ★ |
|---|---|---|
| Want a fill removed | 1 | 4.00 |
| Fill shows the wrong thing for limits or quitting | 3 | 3.33 |
| Don't understand what a fill means | 5 | 2.40 |

- **The one removal request** is for Loop's score circles on daily habits: "it isn't necessary for there to be a bar to fill" `P3#4dd47ff7-0f44-45df-a2f2-5221c329e592` (4★).
- **Limits are the real failure.** A limit that fills reads as success when it's been broken:
  - "if I spend… 4 hours on YouTube… it will light up and say I reached my goal when in reality I didn't" `P3#9a0d8299-a781-470c-8cdf-b86dd868cdca`
  - Streaks' quit habit starts full-coloured, which "confusingly" looks like a completed positive habit `A23#4013137782`.
  - **Our design already handles this:** a cut-down row never fills (A41).
- **"Lighter" as a signal confuses.** "the habit is marked with a lighter color than if it was fulfiled. It is confusing" `A1#12738821325`. Question 49 found 17 such complaints about tinted rows; that's why our rows start white.

### 3. Where it's missing, people ask for it, mostly for in-between progress

29 reviews in 15 apps ask to see a habit's own progress on the list (4.17★). **27 of the 29 want the in-between steps**; 2 want done to stand out.

- **Counts:**
  - "if you have a daily goal of 3, clicking on the circle once should fill up the ring by 1/3" `P2#59bcc2d6-06e7-47eb-a52d-95f4b722015f`
  - "Habits that require more than one completion per day should NOT be marked as done when clicked only one time. There should be a progress bar" `A8#10330137184`
  - Translated from Spanish: water habits should "show the progress bar and not just the number" `P2#2fa2632f-d62d-4fb3-a249-554b68d6eea3`
- **Exactly our design, asked for unprompted:** "each habit's row background could colored from left to right until it is full like a progress bar" `P2#89fe46f4-84ab-4b19-b1e2-847a533b793a`
- **Partial shown as failure hurts.** "the streak is broken and the circle is red all day as I rack up the numbers on that item, which is not motivating" `P24#bccda6b9-ea87-4d34-8975-0ff0b7c00e0b`
- **Done at a glance.** "if the entire habit line would turn green if done - it is hard at a glance to work out what is done and what is outstanding" `A41#11725650343`
- **6 reviews ask for the whole row or bar to take the colour**; none ask to move colour off the row into a small ring.

### 4. When it breaks, people mind

29 reviews (2.59★) complain that a bar or ring didn't fill, filled wrongly or vanished after an update.
- "when a habit is completed for the day it will no longer fill in the bar… A month ago I would have given 5 stars" `A1#13688656315` (3★)
- Done, after a redesign: quit habits "no longer show the color bar when you complete them" `A31#9525164553`
- Part of this is about trust (did my log save?), not the visual itself. Still, people look at the fill to know where they stand.

### 5. Already-made decisions the reviews back up

| Finding | Reviews | Our design |
|---|---|---|
| Week or month goals that never fill until the last day feel like failing | 11 (of the 21 who want their fill changed) | Fill toward the week or month goal (U25) |
| Making the row the control causes wrong inputs (App Store 1's drag-to-fill) | 16 wrong inputs, 17 want a tap instead; 50 love the swipe | Only the round button logs (Question 34) |
| The fill alone isn't enough; people want the number | 3 here, 6 in Question 34 | "5/8 glasses" text beside the fill |
| Limits must not fill | 3 | No fill on cut-down rows (A41) |

## How much it matters

**Reasoned from the numbers, not from any single review.**

- **Small but one-sided.** 66 like, 1 against: about 66 to 1. Counting everyone confused, about 7 to 1.
- **Not a headline feature.** In the two apps whose rows fill, it's praised in 0.06% of reviews, against 0.7% mentioning streaks and 1.7% widgets. People notice it and enjoy it; they don't write about it unprompted, the way they write about streaks, reminders or widgets.
- **Not a reason to switch.** No review chooses or leaves an app over the fill alone. The 29 requests come from people already using the app (4.17★). That makes it polish, not a must-have.
- **Its value depends on the habit type:**

| Habit type | What the fill shows | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Single check (once a day) | Empty → full | Same as the done colour, which is needed anyway |
| Count (5 of 8 glasses) | How far along, at a glance | Real: the most-asked-for case |
| Time (20 of 30 min) | How far along | Real |
| Checklist (2 of 5 steps) | How far along | Real |
| Week or month goal | How far along the period | Real, only if it fills toward the period (U25) |
| Limit or quit | — | Harmful if it fills; ours doesn't (A41) |

- **Why keep it rather than cut it:**
  - It already does the done state, which is needed regardless.
  - It's the only signal readable across the whole list without reading every number.
  - Its known problems (tint legibility, limits, week goals, the row as a control) are already designed out.
  - Removing it would mean designing a second done state and a second partial-progress signal.
- **Why not invest more:** nothing in the reviews asks for a fill style, fill animations or a setting. A second style would double every state to design and test (Question 49).

---

## What to do

1. **Keep the fill as it is**: white row, light tint of the habit's colour from the left, fully tinted when done, solid ✓ (A32, A34).
2. **Keep the number** ("5/8 glasses") beside it. The fill gives the glance, the text gives the exact amount.
3. **Keep it out of limits and quitting** (A41), and fill week and month goals toward the period (U25).
4. **Keep the button as the only control.** The fill shows; it never takes a touch.
5. **No fill setting, no extra investment.** Only 1 review in 1.24 million asks to turn a fill off.
6. **If it ever has to go**, the minimum to keep is a clear done state plus the text; in-between progress would move to a ring on the round button (27 reviews like that pattern in Streaks and Atoms).

---

**Limits.**
- **Keyword retrieval.** Reviews describing the fill without these words are missed, so every count here is a floor.
- **Mostly English.** "Progress bar" was searched in five languages; everything else in English. Translated quotes are marked.
- **Few apps have a row fill.** The praise comes mostly from three apps, so "people like it" means "people who have it like it". The 29 requests from apps without one partly offset this.
- **No direct comparison.** No review compares a row fill with a ring on the button in the same app.
- **Possible solicited reviews.** Habio's five near-identical "visual progress charts are motivating" reviews (App Store 67), a pair of identical HabitKit reviews (`A7#9474887392`, `A7#9475276997`) and Habit Pixel's marketing-toned run (Play 14) sit only in the generic and elsewhere rows. None are in the fill counts.
- **Rating mismatch.** 8 of the 29 "it stopped working" reviews still give 4–5★; they're counted by what they say.
