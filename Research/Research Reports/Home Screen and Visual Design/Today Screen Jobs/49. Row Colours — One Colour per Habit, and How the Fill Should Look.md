# Today Screen, Question 49: Row Colours — One Colour per Habit, and How the Fill Should Look

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 26 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion. This builds on Question 34 (the card is the progress) and the native-list boards in Figma (sections 174:2 and 175:2).

**The question.** Each part of the day is now one card, and its habits are rows inside it. For those rows:
1. Do people like each habit having its own colour, or do they prefer plain white rows?
2. Which fill works better?
   - **(W)** a white row, with a light tint of the habit's colour filling it as progress grows;
   - **(T)** every row lightly tinted from the start, with a darker shade filling it.
3. Should people choose?

**Basis.**
- **Scan:** the App Store and Play Store corpora, searched for colour words.
  - 12,620 raw mentions.
  - Narrowed to habit apps (to-do apps and Finch's pet colours left out) and to the English word colour/color, then deduplicated.
- **Three sets, 968 reviews, all read individually:**
  - per-habit colour (743);
  - shades, tints and legibility (166);
  - plain, neutral or too many colours (100).
- **Coded:** 257 reviews to specific findings, plus 364 that praise per-habit colour.

---

## The answer

1. **One colour per habit: yes, on by default. Users show this clearly.**
   - Colour is the most-praised customisation in the corpus, and its absence is a common complaint.
   - Only 5 reviews are against it.
2. **Fill: use W, a white row with a light tint filling it.**
   - Users show that T causes problems: text disappears on the tint, and partial and full look alike.
   - Nobody compares W and T directly, so the choice of W is partly reasoned from first principles.
3. **Don't make the fill style a setting.** Nobody asks for one. Let people choose each habit's colour instead:
   - a full palette, including greys;
   - a custom colour picker;
   - never random colours, and never behind a paywall.

---

## What users show

**1. Per-habit colour is loved, and missed when it's absent.**

| What people say | Reviews | Mean ★ |
|---|---|---|
| Praise colour-coding their habits ("I love that I can choose different colors for each tracker, makes seeing my progress super easy at a glance" `A3#6686`) | 364 | 4.80 |
| Want to set a colour per habit and can't: missing, random, all one colour, or paywalled | 76 | 4.04 |
| Want more colours or a custom picker | 65 | 4.28 |
| Colour habits by group or category (health, work, morning…) | 38 | 4.66 |
| Want neutral greys, black or white in the palette | 8 | 4.12 |
| Against per-habit colour, or want to switch it off | 5 | 4.00 |

- **All one colour is confusing.** Habitify users with every habit in blue: "The habits are too confusing because they are all the same blue." `A33#2101`
- **Random colours annoy people.** In Habit – Daily Tracker, colours were assigned automatically: "you can't choose which colour is for which item" `A20#3967`. It was one reason for a 1★ review: "all of mine are still purple" `A20#3912`.
- **Missing colour can lose a user.** "the fact that we can't choose different colors for each task/habit is terrible for me… not likely to continue bc of this." `A85#180` (2★)
- **The few who want plain rows get it by picking one colour for everything.**
  - "I was even able to make all the habits the exact same color as my phone background (gray) so its not distracting." `A1#52481`
  - One ADHD user asked for "the option to not color code" `A59#15009`.
  - Another found colour coding "kind of annoying/not useful" `A33#3638`.
- **"Too many colours" is about whole apps, not rows.** 18 reviews (mean 2.17★) complain about colour overload, but they mean whole-app clutter in Fabulous and Me+: art, badges and pop-ups. None of them are about per-habit row colours.

**2. Tinted rows (T) cause the problems. Colour appearing on a white row (W) does not.**

App Store 1 (Habit Tracker) uses T: every row is a light tint of its colour and darkens as it fills. It is also the app whose fill is praised most (Question 34). Its reviewers name T's specific failures:
- **Text disappears, at both ends.** "the text is always white… barely visible or invisible because the colors of uncompleted tasks are too light… when you complete a task and the color darkens, it becomes much less visible… your 'Today' page will often look like a mess" `A1#53903`
- **Partial and full look alike.** "the shades are not too different, so I can't easily tell if it's a full color (which means a complete habit) or if it's a little bit lighter" `A1#49314`
- **"Lighter" as a signal confuses people.** "the habit is marked with a lighter color than if it was fulfiled. It is confusing and not clear." `A1#43759` (1★)
- **Shade changes are hard to read in general.** "Habits change colors from white to grey to black, and other shades of a custom color. What this color changing means… is not intuitive." `A31#4491`

Across apps, **17 reviews** (mean 3.00★) report tinted or shaded rows that are too light, too dark, unreadable, or impossible to tell apart. Related:
- 4 colour-blind users can't tell states apart when only the colour or shade changes.
- 3 say habit colours turn dull in dark mode.
- 5 want "done" to look clearly different, and complain when completion shows little or no colour.

**On the other side:**
- 2 reviewers suggest tinting: "the tile of each habit can be given light shade of its color" `P9#317`.
- 15 praise colours that deepen with progress, almost all of them on streak heatmaps (Everyday), not on today's rows.

---

## What's reasoned, not shown

- **W's signal is "no colour → colour".** A row reads white when nothing is done, tints as it fills, and is fully tinted when done. That is the strongest contrast available, and it works for any habit colour, in dark mode, and for colour-blind users, because the fill's length also carries the progress.
- **T's signal is "light shade → darker shade" of the same colour.** It is weak for pale colours such as yellow and mint, and weaker still in dark mode.
- **W keeps the morning calm.** Before anything is logged, the screen is neutral rather than a wall of colour. Questions 42 and 43 found people overwhelmed by busy lists.
- **The icon always carries the colour.** Even at 8:00, before anything fills, each row shows its colour in the icon, so the screen isn't colourless.
- **W is also what the platforms do.** Apple and Google keep rows neutral and put colour in the icon (Figma 175:2).
- **Why no fill-style setting:** no review asks for one. A second style doubles every state that has to be designed and tested: empty, partial, done, skipped, dark mode and widget. The palette already gives the minority what they want: grey for everything means plain rows.

---

## What to do

1. **One colour per habit.** Suggest a colour when the habit is created, and let people change it at any time. Never assign colours randomly without a way to change them.
2. **A full palette.**
   - Include greys, black and white.
   - Add a custom colour picker.
   - Keep colours free: 6 reviews complain about colours behind a paywall.
3. **Row fill W:**
   - a white or neutral row;
   - a light tint of the habit's colour, filling from the left as far as today's progress;
   - fully tinted when done;
   - text always in the normal text colour.
   - Keep text at 4.5:1 contrast over the tint (Question 34), and test every palette colour in dark mode.
4. **Colour in the icon, always**, done or not.
5. **Later, maybe:** "use one colour for the whole part of the day". 38 reviewers already colour by group; our parts of the day do that grouping already, so wait for demand.

**Limits.**
- **Language:** only reviews using the English word colour/color were read. Non-English reviews that talk about colour are missed.
- **Keyword retrieval:** reviews that describe row colour without using the word are missed.
- **No direct comparison:** no review compares W and T head-to-head. The T problems come mostly from App Store 1, the main app that uses T.
- **Approximate count:** the 364 praise count is every per-habit-colour review that wasn't coded elsewhere or excluded as off-topic (listed in `NOT_IDENTITY`).
