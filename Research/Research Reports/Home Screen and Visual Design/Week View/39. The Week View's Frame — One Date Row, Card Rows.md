# Week View, Question 39: The Week View's Frame — One Date Row, Card Rows

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 24 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion.

**Screen (old Week board, Figma 19:4).**
- The week-strip calendar sits at the top.
- Below it, a grid whose header repeats the same seven days (F S S M T W T, 18–24).
- Habits are flat rows on the canvas, with names cut off ("Brush teeth A…").

**The feedback.**
1. The strip and the grid's date row show the same week. Which one stays?
2. The week view must be made of cards, not a flat canvas, because themes are a key product and need surfaces.

**Basis.**
- **Reused:**
  - Question 12 (the week strip: 21 reviews read, plus round 1's 595);
  - the Home Screen Cards report (the compact table and week-grid findings);
  - the Theme Pack Design References;
  - the Business Model report (themes are part of Plus).
- **Read in full:** Loop's (Play 3) reviews on columns, density and ticks (27,196 reviews screened).
- **Reasoned from first principles:** tap targets, because 7 tappable days must fit a phone's width.

---

## What to do

**1. Keep the grid's date row; drop the strip in Week view.**
- The date row sits once, pinned under the header: weekday letter and date for each column, with **today** highlighted.
- It lines up exactly with the day cells below.
- Swipe the grid for another week. The title follows the period rule ("This week", "Last week", "7–13 Sep", Question 11).
- Tapping a date opens that day in Day view.

**2. Each habit is a card with two lines:**

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ [icon] Water                     🔥 23 days  │   line 1: who + record
│ [✓] [✓] [5] [✓] [✓] [6] [5]                 │   line 2: the 7 days, full width
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- Line 1: icon, title in full, and the record on the right (the streak, or a quit habit's live counter; Question 41).
- Line 2: seven day cells spread across the card's width, lined up under the date row.

**3. Sections stay, with the same name-only headers as Day view** (Quitting, Anytime, Morning…; Questions 37–38). A habit lives in the same place in both views.

---

## Key findings

**1. Two date rows repeat one week.**
- Question 12 already found the strip's jobs are Day-view jobs: today's progress, reaching recent days, a glance at the week.
- In Week view, the grid's columns **are** the week, so a strip above them only repeats them.
- The date row is the one that has to stay, because the cells need it to be read.
- A strip can't label the columns unless it is aligned to them, and then it has become the date row.

**2. Cards, because themes are sold as surfaces (from our own research and business model).**
- The Business Model report puts theme packs at the core of Plus.
- The theme research describes every direction as styling the **cards**: "Habit cards ki pastel fill", "card skins", "opaque habit cards" over a themed background.
- A flat grid on the canvas leaves a theme only the background and the ticks to change.
- The same theme research warns that "Dense grid lo every card full colour ayithe hierarchy pothundi": a dense grid loses its hierarchy if every card is full colour. So each card stays a calm surface, and the colour goes into the day cells (Question 40).

**3. Two lines, because seven tappable days don't fit beside a name (reasoned from first principles).**
- A day cell must be at least 44 pt to tap reliably (Notion C171; Apple's minimum).
- 7 × 44 pt is 308 pt, which leaves about 50 pt for the icon and name on a 390 pt phone. That is why the old board cut names to "Brush teeth A…".
- Putting the days on their own line gives each cell about 46 pt, keeps names whole, and leaves room on line 1 for the streak.
- **Tappable matters:** the Home Screen Cards report's best-rated multi-habit layout is a "Week grid (habits × 7 days, tickable)". Its 35 complaints include grids that are "not tickable".

**4. Density: two short lines, not big tiles.**
- The compact table is the most-praised layout for seeing "all habits at a glance" (248 praise).
- Big cards draw more complaints than praise (25 against 15), and Loop users ask for more columns and more days ("see last 7 days insted of just last 5 days on app main screen" `P3#9466`; 9 such reviews).
- **So each card stays short:**
  - about 78 pt tall;
  - 7 days, the full week;
  - no third line.

---

**Limits.**
- **Height:** two-line cards fit about 7–8 habits per screen, against 11 in a one-line table. People with long lists will scroll.
- **Test:** whether that is acceptable, or a compact one-line option is needed (Loop users ask for more density).
