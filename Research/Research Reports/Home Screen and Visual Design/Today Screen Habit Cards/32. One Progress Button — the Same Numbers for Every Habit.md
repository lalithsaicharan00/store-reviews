# Today Screen, Question 32: One Progress Button — the Same Numbers for Every Habit

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 24 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

> **Revised by Question 34:** the grey box is gone. The card itself fills as progress, the amount/goal + unit text sits right-aligned before a round outlined button (✓ / + / ▶), and done is a solid circle on a fully tinted card.

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion. Day cards round 2. This report **revises Question 29**.

**The feedback.** Water shows "5/8" and Skincare "0/3", but Read (a timer) shows ▶, and Walk (8,000 steps) shows only ✓. It is one control with four looks. Every habit must show its progress the same way.

**What went wrong in Question 29.** The ring showed a number for some types, an icon for others, and nothing for the rest. So "what does this card say?" had four answers.

**Basis.**
- **Reused:** Question 29's tap-to-add evidence (8 reviews), and the Home Screen Cards report on percentages versus plain figures.
- **Read in full:** 16 new matches on progress numbers on the list.
- **Reasoned from first principles:** how one control can show both progress and the action.

---

## What to do

**One button, the same on every card:** a rounded box, **64 × 44 pt**, always on the right edge. It always holds two lines:
- **Line 1: today's amount / the goal,** for example **5/8**;
- **Line 2: the unit,** for example **glasses**.

Its background fills from the left as progress grows. When the goal is met, it turns dark with a white ✓.

| Habit | Not started | In progress | Done | One tap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water, 8 glasses | 0/8 · glasses | 5/8 · glasses | ✓ 8/8 · glasses | +1 |
| Walk, 8,000 steps | 0/8k · steps | 5.2k/8k · steps | ✓ 8k/8k · steps | enter a number (or Health fills it) |
| Read, 20 min | 0/20 · min | 8/20 · running | ✓ 20/20 · min | start / pause |
| Skincare, checklist of 3 | 0/3 · items | 1/3 · items | ✓ 3/3 · items | ticks the next item |
| Call family, 3× a week | 0/3 · this week | 2/3 · this week | ✓ 3/3 · this week | +1 for today, once a day |
| Meds at 14:00 (a single tick) | ✓ in outline | — | white ✓ on dark | done |
| Skipped (any type) | dashed box, "–" · skipped | | | set from the hold menu |

**Rules that keep it identical everywhere:**
1. **Line 1 is always "amount/goal"** for anything with a goal above 1. A single tick (goal of 1) shows an outline ✓ instead of "0/1".
2. **Done always looks the same:** dark box, white ✓, with the final amount kept ("✓ 8k/8k").
   - A done Walk and a done Water now look alike. Before, one showed ✓ and the other a number.
3. **Line 2 is always the unit, in the plural:** glasses, steps, min, items, this week.
   - For a timer that is counting, it reads **running**. Only the running state changes the unit line, because the timer is the only thing that moves by itself.
4. **Numbers from 1,000 up are shortened with k** (8,000 → 8k, 5,200 → 5.2k), so every value fits the same box.
5. **One tap always does the next step:** done, +1, start or pause, the next item.
   - **Hold** opens the menu: Undo, Skip today, Enter a number, Mark all done, Add a note.
   - After any tap, the same **Undo** bar appears (Question 29).
6. **No ▶, +1 or other type icons on the button.** The unit tells you what kind of habit it is: "min" is a timer, "items" is a checklist.

**Why "items" and not "steps" for checklists.** A checklist of 3 and a walk of 8,000 steps would both say "steps" on the same screen (reasoned). The Day Structure decision's "Add steps" wording should be reconsidered for the same reason.

## Key findings

**1. People ask for "amount of goal" and for tapping to add.**
- "a home screen widget which simply shows the weekly progress of a particular habit, with a "3/5" for example, where the number can by incremented by tapping the widget" `P2#10693`
  - This one reviewer describes the whole rule: the fraction, weekly progress, and tap to add.
- Question 29 found 8 reviews asking for one tap to add one. "tapping a progress bar should increment it, not necessarily complete it" `A1#45243`
- People want progress on the main screen:
  - "the only suggestion that i do have for you is to show progress on the home page somehow" `A48#3508`
  - "I can see the progress on the home screen and it is also measured in different units!" `A1#43900`

**2. Plain figures, not percentages.**
- The Home Screen Cards report found 339 reviews wanting progress on the card.
- It also found 111 that find unexplained percentages confusing, and recommended "a plain figure: '5/7 this week'".
- "amount/goal" with a unit is that plain figure.

**3. A timer is a count of minutes (reasoned from first principles).**
- A 20-minute habit at 8 minutes is 8 of 20, exactly as a count at 5 is 5 of 8.
- Showing ▶ instead hid the progress and made the timer look like a different kind of control.
- The tap still starts it. The box filling and the word "running" show that it is going.
- Notion C066: finishing ticks the habit, and it keeps counting past the goal ("24/20").

**4. Several times a day at set times: one card per time.**
- The Day Structure decision gives morning and evening occurrences separate check states. People think "take my 14:00 meds", not "meds 1 of 3".
- So each time is its own single-tick card in its section, with its time shown (Question 33).
- Several times a day **without** set times (water) is one counting card.
- Either way the button follows rule 1.

**5. One look for "done" helps the most frequent scan (reasoned from first principles).**
- The most frequent question on Today is "what's left?".
- If every finished habit turns into the same dark box, the eye can skip all of them at once, whatever their type.

**Limits.**
- **Reasoned, not reviewed:**
  - the timer's "running" line, the "items" wording and the k-rounding;
  - whether people discover that tapping a "0/20 min" box starts the timer. Test with a first-use hint.
- **Large text (C171):** the box must grow in height, not truncate, at the largest text sizes.
