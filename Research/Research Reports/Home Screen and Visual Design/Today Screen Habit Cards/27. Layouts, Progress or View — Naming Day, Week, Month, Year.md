# Today Screen, Question 27: Layouts, Progress or View — Naming Day, Week, Month, Year

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 24 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion. Day cards round.

**Screen.** The View sheet's first heading is **Layouts** ("How your cards look"). Its tiles are Day list, Week grid, Month calendars and Year heatmap.

**The feedback.** "Layouts" feels wrong. Each tile shows your progress for a period, so should the heading be **Progress**? Use it only if the research supports it.

**Basis.**
- **Counted:** how often people use each word for these screens, across 876,180 habit-app reviews.
- **Read in full:** random samples of 45 "weekly progress"-type and 45 "week view"-type reviews, to see what each phrase refers to.
- **Reasoned from first principles:** the clash with the Progress tab.

---

## What to do

1. **Keep the sheet title "View".** This is the word people use for these screens.
2. **Rename the first heading "Time span"**, with the subtext **"How much progress you see"**.
   - The subtext keeps the user's point: every tile shows progress, over a different length of time.
3. **Shorten the tiles to Day, Week, Month and Year,** each with its small picture.
   - Under the title "View", they read as "Day view", "Week view" and so on. That is exactly how people say it.
4. **Don't use "Progress" as the heading.** The Progress tab already owns that word (see finding 3).

---

## Key findings

**1. People call these screens "views", and almost never "layouts".**

| Phrase in habit-app reviews | Reviews |
|---|---|
| "day / week / month / year view" | 378 |
| "daily / weekly / monthly / yearly progress" | 197 |
| "day / week / month mode" | 12 |
| "day / week / month layout" | 6 |

- "I like that I can switch between the daily, monthly, and yearly views of my habits." `P75#8`
- "Finally an app that has a monthly view for each habit right on the start page." `P69#299`
- **In the "view" sample, 39 of 45 mean exactly this:** habits shown over a period. Five are about widgets and one about a diary.

**2. "Progress" usually means something else.** In the sample of 45:

| What "weekly progress" referred to | Reviews |
|---|---|
| General ("helps me track my daily progress") | 23 |
| A screen showing a period, like ours | 11 |
| Stats, reports or widgets | 11 |

- The period meaning does exist: "quickly add my successful habits in the daily view and then switch to monthly progress to look at the big picture" `A55#1178`
- More often, "progress" names the stats: "There should be ana analysis tab also where we can see our monthly/weekly progress" `P2#10452`
- **So the user's reading is valid, but it isn't the main one.** Most people who say "monthly progress" mean stats. That is what our Progress tab holds.

**3. Two things called Progress would split one question in two (reasoned from first principles).**
- The bottom bar already has a **Progress** tab: stats and history per habit.
- If the sheet also had a "Progress" heading, "where do I see my monthly progress?" would have two answers, a tab and a tile, that show different things.
- One name should mean one thing.

**4. Why "Time span" (reasoned from first principles).**
- The only thing that changes between the tiles is how much time the screen covers: one day, a week, a month or a year.
- Round 3 found that bare time words look like "pick a date" when they sit next to a calendar (Question 15). Here they sit in the View sheet, under a heading that names what changes, with a picture on each tile. That removes the date reading.
- We avoided "Period" because in a health app it also means menstruation.

---

**Limits.**
- **Counts are keyword counts;** only the two 45-review samples were read by hand.
- **"Time span" is reasoned, not taken from reviews.** Check it with a first-read test: show the sheet and ask what the tiles change.
