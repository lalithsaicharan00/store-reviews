# Today Screen, Question 34: The Card Is the Progress — Fill, Text and a Round Button

> **Written by Claude (Claude Code)**, 24 September 2026. Authorship of every report is listed in the [Research Reports index](<../../README.md>).

**Status: research, not a decision.** Decisions live in Notion. Day cards round 3. This report **revises Question 32's box and Question 33's line 2**.

**The feedback.**
- Take the core idea of two apps:
  - Habit Tracker (App Store 1): the card fills with colour as you progress;
  - Done (App Store 31, now "Do Habits"): at the end of each row there is just an icon, with no box.
- Put the progress text ("5/8 glasses") vertically centred near the end, and the button last.
- Is a bare icon enough, or should it sit in a small circle?
- Keep the reminder time beside the streak. Drop "3× a week" and "2× a day".
- The layout can be either:
  - (a) progress text under the title, with the streak moved next to the button;
  - (b) the user's version: streak under the title, progress text beside the button.
- The progress then shows twice (fill and text). Which matters most?

**Basis.**
- **Read in full:**
  - 226 App Store 1 reviews about its filling cards, bars, swiping and dragging;
  - 368 Done reviews about its bars, taps and icons;
  - 32 on tap targets;
  - 15 on accidental ticks while scrolling.
- **Reused:** Notion C024 (streaks), Questions 28–33.
- **External:** NN/g's eye-tracking study of flat, weak-signifier controls; Apple's 44 pt minimum target.

This time the two apps were checked through **their own reviews**, not copied (CLAUDE.md rule).

## What to do

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░░│  ← the card fills
│ [icon]  Water                      5/8 glasses   ( + ) │
│         🔥 23 days   14:00                              │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  left: who and when                  right: how far · the action
```

1. **The card is the progress.**
   - Its background fills from the left, in a light tint of the habit's colour, as far as today's progress: 5 of 8 fills five eighths.
   - When done, the whole card is tinted.
   - No more grey box on the right.
2. **Left: who and when.**
   - Icon, then the title.
   - Under the title: the **streak** in its fixed-width slot, then the **time** at a fixed position after it. The time only appears if the habit has one.
3. **Right: how far, then the action.**
   - **Progress text**, right-aligned and vertically centred: 5/8 glasses, 8/20 min, 5.2k/8k steps, 1/3 items, 2/3 this week.
   - A single tick has no text, and the slot stays empty.
   - Then the **round button**.
4. **A round button, not a bare icon:**
   - a 40 pt circle with a thin outline, 44 pt to tap, always at the right edge;
   - the icon inside says what one tap does:
     - ✓ marks a single tick done;
     - \+ adds one to a count or checklist;
     - ▶ starts a timer, and ❚❚ pauses it while it runs.
   - **Done looks the same for every type:** a solid circle with a white ✓ on a fully tinted card.
   - Skipped: a dashed circle with "–", and the text says "Skipped".
5. **Only the round button logs.** Tapping anywhere else opens the habit. Hold the button for the menu (Undo, Skip, Enter a number, Add a note).
6. **Frequency words go.**
   - A weekly goal's text already says "2/3 this week".
   - A habit done several times a day shows each time as its own card, with its time.
   - So "3× a week" and "2× a day" add nothing.

**Why (b), the user's layout, and not (a).**
- The right-hand column pairs **"how far"** with **"do it"**. You read "5/8 glasses" and tap + right beside it.
- Down the list, the numbers and buttons form one column, and the eye checks progress without moving between lines.
- The left keeps the things that change slowly: the name, the streak, the time.
- Layout (a) would put the streak beside the button. A number with a flame, beside a circle with an icon, is exactly the "icons mean nothing" confusion Done's reviewers describe.

## Key findings

**1. The filling card is the most-praised part of both apps.**
- **App Store 1:**
  - 18 reviews praise the fill itself:
    - "v satisfying to see the full bars when I have completed them" `A1#45600`
    - "seeing an empty bar each morning can motivate you to get started" `A1#45099`
  - 33 more praise swiping it full.
  - One reviewer says the whole card filling is what makes completion visible, and that a thin bar is not enough: "on the Apple Watch when you complete a habit the whole item isn’t coloured in like it does on the phone app" `A1#45581`
- **Done:** 16 reviews praise the bars filling: "It is so satisfying to click on the bars and see them become colorful." `A31#3794`
- **So:** fill the whole card, not a strip.

**2. The fill alone isn't enough: people also want the number.**
- **Done:**
  - "adding a “unit” count beside each bar" was the request that made one reviewer call the app a life changer `A31#729`.
  - Another asked to "add count number in addition to bar graph" `A31#669`.
  - Colour alone confused one reviewer: "Color coding is confusing." `A31#4491`
- **App Store 1:** 4 reviews ask for counts to be visible within the fill.
- **So** the two signals do different jobs:
  - the fill is read at a glance, across the whole list at once;
  - the text gives the exact figure and unit.
- They complement each other. Neither is redundant.

**3. A bare icon is too weak a signal. Use a round button.**
- **Done's minimal controls confused people:**
  - 9 reviews say they couldn't tell how to log, or whether a tap had registered:
    - "Icons mean nothing" `A31#5135`
    - "It's not intuitive to double tap habits to complete them." `A31#5089`
    - "I cannot be sure I tapped the goals or not." `A31#1879`
  - A 5-star review still says "Once you get used to the meanings for where you press a bar or icon, it becomes second nature" `A31#4823`. That means it had to be learned.
- **Across the corpus:**
  - 5 reviews simply can't find how to tick: "I don't know how to check off that I've done something" `P33#1478`
  - 9 say the controls are too small to hit.
- **External:** NN/g found that controls with weak signifiers cost people about 22% more time on the page, because they have to hunt for what is clickable.
- **So:** a thin outlined circle is still minimal, it says "tap me" without being learned, and it gives a clear 44 pt target.

**4. Don't make the whole card the tap target.**
- **Scrolling ticks things by accident:** "This also sometimes happens when I scroll down the list." `A10#17251`. 4 reviews say this.
- **App Store 1's drag-to-fill caused wrong inputs:**
  - "it’s easy to drag the progress bar too far in the habit list" `A1#54079`
  - "a little swipe in the app, made my reading hours record gone wrong" `A1#50586`
  - 10 reviews ask for a tap or button instead of the swipe.
- **So** only the round button logs.
  - The card fill shows progress; it isn't a control.
  - Swiping could come later as an optional shortcut, since 33 reviews love it, but it isn't the main way to log.

**5. Keep the streak on the card; cut the frequency.**
- **Done removed the streak from its rows.** 13 reviews here object; the Notion card C024 counts 52 for that app.
  - "habit doesnt show streak on the button anymore" `A31#2327`
- **Frequency words:** nothing in the evidence asks for them on the card.
  - Weekly progress already reads "2/3 this week".
  - Several-a-day habits already show their time.
  - Removing them follows C006 (stay minimal), reasoned from first principles.

---

**Limits.**
- **Contrast:** text over the tint must keep 4.5:1 contrast, so tints stay light, even for strong habit colours.
- **Reasoned, not reviewed:** the ▶ / + / ✓ icons are an action vocabulary. Test that people read + as "one more" and ▶ as "start the timer".
- **Swipe:** loved by 33 App Store 1 reviewers, but left out for now. Revisit it as an optional shortcut.
