# Home Screen — What People Fundamentally Need

Written by Claude (Claude Code), 21 November 2026.

**The question (the user, 21 Nov 2026):** read the reviews again, one by one, not by keyword, and say what people are fundamentally trying to achieve on a habit app's main screen, whatever the app, ignoring features such as routines or quit habits.

**What was read.** 2,195 reviews, every one read in full and in its own language, and coded by hand:

- **Set A, targeted:** 995 reviews. These are every review already cited as evidence in the Today-screen reports. They are about the home screen by selection.
- **Set B, random:** 1,200 reviews drawn at random (seed 20261121) from the 222,078 habit-app reviews of 150 characters or more. Nobody filtered them by topic. This set shows what comes up when nobody asks.

The map was checked by script: every review coded once, no unknown codes, nothing left out. The codes and the per-review index are in [Home Screen Core Needs Evidence](<Home Screen Core Needs Evidence/Per-Review Index.md>) ([codebook](<Home Screen Core Needs Evidence/Codebook.md>)). Percentages are reviews that mention the need; one review can mention several. Set A shows what people say once the topic is the home screen. Set B shows how common each need is.

## The short answer

People don't come to a habit app for routines, pets or quit counters. Those are only means. Underneath, users show four needs, in this order:

1. **"I forget. Hold it for me."** The app is a second memory: it keeps what I mean to do and puts it in front of me without my having to remember to look.
2. **"Let me see it all at once."** One glance answers "what's on today and what's left". Anything hidden on another page, in a folded group or below the fold gets forgotten.
3. **"Don't make it heavy."** A long or busy list overwhelms, and an overwhelmed person stops opening the app. Small, calm and few beats complete.
4. **"When I do it, let me feel it."** A quick tick that feels good and shows I'm moving, even a little.

Two conditions sit under all four:

- **Be kind when I slip.** Shame makes people quit.
- **Be trustworthy.** A reminder that doesn't fire, or a tick that disappears, breaks the whole deal.

"Checking what's left" is real, but it is one part of need 2. It is not the core. The core is **"I can't hold my day in my head. Carry it for me, show it to me simply, and make doing it feel good."**

How to read the table:

- **Set A puts "see my day" first** because that is how its reviews were chosen. When people talk about the main screen, half of them talk about seeing everything at once.
- **Set B is the honest base rate.** Most random reviews never mention the home screen at all:
  - 216 of 1,200 (18%) are only about money, surprise charges or free limits;
  - the rest are generic praise, mental health and bugs.
- **Among the 654 random reviews that do talk about daily use**, these needs lead:
  - feeling the tick and progress (36%);
  - not too much (29%; "simple" praise is part of this and is the softest signal);
  - being reminded (24%, rising to 32% with "outside the app").
- "Seeing my day" is mostly said by people who already care about the layout.
- **Remembering is the only need that ranks high in both sets.** In the random set, REMEMBER is the single most common code (150 reviews, 12.5%), more common than surprise charges (136) or generic praise (131).

## What each need really is

### 1. "I forget. Hold it for me."

Users show again and again that the app is valued as an outside memory.

- "I m struggling with remembering what I have to do and this app helps me" (B299, `A1#53623`, id 10222020560).
- "instead of writing them down and forgetting they are all right here in front of me daily" (B705, `A48#2852`, id 3601787661).
- "keeps listed items @ the forefront of yr mind" (B678, `A76#6105`, id 1342033056).

It is strongest for people who say their memory or attention works against them: ADHD, autism, amnesia, depression, "mom brain" or an ageing memory. A reviewer with DID keeps the app's widget on the Home Screen "so we always see" it, and it reminds them to eat (B708, `A10#62252`, id 8714603630).

The worst failure is an app that adds one more thing to remember: "The app is supposed to remind me about things, not add another one to remember!" (B631, `P8#13333`, id 38505ba4-ed16-42e4-956a-bea305229c4c).

**Part of the same need: "Did I already do it?"** (VERIFY, 19 in A, 9 in B). An empty mark that could mean "didn't" or "forgot to log" undermines the memory the app is meant to be.

**Reaching people without being opened** (widget, watch, lock screen, notification actions) is how this need is met when the app is closed. "The tool to show your day on the phone's home screen … I wouldn't need to open the app all the time" (B334, `P2#8980`, id 2f80541a-9cdc-4e82-97fd-c68c702c6fbe).

### 2. "Let me see it all at once."

This is the most frequent code in set A: WHOLE_DAY, in 196 reviews and many languages (at a glance, 一目了然, 한눈에, auf einen Blick, наглядно). It comes in three forms:

- **What is on today, in one place.** Users complain when to-dos, routines and habits are split across tabs, pages or folded groups. A forgetful user put it plainly: separate lists mean they forget, and everything must show at once (A426, `A18#889`, id 11917633623).
- **What's left.** WHATS_LEFT: 55 in A, 7 in B.
- **Below the fold is gone.** Big cards, pets that push the list down, ads over rows, or scrolling past many items all make people miss things (B309, B374, B833).

This need is about *not missing anything*, so it serves need 1. People don't want an overview for its own sake. They want to trust that nothing is hiding.

### 3. "Don't make it heavy."

This need is as common as being reminded, and it pulls the other way. Wanting everything visible collides with being overwhelmed by everything.

- "I often feel overwhelmed when I look at all the things I want to accomplish. Skipping them makes me feel guilty and leaving them to rot does the same." The reviewer asks for groups they can fold away (B1096, `A10#36084`, id 12331761333).
- One user had "too many habits logged in my day and I felt overwhelmed", and restarted "one step at a time" (B716, `A24#38810`, id 6216618102).
- A busy screen makes people stop opening the app. One user said that with so much to read on opening, "you'd rather not open it" (B744, `A24#21897`, id 8209334117).
- Some praise a cap. One user said the six-goal limit forces you to think about what matters: better six of six than six of twenty (B358, `A23#1641`, id 1457266158).

People resolve the tension in three ways:

- grouping or time of day, so they see only a part;
- one thing at a time;
- done items getting out of the way (DONE_AWAY: 83 in A). A meaningful minority want done items kept as proof of effort on bad days (DONE_KEEP: 19 in A; A934, `P84#29899`).

The fundamental need is **to see all of it without feeling all of it.** Routines and sections are one way of meeting it, not the need itself.

### 4. "When I do it, let me feel it."

This is the largest daily-use theme in the random set.

- "The micro-hits of dopamine each time you do something and check it off" (B1031, `P65#1994`, id 707933b8-d388-4366-ba16-5c37ddef071f).
- "When I finished nobody told me anything … then I found this app" (B965, `A24#6798`, id 6107864070).

It has three parts:

- **The tick is quick and on the main screen.** Fiddly swipes, forced quotes after each tick and unresponsive taps are complained about (B499, B811).
- **The tick lands somewhere.** A sound, a fill, a pet's energy.
- **I can see I'm moving.** A percentage, a streak, a week filling up.

Pets, games and celebrations are this need in costume.

### 5. Kind when I slip

Small in count (about 4–5% in each set), but it decides whether people stay.

- What people praise: "not penalized … You can just complete it the next day" (B760, `A10#43623`); a percentage instead of all-or-nothing for "someone who suffers from perfectionism" (A617, `A41#350`); "not get hung up on all-or-nothing thinking" (B674, `A76#5075`).
- A few like a little pressure: "just the right level of guilt" (B1049, `A48#420`). So the need is *no shame*, not *no stakes*.

### 6. Trustworthy

In the random set, broken basics (12% of reviews) count for more than any single feature. The failures are:

- reminders that never come;
- ticks that vanish ("They disappear in front of my eyes and I can't remember which were filled in", B1171, `A46#1806`);
- streaks lost to an update ("Who can possibly remember the last TWELVE DAYS?", B813, `A13#12466`);
- data lost when changing phones.

Each one breaks need 1, because people stop being able to rely on the app as their memory.

## Surface vs fundamental

| What reviews ask for (surface) | The need underneath |
|---|---|
| Routines, morning/evening sections, categories | See all of it without feeling all of it (3), in the shape of my day (5) |
| Widgets, watch, lock screen, notification Done | Hold it for me, even when the app is closed (1) |
| Pets, games, sounds, confetti | Let me feel it when I do it (4) |
| Streaks, percentages, charts | Show me I'm moving (4), but kindly (6) |
| Done rows sink or hide | Don't make it heavy (3); a few want proof kept (4) |
| "Show only what's due" | Don't make it heavy (3); don't count it against me (6) |
| Backfill, fix a day | Keep my record true (1, 7) |
| Quit counters, "days since" | Hold it for me and show I'm moving (1, 4) |

## What this means for Today (reasoned from the evidence above)

- **Today's first job is to be the person's outside memory.** Today must:
  - put what's due in front of them;
  - answer "did I do it?" at a glance;
  - reach them when they don't open the app.
- **Show the whole day, made light.** Everything must be on one screen, with nothing hidden on other pages. Structure, a calm look and done items stepping back keep it from feeling heavy.
- **One quick tick that lands.** It must be instant and on the row, with a small, real response and visible movement.
- **No shame, and never break the memory.** Reliability is part of the core, not a nice-to-have.

## Limits

- One person read and coded every review (Claude, in one pass per batch). The codes are judgement calls; the per-review index lets anyone check them.
- Set A was built from earlier reports' citations, so it over-represents layout talk by design. Set B corrects for that but is long-review-only (150+ characters), so very short reviews are not represented.
- Set B is weighted toward the apps with the most long reviews. In the random draw these are Fabulous, Finch and Me+, so payment complaints and mental-health talk are heavier than in a tracker-only corpus.
- "SIMPLE" in set B is often bare praise and is the softest signal in need 3.
