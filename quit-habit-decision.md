# Should quit-habits be a feature or a separate app?

**Recommendation: build it as a feature. Do not ship a generic "quit tracker" app.**

Evidence below is from 56,653 reviews of the category's #1 app, 10,621 reviews of the
leading quit tracker, and search results across the App Store.

---

## 1. The generic quit market has a low ceiling

Standalone quit apps, US ratings:

| App | US ratings |
|---|---|
| Days Since: Quit Habit Tracker | 19,073 |
| Quitzilla: Quit Tracker | 1,473 |
| Quit Bad Habits & Addiction | 1,061 |
| Days Without: Break Bad Habits | 6 |
| ZeroBadHabits | 0 |

One app has traction. The rest are near zero. That is not a market with room in it —
that is one incumbent and a graveyard.

## 2. The real money is in verticals, not "quit anything"

| App | US ratings |
|---|---|
| I Am Sober | 186,328 |
| Smoke Free | 57,263 |
| Reframe: Drink Less | 44,339 |
| Sober Time | 41,632 |
| Nomo — Sobriety Clocks | 16,398 |
| QuitNow! | 13,572 |

These beat every generic quit app by 3-10x. Someone quitting smoking searches
"quit smoking", not "quit tracker" — and they want lung-recovery milestones, craving
support and a community, none of which a generic counter provides.

So a generic quit app loses on both sides: too vague for the vertical searcher, and
redundant for the habit-tracker user.

## 3. Quit is already becoming table stakes

8 of the top 40 habit trackers explicitly market breaking bad habits — including
Habit Tracker (#1), HabitKit, Streaks and Simple Streak.

**The #1 app already ships a Quit tab.** Shipping it doesn't differentiate you.
*Not* shipping it is starting to look like a gap.

## 4. Users are not asking for it — because the leader already has it

Of 56,653 reviews of the #1 app, only **164 (0.29%)** mention quitting. But reading
them, they are overwhelmingly **complaints about the existing quit feature**, not
requests for one:

> "the streak counter is sometimes wrong for Quit habits"
> "It is not possible to track if you want to quit drinking alcohol. You can only choose a max period of 1 month with a 2 drink minimum?"
> "When habits are set to 'Quit' with 0 per day — nothing is readable because of the all dark colors"
> "I click the QUIT habit tab, I get the form error"
> "Bad for quitting habits"

**That is the actual opportunity.** The leader ships quit-habits badly. Doing it
properly is a differentiator inside your existing app — and costs you nothing in
downloads, because the traffic is already coming for habit tracking.

## 5. The two audiences barely overlap

Only **2.4%** of Days Since reviewers ask for good-habit building. Quit users mostly
want one thing: a clean day counter that does not reset.

This cuts both ways. It means a combined app will not cannibalise a quit app — but it
also means a separate quit app gains you almost no cross-sell. Two apps, two ASO
efforts, two support queues, for two groups that don't migrate.

## 6. What people actually quit

From 10,621 Days Since reviews:

| Category | Share of reviews |
|---|---|
| Alcohol | 5.0% |
| Smoking / vaping | 3.7% |
| **Social media / phone** | **2.0%** |
| Sugar / junk food | 1.0% |
| Weed / drugs | 0.7% |
| Nail biting, caffeine, gambling | 0.6% |
| Porn | 0.2% |

Alcohol and smoking are locked up by the verticals. **Social media and phone use are
not** — no large dedicated app owns it, and it's the third-biggest quit reason. That's
the one quit niche with genuine room.

---

## What to do

**In your habit app, build quit as a first-class habit type:**

1. **Days-since counter, not a streak.** Quitting is "days clean", not "did it today".
2. **A relapse must not wipe history.** The most-repeated complaint in the Days Since
   corpus. Log the slip, keep the record, show best-ever vs current.
3. **Allowances, not just zero.** "2 drinks a week" is a real goal; the leader caps at
   one month and users complain loudly about it.
4. **Milestones with meaning.** 1/7/30/90 days, plus money saved — Days Since's
   happiest reviews (4.77 average) are milestone screenshots.
5. **Readable in dark mode.** A literal, fixable complaint about the #1 app.

**Do not** build a generic quit app. It would compete with your own app for the same
keywords, cap out around 19k ratings, and read as a repackage.

**If you later want a second app, make it vertical and genuinely different** — a
phone/social-media quit app, given that niche is unclaimed. That is a different
product with different content, not a reskin, so it wouldn't look like spam.

## The bonus: keyword surface

Days Since ranks **#3 overall** in this category on ranking strength — #1 in 10 rich
markets — purely on quit terms. Adding a proper quit mode lets one app compete for
"quit habit", "break bad habits" and "sobriety tracker" alongside habit keywords,
without splitting your ASO across two listings.
